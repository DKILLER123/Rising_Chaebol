## 5. WRITING & DIALOGUE RULES (CRITICAL)

1. **NO speaker tags.** Never "Eun-jung said:", never "Park Ji-yeon shouted:". Use action beats and voice-distinct diction so readers always know who speaks. The original already does this — keep it.
2. **Dialogue formatting in XHTML:** each spoken paragraph uses `class="dialogue-line"`. Multi-sentence speeches by the same speaker stay in ONE paragraph. Rapid back-and-forth = separate short paragraphs.
3. **Natural K-drama dialogue:** contractions, interruptions ("I— okay, fine"), emotional subtext, playfulness. Avoid stiff machine-translation phrasing. Sound like a Netflix K-drama subtitle script at its best.
4. **Honorifics preserved** as listed above. Eun-jung calls him "Song Ji-yeon-ssi" early, "Ji-yeon-ssi", later "oppa" once feelings develop (ch.71+). T-ara members call him "oppa" (they're all born 1986-1989 vs his 1990... NOTE: actually several are older — So-yeon/Qri/Bo-ram are older than him, so THEY would say "Ji-yeon-ssi" or "Song Ji-yeon-ssi"; Eun-jung (1988) is older too → she uses "Ji-yeon-ssi"; Ji-yeon/Hyo-min(1989)/ maknae line can use "oppa". Follow the original Chinese (xi/欧巴) as the guide: 恩静 uses -ssi until romance deepens; 智妍 uses oppa; 雪莉 uses oppa; 智媛 uses -ssi; 荷拉 uses oppa-ssi or oppa; 允儿 uses -ssi; 珠泫 uses -ssi.)
5. **MC's inner monologue:** keep his snarky fan-boy commentary (e.g. "White-haired Eun-jung, forever the GOAT!" style jokes → localize as K-pop stan humor: "bias", "ult", "visual", "stan"). He was a fan — let it show.
6. **Romance/intimate scenes:** DO NOT fade to black. The original writes sensual, emotionally-detailed scenes (kisses, undressing, the night together in ch.61) that stop short of explicit anatomy. Translate with full sensory and emotional fidelity — heat, trembling, breath, heartbeat — elegant but visceral, R-rated romance-novel register, never pornographic. Keep every beat.
7. **Chinese junk / ads / redundancy:** the source is clean; still, remove any leftover junk lines, repeated sentences (the source occasionally duplicates a paragraph at chapter boundaries — keep ONE), and translate-nothing weirdness.
8. **Numbers/currency:** ₩ for KRW ("₩400 million", "₩4 billion"). 1亿 = 100 million; 10亿 = 1 billion. Keep won amounts exact.
9. **Chapter titles:** translate the numbered title meaningfully & punchily (see per-batch title list). Chapter numbers in the header use "Chapter One/Two/…" word form via .chapter-number.
10. **Scene breaks:** use the `.scene-break` paragraph (`✦ ✦ ✦` between scenes when the original jumps).
11. **System messages** (`【…】`) → `.system-block` markup (template below). **KakaoTalk chats** → `.chat-container`. **Phone calls** → `.phone-call`. **Web comments/netizen posts** → `.comment-thread`. **Text messages** → `.chat-container` or `.app-screen`. **Song lyrics on stage** → `.lyric-block`/`.performance-block`. **Flashbacks** → `.memory-block`. News/media → `.news-digest`. Use them whenever the context appears — this book is half showbiz-media texture.

## 7. QUALITY BAR (what "Version 3" means)

- Reads like a professionally translated K-drama light novel: flowing prose, punchy pacing, emotional interiors, comedic timing.
- NO machine-translation stink: vary sentence length, cut redundant restatements, make pop-culture references land naturally in English.
- Preserve every plot beat, every joke's intent, every emotional beat — you may rephrase freely but never drop content or add new plot.
- 1 Chinese paragraph ≈ 1 English paragraph (may merge micro-paragraphs for flow; keep dialogue paragraphs separate).
- Emotional depth in romance scenes (rule 5.6 above): full sensory fidelity, tasteful.
- Keep Korean romanization standard (Revised Romanization): e.g. "Apgujeong", "Cheongdam-dong", "Gangnam-gu".
