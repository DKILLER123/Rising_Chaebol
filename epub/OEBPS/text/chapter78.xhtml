Chapter Seventy-Eight

A Good Sport, That Goo Hara

SATURDAY NIGHT · STILL GRILLING Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Umi barbecue house — the inner room

A couple!

Goo Hara had watched the whole dinner with her own eyes, and now the word spun several laps around her mind without asking permission.

On the phone, Sulli had said this oppa traded like a genius — that the stocks he’d picked for her had made serious money. But sitting here, watching the two of them live and in person, Hara couldn’t rule out the love-goggles hypothesis. A peach in love thinks her man is right about everything; whatever he says, she files it under scripture. And once those goggles are on, “the Oracle of Seoul” is the kind of thing you can say with a completely straight face.

So the question stood: was the man genuinely brilliant — or a boyfriend being inflated by a smitten girlfriend?

But with Hara’s personality, asking that out loud was impossible. So she weighed her words, gram by gram.

“Sulli’s told me you run her stock account — that the returns are very good. I have some idle funds of my own I’d like to put to work. About one-point-five billion won. That would buy a commercial property outright — rent of seven, eight million a month. Honestly… property carries far less risk than stocks.”

The load-bearing sentence was the last one. Translation: I can buy a building, lie flat, and collect seven or eight million won a month — close to a hundred million a year — without a single heartbeat of risk. Stocks are the gamble. It was a public-service announcement, addressed over Song Ji-yeon’s head directly to Sulli, in case the girl was about to volunteer her unnie’s money to a stranger: unnie doesn’t need a wealth manager.

Song Ji-yeon heard every layer of it. He genuinely didn’t mind.

He had never planned to manage her account in the first place.

But he did quietly revise an estimate. The little mermaid — tiny as a package — was carrying real money.

Which tracked. Just as Yoon-ah out-earned everyone in SNSD — people called her the landlady of the Peninsula — Goo Hara was KARA’s top earner. A private vault of this size was simply the industry’s normal bookkeeping.

Idly, a question surfaced: could the Handbook even bind her…?

Purely as a curiosity question, to be clear — what bond would she roll? What ability? Anyone who has ever played a collectible-card game knows the condition: you see a card, and you need to see its stats. And not just her. Him, all of them — every goddess on the planet. Pity his collection currently stood at three.

Though honestly, you couldn’t blame him. The Handbook’s binding requirement was making a goddess’s heart flutter — and for a man whose combined dating record across two lifetimes read a flat zero, that bar stood somewhere near the moon.

“Wait—” Sulli swallowed her beef, staring at her unnie with open shock. “Only seventy, eighty million a year…?”

The truth was, Sulli had never cared about investing. Her first trip to the exchange had been pure whim — her unnis were putting idle money to work, so she’d copy them, and before that, “investment” was a word that happened to other people. Then oppa had appeared, and oppa said buy, so she bought, and thinking had never once been load-bearing. The actual quality of his calls had simply never crossed her mind.

But now, hearing unnie’s property math — she ran a quiet audit of her own. One month. She counted on her fingers, inwardly, and the figures lined up like ducks.

One Month of Oppa — a private audit

Samsung Electronics₩100,000,000 in · +17% over one month → +₩17,000,000

Sewon Electronics₩300,000,000 in · +20% in two days → +₩60,000,000

Total+₩77,000,000 — in one month

Gulp.

Seventy-seven million won. One month. That was practically a full year of rent on unnie’s one-and-a-half billion.

“What!” Hara’s brows pulled together. “Is seven or eight million a month SMALL to you? It’s less than we make per show, yes — but it’s stable. It doesn’t disappear just because next month has no schedule.”

An idol with no schedule earns exactly zero — Hara knew that arithmetic better than anyone. f(x) was riding high right now, calendars stuffed wall to wall; of course seven million looked cute to the girl. Give it a few years. Who could promise a seven-million-won month then? This peach had been famous for what, two minutes, and already— As the unnie present, she had a duty to say something.

“No— no! Unnie’s got it wrong!” Both of Sulli’s hands went up, waving the misunderstanding away.

She grabbed the black leather bag off the empty chair beside her, wrestled the zipper open, dug, produced her phone, swiped twice, tapped the brokerage app’s red-and-white icon, typed in a six-digit PIN — account overview. She didn’t so much as glance at it. She just held it out.

“Unnie. See for yourself.”

Goo Hara took the phone. Her gaze dropped to the screen: a trading summary, awash in red and green. Across the top, one bold line of black type — total assets, and the month’s cumulative gains.

Her eyes snagged on the gains figure.

Ones, tens, hundreds, thousands… ten-thousands… hundred-thousands… millions… ten-millions.

She counted them the way you count stairs in the dark. Then her vision did a small, quiet swim.

₩77,000,000.

“Hold on.” This time it was Hara’s turn for the open-mouthed stare. “How did you make this much in one month?”

In her head, Sulli was still a kid who hadn’t finished growing. Stock profits, sure — she’d assumed pocket change and beginner’s luck. A single month of gains standing level with her property’s entire year of rent — no wonder the girl had reacted to “property beats stocks” like that. If Hara could pull this much out of the market every month, she’d look down on seven or eight million in rent too.

Her gaze slid, without anyone’s permission, toward Song Ji-yeon. This could not be Sulli’s doing. It was all him. She replayed the phone call — I know an oppa, he’s really good at investing!

You said good. You did not say this.

That’s not making money. That’s running an excavation on a gold mountain.

Song Ji-yeon found himself on the receiving end of her stunned stare and got a little sheepish about it. He scrubbed a hand through his hair. “Luck. Pure luck.”

“It is NOT luck.” Sulli shook her head hard enough to endanger the beret. “Oppa is the Oracle of Seoul.”

He laughed and shook his head. Not that he was trying to run modesty as a personal brand: a returner starts the game with a joker taped to his forehead, and on top of that there were the Forecast Cards. On the subject of stocks, honestly — the Oracle of Omaha could carry his bag.

Across the table, Hara was busy revising a second estimate: she hadn’t expected modest. A man who could pull that kind of money out of the market inside one month should, by the natural law of men, be viewing the world through his nostrils by now.

So he really was good. And Sulli hadn’t been inflating the man she liked.

She regretted her little speech — he had absolutely caught the subtext, she knew he had. But a good woman knows when to bend. She pressed her lips together, turned, and looked him square in the face.

“Song Ji-yeon-oppa — would you manage mine too?!”

“Right! Oppa, manage unnie’s account too!” Sulli, seeing the gap in his defenses, clamped onto his arm and shook it for emphasis. “You’re already managing one of us — what’s one more? And we’ll pay you a management fee!”

Song Ji-yeon, meanwhile, had locked up completely.

Weren’t you the one who didn’t need a wealth manager?

Had he misparsed the entire barbecue course?
