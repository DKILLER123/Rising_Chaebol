Chapter One Hundred Fourteen

Hungry? I’ll Make You Something Downstairs

WEDNESDAY MORNING ShiningYouth Entertainment — T-ara’s practice room Two hours of choreography, six empty stomachs, one delivery still missing

The music cut out.

All six members of T-ara collapsed onto the wooden floor as if somebody had pulled the plug on the entire room.

Two straight hours of high-intensity practice had soaked them through. Their shirts clung to their backs, their breathing came in rough bursts, and every muscle below the waist had started filing formal complaints.

REHEARSAL · AFTER THE TENTH RUN “SWEAT, MIRRORS, REPEAT”

The Crown between takes — no stage lights, no camera, only the honest shine of a morning spent earning the choreography.

Top — a charcoal short-sleeve rehearsal shirt, darkened at the collar and close across the shoulders

Bottom — black athletic leggings built for movement, not for flattering anyone’s appetite

Hair — tied back and already escaping at the temples

Carry — one white towel, repeatedly promoted from prop to emergency equipment

The look of an idol who has run the robot formations until the body remembers them again — sweat first, glamour later

Practice-room rule: if the floor is comfortable enough to lie on, someone will lie on it. Usually all six.

Jeon Bo-ram had flattened her small body into a perfect starfish, chest rising and falling hard beneath her thin T-shirt.

“I’m done. I’m completely done. My legs aren’t mine anymore. And this floor may be better than the old one, but it’s still hard when you collapse on it…”

Park Hyo-min sat cross-legged, snatched up a towel, and scrubbed at her face with no particular plan beyond removing the sweat.

“Seriously. I’m exhausted. So-yeon-unnie, when is the delivery you ordered getting here? If it takes much longer, I’m going to faint on this floor.”

“Lovey-Dovey” and “Sexy Love” were both fast-tempo title tracks. They demanded stamina, and the six of them had been drilling them since nine that morning. Ten-something full runs in roughly two hours had left everyone hungry enough to eat the practice-room mirrors.

Park So-yeon sat with her back against the mirror. She laughed despite herself.

“I called less than twenty minutes ago. How could it be here already? The bossam and jokbal place is famous for its queue. Be patient.”

As T-ara’s leader, So-yeon usually handled practical matters like ordering food.

“Aigoo, it takes that long?”

Hyo-min groaned and rolled onto her back.

“If I’d known, I would’ve ordered jajangmyeon and fried chicken.”

✦ ✦ ✦

So-yeon laughed and hooked a foot against Hyo-min’s calf.

“You’re the one who insisted on bossam and jokbal because you were craving them.”

“Unnie, it’s a practice day. If we don’t eat properly, where are we supposed to get the strength to dance?”

Park Ji-yeon crawled over to join the argument, her voice full of immediate agreement.

“Exactly! Hyo-min-unnie is right. How can we dance without eating enough?”

So-yeon could only smile helplessly. The group had several professional eaters, and she had long ago accepted that logic would not defeat them.

“Just wait a little longer. It should be here soon.”

Lee Qri fanned her face with one hand, her long fox eyes narrowing as she looked around the room.

“But seriously, it’s been a long time since all six of us gathered for a proper rehearsal like this. When we started ‘Sexy Love’ this morning, my hands and feet felt like they belonged to somebody else.”

They had not rehearsed properly in more than a month, not since the Jeju concert. Qri had almost forgotten how loud the others could be when they were together.

She used to think the noise was irritating. Today, it felt good.

“Exactly!”

Bo-ram nodded so hard that her hair bounced.

“I felt the same way. I nearly collided with Hyo-min during the robot formation. We’ve gone so long without a full-group rehearsal that the choreography has gotten rusty. Even my muscle memory is protesting.”

Ham Eun-jung let out a quiet breath.

“A lot has happened lately…”

The bullying scandal, the Black Ocean at Jeju, and all six members transferring their contracts to ShiningYouth had happened within barely two months. T-ara’s recent history had been one rough turn after another.

But Eun-jung believed things would get better. She believed Song Ji-yeon would pull them out of the whirlpool of public opinion and put them back on top.

The thought brought a smile back to her face.

“It feels so good to be back in the practice room, sweating like this. We need to rebuild our timing before next week’s Japan performance.”

Park Ji-yeon suddenly remembered a different problem from the morning’s rehearsal. Her mouth pushed into a pout.

“We do need to rebuild our timing… but, Eun-jung-unnie, shouldn’t you rein in your range of motion a little?”

She had nearly been hit by Eun-jung’s fists several times.

“Unnie! You were boxing! There were several turns where I didn’t dare get close because I was afraid you’d swing an arm around and punch me in the face!”

The exhausted room exploded into laughter.

Hyo-min slapped her thigh, laughing so hard she rocked backward.

“Ji-yeon is right! Eun-jung-unnie danced like she had the strength of a whole ox herd left over. The energy was incredible — you looked ready to start a fight.”

Eun-jung’s cheeks colored under the sisters’ attack. She rubbed her forehead and tried to defend herself with what dignity remained.

“Chincha, Ji-yeon-ah, stop slandering me. I was being careful. Was it really that exaggerated?”

When the others only laughed harder, she raised a fist and gave Ji-yeon a threatening little shake.

“Keep accusing me and I’ll really punch you later.”

“Aigoo! Eun-jung-unnie is attacking me! Somebody save me!”

Ji-yeon darted behind Qri, peeking out to make a face.

Eun-jung got to her feet, caught the little dragon, and the two of them rolled across the floor in a tangle of laughter and mock violence.

Once the noise settled, Qri remembered Ji-yeon’s complaint and looked back toward So-yeon.

“So-yeon, are the aftereffects of your accident really gone? You’ve been a little behind the beat today. Your movements are too small — almost limp.”

The practice room quieted at once.

Eun-jung stopped wrestling with Ji-yeon and sat up, staring at So-yeon. She had noticed it too. They had lived together and worked together for more than three years; the members knew one another too well to miss a change that obvious.

So-yeon was not the strongest dancer in the group, but she could always keep up and hit her marks. Today she had been late to the beat and soft in the arms, practically marking time.

So-yeon’s body stiffened. She tucked a loose strand of hair behind her ear and gave them an embarrassed smile.

“What? I recovered ages ago. I just haven’t done any high-intensity exercise for more than a month. Jumping straight into this much practice made my whole body feel weightless, and my stamina hasn’t caught up yet. Give me two more days and I’ll be fine. Don’t worry.”

She sounded certain. Inside, she was not.

The damaged area still ached whenever she made a larger movement. Her stamina had not returned, and when she danced too hard, her breathing went uneven. As a main vocalist, uneven breathing was not a small problem.

So she had chosen to mark the choreography when she needed to, conserving enough strength to sing properly.

She lied because she did not want the others to worry. This Japan schedule was a chance they had finally earned, and she could not bear to make the members who had only just begun smiling again look over their shoulders at her.

Qri and Eun-jung still looked ready to ask more.

So-yeon’s phone vibrated.

Bzzzt.

“Aigoo, that must be the delivery!”

She sprang upright as if her phone had thrown her a lifeline.

“The front desk must be telling us to come down. You all rest here — I’ll collect it.”

She did not give anyone time to object before hurrying toward the practice-room door.

Saved. If I stayed another minute, Qri and Eun-jung would dig all the way to the bone.

Qri was too sharp not to hear that something was wrong. Eun-jung knew So-yeon too well after all those years as roommates. She would know a lie when she heard one.

Ji-yeon watched So-yeon leave and muttered under her breath.

“Gulp. So-yeon-unnie must be starving. She ran faster than I would have.”

Hyo-min and Bo-ram were already thinking about food and missed the subtext. Qri and Eun-jung exchanged one look and understood each other immediately.

So-yeon was forcing herself through it.

Neither said anything in front of the three members still happily discussing lunch. Once the Japan trip was over, they would take So-yeon to a hospital and make sure she got a proper examination.

✦ ✦ ✦

Not long afterward, So-yeon returned with several enormous delivery bags.

They had not stopped at bossam and jokbal. There were spicy rice cakes, sliced bread, hand-cut noodles, and another seven or eight odds and ends packed into the order.

“Food’s here! Come help!”

She put the bags in the middle of the floor and greeted everyone with forced brightness.

“Finally!”

Ji-yeon pounced first and began opening plastic bags at professional speed.

“It smells amazing! Bossam is my favorite!”

Hyo-min wiggled over and sat beside her, helping Ji-yeon and So-yeon open the containers. Bo-ram and the others gathered around, and soon all six were sitting in a circle, eating as if they had not seen food in a week.

They talked around mouthfuls about the choreography, pointing out the turns that still felt strange and the transitions that needed another run.

So-yeon ate two bossam wraps, then attacked the vegetable salad with the enthusiasm of someone filing a complaint against it.

Hyo-min and Ji-yeon praised every bite of the feast. So-yeon looked down at the bowl of leaves in front of her and felt her spirit leave her body.

She had always gained weight easily. A few extra bites went straight to her face. Now that they had changed agencies, Song Ji-yeon was not demanding that they maintain the harsh weight rules Kim Kwang-soo had used.

But they were about to perform publicly in Japan. An idol still needed some idol discipline.

She had gained five kilograms during the two months of rest and looked visibly softer. She had to lose it within the week.

So she had eaten two bites and was now sitting beside her water, stomach growling, while Hyo-min rolled pork into lettuce and mixed-radish salad.

Dance until my legs stop belonging to me, ache like I’ve been run over, and I still can’t have one proper meal? What kind of life is this?

Hyo-min noticed the longing stare without even looking up. As the member blessed with a metabolism that seemed immune to food, she knew the expression well.

Whenever So-yeon dieted, she watched Hyo-min eat with the sorrow of a woman viewing a feast through glass.

Today, Hyo-min decided to tease her.

She turned away with exaggerated care, stuffed an entire bossam wrap into her mouth, and puffed out both cheeks like a squirrel with a winter supply.

Then she turned back, chewing with great effort and wide innocent eyes.

“Unnie… munch, munch… why are you staring at me? Do you want some too? You can’t have any, though.”

So-yeon’s mouth twitched.

“Eat your food. Be careful you don’t choke.”

She turned away from the table’s many temptations, took a long drink of warm water, and attacked her salad with wounded dignity.

Bo-ram suddenly remembered Song Ji-yeon.

“Eun-jung, aren’t you going to invite Song Ji-yeon to eat? It’s lunchtime. He probably hasn’t eaten either, has he?”

There was too much food, So-yeon was barely eating, and they would not finish it all. Calling Ji-yeon to share seemed perfectly normal.

It isn’t because I want to see Song Ji-yeon. It’s because wasting food is wrong.

Eun-jung nodded. She did not know whether he had eaten; the morning had been consumed by rehearsal, and neither of them had messaged.

If Bo-ram had not reminded her, she might have gone all day without contacting him.

She reached for her phone.

Ji-yeon stopped her with a knowing look.

“Oppa is probably out apartment hunting with Han So-hee. He isn’t even at the company. A message from you might not get an answer.”

The sourness in her voice was impossible to miss.

You finally think of messaging him now? I told you to make a move. You didn’t. It’s too late.

Eun-jung paused, then smiled.

“Oh. He’s helping So-hee look at apartments? Then it’s fine. We can eat by ourselves.”

Ji-yeon stared at her.

“Yah, unnie! I told you not to call, and you really aren’t going to?”

She had expected Eun-jung to argue. Instead, Eun-jung simply put the phone down.

“If Song Ji-yeon has something important to do, let him do it. His work matters.”

Ji-yeon nearly choked on her own disbelief.

Apartment hunting with that little fox? What kind of important work is that? When Eun-jung-unnie loses oppa for good, she’ll have plenty of time to cry. This family still needs me.

Bo-ram looked from one face to the next.

JEON BO-RAM · INTERNAL BULLETIN BOARD

1Who is Han So-hee?

2How did everyone learn about a person I have never heard of?

3Why am I being left out of my own group’s intelligence briefing?

Bo-ram had, once again, discovered that the rest of T-ara possessed a private information network to which she had not been granted access.

✦ ✦ ✦

WEDNESDAY · NOON Han So-hee’s new apartment, Gaepo-dong Moving boxes cleared, refrigerator stocked, one bed under inspection

At the same time, Song Ji-yeon had taken Han So-hee back to his large apartment to collect the things she had packed. They had stopped at a nearby shopping center afterward to buy household supplies.

He paid for everything.

So-hee did not have much money, and she agonized over every purchase. Song Ji-yeon eventually lost patience and simply bought everything she needed himself.

They carried the bags to the new apartment, put the food in the refrigerator, arranged the kitchenware, placed the toiletries in the bathroom, and made the bed. Only then did the place begin to look lived in.

NEW ADDRESS · FIRST AFTERNOON “THE QUIET HOUSE”

The actress’s first frame outside the basement — clean light, new dishes, and a room that has not yet learned her habits.

Top — a pale-blue short-sleeve home T-shirt, clean and plain by deliberate choice

Bottom — soft charcoal lounge shorts, practical enough for unpacking and sitting still

Hair — long and dark, worn loose after a day of moving

Set — a small table, new bowls, stacked boxes and the first signs of an independent home

No borrowed room, no damp walls, no landlord’s deadline — only a quiet apartment and the unsettling freedom of having a door that belongs to her

The first household expense was paid by the company. The first feeling of ownership was hers.

Song Ji-yeon lay back on So-hee’s bed. He was not tired; he was testing whether the mattress was comfortable.

He checked his phone. It was already noon. Neither of them had eaten breakfast, and the hunger finally reached him.

“I’m hungry. Are you? We should go out and eat something together to celebrate your new home.”

There was no answer.

He had just started to sit up and see what she was doing when a figure dropped onto him and pushed him back against the mattress.

By the time he gathered his thoughts, they were already wrapped around each other. Han So-hee knelt over his waist, bent down, and breathed against his ear.

“I’m hungry too. So… should I make you something downstairs?”
