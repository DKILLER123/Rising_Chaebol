Chapter Seventy-Six

Hyo-min’s Method for Picking Men

SATURDAY · PAST NOON Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Unit 201 — Park Ji-yeon’s flat, the study

The quiet lasted exactly as long as it took Park Ji-yeon to reach the study door, at which point she dove through it like a woman possessed, already reaching for the chair.

Song Ji-yeon watched her go, and the question assembled itself against his will: was this… actually an idol? The profession kept printing certificates, and yet what stood in that doorway looked increasingly like a straight-up gaming addict.

Actually — he was fairly sure that in his last life he had scrolled past clips of the little dragon gaming. Fan-shot, subtitled, the works. The word addict had been right there in the titles.

…So, confirmed. Diagnosed years early, and now, apparently, terminal.

Park Ji-yeon cared nothing for his assessment. The PC existed. She existed. Everything else could queue.

Of the black-and-white pair, she claimed the white chair. She dropped into it, dialed the height with two practiced clicks, and hit the power button. The machine woke clean; the desktop slid in without a stutter; she hummed her satisfaction like an inspector signing off a bridge.

Browser open. Downloads, one by one: League of Legends. MapleStory. KartRider. The progress bars sprinted — this connection was a different species from the dorm’s — and her mouth curved up on its own as they filled.

And she did not forget her partner. She leaned over, woke the second machine too, and queued the identical games on it, one by one. A duo shared a library. That was simply the law.

“He’s going to praise me so much when he sees I set his up too.”

She giggled at nobody, thoroughly pleased with herself, leaned back into the new chair, and watched the bars fill.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the living room, Song Ji-yeon had not followed her in.

His morning, itemized: doorbell, eight-thirty. Yongsan. The mall. The bedding run. The hauling. He had, by rough count, sat down zero times. Now the little dragon was fully provisioned and the delivery crew was gone, and the man needed five minutes of horizontal before he could face a desk.

He sank into the sofa. Just to reset. Then he’d go game with her.

The sofa had other plans. It was, without competition, an excellent sofa.

Within some unmeasured interval, lying there with his eyes closed, he simply crossed the border into sleep.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the study, the downloads finished and installed. Park Ji-yeon launched League of Legends, logged in, and took the new rig into a custom game to test the feel — mouse glide, keyboard feedback, the small detonation of light off a thirty-two-inch wall.

Verdict: the ₩10 million had been an insultingly good deal.

She ran one bot match, went rampaging through it like weather, and exited satisfied. Then she remembered her partner and turned—

—to the empty chair beside her.

“Why’s it so quiet out there…” She muttered it, stood, and headed for the living room. “Oppa! Come look, it’s all set up! I loaded your games too!”

Silence answered.

She leaned around the doorway — and there he was. Flat on his back on the sofa, out cold.

His head had tipped to one side, one arm loose off the cushion, chest rising and falling on a slow, even clock. The afternoon sun came in low through the full-length windows and laid a band of warm light across him.

“Asleep…?”

She made her steps small and went over, looking down at him.

And the ledger occurred to her. She had dragged him out of bed at dawn. Conscripted him for a morning of labor. Hauling, up and down, no break, no complaint — not one, the whole day. If someone had done that to her, she’d have detonated by ten and demanded compensation.

A small guilt arrived. It sat down and stayed. She couldn’t bring herself to wake him.

So she slipped into the bedroom instead, and came back with the thin blanket from the shopping haul — this morning’s purchase, still sealed, pale gray, soft as a cloud in a plastic sleeve. She peeled it open, knelt at the sofa, and laid it over him gently.

And then — did not leave.

She knelt there on the rug instead, and a laugh escaped her.

“I’m so wifely. Whoever marries me is set for LIFE.”

Had Song Ji-yeon been conscious to hear the word wifely awarded on the strength of one (1) blanket, he would have sat bolt upright and diagnosed her, out loud, as a clinical narcissist.

The self-admiration ceremony ran its course. Then, with nowhere better to go, her gaze drifted back down to his face.

Sleeping, Song Ji-yeon was a different product line from waking Song Ji-yeon.

Awake, he ran on a lazy drawl — everything half a joke, nothing entirely serious, a man permanently leaning on something. Asleep, the sharpness left the outline. The brow went smooth. The lashes — thick, long, absurd — laid a small shadow down under each eye.

She stared for half a second longer than she’d planned, and a thought surfaced without clearance:

…Oppa’s really good-looking.

She had never actually studied his face before. Filed under tall, good build, dresses well, cross-referenced with oppa, never examined at close range. Definitely never like this — this close, this quiet, this long, and him unable to run.

Good skin. A high, straight nose bridge. And the mouth — the line of the lips was… nice, actually.

And suddenly she understood, at the molecular level, why the unnis were so devoted to the project of having boyfriends. Pick a pretty one, and the daily view alone is a benefits package.

She suddenly, mildly, wanted to try it. She was nineteen. She had never once, properly, dated. What was it even like?

And if she did — fine, standards: someone funny to talk to, like oppa. Money: non-negotiable, like oppa. And looks: oppa-grade. She was not, under any circumstances, signing up for what So-yeon-unnie had — a boyfriend practically from another geological era. Dating meant young men. Young!

…Her current social circle, though.

She ran the census, and it came back grim. Since the bullying scandal, young men approached her approximately never. Nobody wanted the article, the looks, the whispered isn’t she the one from—

Gulp.

The final tally was in. Her entire circle of young men now contained exactly one name. Song Ji-yeon-oppa.

Park Ji-yeon, the face the public had crowned the most beautiful of 2012, had been rationed down to one available man.

So… then… oppa wouldn’t exactly be out of the question…

The dangerous thought surfaced, and her cheeks went warm entirely without authorization.

She shook her head hard, twice, evicting the squatters.

No. Oppa was great — oppa was extremely great — but she was not going to fight Eun-jung-unnie over a man. Eun-jung-unnie was the best unnie she had. That line did not get crossed. Whatever those two were — whatever unclear, blushing, breakfast-making situation the unnies were all pretending not to see — he was off the menu. Permanently archived.

But her eyes didn’t leave.

Looking was free.

And it wasn’t as if oppa didn’t look. He kept sneaking glances at her legs — genuinely believed he was being subtle about it, poor man, when the little dragon had logged the pattern weeks ago. If oppa could window-shop, she could window-shop. What was good for the goose was good for the dragon.

Armed with this airtight logic, her courage inflated to unlawful size. She had never examined a man this closely before — this was practically field research.

Her gaze traveled down from his cheekbone — and landed directly on his throat, where his Adam’s apple gave one small roll as he swallowed in his sleep.

Park Ji-yeon experienced a moment of pure scientific wonder.

Why is it so prominent?

Her hand drifted up to her own throat. Flat. Smooth. Structurally boring. She looked back down at his: that small ridge of bone riding gently up and down on his breath, absolutely refusing to be ignored. She had never observed one at this range. It was, in the nerdiest possible sense, fascinating.

And into the laboratory of her mind walked Park Hyo-min, uninvited, carrying a lecture.

Hyo-min-unnie’s famous method for picking men — delivered with a straight face in a dorm kitchen sometime last year: a man’s capability in that department, you can read straight off his nose and his Adam’s apple. The bigger the nose, the more the apple stands out — the stronger the man.

Park Ji-yeon’s eyes completed the audit on their own. The high nose bridge: confirmed. The prominent Adam’s apple: very much confirmed.

Gulp.

…So oppa’s capability is—

“Bah!! Pervert Hyo-min-unnie — she’s rotted my brain!!”

The curse came out in the tiniest of whispers, aimed at an absent woman. And yet — her fingers had started twitching. The urge had arrived, and it was strong: to reach out, just once, and touch that little jutting bone. To settle, empirically, whether it felt as hard as it looked.

Her face went hot. She bit her lip. Hesitation held out for a respectable few seconds.

Then it lost.

Her hand came up. The fingertip approached — slow, slow, millimeter by millimeter, straight for his throat—

The phone detonated.

Bzzz-bzzz— bzzz-bzzz—

Park Ji-yeon recoiled like she’d touched a fence. Song Ji-yeon’s eyes snapped open.

Four eyes, one sofa, zero explanations.

She fled. Scrambling, rolling, all limbs at once — a startled rabbit in socks, over the rug and through the study door.

“Ji-yeon?”

No answer.

…What is that kid doing.

He didn’t chase the question. The little dragon was back at her computer, presumably. Dragons returned to hoards. This was known science.

In the study, back pressed to the wall, Park Ji-yeon’s heart was going off like a war drum and her face could have kept a griddle in business.

What— what was I DOING?! What was I about to—?!

She clamped both hands over her face. Inside the skull: porridge. Fully cooked.

✦ ✦ ✦

Song Ji-yeon, innocent of everything, rubbed his eyes awake, fished the still-buzzing phone off the coffee table, and checked the screen. Sulli.

He answered, sleep still in the gravel of his voice.

“Sulli. What’s up?”

Her voice came through at full wattage, running about a step ahead of itself, the voice of a girl carrying good news it physically pained her to hold.

Phone · Sulli → Song Ji-yeon

“Oppa! Are you free today?”

A cold, premonitory shiver walked down his spine.

Why did that sentence sound like that.

His eyes went, on pure reflex, toward the study. Eight-thirty this morning, a different girl had stood at his front door wearing this exact expression of sunshine, and asked this exact question — Oppa, are you free today?

He had then been worked like a rented ox until noon.

History was lining up for a rerun. He put his guard up and chose his words the way a man chooses footing on ice.

“…Why. What is it.”

“Oppa, I’m going to the airport! Picking someone up. Do you have time to come with me? I’ll introduce you to a total beauty.”

Airport pickup. Small errand, low wattage, capped duration. His spine unclenched.

“Fine. I’ll swing by your dorm, and we’ll head straight to the airport.”

He appended, privately, in the internal terms and conditions: this was not curiosity about the beauty. It was simply that the afternoon had a hole in it. Also, as for duo-queuing with Park Ji-yeon — well, look at that. Errands. A man of his word simply could not stay.

The call ended. He sat up from the sofa — and registered the pale gray blanket that had been laid over him at some point during his blackout. Park Ji-yeon’s work, obviously.

He lifted it, folded it properly — corners squared, hotel-grade — and set it on the armrest.

Only then did he walk to the study.

The door stood open. From his angle, all he could see was her back: parked in the new white chair at correct angles, left hand on the keyboard, right on the mouse, body canted slightly forward, motionless, eyes locked on the screen — the silhouette of a person deeply occupied.

“Ji-yeon.” He leaned on the doorframe. “Something’s come up — I’ve got to head out. We’ll duo another day.”

Inside the study, the silhouette stiffened by a millimeter.

She did not turn around. She could not turn around. She absolutely could not let him hear her heartbeat, which was currently performing percussion at festival volume.

So she prayed, silently, in a loop: don’t come over. Don’t come over. Oppa, whatever you do, do not walk over here.

The scene was on repeat behind her eyes — her fingertip, his throat, the millimeter that remained, the phone detonating, and then his eyes opening straight into hers. Her cheeks were hot enough to fry an egg. Does he know? Did he see? If he thinks I was doing something weird— will he tell Eun-jung-unnie? And if Eun-jung-unnie finds out, what will she think of me— Eun-jung-unnie, I was only LOOKING, I was only curious about oppa’s Adam’s apple, I was not doing anything to a sleeping man, I swear—

She stared at the monitor with everything she had. League of Legends glowed gorgeously back at her, all color and motion, resolving into nothing — a pure blur of light. Her eyes had stopped delivering images.

She gathered her entire remaining self, and made her voice come out the way it always sounded.

“Okay, oppa.” Head perfectly still, tone a masterpiece of composure. “Come home early.”

Four words. They cost her roughly her whole annual budget of acting.

The right hand stayed clamped to the mouse, knuckles paling. The ear-tips had gone the color of a warning light.

“Mm.”

Song Ji-yeon thought nothing of any of it. Extracting a gaming addict from a new rig required surgery and anesthesia; a send-off was never happening. He nodded at the back of her head, turned, and let himself out of her flat.

The front door clicked shut — and the whole world seemed to go quiet with it.

Park Ji-yeon’s rigid spine unlocked all at once. A long exhale left her, and she poured backward into the gaming chair like something being fished out of water, limbs everywhere.

She raised one hand and pressed it over a cheek that was still, frankly, griddle-grade.

So close. It was THAT close. One more millimeter and my finger— PARK JI-YEON. What were you THINKING?!

✦ ✦ ✦

Song Ji-yeon knew none of it. And even if he had, there would have been no ceremony — it’s an Adam’s apple. People have touched worse.

He rode the elevator down, walked into the underground garage, and slid into the Mercedes. The engine turned over; the black body pulled out of its slot and merged, unhurried, into Seoul’s Saturday afternoon traffic.

Hands on the wheel, mind drifting, he turned Sulli’s phone call over. A total beauty. Airport arrivals. Someone worth picking up.

Sulli’s known universe was not large. He ran the roster. f(x) had no comeback schedule these months — Sulli had said so herself — which meant the people around her weren’t flying anywhere. Victoria and Krystal: eliminated on the spot.

Which narrowed the field down to one name. Maybe two.

And of those, there was exactly one he was — interested was a mild word for it.

If the beauty Sulli was fetching from the airport was the one he had in mind… the corner of his mouth curved up, all on its own.

He would honestly, genuinely, like to meet her.

(This was, for the record, the real reason he’d said yes. The free afternoon thing had been PR.)

Foot a little heavier on the pedal, the Mercedes slid into the left lane and pointed itself toward the SM dorm.
