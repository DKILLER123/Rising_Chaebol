Chapter Ninety

The Dragon’s Call

MONDAY · LATE MORNING Mapo-gu, Seoul SAVORY F&B headquarters, top floor

The contract-transfer consent form had just landed on the desktop — clean, twelve clauses, signature and fingerprint blocks waiting at the bottom — when Song Ji-yeon’s phone lit up with a KakaoTalk ping.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung ♥

Ham Eun-jung

oppa! Are you buying T-ara?!

Song Ji-yeon

Yes. Just missing your six consent forms. Should be done this week.

He set the phone face down on the desk, leaned back, and let a small smile work across his mouth.

That easy. Six signatures on a piece of paper, one last handshake with Kim Kwang-soo, and T-ara belongs to ShiningYouth Entertainment.

One week. Maybe less.

✦ ✦ ✦

MONDAY · LATE MORNING Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Park Ji-yeon’s apartment, bedroom

Eun-jung read the reply three times in a row, and each pass squeezed something tighter in her chest. Then she did the math: it was Monday, signing this week, six members to round up — and the only one she still hadn’t reached lay bundled into a blanket-burrito two meters from her, eyes welded shut, pretending very hard that the room contained no one named Ham Eun-jung.

She tossed her phone onto the bedspread and pounced.

Her whole weight dropped across the burrito, hands scrabbling for the corner of the blanket so she could dig out the little dragon’s head.

“Ji-yeon! Come out! Big news!”

She was so brightly lit up with the news that she couldn’t bear to keep it inside her own chest for another second.

A muffled sound came up from inside the burrito.

“Yah, unnie, I’m really sleepy, I don’t care about any news, let me sleep a little more.”

Eun-jung’s mouth popped into a pout.

“Even the news that Song Ji-yeon-oppa is buying our T-ara — you don’t care?!”

She knew the one thing in this world the little dragon actually, desperately cared about was this — the buying-back of T-ara. Because the little dragon truly loved the stage. She didn’t want to leave it.

For company reasons that had nothing to do with the girls themselves, T-ara’s goodwill on the Peninsula had been spent down to nothing. Outside the schedules they’d signed before the storm, they had no domestic activities anymore, and the overseas work had thinned to almost none.

So the promise Song Ji-yeon had made — taking them to develop in China — really was their only road out.

All six of them cared. Deeply.

The burrito stirred. A head poked out. Bleary, she squinted up at her unnie.

“What — oppa is buying T-ara?”

Eun-jung smiled and nodded.

“Ne. Oppa just messaged me. He’s already preparing the transfer contract.”

The words barely landed before Ji-yeon came visibly, violently awake. She sat bolt upright, threw her arms around Eun-jung, and pressed her face into the crook of her unnie’s neck, nuzzling it like a puppy but with an accusing edge to her voice.

“Qinjia — unnie, you’re not messing with me, are you? Oppa is really buying T-ara? Why didn’t oppa tell me himself… Unnie! If I find out you’re lying, you are dead.”

Eun-jung peeled the little dragon’s head off her shoulder and pushed it firmly back.

“Why would unnie lie. Don’t believe me? Call oppa yourself.”

She slipped free of the hug, stood up at the bedside, and waited — arms folded, eyes patient — for Ji-yeon to do exactly that.

The little dragon heard her unnie’s matter-of-fact tone and the grievance on her face flipped in an instant into expectation.

“Yah — I’m calling oppa right now to ask!”

She kicked the blanket off and began the dig for her phone.

Oppa had said that the second he signed for T-ara, he’d take them to develop in China, and she was looking forward to it more than she could put into words. After all, their name in Korea right now was only barely above a rat in the street — and she didn’t want her idol life to be over. Going to China was better than quietly going dark.

While she was thinking, her hand closed on the phone under the pillow. She yanked it free and dialed Song Ji-yeon directly.

Eun-jung stood at the bedside, watching the little dragon work, and let her mouth pull sideways.

This little dragon doesn’t believe me. All these years I’ve fed her for nothing. Heartbroken, honestly.

The phone rang twice before it picked up.

Phone · Park Ji-yeon → Song Ji-yeon

“Ji-yeon?”

“Oppa, Eun-jung-unnie says you’re buying T-ara — is it true?”

“True. What, you think Eun-jung would lie to you?”

“Of course she’d lie!”

Ji-yeon’s voice went full righteous mode.

“Eun-jung-unnie would do anything to get me out of bed! She just tried to con me with a ’we’re all having dinner tonight’ line — and there was no dinner!”

Eun-jung, standing at the bedside, rolled her eyes so hard they nearly fell out.

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon, I just said get up first and I’ll treat you to dinner — how is that a con?”

Ji-yeon slapped a hand over the phone’s mic and dropped her voice to a fierce whisper.

“That is a con — unnie was clearly baiting me with food.”

Eun-jung laughed, half angry.

“Oh, you’d know! And if I hadn’t baited you, would you have gotten up? Wait — I did bait you, and you still didn’t get up!”

Ji-yeon just laughed hehe and didn’t take the fight any further. She put the phone back to her ear and turned her voice sweet and sticky as honey.

“Oppa, you heard all that? Eun-jung-unnie is lying on purpose — that’s why I don’t believe her about the T-ara thing either.”

Song Ji-yeon had heard every word. It told him, plain as daylight, that the little dragon had pulled another all-nighter last night — and that Eun-jung had spent a herculean effort dragging the dragon out of bed this morning.

“I heard. But your unnie means well. Did you game till late again last night?”

Ji-yeon’s eyes slid sideways.

“I didn’t pull an all-nighter. I just… played a little late.”

“Till when?”

“…around one a.m. ish.”

She cleared her throat and steered the conversation back on track.

“Oppa, never mind the details — you’re really buying T-ara? I thought you were still negotiating with the company.”

His gaze drifted to the contract on his monitor.

“Pretty much settled. ShiningYouth’s license is in hand. The acquisition contract is ready. All that’s missing is the six of you signing the transfer consent form — and the last bit of paperwork with CCM. Then T-ara officially belongs to ShiningYouth Entertainment.”

He added, in case the term meant nothing to her:

“ShiningYouth — that’s the entertainment company I founded. You’d be the company’s first artists.”

Ji-yeon’s mouth ran one beat ahead of her brain.

“So once we sign the consent form, we belong to you?”

Song Ji-yeon heard himself smiling. The little dragon had a way of phrasing things that landed sideways.

“Belong to me.” Honestly — he wouldn’t mind all six of them belonging to him. The Crown’s six Jewels, the bond clicking shut at once, straight to the moon. Not a bad day’s work.

A small silence sat on the line. Then Ji-yeon caught the slip, and her face went pink.

“I mean — belong to your company.”

He let the pause stretch a half-second longer than comfortable.

“Close enough. What — you don’t want to belong to me?”

Ji-yeon’s tongue fired before her head could catch it.

“Of course I want to!”

The sentence landed, and she froze.

Song Ji-yeon’s laugh came low through the phone — quiet enough that only she would hear it.

“Good. From now on you’ll be ShiningYouth’s artists. As your Sajangnim, I’ll take good care of you.”

“Oppa, stop saying nice things.” Ji-yeon pulled herself together, tried to make her voice sound ordinary, and moved on. “Oppa, you said once you bought T-ara you’d take us to develop in China — is that true too?”

“Of course it’s true.”

He didn’t dress it up. The Peninsula market wasn’t a place the six of them could afford to keep waiting in anymore. Stay and wait for an opening that wasn’t coming, or pivot to a new market and start over — the China market was big enough that, run properly, they’d break through. A Korean agency eyeing China in 2012 was the most ordinary thing in the business.

It’s the one piece I can’t fix for them right now.

The people targeting T-ara sat at the Peninsula’s apex. Not a fist a twenty-two-year-old restaurateur could punch through, not yet. He had to wait. Had to dodge the edge of that blade.

But in a few years — when he was built — there might still be a way to clear the six little bitter gourds’ names. Set the record straight on what had really been done to them.

And he believed, quietly, that the wait wouldn’t be long.

On the other end of the line, Ji-yeon’s eyes had brightened, slowly, beat by beat.

She had been on a stage since her debut. The lights and the cheers were the air she breathed. Even as T-ara cratered, she had never, in her honest heart, wanted to quit.

She was only afraid. Afraid of the day she would climb up onto a stage and find that there was no stage left for her to stand on.

And Song Ji-yeon’s arrival was a beam of light thrown suddenly across her.

A new hope.

“Then — when do we leave?”

Song Ji-yeon laughed under his breath.

“We haven’t even signed yet and you’re already planning the move abroad? The company has to be set up first, the new album, the stage, the promo plan — that’s at least one to two months after the contract transfers.”

“One to two months?” Ji-yeon’s brows knitted. “That’s fast?”

“Founding a label from zero, building the group back up, producing songs, booking shows and CFs — that pace is already not slow.”

“But I want to go to China now.”

“In that much of a hurry — you can’t stand Korea anymore?”

His voice carried the easy teasing lilt of a man poking at a friend.

Ji-yeon went quiet for a few seconds.

“I just don’t want to leave the stage.”

The line came out soft. Almost weightless. And it landed, heavy, on both ends of the phone.

Neither end spoke for a moment.

Song Ji-yeon knew, perhaps better than the little dragon realized, how much that stage meant to her — to all six of them. It was the only reason a group that had been dragged through what T-ara had been dragged through was still standing at all. Plenty of Peninsula girl groups had disbanded for less. T-ara had held. Love of the stage was the only explanation he could find.

At the bedside, Eun-jung’s expression softened too.

She understood. She always had. T-ara had stood in the brightest spot there was, and then the bullying scandal had dropped them straight to the floor. They had worked. They had tried to start over. The Korean entertainment industry didn’t give fallen stars a long rope. These days they still wore the name T-ara on paper, but in fact they were already at the bottom of the well — pinned in place, with no rope to climb.

Song Ji-yeon let the teasing lilt fall away from his voice.

“Don’t worry. As long as you want to keep standing on a stage — I’ll make sure you have one.”

“Really?”

“Really.”

“Then you can’t lie to me.”

“When have I ever lied to you?”

Ji-yeon thought very hard. Then her voice came back, righteous as a small court prosecutor.

“Last night. You lied last night.”

“I lied about what?”

Ji-yeon’s mouth pursed into a perfect pout. Men’s promises are smoke, she thought darkly. The unnie collective had been warning her about men for a reason.

“Hmph! You promised to be my meal-buddy — and then you didn’t come home and cook at all last night. I starved the whole day.”

She has very conveniently forgotten that she starved because she didn’t order takeout. Classic.

Guilty, he leaned into the only honest defense he had.

“I did have a business dinner.”

“With who?”

“You don’t need to know.”

Ji-yeon’s voice jumped a register.

“Why can’t I know? Were you with another woman?”

At the bedside, Eun-jung’s shoulders went faintly stiff.

She hadn’t expected the little dragon to lob the question that directly. Her eyes dropped to the phone in Ji-yeon’s hand before she could stop them.

Not yet. I don’t want her to find out — not like this, not in the middle of a tantrum about dinner.

She reached over, plucked the phone out of Ji-yeon’s grip mid-sentence, and put it to her own ear.

Phone · Ham Eun-jung → Song Ji-yeon

“Oppa — since the consent forms are ready, when do you come to us to sign?”

“I was going to track you down one at a time today. I don’t even have contact info for the other four — I was just about to ask you for it.”

Eun-jung chose her words carefully, picking a route around the dinner tantrum.

“Oppa — how about this. Tonight, dinner at Ji-yeon’s place. I’ll bring Bo-ram-unnie and the others over. All six members, you, a meal — pre-celebrate joining your company. And while everyone’s at the table, we sign the consent forms all in one go. Locks the whole thing down. What do you think?”

Song Ji-yeon thought about it for a beat and then laughed.

“Good. No problem. Dinner and signing in one shot — efficient.”

He’d already been quietly dreading the alternative. Hunting down T-ara’s six members across Seoul one by one — Eun-jung and Ji-yeon he could reach easy, but the other four he barely knew, and he didn’t even have their numbers. Chasing them across the city would have eaten the whole day.

At Ji-yeon’s place, though — his own apartment sat one floor below. Sign, eat, walk the stairs down, sleep in his own bed. If he could drag Eun-jung home with him after — that was a sweet day, end to end.

“Then it’s set. Tonight — Ji-yeon’s place. We’ll wait for you.”

Eun-jung had no idea what was running through his head. She only wanted, desperately, to get off the phone before the little dragon wrestled it back and started asking about the other woman again.

“Ne, oppa. See you tonight.”

She hung up — fast.

Ji-yeon yelped.

“Unnie — why’d you hang up?! I still wanted to chat with oppa!”

Eun-jung let her forehead drop into her hand. She lifted her free hand and pressed one accusing fingertip into the middle of the little dragon’s forehead.

“You. Oppa’s at work. He doesn’t have time to chitchat with you. Up. We’re going to the securities center to open your stock account, and then I’ll text the members about tonight’s dinner and signing. Remember — tell them five o’clock.”

“Yah, unnie, that hurt!” Ji-yeon batted the finger away and gave her the pouty face. “No, unnie, I don’t want to go to the securities center — can we just not open the account? I’m really, really sleepy. Let me catch up on sleep first, please?”

She’d already stopped looking at her phone. She was halfway back into her burrito when Eun-jung’s hand shot out — quick as a flash — grabbed a fistful of blanket, and ripped the whole thing off the mattress and onto her own body, denying the dragon any cover at all.

“You’re already awake — good girl, no more sleeping. Get the errand done early, feast tonight.”

Stripped of the blanket, Ji-yeon had no moves left. She surrendered.

“Fine! But I eat first. Then the securities center.”

Eun-jung shook her head, half-amused.

“Fine. Unnie will cook. Now text Bo-ram-unnie and the others — tell them oppa is buying T-ara and that we’re doing dinner and signing tonight. Tell them to come at five.”

She was genuinely afraid the little dragon would call the members over too early and put them to work lugging things. It was one dinner. She could handle it alone.

“I know, unnie, I’ll be clear with them. You — go cook for me. I want samgyetang!”

Eun-jung smiled, shook her head again, dropped the phone into Ji-yeon’s lap, and walked out of the bedroom.

Ji-yeon picked up the phone and started tapping out the group message.

✦ ✦ ✦

MONDAY · LATE MORNING Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Park Ji-yeon’s apartment, kitchen

Eun-jung stepped into Ji-yeon’s kitchen, pulled the fridge open, and froze.

Then she laughed, disbelieving.

“Yah, Park Ji-yeon, where’s your food? The fridge is all snacks. There’s nothing in here but snacks.”

A weak voice drifted in from the bedroom.

“Ah — unnie, you know I can’t cook. Just make whatever you find.”

Eun-jung surveyed the rows of snacks. She did, eventually, find two packs of instant ramen shoved in the door.

She filled a pot, set it on the stove, and dropped both packs in — one for the little dragon, one for herself. She’d eaten breakfast before coming over, but it was eleven now and her own stomach was rumbling too.

She wanted to crack two eggs into the pot. She opened every cabinet, every drawer. There wasn’t a single egg in the apartment.

Can this girl actually live alone?

She made a private note: drag the little dragon out for a real grocery run, every week, and stand over her while she bought actual food. This was unsustainable.

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon — how have you been surviving the last few days? Off snacks? There’s nothing in this house.”

The reply from the bedroom came sweet and syrupy — Ji-yeon was, by then, mid-text-chat with Park Hyo-min, and her voice carried the unbearably cute, wheedling aegyo of a girl who knew exactly what she was doing.

“Unnie — that’s exactly why I can’t live without you!”

Eun-jung stood at the stove, speechless. The decision hardened into concrete: today, after the securities center, she was dragging Park Ji-yeon to the supermarket, and every week after that she would remind her to buy the things a human being needed to stay alive.

The ramen finished boiling. Eun-jung ladled out two bowls, hauled the little dragon upright out of the bed, and they ate together at the small kitchen table — two packs of instant ramen, no eggs, between them.

When the bowls were empty, Eun-jung shoved Ji-yeon into the bathroom, waited until the sounds of tooth-brushing started, and didn’t let up until the little dragon was washed, dressed, and standing — grumbling — by the front door.

They stepped out together. The door closed behind them. The afternoon’s errands had officially begun.
