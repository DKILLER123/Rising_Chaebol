Chapter One Hundred Eighteen

Gulp—You’re the President of an Entertainment Company?

FRIDAY MORNING Cheongdam-dong — Park Ji-yeon’s apartment A punctual dragon, a pair of high heels, and one promise she intends to collect

The next morning, Park Ji-yeon did something rare: she did not sleep in. She arrived at Song Ji-yeon’s apartment early.

Song Ji-yeon heard the movement in the living room and came out of the bathroom with a toothbrush between his teeth.

At the entryway stood the little dragon, already dressed and ready. She had even drawn her signature full eyeliner. Song Ji-yeon raised an eyebrow and spoke around the toothbrush.

“Well, well. The sun’s rising in the west? You’re up early today?”

Park Ji-yeon put both hands on her hips and gave a small, offended snort.

“Of course I am! I’m extremely punctual. Don’t look down on me, oppa.”

There was only one thing in her mind: get through next Thursday’s Japan concert rehearsals and performance without a hitch, then hurry home to collect on last night’s “promise” with oppa.

For that reason, after returning to her room the night before, she had done something almost unheard-of. She had not played with her phone. She closed her eyes early, and when the alarm rang that morning, she sprang out of bed at once.

“All right, all right. You’re the most punctual person alive. Give me two minutes.”

Laughing, Song Ji-yeon retreated into the bathroom to finish washing up.

When he came back out, he found Park Ji-yeon lying corpse-like across the sofa again.

How does this little dragon manage to love lying down so much?

He walked over and looked down at her.

“Up. Put your shoes on. We’re going to the company.”

Park Ji-yeon sat up with a bright smile and stretched both arms toward him, silently asking to be carried.

“Seriously? You don’t want to walk those few steps?”

The sofa and entryway were only a few steps apart.

“Oppa, can’t you just carry me over?”

Song Ji-yeon sighed, bent down, and picked her up. Park Ji-yeon hung off him with both arms looped around his neck as he carried her toward the entryway.

Once they arrived, she jumped down and efficiently put on her high heels.

“Do you need to wear heels for rehearsal?”

Song Ji-yeon knew T-ara performed in heels, but did they really need them for practice too?

He remembered that all six women had worn flat shoes the day before.

Park Ji-yeon puffed out her cheeks and answered in a pitiful voice.

“Yes. The performance is coming up, so So-yeon-unnie said we should wear heels today and get used to them in advance. Otherwise, it would be embarrassing if we made a mistake onstage. I haven’t worn heels in a long time, so I’m not used to them yet. My feet hurt after only a few steps. That’s why I asked you to carry me.”

Song Ji-yeon did not believe a word of it. It was only a few steps from her floor to his apartment. How much could her feet possibly hurt?

She simply wanted to stay on him.

“Then do you want me to carry you downstairs too?”

Park Ji-yeon bent over to adjust the heel of one shoe. At his words, she opened her eyes wide and stared at him in disbelief.

She did not believe for a second that oppa would actually carry her downstairs.

“Jinjja? Then I’ll trouble you, oppa.”

Song Ji-yeon laughed. The little dragon was not even pretending anymore.

After they had spoken honestly the day before, the little dragon had become much more forward.

Now she was willing to let him carry her downstairs.

Before, even the slightest intimate contact had sent her running in panic.

Once she had her shoes on, Song Ji-yeon wrapped his arms around her from behind and carried her out the door.

Park Ji-yeon had thought he was joking. She only realized he was serious after he carried her outside. Her entire body shrank into his arms, and she did not dare show even a sliver of her face.

They went downstairs. Song Ji-yeon placed her in the front passenger seat, then got behind the wheel and drove her to the company.

On the way, he turned the wheel and reminded her casually:

“Practice properly once you get to the studio. The Japanese stage has strict requirements.”

Park Ji-yeon sat in the passenger seat, nodding so hard she looked like a chick pecking grain. Her mouth, however, complained without pause.

“I know, I know! Oppa, you’re so nagging, just like my mother. The stage is my life. I love performing more than you do, all right? You don’t need to chant at me every day!”

“I’m annoying you? Fine. Don’t ask me to pay for lunch later.”

Song Ji-yeon gave her a sidelong glance.

“Yah! How could I not? The company president paying is only proper!”

Park Ji-yeon changed expression instantly, leaned over with a grin, and tugged at his sleeve.

They bickered all the way to the building. Soon, the car stopped beneath ShiningYouth Entertainment.

They entered the lobby and took the elevator to the third floor, then split up. Park Ji-yeon darted into the practice room to join the other members, while Song Ji-yeon continued upward at an unhurried pace to the president’s office on the fourth floor.

FRIDAY MORNING ShiningYouth Entertainment — the president’s office A young company, two acts, and an unusually quiet schedule

Song Ji-yeon settled into the broad office chair and casually flipped through the schedule Park Seon-young had delivered.

The company had almost nothing urgent to do.

More accurately, it was quiet to an excessive degree.

INTERNAL · SHININGYOUTH ENTERTAINMENT

Friday Operating Picture — The Roster Before the Breakthrough

One week old · two acts · domestic momentum still pending

T-ara — six established idols, caught in the troll controversy, with their domestic schedules effectively frozen. The next move is outward: Japan first, then the mainland.

Han So-hee — a newly signed complete beginner whose artist file is not even finished. No name recognition, no serious auditions yet, and no reason to hurry the long game.

Company status — commercial staff have approached television stations and production teams, but the response is weak. For now, earlier capital is keeping the lights on.

A quiet agency is not necessarily a failing agency. Sometimes it is an agency waiting for its first public result.

ShiningYouth currently had only two groups of artists under its name. One was T-ara, caught in a storm of public opinion with all domestic work on hold. The other was Han So-hee, newly signed and not yet fully entered into the company’s artist records.

The business department had spent the past two days trying to contact several television stations and production companies in the hope of pushing the agency’s artists forward. The results had been minimal.

The aftershocks of T-ara’s troll controversy had not settled. Nobody on the Peninsula wanted to touch the problem, so for this stage the group could only develop overseas. Han So-hee, meanwhile, was a complete newcomer with no reputation. Even a decent audition was difficult to secure.

The whole company was essentially living off its savings, with Song Ji-yeon’s earlier capital keeping the operation running day by day.

Song Ji-yeon was not worried at all.

According to his plan, once next week’s Japanese concert was over and the new album created noise in Japan and overseas, he would shift T-ara’s center of gravity toward the mainland.

They would study Chinese for a few months, then go to China for commercial performances.

As for Han So-hee, he would first have Lee Hye-jin take her to several high-end print-model jobs to build her name. Once her looks had circulated through the industry, there would be plenty of time to discuss acting.

With nothing else to do, Song Ji-yeon opened the computer and logged into his stock account.

The candlestick chart on the screen was a field of red.

On Monday, using his prediction, he had bought shares in BukwangPharm, the biotechnology company, and put a full twenty-four billion won of leveraged capital into the position.

It was Friday. Spurred by favorable American stem-cell policy, the stock had hit its daily limit for four straight days, for a cumulative rise of seventy percent.

Song Ji-yeon checked the time. Once the afternoon market opened at two, he planned to liquidate the entire position and lock in the profit.

Cumulative rise+70%

Expected pure profitMore than ₩18,000,000,000

The forecast had done its work. The disciplined exit was the work now.

Looking at the more than eighteen billion won in pure profit about to arrive in his account, Song Ji-yeon leaned back in his chair, his good mood lifting the corners of his mouth.

By noon, Ji-yeon and Eun-jung had sent him messages on KakaoTalk asking whether he wanted to come downstairs and eat delivery food with them. Hyo-min wanted to order jajangmyeon and sweet-and-sour pork.

Song Ji-yeon remembered the Good Luck Card he had obtained after Park Ji-yeon kissed him the night before.

He had used it casually that same night.

But an entire morning had passed. Apart from the stock climbing exactly as predicted, he had not even picked up a fallen leaf in the real world.

“Forget it. You girls eat. I’m going out for a walk at lunch.”

He replied, slipped the phone into his pocket, and stood.

He intended to wander the street and see what unexpected surprise the Good Luck Card might bring him.

FRIDAY · EARLY AFTERNOON Cheongdam-dong commercial street — then a small Chinese restaurant One useless walk, one bowl of noodles, and two familiar trainees

Song Ji-yeon wandered around the commercial streets of Cheongdam-dong for half an hour. Nothing happened. He simply drifted along.

The autumn wind pushed two fallen leaves across the pavement while pedestrians hurried past.

At a crossroads, Song Ji-yeon could not stop himself from complaining internally.

As expected… Park Ji-yeon’s Good Luck Card is the weakest of all the goddesses’ abilities. It is pure psychological comfort.

His stomach chose that moment to growl.

After walking for so long, he was hungry too. He rubbed his stomach and began looking for lunch, then noticed a medium-sized Chinese restaurant around the corner.

Its red-and-white sign was impossible to miss. In large, square Chinese characters it read: Kangshifu Braised Beef Noodles.

Song Ji-yeon froze, nearly laughing.

Was it an instant-noodle shop, or did it actually make beef noodles?

Who expected to see a name that down-to-earth on a Seoul street?

Overcome by curiosity, he went inside.

The restaurant was not large. Seven or eight wooden tables filled the room, all of them clean. It was lunchtime, but there were few customers: only one table of middle-aged men in the corner, slurping noodles.

Behind the counter stood a slightly heavyset man in his forties, wearing an apron and watching videos on his phone.

“Boss, one bowl of noodles.”

Song Ji-yeon greeted him in Chinese.

The owner heard the familiar language, raised his head, and immediately broke into a broad smile.

“A fellow countryman! Come in, come in! What would you like? The specialty is braised beef noodles. Hand-pulled noodles, completely authentic!”

Song Ji-yeon sat at a four-person table near the door and teased him.

“Your shop name is ambitious. Did Kangshifu make you pay a franchise fee? Don’t tell me you just unwrap instant noodles and boil them.”

The owner laughed loudly and waved both hands.

“How could I? My family name is Kang. Back home, everybody called me Master Kang. When I came here to open a shop, I figured the name was friendly and already famous, so I used it. The beef brisket is braised by hand. It is absolutely not instant noodles!”

“All right. In honor of the name, give me a large bowl of braised beef noodles, with extra cilantro.”

“Coming right up! Please wait!”

The owner cheerfully turned toward the kitchen.

Song Ji-yeon pulled a pair of chopsticks from the holder on the table and poured himself a glass of barley tea.

He had barely set the cup down when the welcoming bell above the door rang brightly.

Two young girls in casual hoodies entered one after the other.

Song Ji-yeon glanced up. A playful smile immediately appeared at the corner of his mouth.

Well, I didn’t expect to run into them here.

They were none other than the Japanese JYP trainees he had met by chance at the Japanese restaurant: Hirai Momo and Minatozaki Sana.

The two girls were speaking quietly in Japanese.

“Momo-chan, are you sure this is the place? Are noodles from a Chinese restaurant really good?”

Sana had her arm linked with Momo’s. Her clear, puppy-like eyes held a little curiosity and hesitation.

“They have to be good! Unnies from different trainee groups told me. The beef in this restaurant’s noodles is enormous, and the noodles have an amazing chew!”

Hirai Momo gestured earnestly, her eyes full of hunger.

“But what if the flavor is strange? I’m a little afraid of spicy food…”

“Come on, let’s try it. I’m so hungry I’m about to collapse!”

Before she could finish, Momo looked up and met Song Ji-yeon’s amused gaze from the table by the door.

She stopped dead. She recognized him.

Sana, who had not noticed, nearly walked into Momo’s back.

“Momo-chan, what’s wrong? Why did you stop?”

Curious, Sana followed Momo’s eyes. She too froze when she recognized the senior they had met once before.

Song Ji-yeon took the initiative and waved.

“We meet again.”

The two girls recovered and hurried forward two steps. They bowed in perfect unison and greeted him in bright Korean colored by a heavy Japanese accent.

“Annyeonghaseyo, sunbaenim!”

Their form was flawless, even more respectful than many local trainees.

In their minds, Song Ji-yeon had eaten at the same table as T-ara seniors Ji-yeon and Eun-jung. He was exceptionally handsome, so they had assumed he must be an actor or idol senior from a major company.

Besides, when they had prepared to pay at the Japanese restaurant, the owner had told them that a handsome customer had already settled the bill.

If he were not a senior, why would he treat them to such an expensive meal?

Watching the girls stand rigid with nerves, Song Ji-yeon shook his head with a laugh.

“Stop calling me sunbaenim. Sit down. Just call me Song Ji-yeon. I’m not your senior—I’m neither an idol nor an actor, so there’s no need for such a formal bow. And what are your names? I still don’t know them.”

The two girls straightened and exchanged a glance. They had understood only the last sentence, so they answered almost simultaneously.

“I’m Minatozaki Sana.”

“I’m Hirai Momo. Could you speak more slowly? Our Korean isn’t very good. We didn’t understand.”

Song Ji-yeon blinked, then smiled helplessly as he realized the problem.

“Right. Your accents are still pretty strong.”

They were both foreigners, after all. It was natural that their Korean was not fluent.

He repeated what he had said in soft, careful Korean to make sure they could understand.

“Nani?”

Sana rubbed her small, round face and blinked in disbelief.

“You’re not an entertainment senior? But sunbaenim… no, Song Ji-yeon-oppa, you look exactly like an idol!”

Momo tugged at Sana’s sleeve and reminded her quietly in Japanese:

“Sana-chan, that’s rude.”

Then Momo lowered her head slightly and addressed Song Ji-yeon shyly.

“Song Ji-yeon-ssi, thank you very much for the Japanese restaurant the other day. The owner told us that you paid our bill. We had been hoping to find a chance to thank you in person, and we never expected to meet you here.”

“It was only one meal. I happened to be there.”

Song Ji-yeon pointed at the empty seats across from him.

“Why are you still standing? Sit down and talk. You came for beef noodles too?”

“Yes!”

At the mention of food, Sana’s nervousness disappeared. She pulled Momo onto the bench across from Song Ji-yeon.

“I heard the beef noodles here are amazing! I’ve wanted to try them for ages. What do you think, oppa?”

The dog-like personality really is obvious. Momo is still calling him Song Ji-yeon-ssi, and Sana has already moved on to oppa.

“I only just arrived, so I haven’t tasted them yet.”

He answered slowly in Korean, then asked:

“What are you ordering? Braised beef noodles too?”

Momo nodded. Sana studied the menu on the wall, then chose braised beef noodles too.

Once both girls had nodded, Song Ji-yeon called into the kitchen in Chinese.

“Boss, add two more bowls of braised beef noodles.”

“Sure thing! I’ll get started!”

Sana’s ears caught the Chinese.

“Gulp—oppa, you speak Chinese too?”

She looked at him with open admiration. He spoke Korean, and now he was speaking even the famously difficult Chinese with such fluency.

Song Ji-yeon smiled helplessly at the puppy-faced awe.

“I’m Chinese. Of course I speak Chinese.”

Sana had not expected him to be Chinese.

That made him seem even more impressive. Only foreigners knew how difficult Korean could be to learn.

She and Momo had already been studying it for five months.

“Then how did you learn Korean? Momo-chan and I have studied for months, but when people say many words quickly, we can’t understand them.”

“Well…”

Song Ji-yeon thought about it. He had barely spent any effort learning Korean. For a Chinese person, Korean could feel almost as easy as learning a phonetic system.

“There’s a shortcut for Chinese people learning Korean. If I explain my method, you won’t understand it.”

“Oh. All right.”

Sana tilted her head and did not dwell on the shortcut, because the owner had already carried out Song Ji-yeon’s bowl of noodles.

The braised beef noodles steamed on the table. The rich aroma of beef fat mingled with cilantro and filled the restaurant. Large pieces of tender beef covered springy, hand-pulled noodles in a glossy red broth.

Sana swallowed hard.

Song Ji-yeon heard her and smiled.

I didn’t expect Sana to be such a glutton.

He pushed his bowl toward her.

“You eat first.”

“Jinjja? Oppa, you’re such a good person!”

Sana looked delighted. Momo, beside her, grew embarrassed and tugged harder on Sana’s sleeve, telling her not to make a spectacle of herself.

Sana clearly misunderstood.

“Momo-chan, do you want some too? But you ordered your own. If you want to taste mine, I can share a little—but you can’t have the meat.”

Momo’s face turned red. She gave Sana a complicated look.

Am I this glutton? I’m telling you to watch your image! Don’t you understand that Song Ji-yeon-oppa ordered this first? He gave it to you—could he possibly only be being polite?

Song Ji-yeon saw Momo’s helplessness.

“It’s fine. The three bowls are the same. I’ll wait for the others. Let her eat first.”

“I’m sorry. Thank you, oppa.”

Sana was already eating.

Before long, the other two bowls arrived. Because Song Ji-yeon was a fellow countryman, the owner had added extra meat to all three. Each bowl was piled high.

Momo stared at the noodles in front of her, appetite surging.

“Then—itadakimasu!”

Song Ji-yeon paused. Was Momo really that well-mannered?

He looked at the puppy beside her, who was eating at top speed.

There is no comparison without contrast.

“Please enjoy.”

Only then did Momo pick up her chopsticks. She lifted the noodles slowly and brought them to her mouth. Her eyes lit up at the first bite, and she covered her mouth.

“Mmm—sugoi. So good.”

Song Ji-yeon smiled and began eating too.

When the three had finished, Momo insisted on paying. Song Ji-yeon had paid at the Japanese restaurant last time. If he had been an entertainment senior, that would have been natural, but now that she knew he was not, she felt uncomfortable leaving him to pay again.

Song Ji-yeon did not stop her. A bowl of noodles cost little, and if she wanted to pay, she could.

“Fellow countryman, come again!”

“I certainly will.”

The three left Kangshifu Braised Beef Noodles. They said goodbye at the crossroads: JYP Entertainment stood in one direction, ShiningYouth Entertainment in the other, and the restaurant happened to sit between them.

Sana waved.

“Oppa, we’re going back to the company. Bye!”

“I have to go back to my company too. Bye.”

“Company?”

Momo was curious. They had met Song Ji-yeon twice now, so she asked:

“Oppa, which company nearby do you belong to? If we have time, we’ll go find you and play.”

Song Ji-yeon smiled and pointed toward the ShiningYouth building.

“Me? I’m the owner of ShiningYouth Entertainment. If you want to find me, just go there.”

“The owner?”

The two girls spoke at once.

“Gulp—you’re the president of an entertainment company?”

Both their minds short-circuited. Song Ji-yeon only smiled.

“Yes.”
