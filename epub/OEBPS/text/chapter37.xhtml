Chapter Thirty-Seven

Baby, Why?

SEPTEMBER 2012 · NIGHT Cheongdam-dong, Seoul outside the Tteurak hanwoo house

“Song Ji-yeon-ssi — thank you for tonight. For everything.”

“Hold on.” He laughed, shaking his head. “This dinner was supposed to be your treat. How did I end up the host again? Go home and get some rest. Wait for my news.”

“Ne.”

Six voices, perfectly in unison — and underneath them, something that hadn’t been in that word for a long, long time: a lilt. Lightness. Girls looking forward to tomorrow.

He stood on the curb until the taxi carrying all six of T-ara had turned the corner, then walked back to where his own car was parked and drove home to Cheongdam-dong.

He’d barely stepped through the door when his phone buzzed. A message from Ham Eun-jung.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Ham Eun-jung

Home safe. Good night.

Ham Eun-jung

[Sticker: a bunny burrowing under its blanket until only its ears remain]

Song Ji-yeon

Good night.

He smiled at the screen a moment longer, then carried himself to the study.

Laptop open. Brokerage logged in. On the screen, the Samsung Electronics daily chart closed out the session with a fat, healthy bullish candle — the price had quietly climbed to ₩1,210,000 a share, and the unrealized gain sitting on his position had pushed past half a billion won.

“Nowhere near enough.”

He ran the numbers on everything the near future was going to cost him, tipped his head back against the sofa, and sighed at the ceiling. You never find out how little money you have until the day you actually need it.

There were times he genuinely envied idols. Half of them dripped money. Sajangnim or not, if you counted the actual cash in his hands, there were long-famous idols out there who could out-liquidity him without breaking a sweat. Every won of liquid capital to his name was sitting in that brokerage account — four billion… no, call it four and a half, once you folded in this week’s paper gains.

Four and a half billion won sounded like a fortune. It was not going to survive what he had planned.

ONGI needed new stores, and key-money deposits alone would eat a man alive. SAVORY F&B’s own accounts were stretched to the seams — barely enough left in them beyond a thin cushion for this month’s payroll — so every new door would have to be funded out of his own pocket. Three directly operated stores in every district of Seoul; call it seventy-some stores once the plan filled in. Each one wanted roughly ₩3 billion in key money before you’d even signed the lease, and once the deposit, the build-out, and the monthly rent were all in, a conservative figure per store ran to about ₩10 billion. Which worked out to ₩720 billion. Before a single grill was lit.

Then there was T-ara. Buying out their contracts, conservatively, another ₩30 billion.

And that wasn’t even counting what he’d have to pour into them after signing. Just thinking about it made his scalp prickle.

Private Ledger · What the Plan Costs

Expansion subtotal₩720,000,000,000

Liquid capital on hand₩4,500,000,000

The gap₩750,000,000,000 — and his ₩4.5B isn’t even the rounding error

“Useless system!!!”

He said it quietly, at the ceiling. By temperament he was a slow-and-steady man; even with no cheat at all, his memories of the next decade would have built him a perfectly fine life. The problem was that he did have a cheat — and the cheat, bless it, was a lemon.

Count it up. Everything the Goddess Handbook had ever actually paid out came to three things.

From Ham Eun-jung: a 2012 Samsung Electronics chart, and a Stock Forecast Card.

From Sulli: one 2.5× body enhancement.

As war chests went: appetizers. He was a returner — he’d known Samsung’s 2012 by heart before the Handbook ever printed it. And the Stock Forecast Card was garbage among garbage. What he could no longer recall was which stock went vertical in which week — the exact timing of every monster rally had blurred years ago. What he remembered perfectly was which companies printed money for a decade straight. That knowledge wouldn’t make him rich on any schedule that helped him now, but held long enough, he’d never go hungry.

As for the body enhancement — marginally useful. Extremely marginally. It’s a civilized society. What exactly was I planning to do with a sprinter’s legs and washboard abs?

No. The only prizes worth salivating over were the bond-completion rewards.

Except collect was doing a lot of work in that sentence. He still hadn’t cracked what the Handbook meant by contact — and until he cracked contact, collection was a word for other, luckier men.

“Aish.” He sighed. “Fine. This week — make some time for Sulli. See if I can’t shake a contact reward loose out of her.”

✦ ✦ ✦

THE NEXT MORNING Mapo, Seoul SAVORY F&B — the president’s office

He’d just worked through a stack of routine store-operations papers when the internal line rang. Kim Min-hyun, the legal director.

Internal line · Kim Min-hyun → Song Ji-yeon

“Sajangnim — we’ve made first contact with CCM.”

The voice coming through the receiver carried a gravity it rarely bothered with.

“Kim Kwang-soo — that old fox. With his mouth, he’s saying T-ara are the company’s meritorious veterans, that he’d hate, truly hate, to let them go. But between every line he’s saying: name the right price, and everything can be discussed.”

“What’s he asking?”

Ji-yeon leaned back in the chair, fingers drumming the desktop without his permission.

“Five billion won.” A pause. “And one more thing, Sajangnim — he insists on a face-to-face meeting with the representative of the final acquiring party.”

“Five billion.”

A short, cold laugh. Greedier than he’d budgeted for. A girl group whose name was mud from Busan to the DMZ, and the old fox had the nerve to quote a premium on it. What was the man picturing — the SNSD playbook? Ride out a Black Ocean, weather a few years of hate, come back as the nation’s girl group and laugh all the way to the bank?

Internal line · Song Ji-yeon → Kim Min-hyun

“Tell him three billion. Not one won more. And after that — stall him. The overseas parent company needs to run its approval procedures; he can wait for word. For the next few days you’re going to do absolutely nothing. Let him hang.”

“Understood, Sajangnim.”

He set the receiver down and stood, walking to the great floor-to-ceiling window.

If Kim Kwang-soo didn’t want to sell yet, fine. He was in no hurry either. Time stood on his side of the board — the bullying scandal could keep flying around for a good while yet, and he had all season to outlast an old fox.

Company business done, he glanced out at the brilliant midday light, and a thought surfaced out of nowhere. Phone out. He dialed Sulli’s road manager.

Outgoing call · Song Ji-yeon → Manager Kim

“Manager Kim — where are they shooting Sulli today?”

✦ ✦ ✦

SAME DAY · EARLY AFTERNOON Yeouido, Han River Park To the Beautiful You — location set

Half an hour later, the Mercedes pulled up in front of an upscale French pâtisserie in Cheongdam-dong. He came back out with an armload of ribboned paper bags — macarons and fruit tarts, purchased in crew-sized quantities.

Back at the Han River Park location set, the atmosphere ran noticeably lighter than on his last visit.

He spotted Sulli at once, parked next to the director with a script in hand, deep in a discussion about the next scene. Blue-and-white training clothes today, short hair windblown into soft disarray. The moment she registered the dessert bags in his hands, her eyes lit up like a marquee — she apologized to the director mid-sentence and came fluttering across the grass at full speed, a small butterfly with zero regard for aerodynamics.

“Oppa! What are you doing here again?!”

Delight she couldn’t have hidden if she’d trained for it.

“I was passing by. Figured I’d check whether the director’s been yelling at you.” He handed the bags over. “Here. Afternoon tea for the whole crew.”

He was not going to tell the girl that he was here to farm a contact reward off her.

“Whoa — these are Pierre Hermé!” She’d caught the logo on the bag, and the shriek carried halfway across the set.

She was still exclaiming over it when her foot found a cable. A yelp; a flail; her entire center of gravity pitched forward—

His arm was already moving. One long reach, and she landed — soft, startled, perfectly caught — against his chest. A faint peach scent came up with her. Through his palm he could feel the exact instant her body went rigid and her heartbeat slammed into a sprint.

“Careful.” He steadied her shoulders until her feet were under her again.

And the moment his fingertips pressed the thin fabric of her training jacket, at the back of his mind, that long-absent chime rang out.

Goddess Handbook · Contact with Sulli — Goddess Ability triggered successfully. Obtained: one Tenfold Rebate Card.

How. HOW does this thing trigger. Answer me. Look me in the eyes. Tell me. WHY. Baby, WHY?!

Somewhere deep in Song Ji-yeon’s skull, ten thousand horses began to stampede.
