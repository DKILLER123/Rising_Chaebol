Chapter Eleven

Eun-jung-unnie, Going Cheap

SEPTEMBER 2012 Apgujeong-dong, Seoul ONGI HOTPOT · THE ENTERTAINMENT ZONE

Eun-jung turned the blue membership card over, twice, then stole another look toward the entertainment zone. Warm light in there: a row of massage chairs standing shoulder to shoulder, and past them a glassed-off manicure corner — pink padded stools around low tables, polish bottles ranked in tidy rows, their colors gleaming soft and slow under the lamps.

“Want to try something?” Ji-yeon followed her eyes over. “You’ve got the points. Five hundred covers a basic hand treatment plus a one-color manicure.”

She pulled her gaze back, shook her head, and held both hands up for display, eyes bright under the cap brim. “Not today. Had these done a few days ago — I’m not ready to change them yet. Next time I want a new set, I’ll come get it done here.”

He watched the ten fingers waving in front of him. Sure enough — every tip washed in a soft red-bean rose.

Side by side they walked out of ONGI, the night air rolling over them with the first chill of early autumn. Two lanterns burned warm above the door, stretching their shadows long down the pavement — long, and overlapping.

“Where are you off to? I’ll drive you.” Car keys out.

She pointed straight up. Behind the mask, her voice came out muffled but smiling. “Forgot already? I live in this building. One elevator ride, door to door. No driving required.”

He followed her finger up. Right. Her apartment sat directly on top of this restaurant. It had slipped his mind.

He nodded and let it be.

“Then I’m off to the office. Today’s revenue won’t post till late — could be a long one.”

“Go on, then. Drive safe.”

He walked to the car — and before pulling the door open, looked back at her one more time.

Eun-jung stood beneath the lanterns at ONGI’s door, the warm light laying a soft rim around her whole silhouette.

“I’m off.” A wave of one hand, and he folded down into the driver’s seat.

The engine turned over; the headlights woke; and the black sedan slid out of its spot and folded itself into the main road’s traffic.

Eun-jung stood on the curb and watched it go — the taillights shrinking to two small red points, then gone around the corner.

She was still standing there.

A gust of night wind flipped her hood; she didn’t fix it. She just stood there, staring down the street where the car had disappeared, the corners of her mouth under the mask curved at an angle she herself knew nothing about.

“Still watching?”

A voice arrived from behind her, teasing with the shamelessness of a person holding all the cards.

“The car’s been gone a while, you know.”

Then — click — a second voice, even worse: “Bo-ram-unnie, come look at Eun-jung-unnie. Right now. ‘Going cheap’ is written all over her face. Tsk, tsk, tsk.”

Eun-jung spun around. Jeon Bo-ram and Park Ji-yeon stood behind her, conjured out of nowhere: Bo-ram with her arms crossed, wearing the face of a woman settled in for good theater; Park Ji-yeon with a hand clamped over her own mouth, shoulders shaking, phone aimed dead at Eun-jung.

“You—” Her voice jumped half an octave. “Why are you — what are you two doing HERE?”

“Dinner. Obviously.” Ji-yeon waggled the phone. “What we didn’t expect was the bonus feature: Eun-jung-unnie’s drama finale. The heroine stands at the roadside watching the hero’s car vanish into the night, unable to take one single step. A classic of the genre. I got all of it on video — I plan to enjoy it slowly, at home, perhaps with snacks.”

Bo-ram stepped in and waved a hand in front of her eyes. “Eun-jung-ah. Come back to earth. The car is genuinely gone. You can stare until sunrise — it will not produce a second car.”

Eun-jung smacked the hand away. Under the mask, her face was hot enough to fry an egg.

On any ordinary day, being caught at dinner with a friend would have added up to nothing. But she had lied on her way out of the dorm — and that turned nothing into something.

And now she couldn’t even diagnose herself: was the burning embarrassment at being caught, or shyness at being teased… or a thorough blend of both.

“I was not — I was getting AIR!”

“Ahh. Air.” Bo-ram drew the word out like taffy. She traded one look with Ji-yeon, and the two of them said it together: “Air’s lovely tonight. Wonderful breeze.”

Eun-jung wheeled around and marched for the lobby, stride fast and furious.

Ji-yeon trotted after her and put her mouth right at her ear. “Unnie — so THAT’S your President Song. Pretty handsome, no wonder you—”

“YAH! Park Ji-yeon, close your mouth!”

“I haven’t even said anything yet!”

“Then say NOTHING!”

Bo-ram brought up the rear at her own leisurely pace, watching the chase ahead of her — one fleeing, one pursuing — and shook her head. The smile wouldn’t stay down no matter how she pressed.

The way their six worked, even if Eun-jung really did start dating someone, she’d never have hidden it from them…

The two of them kept cornering Eun-jung like this purely out of habit — the same way the entire group ambushed Bo-ram herself the second any opening showed. It was just how the team expressed affection.

She would, of course, never admit to having a wicked streak of her own.

✦ ✦ ✦

SAME NIGHT PIEN PLUS, SIXTH FLOOR HAM EUN-JUNG’S APARTMENT

The three of them — bickering the entire way, curb to elevator — arrived at Eun-jung’s apartment.

PIEN PLUS, sixth floor. Eun-jung opened her front door wearing the expression of a woman whose soul had already left her body, and watched the little dragon squeeze past her into the apartment. Blood pressure: climbing.

“YAH! Park Ji-yeon! Phone. Now. Or you and I are going to have a very serious conversation.”

Ji-yeon slithered behind Bo-ram in one step, phone held high, grinning the grin of a cat that had gotten into the cream. “Nope. Nope nope. This is evidence. Try anything, unnie, and tomorrow morning it goes straight into the group chat — headline: ‘T-ara Vocalist Gazes Longingly After Mystery Man at Night — Dating Rumors Suspected.’”

“PARK JI-YEON! I dare you!”

Eun-jung lunged, jaw tight — and Bo-ram caught her clean out of the air. Small as Bo-ram was, the strength was real: two arms out and Eun-jung was locked, waist to shoulder, exactly where she stood.

“Ji-yeon. The door.” Calm command.

“On it!” The door swung shut; the lock clicked home.

Eun-jung experienced the distinct sensation of being a turtle in a jar.

The next ten minutes can only be described as anarchy. Eun-jung attempted breakout after breakout; Bo-ram held the perimeter with a waist-lock that would not be moved; and Park Ji-yeon circled the two of them, filming and doing commentary: “The situation is extremely volatile, ladies and gentlemen. The suspect, one Ham Eun-jung, continues to resist — but our officers have the scene fully contained—”

“PARK JI-YEON — YOU’RE DEAD!”

Eventually the two of them had her pressed into the sofa — Bo-ram at her left, Ji-yeon at her right, a sandwich with no exits.

“Alright. No more struggling.” Bo-ram patted her shoulder with the gravity of a judge. “Confession gets leniency. Resistance gets severity. Talk. Who is this President Song?”

Eun-jung buried her face in the sofa cushion, voice coming out flattened. “Who is he. A friend. We’re friends.”

Park Ji-yeon could tell, plainly, that the red currently occupying Eun-jung-unnie’s face was not the flustered kind that teasing produces. It was the real kind. Her eyes went huge. Wait. Wait wait wait. My Eun-jung-unnie is actually — falling in love???

“Friends?” The volume climbed again. “Friends — and you watch his car drive away for TWO MINUTES? Unnie, do you understand what two minutes is? I watched the entire thing. From the car pulling out to us walking up behind you, you never changed your pose. Not once.”

Her ear tips went red again.

Bo-ram strangled a laugh into a cough. “Eun-jung-ah. How long have you known him?”

Silence.

“Hm?”

From the cushion, thoroughly muffled: “…five days.”

“Come again?”

“FIVE DAYS!” Eun-jung surged upright, face scarlet. “Today makes five days! It’s the second time we’ve ever met! Are you satisfied now?!”

The living room held two full seconds of silence.

Then Park Ji-yeon screamed. “FIVE DAYS? Second meeting?!”

She took Eun-jung’s face in both hands and turned it left, then right, her own expression wrecked with grief. “Unnie. You are T-ara’s Ham Eun-jung. Girl-crush on command, sweetheart on command! And you’re doing THIS! A second meeting and you’re standing on a curb spacing out for two straight minutes — do you have any idea how cheap that makes you look?!”

Bo-ram, beside them, had given up entirely and was pounding the sofa arm. “I can’t — I’m finished — Eun-jung-ah, in all the years I’ve known you, I have never once watched you act like this over a man.”

Eun-jung smacked the hands off her face and dove back into the cushion, her voice dripping with the dignity of the doomed. “WE ARE FRIENDS! Believe whatever you want!”

Park Ji-yeon and Jeon Bo-ram looked at each other.

“We don’t,” they said, in unison.

Eun-jung released a wail of pure despair.

She really should have checked her horoscope before leaving the house tonight.

No — correction. She should never have gone back to the dorm at all.
