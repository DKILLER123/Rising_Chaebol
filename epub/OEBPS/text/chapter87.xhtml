Chapter Eighty-Seven

Oppa Really Isn’t a Con Man

EARLY EVENING Apgujeong-dong, Seoul the department store — Bulgari boutique, then the lot

Leaving the f(x) dorm, Song Ji-yeon didn’t hesitate for a second. He swung the wheel around and aimed the Mercedes back at the department store he’d just left.

He’d given the little peach her gift; he wasn’t about to forget Eun-jung’s share. This time, though, he wasn’t going to give her a watch.

He toured the floors for a while and finally settled on a Bulgari jewelry necklace for Eun-jung. Thirty-six diamonds, set around a central moonstone. The instant he saw it he thought the cool pale shimmer would suit her temperament exactly — so he bought it on the spot.

The price tag read ₩99,800,000. Exactly the right spend to push a Tenfold Rebate Card up against its ceiling without going a single won over.

He stepped out of the Bulgari boutique with the bag in his hand, and the Goddess Handbook’s rebate notification bloomed quietly in the back of his mind.

Goddess Handbook — Tenfold Rebate Card

Ding! Tenfold Rebate Card triggered on purchase.

Spend₩99,800,000

Rebate₩998,000,000 — deposited to host’s bank account

Card consumed. One rebate card remaining.

He stopped walking. Phone out, banking app open. The balance stared back at him.

SEOUL BANK · PERSONAL ACCOUNT

Available balance₩2,811,420,000

Latest deposit+₩998,000,000 (rebate, business income)

Of which: liquid for rebate-card spend₩1,000,000,000 earmarked

“Money this easy is borderline illegal. Pity it’s capped — if the ceiling were any higher it’d be perfect.”

Hefting the Bulgari bag, he allowed himself the one dry observation, then made a straight line for the underground lot.

The rebate card was, undeniably, a thrill. Drop a hundred million, watch a billion land. There were worse ways to spend an evening.

But distant water doesn’t put out a nearby fire. One card a week, and even if he ran the math out to its absolute ceiling, he’d clear maybe ten billion in a week — and that was assuming the spend pool never ran dry. Against the sums he actually needed, it was pocket money. Entertainment. The kind of game you played in the back of a taxi on a slow afternoon.

Fun, sure. A band-aid.

The technologies sitting in his handbook vault told a different story. The zero-sugar soju synthesis. The TikTok source-code dossier. Each one of those, the day he could light the fuse, needed startup capital in the hundreds of billions — proper industrial-scale money, the kind you couldn’t get from kissing Sulli once a week.

By that math, the rebate card was the long game’s pocket change. Real capital — the kind that bent industries — would have to come out of the market. The Stock Forecast Card was the engine. The rebate card was the dice he rolled between trades, just to keep his hands busy.

So: necklace in hand, Eun-jung owed a moonstone, two billion in the bank — and not nearly enough to fund the empire in his head. Back to the office. The market opens in the morning.

✦ ✦ ✦

SAME EVENING Cheongdam-dong, Seoul the f(x) dorm — the living room

Back at the f(x) dorm, Sulli was sitting on the sofa, head bowed over the silver-white Cartier on her wrist, a smile she could not for the life of her suppress tugging at the corners of her mouth. Every so often she lifted her wrist up and turned it under the light, watching the small bright shatter of the diamonds; the more she looked, the more she liked it.

Krystal sat cross-legged at the other end of the sofa, phone in hand, but her eyes kept drifting back to Sulli. The expression on her face was a studied one.

This little peach was making such a show of not accepting the gift ten minutes ago. Now look at her.

“Yah, Sulli — you’ve been staring at that thing for almost an hour. Aren’t you tired?” Krystal finally broke. “It’s a watch. One watch. Is this really necessary?”

Krystal knew luxury better than Sulli did. She had a fashion-obsessed older sister, after all.

In her eyes the watch was certainly expensive — but, you know, just a watch. She could buy one too, if she really wanted.

What she could not figure out was why Sulli was making such a state of it.

“What do you know!” Sulli didn’t even look up. Her fingertips brushed the dial with absurd delicacy. “This is the first present oppa ever gave me!”

Krystal made a face. Fine. Anything that touches love, in the eyes of a girl whose head is full of it, multiplies its value a hundredfold. Love is priceless, after all.

She looked at Sulli’s insufferably smug profile and felt a twitch of irritation. She decided to push.

“You’re treasuring it that much — you really think Song Ji-yeon-oppa’s your boyfriend now? Only — oppa never actually confessed to you. Are we in a fantasy here, or are we doing the object-as-substitute-for-the-person thing…”

“Yah! Jung Soo-jung!” Sulli went scarlet. Humiliated and furious in one breath, she grabbed the throw pillow off the sofa and hurled it at her.

Krystal swayed sideways with the ease of long practice. The pillow clipped past her shoulder and smacked into the shoe cabinet in the entryway.

And at that exact moment, the sound of a key turning in the lock came from the entryway.

Both of them froze. Their heads swung in unison toward the door.

It swung open. A figure in a casual jacket, hair pulled back in a simple tail, stepped in.

Victoria had a suitcase in one hand and several shopping bags in the other. Her face wore the bone-deep tiredness of a long flight, but her spirits looked intact. The moment she crossed the threshold, her gaze landed on the lone throw pillow lying on the floor — and then moved to the two youngest members in the living room, wearing very different expressions. One of her eyebrows rose a fraction.

“What’s all this? You two fighting?”

Krystal recovered first. She put on a placating face, sprang off the sofa, and crossed over to take the shopping bags out of Victoria’s hands.

“Vic-unnie, you’re back — you must be exhausted. We weren’t doing anything, we were just… messing around.”

Sulli scrambled up too, hiding the wrist with the watch behind her back, guilt already stitched across her face, and squeezed out a smile.

“Vic-unnie, you’re back! How was the schedule?”

Victoria’s eyes swept once over each of their faces. Something wasn’t sitting right.

She changed into slippers, walked into the living room, leaned the suitcase against the wall, and stretched. “Fine, just tired. They packed this trip back to back — two days nonstop. Finally over.”

She dropped onto the sofa, picked up the water pitcher on the coffee table, poured herself a glass, drank half of it. Her eyes passed, apparently by accident, over Sulli — the girl was still standing where she’d stood, posture stiff, smile strained, both hands hidden behind her back, looking for all the world like someone hiding contraband.

And there it was. The wrong note. When she came back from a schedule before, this little peach would come cling to her immediately. What was different today?

Victoria set down the glass. Her eyes narrowed, just slightly.

“Sulli.”

Sulli’s pulse jumped. In her head she was already blaming Vic-unnie for not calling ahead — if she’d just given them ten minutes’ warning, Sulli could have stashed the watch somewhere safe. Now the thing was right there on her wrist. If Vic-unnie saw it, asked where it came from, and Sulli had to say Song Ji-yeon-oppa gave it to her — was there any version of this that didn’t end in death?

She forced the answer out, the pitch of her voice climbing an entire tone.

“Ne?”

Victoria tilted her head, faintly curious. What was this little peach doing with her hands behind her back?

“What are you hiding?”

Sulli’s body went stiff. The smile on her face froze solid.

She took an instinctive half-step backward, the words stumbling out.

“Nothing — nothing, unnie, I’m not hiding anything.”

Victoria had only meant to feel her out — she’d read the guilty posture and decided to bluff. She hadn’t expected the girl to fold this fast. The stammering was, in effect, a written confession.

Victoria’s gaze slid past her to Krystal, still standing to one side.

“Is that right. Soo-jung, you tell me.”

Krystal, just called on, was speechless. Wait — how did your war draft me?

She glanced at Sulli, then at Vic-unnie, and did the math. Sulli was easier to coax back into a good mood. Vic-unnie, once she got mad, was impossible. So she picked her side, decisively, and opened her mouth.

“Unnie, actually—”

“Jung Soo-jung!” Sulli’s head whipped around. Her eyes went round as saucers, the warning in them unmistakable: If you sell me out, I will never speak to you again.

Caught between two unnies she could not afford to offend, Krystal gave up. She shrugged, spread her hands, and produced the most innocent face in her repertoire.

“Unnie, actually… I just got back to the dorm myself. I really don’t know what happened.”

Victoria watched the two of them trade eye-contact volleys and grew more certain by the second that something was wrong. She didn’t push. She leaned back against the sofa, folded her arms, and surveyed Sulli with a quiet, appraising gaze.

“Sulli. Show me your hand.”

Sulli bit her lip and didn’t move. Her face was a small war.

“Unnie… there’s really nothing…”

Victoria’s tone was the kind that did not invite further discussion — the elder-sister authority that f(x)’s leader could deploy without raising her voice.

“Show me.”

She wanted to see what this little peach had on her that was worth all this theatre.

Sulli fought it out for two seconds, then surrendered. Head down, she drew her hidden hand out from behind her back, slowly. On her wrist, the silver-white Cartier Ballon Bleu caught the living-room light and gave back a low, luxurious gleam.

The instant the hand came out, Victoria saw the watch.

She didn’t understand, at first, what was worth hiding about a watch. But knowing Sulli the way she did, she asked anyway.

“This watch… where’d it come from?”

Sulli couldn’t look her in the eye. Head down, her voice went as small as a mosquito humming.

“It was… it was a gift from someone…”

Victoria’s suspicion went up another notch.

“From who?”

Sulli bit her lip and said nothing.

A bad feeling started in Victoria’s chest.

Her gaze turned back to Krystal.

“Soo-jung. You say.”

Krystal glanced at Sulli, saw no veto in her face, and answered.

“It’s from Song Ji-yeon.”

“Song Ji-yeon?! That con man.” Victoria’s voice jumped an entire octave. She was on her feet. “When did he even get here?! Seriously — going behind my back again, contacting you. Everything I told you, in one ear and out the other!”

Victoria was, at this point, properly angry. She’d been gone two days. The man had broken into the house while her back was turned.

And — why was Sulli still in touch with that con man?

And sending her a watch — what was that supposed to mean?

Sulli blinked. The nerves were getting worse.

“Unnie, it’s not like that! Oppa really isn’t a con man!”

The defense only stoked Victoria higher. She let out a long breath through her nose — the disappointed-older-sister exhale.

“Sulli-ya. You’re still young. Men out there wear one face in front of you and a different one behind your back. How many girls in this industry have you heard of getting taken by some man? This… Song Ji-yeon. You don’t even know what he’s after, coming at you. Look — he’d barely met you before he tried to use you to get me to be his — what was it — zero-sugar soju brand ambassador. And what came of it? Zero-sugar soju is a concept. It isn’t even in production. You think a man who plays that kind of empty-hand bluffer’s game is a good person?”

“Now he’s giving you presents. You think I don’t know what kind of calculation is running in his head?”

Sulli’s own temper was rising. Why did Vic-unnie keep deciding Song Ji-yeon-oppa was a bad man, a con man? She didn’t want him slandered — not even by Vic-unnie.

She dug in and fired back.

“Vic-unnie… oppa isn’t a con man. He’s never lied to me. He’s never once asked me for anything. And if he were a con man, why would he be the one spending money on me? Giving me presents?”

Victoria leaned against the doorframe, arms folded, her face hard.

“That’s exactly what worries me. Sulli, listen to unnie. There’s a kind of man in this world who specializes in buying a girl’s trust with little gifts, then slowly draining her dry. You think he’s wonderful now. The day he asks to borrow money, you’ll find out what regret tastes like.”

“I— I don’t have any money for him to take!” Sulli was near-shouting now, eyes going wet. “Unnie, you know — everything I’ve made since debut is sitting in my mom’s account! I’ve got some living expenses, that’s it. There’s nothing else. What could oppa even take from me?”

Victoria was caught for a beat, but her tone stayed sharp.

“Have you told him how much you actually have? Maybe he thinks you’re sitting on a fortune.”

“I didn’t!” The wronged note crept into Sulli’s voice. “I never mentioned money to him. Not once.”

“Then he’s after — ”

The two of them were still at it when Krystal, who had been sitting on the sofa watching this whole time, finally broke. If they kept going, the dinner reservation tonight was dead — and she had dressed for it.

She put in, mildly, from the sofa:

“Um… Vic-unnie.”

Victoria turned to look at her. Krystal raised a hand and gestured at the watch on Sulli’s wrist.

“This model is the Cartier Ballon Bleu, set diamonds, the new release. Boutique retail runs about — give or take — a hundred million won. I already checked it for Sulli. It’s genuine — papers, anti-counterfeit code, the works.”

She paused, then turned to Victoria.

“Think about it, unnie. What kind of con man, to fleece a girl with zero savings — a girl who has to nickel-and-dime her bubble tea — what kind of con man opens by sinking a hundred million won on a watch for her? The cost-to-target ratio is insane. If he’s running a game, the playbook is to fleece first, pay later. You don’t drop a hundred million on the mark before the trap is even set.”

Sulli heard Krystal take her side, and the first wave was a small, warm flood of gratitude — she hadn’t known the watch cost a hundred million won. She’d known it was a name brand, surely expensive, but a hundred million…

Oppa really is too good to me.

And then the second wave was indignation. Nickel-and-dime her bubble tea? Was she really that broke? She was very generous, thank you. So what if she’d let Krystal order two cups today.

Was holding a grudge really necessary?

Krystal’s little speech left Victoria, for a long second, without a reply.

She knew Krystal’s knowledge of luxury pricing was reliable. This didn’t sound invented.

But — a hundred million won. On a watch. As a gift.

Victoria still couldn’t quite believe it. This Song Ji-yeon, the con man — why would he spend that kind of money on Sulli?

She was just opening her mouth to argue when Sulli’s head snapped up — as if she’d just remembered something.

“Unnie! Wait — ”

Sulli’s eyes went bright. She turned and bolted for her room.

“I have proof! Oppa really isn’t a con man — I can show you!”

Krystal’s argument had flipped a switch in Sulli’s head.

Right. Vic-unnie kept calling oppa a con man because she was afraid oppa would take her money.

But her stock account was sitting right there with every won oppa had made for her — and every trade had a record.

If she showed Vic-unnie that, there was no way unnie could still think oppa was a con man. Right?

She blew into her room like a small hurricane, and was back almost as fast, laptop cradled in her arms, fingers flying across the screen.

“Unnie, look!” Sulli held the laptop out in front of Victoria. The trading platform filled the screen. “This week, oppa traded the account for me — last week alone, I made sixty million!”

Victoria blinked, took the laptop reflexively, and looked down.

The history page was open.

STOCK TRADING HISTORY · ACCOUNT HOLDER: CHOI SULLI

Holding 1 — Samsung Electronics21 days · profit +₩17,000,000

Top-up+₩300,000,000 (To the Beautiful You filming fee)

Holding 2 — Sewon Electronics2 days · profit +₩60,000,000

Holding 3 — Samjin Group (today, 10 A.M.)₩360,000,000 in · +6% in half a day

Principal currently in account₩364,000,000

Audit trailEvery trade on record. Buy, sell, P&L — all logged on the exchange.

Trade by trade, clean as a ledger. Buy, sell, every entry showing a tidy floating profit.

“This… is your account?” Victoria’s voice had gone dry.

“Mine, mine!” Sulli nodded fast, the small smugness she couldn’t quite hide creeping into her face. “I let oppa manage the stock account for me — I never had time to look at it, I didn’t actually think he’d be making me money. Look at last week’s return — sixty million! In two days!”

Victoria said nothing. She just stared at the screen, fingers scrolling slowly down the page — until they reached the principal line.

₩364,000,000.

The instant the number landed, Victoria’s pupils shrank. Her whole body went still.

The principal in this account was over three hundred and sixty million won.

She had always assumed — whenever this silly girl mentioned “playing stocks” — that the account held, at most, a few tens of millions. Some pin money scraped together from living expenses.

If Song Ji-yeon was running the long con, those few tens of millions would be beneath his interest.

But here it was, in black and white. Three hundred and sixty-four million. If he was a con man, this was the moment he’d be netting the catch. And yet — not one won had left the account.

It was all sitting in Samjin Group right now.

He had not taken a single won into his own hand.

And — if he really meant to bleed her — why would he go to all this trouble to trade the account for her, and put a real sixty million in actual profit in her pocket?

Let alone sink a hundred-million-won Cartier on top of it.

What con man, in the entire history of cons, puts up real cash to help his mark’s money multiply?

Unless…

Unless he’s actually helping her.

Victoria’s hand on the laptop tightened without her noticing, the knuckles going faintly white.

She looked down at the silver-white watch on Sulli’s wrist. Then at Sulli’s face, so earnest it was almost painful. And somewhere inside her chest, the wire that had been pulled tight all this time quietly went slack.

Had she really gotten him wrong?

Once the thought surfaced, it caught like wildfire — no amount of pressing would stamp it back down.

Sulli pointed at the equity curve on the screen, eyes shining as she looked up at Victoria.

“See, unnie? Oppa really didn’t lie to me. All my money’s still in the market — and look, the Samjin Group he bought this morning is already up.”

Victoria didn’t actually think one trading screen proved anything. She kept her voice hard.

“Hmph. Could be a show he’s putting on for you. Long line, big fish. Wait until you pour more in, then sweep the whole pond.”

Sulli made a face — but she’d noticed Vic-unnie wasn’t anywhere near as worked up as before. She smiled.

“Don’t worry, unnie. That’s all the money I have this year — the agency doesn’t settle my fees until year-end. So unnie can relax. Even if oppa were running a con, the most he could take is the three hundred sixty million.”

“Yah, Choi Sulli. Don’t go thinking three hundred sixty million is small. Three hundred sixty million is enough to live on for a lifetime. You know that?”

Victoria’s voice came out rougher than she meant it to. Sulli, chastened, ducked her head.

Krystal, on the sofa, watched the two of them and said nothing. The math in her own head, though, was running on a different track entirely.

She was comparing. Quietly. In the back of her mind.

She knew — everyone in the f(x) dorm knew — about Kwon Young-il. The man who’d been circling Victoria for months now, offering her fashion resources the way other men offered flowers: constantly, lavishly, on paper. Magazine covers. Runway slots. Brand introductions. He’d been promising them since the spring.

To date: not one had materialized. Not a single resource. Not one introduction that actually landed. The man’s mouth was a printing press for hollow commitment.

And — most tellingly — Kwon Young-il had never once put anything worth eight figures in Vic-unnie’s hands. Not a watch. Not a piece of jewelry. Not one concrete, hold-it-in-your-palm token of the alleged fortune he kept saying he’d unlock for her. He talked a great fashion empire. He had never bought her so much as a handbag.

Krystal’s gaze drifted, against her will, back to the silver-white Cartier on Sulli’s wrist.

A hundred million. Up front. No strings. No ask back. The mark had zero savings. He’d known that when he paid.

Compared to the man circling Vic-unnie — the man whose every promise was a paper check, whose every “next month” became the next month after — Song Ji-yeon came out, frankly, looking very, very good.

Krystal filed the thought. She would not say it aloud. Not in front of Vic-unnie, not in front of Sulli.

But the next time a certain someone came up in conversation, she was not going to let the word con man pass unchallenged.

Across the room, Victoria was still holding the laptop. She hadn’t moved to give it back.

The Cartier winked quietly under the light on Sulli’s wrist, the curve of the equity graph glowed soft green on the screen, and the room — for the first time since Victoria had walked through the door — settled into something that, while not exactly peace, was no longer war.
