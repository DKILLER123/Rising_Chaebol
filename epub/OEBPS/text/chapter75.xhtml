Chapter Seventy-Five

Am I the First Woman to Know Oppa’s Passcode?

SATURDAY MORNING · EIGHT THIRTY Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Unit 101 — Song Ji-yeon’s bedroom

Ding-dong. Ding-dong.

The doorbell dragged Song Ji-yeon up out of sleep by the collar. He fumbled the phone off the nightstand and squinted: eight-thirty in the morning.

“Who is it… at this hour… don’t these people sleep.” The complaint left the factory pre-loaded with sleep.

Last night had run long. Home, shower, bed — and then KakaoTalk with Eun-jung, one line pulling the next, until somewhere past one he’d finally put the phone face-down and closed his eyes. Eight-thirty meant seven hours and change, and for a man whose biological norm was wake when the sun says so, this was not rest. This was extraction.

Ding-dong. Ding-dong.

Still ringing. One after another, no exit strategy. He sighed, scrubbed a hand through his ruined hair, surrendered to fate, shuffled to the entryway in his slippers, and put an eye to the peephole.

If it wasn’t the little dragon, then who else in the world could it be.

Park Ji-yeon stood outside in a loose white tee and pale jeans, hair down and unbothered, face bare — on tiptoe, one eye screwed nearly shut, actively trying to look in through his peephole, lips moving in quiet negotiation with herself.

He pulled the door open mid-research, yawning.

“Ji-yeon-ah. It’s the crack of dawn. What is it?”

The girl’s nervous system registered none of his condition. The open door registered instantly, and her whole face lit up like a sign flipping to OPEN.

“Song Ji-yeon-oppa! Good morning!”

He leaned on the doorframe and looked at her — battery at one hundred percent, output at one hundred and ten — and felt his sleepiness double.

I genuinely want to thump her one.

“What ‘morning.’ It’s eight-thirty. Do you know what time I finally slept last night?”

“Aigoo, oppa!” The complaint was simply deleted from the record. “You’re not busy today, right? Come shopping with me!”

Her internal logic, for the record: yesterday the group had gotten its marching orders — months of nothing, besides the overseas dates already signed — and she had spent a decent interval being sad about it, before arriving at the truth. This was a vacation. A real one. The first since debut.

So this morning she had woken up in her big empty flat, looked around at all the empty, and decided: furnish it. She had drafted the list. Then she had audited the list and discovered it required staff.

The unnis were all girls, and she had already fully exploited their goodwill once, during the move. However thick a skin she carried — and she carried a professionally thick one — a person could not simply keep fleecing the same five women on rotation.

Hence: the oppa one floor down. Guilt problem solved at the source.

Song Ji-yeon nearly laughed himself into a coughing fit.

So this — the dawn raid, the doorbell, all of it — was a labor requisition. He was being press-ganged as a pack mule.

“Shopping.” He gave the word no decoration whatsoever. “What on earth needs buying at eight-thirty in the morning?”

“I barely moved in! The place is still empty!” She said it with the conviction of someone stating physics. The big eyes blinked at him, twice. “Come with me, oppa. Going alone is so boring.”

Song Ji-yeon looked at her for a moment. Language failed to organize itself.

“Oppa. Come on.” She registered the reluctance and pivoted instantly to the pity offensive, eyes going wide and wounded.

It was shameless, and it worked. Without the stage armor — no lashes, no styling, just that fair, soft face carrying its last remnant of baby fat — Park Ji-yeon looked about as easy to refuse as a stray in the rain.

He ran the math. Already awake. Re-sleep: not happening. Fine.

“All right, all right. I’ll come.”

“Jinjja?!” She bounced on the spot, hands flying up. “I KNEW it — oppa’s the best!!”

He shook his head at the celebration and turned for the bathroom.

“Wait there. I’m washing up.”

“Ne!”

“Wait there,” as a concept, lasted zero seconds. She came in right behind him, changed into the house slippers at the entryway on her own authority, and attached herself to his wake like a small satellite.

She had her reasons. She had to supervise. What if oppa un-agreed? Then the whole morning would be wasted.

He stood at the sink, toothbrush working. She stationed herself against the bathroom doorframe, arms crossed, and opened the briefing.

“Oppa, I am buying a LOT today. Both guest rooms have nothing — no mattresses, no bedding, nothing. Once I buy it all, next time the unnis drink at my place, nobody has to go home.”

“And oppa — I’m converting the study. Into a gaming room. I said it the day I signed the lease: this apartment WILL have a room whose entire purpose is games.”

He answered with a foam-muted mm.

“I’m getting a huge backlit monitor — the biggest they make! Movies and games, both incredible!” The briefing gained altitude; her hands entered the presentation. “And a gaming chair! The kind that goes flat! You grind until you die, and then you just sleep right there!”

He rinsed and splashed water over his face. Through the running water, the briefing continued to arrive at full fidelity.

“And the PC has to be the best one! Something that runs every game there is! Oppa — desktop or laptop? I feel like desktop for performance, but laptop for carrying…”

“How about… one of each.”

He toweled off and caught her eye in the mirror — the girl was now fully gesturing at an imaginary battle station — and a laugh got loose before he could vet it.

“You wish.”

“Hehe. Anyway — oppa has good taste, so you’re advising on everything!”

“Fine, fine. Your call on all of it.”

“Yay!! Oppa’s the best!!” She nearly achieved liftoff.

He went into the bedroom and pulled a plain white tee and casual slacks from the closet. From the doorway came the ongoing broadcast — a small head poked around the frame, still transmitting.

“Oppa, what color for the chair? White’s so pretty, but it shows everything… black’s cool, but it’s boring… ugh, this is so hard…”

Dressed, phone in hand, he walked over to her and flicked her once on the forehead.

“Stop deciding at my door. Decide at the store — slowly, with the actual chairs.”

“Aiyo!” She clutched her forehead, wrinkled her nose at him — and then grinned and waved him forward. “Let’s go, let’s go! Oppa, we’re departing!”

He took one look at her total inability to stand still, laughed, pocketed the car keys off the shoe cabinet, and followed her out.

✦ ✦ ✦

The Mercedes rolled out of the underground garage with Park Ji-yeon in the passenger seat, transmitting continuously.

“Oppa, faster, faster!! I dreamed about the new gaming room last night! I was WINNING!”

She was pitched forward against the seatbelt, both hands grappling the center console, visibly attempting to accelerate the car through willpower.

Song Ji-yeon absorbed the noise in a state of advanced fatigue — yawned once, one hand on the wheel, the other rubbing the sleep out of an eye.

“Got it, got it. Keep talking and I will deposit you at a bus stop. Believe me or don’t.”

He had simply never measured the girl’s range. Park Ji-yeon’s chatter had a full tank and no fuel gauge.

“You wouldn’t.” Grinning, entirely unthreatened, she leaned in closer instead of retreating. “Oppa — do you play games?”

“Barely.”

Last life, he had — college years, Koreatown PC bangs, T-ara on the earphones, ladder points on the screen. This life, between the money and the women, the hours had simply never materialized. Office slacking exempted, obviously. A man is entitled to a few rounds on company time.

“Eh?! Then what do you even DO?” Genuinely appalled. “On your days off, I mean.”

He considered it. Days off… his days off currently consisted of talking to Eun-jung, or talking to Sulli.

…Huh. Put that way, his life was a corridor with two doors.

“Reading. Working out.” Two hobbies selected for their résumé value.

Away from home, a man authors his own references.

“Pfft. You’re basically a grandpa.” She flapped a hand. Then her eyes rolled up into a scheme. “Oppa — then buy a computer too! The same as mine!”

He glanced sideways. Whatever was brewing in the gourd, the lid was on tight.

“What for? I’ve got no time to play.”

“Who says you have no time!” Fingers deployed, counting. “You come home to sleep, don’t you? So — evenings, a little. And weekends? Weekends exist!”

Then she pressed both palms together, and the big eyes came up at full luminosity.

“Please… pleeease? Just buy it to play with me, okay? You can put the computer in MY study. And then… we duo-queue!”

Her logic was sincere: she wanted a partner. Of every soul she knew, only Sunny-unnie actually gamed — and Sunny-unnie’s schedule was a black hole; the woman hadn’t touched a ranked match in a year.

Song Ji-yeon heard put the computer in my study and nearly stood on the brake.

Install hardware at her place. Was this the modern cohabitation invitation? Some new model he hadn’t been briefed on?

Then he took one look at her face — open, guileless, broadcasting nothing but games games games — and understood he was overthinking a nineteen-year-old. The concept of male-female boundaries had not yet finished loading onto that system.

“Not afraid I’ll move in and never leave?” He teased on purpose.

“Not even a little! Oppa can stay as long as oppa wants!” She read the softening in real time and immediately pushed the stack. “Oppa — my door code is 0110. Come over and game whenever you feel like it.”

That actually made him laugh. He hadn’t even agreed yet, and the girl had already handed over her house keys as a promotional incentive.

He shook his head. After an offer like that, saying no would make him the difficult one.

“Fine. One rig, stationed in your study.” A pause, and then, because fair was fair: “But — since I now hold your state secret, here’s mine: 0818. Next time you come down, let yourself in. And stop ringing my bell, it’s loud.”

“Jinjja — 0818, memorized!”

The second half of his sentence had not landed at all. She was already floating on the honor of holding oppa’s door code, mouth curving up in open delight.

“Oppa — am I the first girl to know your door code?”

He thought about it. Family aside… the first woman to know it was, in all honesty, probably Eun-jung.

But Eun-jung, at present, wanted exactly zero members of T-ara to know that the two of them were anything at all.

“No. My mother was first.”

“Yah! Family doesn’t count! I said girl! A girl!”

He smiled, said nothing further, and kept his eyes on the road. Some questions you let the navigation answer.

SATURDAY MORNING Yongsan, Seoul the Yongsan Electronics Market

The car slipped into the Yongsan district, and the city changed registers — towers of a certain age standing shoulder to shoulder, their skins layered edge to edge with billboards: components, cameras, audio, everything with a plug on it.

In 2012, this was still the Peninsula’s grand bazaar of electronics. Even at this hour on a weekend, the streets were already moving, the sidewalks bright with foot traffic.

He found a parking structure, and Park Ji-yeon went into idol protocol — mask up, cap on — before the two of them got out.

Then the protocol collapsed instantly, because the girl was off like a bird the second the cage opened — head swiveling at every storefront, eyes multiplying to take in the stock.

“Whoa… oppa, it’s HUGE here!”

First visit to Yongsan, for the record. It showed.

“Stay close. Don’t get lost.”

They walked into one of the towers — ELECTRONICS CITY, said the sign — and the air inside arrived first: warm plastic and circuit boards, the smell of a thousand small shops breathing. Closet-sized storefronts, aisles you had to walk through sideways, wire bundles running the ceiling like ivy, walls papered in spec sheets and posters. At every threshold a salesman stood radiating hospitality: just browsing? what are you looking for?

Park Ji-yeon edged unconsciously closer to his orbit. He simply cruised past the enthusiasm, impervious. He had a target profile: a big shop. A legitimate-looking one. An establishment, not a stall.

He found it on the third-floor corner — a clean, orderly storefront with PROFESSIONAL BUILDS · QUALITY GUARANTEED lettered across the glass.

“This one.”

Inside, a modest floor shelved to the ceiling with monitors and towers. Behind the counter, a man in his forties, comfortably built, sat reading a newspaper — which he lowered, stood, and traded for a professional smile as they walked in.

“Welcome, welcome! What are we looking for today?”

“Boss — we’re building PCs.” Park Ji-yeon beat him to it by a full stride, already pointing at a tower on the shelf with the energy of someone ordering for the table. “The BEST kind!”

At the word best, something behind the boss’s eyes changed channels. His gaze ran a quick lap over the two of them — plain tees, jeans, nothing loud — but the bearing didn’t match the clothes, and merchants can read bearing the way sailors read sky. Verdict, logged instantly: two big fish.

“Of course!” The smile upgraded a full sincerity tier. “We run the whole range here — starter builds to full-enthusiast flagships. And what would the primary use be? Gaming? Work?”

“Gaming!” Zero hesitation. “It has to run EVERYTHING! Max settings, zero stutter! And the monitor — the biggest! And a gaming chair — the kind that reclines flat!”

The entire requirement set, discharged in one breath. The boss blinked through it, and somewhere behind his ribs, a cash register went off.

This was the perfect customer. No knowledge, full wallet, requirements pre-sorted into the two categories that matter: expensive and good.

He cleared his throat and entered his performing register.

“Madam has walked into exactly the right shop. High-end gaming rigs are our house specialty. Per your requirements, allow me to sketch the current top of the market—”

Paper, pen, and the torrent began.

“CPU — Intel’s newest i7-3770K. Four cores, eight threads, three-point-five gigahertz. That runs anything with pixels. Motherboard — ASUS Republic of Gamers, the ROG line, built for exactly this. Memory, sixteen gigs, G.Skill, no discussion. And the graphics card — Nvidia’s GTX 680. This year’s king of cards, four gigs of video memory. There is no game on the market that card cannot carry on its back—”

The spec sheet poured on. Park Ji-yeon absorbed it like weather — understood nothing, impressed by all of it.

Song Ji-yeon saved them both.

“Boss.” He raised a hand. “Skip the details.” One finger at Park Ji-yeon, then back at himself. “Two sets. The best you just described — twice.”

“T—two sets?”

The pen actually wobbled in the man’s hand. He’d been selling to the girl. The quiet one was buying too?

“Two sets.” Song Ji-yeon nodded, tone flat as a man buying two cabbages. “Best of everything. Monitors, keyboards, mice, headsets — and the chairs — two of each. Money is not the variable here. Quality is.”

“Aigoo — yes, sir!!” The boss’s voice cracked audibly on the second syllable. “You leave it to me — I’ll have it built perfect, I swear it on the shop!”

One morning’s take: roughly half a month of normal business. The man came alive. Tea was poured. Water was fetched. Brochures materialized across the counter, open to the color pages — please, choose finishes at your leisure.

Park Ji-yeon got approximately four pages into the gaming-chair section and stalled.

“Oppa… white or black? White’s so pretty, but it shows every speck of dust. Black’s cool too — and black forgives.”

She was not a pink girl, for the record. Neutral solids only — black, white, the occasional gray. It was, in its way, very her.

“Buy both. One’s yours, one’s mine.”

“…Right!! Oppa’s a genius!”

He personally had no stake in chair aesthetics. A man who had survived the seating of Koreatown PC bangs in his first life feared no chair on this earth.

The two of them picked through the rest like tourists — a mousepad migrated into the pile, then a gamepad, then a webcam, then things neither of them could later justify. When the boss finally ran the arithmetic, two flagship builds plus the full peripheral annex came to a number just north of ₩10,000,000.

For 2012, that was not computer shopping. That was a vehicle.

Park Ji-yeon didn’t blink. Card, keypad, four digits, done.

“I’m paying oppa’s too. Consider it thank-you wages for today’s conscription.”

Song Ji-yeon didn’t argue it. Ten million wouldn’t register on her books, and if the girl wanted to buy her helper a computer, that was between her and her account balance. Neither of them was built for the ceremony of pretending to fight over a bill.

The boss looked at the printed slip climbing out of the card machine and his face folded entirely into smiles.

“Honored guests — one moment’s patience! I’ll have my boy bring the new stock up from the warehouse, and we unbox it right here in front of you! When it’s built I’ll deliver it personally — install, tune, test, all of it, free.”

“Good. Much obliged.”

They left a phone number and an address, and then Song Ji-yeon took her through the neighborhood for round two — another mall, a different department: comforters, sheets, duvet covers, pillows. Bedding, at industrial volume.

By the time everything had been hauled up to her flat and squared away, the computer shop called. He went down for round three — escorting the boss and his boy back up with the towers and the chairs.

Then it was unboxing, assembly, cabling, OS, drivers — a full production line in the study. Park Ji-yeon orbited the operation at high speed, fetching screwdrivers nobody needed and steadying monitors that were already steady, contributing nothing but morale. Buying a computer, staged with the logistics of a house move.

Song Ji-yeon, for his part, lay flat on her sofa with his phone, conserving what remained of his life force.

By noon, both machines were alive.

The study had become a small command center: two new desks standing side by side, two thirty-two-inch panels glowing with desktop wallpapers, and the two gaming chairs — one black, one white — standing sentry.

“Boss. Appreciate the work.” Song Ji-yeon handed over a ₩50,000 note with the receipt.

“Aiyo — you shouldn’t, you really shouldn’t—” said the mouth, while the hand accepted it at professional speed, and the face folded back into its creases of joy. “It’s nothing! It’s nothing! Honored guests — anything at all, any computer question, any hour — you call me. I mean it. I’ll be there.”

The satisfied boss gathered his tools and left, and the flat finally exhaled.
