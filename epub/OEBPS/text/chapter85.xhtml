Chapter Eighty-Five

The Joy of Spending Money

MONDAY · LATE MORNING Mapo-gu, Seoul SAVORY F&B headquarters, president’s office

Song Ji-yeon stared at the long tail of zeros in Goo Hara’s brokerage account, and his finger stopped on the mouse.

Fifteen. Hundred. Million. Won.

He double-checked the number twice; the surprise still wouldn’t quite settle. It wasn’t that he’d never seen that much money — it was that he hadn’t expected Goo Hara to trust him with it. A girl he’d met once. Whose background, if he was being honest, he didn’t even know very well.

She’s not the least bit worried I’d just take it and disappear to the far side of the planet?

He leaned back in the chair and looked at the figure on the screen, and a certain face surfaced in his head — the little mermaid’s, exquisitely pretty and faintly, deliberately, distant.

Their meeting had been almost impromptu. She hadn’t asked him much of anything; they’d chatted for a few minutes, and the next morning her account password had arrived in his inbox.

“This little mermaid… she’s got nerves.”

He shook his head, a laugh escaping him — and, underneath it, something warm moving through his chest without permission.

Fifteen hundred million won was no small sum even for a working idol. To hand it over this cleanly meant one of two things: she was hopelessly naïve, or she genuinely trusted him.

Either way, he was not going to be the man who let that down.

He sat up straight, fingers flying across the keyboard, and slid into the trading platform.

No hesitation. He queued the entire ₩1.5 billion sitting in Hara’s account as a buy order for Samjin Group.

Then he put in the rest of what was sitting in his own — ₩5 billion, every last won of it.

And, of course, he switched the leverage back on. Two times.

Two-times leverage meant the risk doubled — but so did the upside. The Stock Forecast Card had never been wrong. He trusted it.

A total of ₩11.5 billion in capital rolled into the market.

SAMJIN GROUP · POSITION OPENED

Goo Hara account — principal₩1.5 billion (cash, no leverage)

Song Ji-yeon account — principal₩5.0 billion

Because the size was big, the retail sell-side simply didn’t have that many matching lots lined up.

He had to fall back on the broker’s smart-routing engine — buying in piece by piece, order by order, as the matching sell orders surfaced.

Song Ji-yeon settled into the chair, watching the trade confirmations tick up in both accounts, his finger tapping an idle rhythm on the desktop.

The on-screen numbers jumped in single increments; the buy-side progress bar crawled forward like a snail in no particular hurry.

He waited. Got bored. Picked up the phone, opened the banking app.

He pulled up the Peninsula Bank corporate account Park Seon-young had sent over earlier that morning — ShiningYouth Entertainment’s new account — and transferred ₩4 billion across in a single wire.

The transfer screen flashed Processing. He watched the little spinner for a few seconds, set the phone down on the desk, and turned his eyes back to the monitors.

The stock buys were still printing.

The moment he looked away, the phone buzzed. Screen lit; a KakaoTalk bubble popped up — Park Seon-young.

KakaoTalk · Park Seon-young

Park Seon-young

Sajangnim! The ₩4 billion just landed! I verified it twice — every won accounted for!

Song Ji-yeon

Good. Push the business license hard. The moment it’s in hand tomorrow, you notify Director Kim immediately.

Song Ji-yeon

Not at all, not at all — serving you is an honor, Sajangnim! 🙇‍♂️

Song Ji-yeon read the message, the corner of his mouth twitching up. This Park Seon-young — efficient on the work, and as for the brown-nosing, the man was at the absolute top of his craft.

No wonder he made department director before he turned thirty-five. Honestly — if not him, then who.

He smiled, set the phone down, leaned back in the broad president’s chair, and let out a long breath.

Four billion won had landed in ShiningYouth’s corporate account. Now it was just: wait for Park Seon-young to bring back the business license, then sit down with Kim Kwang-soo and sign the buyout contract. After that, T-ara was his.

After a month and a half of fumbling around in the dark, the thing was finally getting done.

He picked up the phone again, opened the banking app, and looked at the balance on his personal account.

One hundred five million, eight hundred thousand won.

Of the ninety-odd billion sitting across his accounts, five billion was riding into Samjin, four billion had gone to the new company. What he had left, in cash he could actually move, was ₩105.8 million.

Song Ji-yeon closed his eyes. Let his awareness sink into his mind, and opened the Goddess Handbook.

The interface was the one he knew — still the same thin booklet, only four pages.

He turned to Sulli’s page. In the upper-left, her card. On the right, her Goddess Bond and Goddess Ability. Down below sat two skill cards, both labeled Rebate.

Goddess Handbook — Sulli’s Page

GoddessSulli (f(x))

BondMama V’s Love (with Victoria) — pending activation

Goddess AbilityTenfold Rebate Card ×2 (one-time consumables)

Card effectSingle purchase triggers 10× spend as cash rebate

Per-card spend cap₩100,000,000

Per-card max rebate₩1,000,000,000

Both cards obtained through contact with Sulli: one when he caught her on the set, one when she kissed him in the car.

Song Ji-yeon stared at the two cards. The corner of his mouth curled up of its own accord.

Both cards had come from Sulli.

One — the day on the drama set, when she’d started to topple and he’d caught her in his arms. The other — the afternoon the little peach had kissed him, hard, in the back seat of the car, and then bolted, leaving him alone in the vehicle, half-laughing, half-cursing.

He could still feel the bite of it. She’d leaned in and just — bitten him, then vanished, leaving him there not knowing whether to be charmed or offended.

Criminally underused cards. I just never had enough liquid cash to feed them properly. So they sat.

Now, finally, he had money.

Only a hundred million won — but it was exactly enough to push both cards to their ceiling.

“Two cards, ₩100 million spend each, almost ₩2 billion back.” He did the math out loud, finger tapping twice on the desktop. “Drop ₩200 million, walk back with ₩2 billion — and two luxury items thrown in for free.”

By any arithmetic, that was a trade.

The problem was — how did a man spend a hundred million won in a single swipe?

Hit a Gangnam club and pop a hundred bottles of Black Champagne?

Tacky. And the very definition of waste.

He wasn’t the club-flexing type. A bottle of Ace was ten-odd million won; popping a hundred of them served no purpose on this earth except posing.

Buy a car?

A ₩100 million car sat in an awkward band — too much for the merely decent, not enough for the genuinely good ones. Anything he’d actually want ran two or three hundred million. Anything cheaper, he wouldn’t be caught in.

The Mercedes in the garage wasn’t a top-tier car, but it drove well enough, and he didn’t need to swap it any time soon.

His finger kept tapping the desktop, mind racing.

If he was being honest — past cars and houses, the only things he could think of that swallowed ₩100 million in a single swipe were watches and high jewelry.

“Decided. A serious watch it is.”

The old line about poor men finishing cars and rich men playing with watches — every watch-guy on earth had heard it. Whatever. He’d earned the right to be a cliché.

One hundred million wouldn’t buy the top of any house’s line, but it was more than enough for an entry-luxury piece from a real name.

And it wasn’t an impulse. He was, after all, a sajangnim now — and showing up to meetings with no watch on the wrist looked like he couldn’t afford one. A watch was a self-respect purchase.

Song Ji-yeon stood, pulled the suit jacket off the back of the chair, shrugged it on, dropped phone and wallet into his pockets, and walked out.

Down the corridor, past the pantry, he nearly bumped into Kim Min-hyun coming out with a coffee.

“Sajangnim — heading out?” Kim Min-hyun clocked the jacket and asked it lightly.

“Ne. Personal errand. Anything important this afternoon, call me.”

“Ne, Sajangnim. Safe travels.”

Song Ji-yeon pushed through the front doors and into the afternoon sun.

He crossed to the parking lot, pulled open the Mercedes’ door, dropped into the driver’s seat, and turned the engine over.

He slotted it into gear, feathered the gas, and the car slipped out of the lot and merged into Seoul’s afternoon traffic.

The streets outside the windows were full and warm — pedestrians and cars tangled together, the sun slanting through the windshield and throwing scattered coins of light across the wheel.

He drove south along the avenue by the Han.

His destination was Cheongdam-dong.

Cheongdam-dong wasn’t just the entertainment industry’s backyard; it was Seoul’s famous old-money neighborhood — luxury flagships, fine-dining rooms, private salons stacked one after the other. The single best neighborhood on the peninsula for setting money on fire with style.

Twenty minutes later, the car slipped into the Cheongdam shopping district.

The storefronts upgraded before his eyes — display windows full of finely made things: fashion, jewelry, watches, leather goods, an embarrassment of inventory.

Song Ji-yeon pulled into the underground garage of a large department store, killed the engine, pocketed the keys, and stepped out.

Inside, the afternoon mall was quiet — clusters of well-dressed customers drifting the wide aisles at their own pace.

He ignored the women’s wear and cosmetics floors entirely, took the elevator straight to the level dedicated to fine watches and jewelry.

The doors opened onto a row of softly lit, expensively restrained boutiques — Rolex, Omega, Vacheron Constantin, Patek Philippe — names he knew, glowing quietly on glass fronts.

His target was set. He walked straight to the Vacheron Constantin boutique.

Patek Philippe’s off the table — buyer qualifications, six-month wait at minimum for any piece worth having, and the entry model doesn’t even hit the rebate cap. Skip.

Vacheron Constantin fit the target better.

The boutique interior was all dark wood and cream light; the glass vitrines threw soft halos back at him.

In the window, several watches lay on black velvet trays like exhibits in a museum, each one a small piece of engineering art.

He pushed the door open. A small bell chimed.

A well-dressed saleswoman glanced up, ran a quick eye over him — the cut of the suit, the way he carried it, the unhurried confidence of someone who didn’t need to ask the price.

Her professional smile brightened by two notches, and she stepped forward.

“Welcome to Vacheron Constantin, sir. How may I help you?”

“I’d like to see the Overseas collection. Budget around one hundred million won.”

He’d picked Vacheron for a reason. Back in his previous life in LA — somewhere in the long late-night scroll through watch forums and YouTube reviews — he’d fallen for the Overseas line. He’d never had the money for it then. Now he did, and a small, old want was finally getting settled.

The saleswoman’s heart jumped a notch.

One hundred million won budget. Real client.

Her smile went from professional to genuinely warm, and she made a small bow, gestured please.

“Of course, sir. Please wait one moment — I’ll bring a few selections that suit your range.”

She went to the vitrine, lifted out two watch boxes with the care of a woman handling a newborn, set them on a black velvet tray, and carried them back.

“Sir, this first piece is the Overseas ultra-thin skeletonized.” She opened the first box, voice settling into her professional pitch. “Steel case, blue skeletonized dial — you can see the movement running directly through the dial. Very visual. Ninety-eight million won.”

Song Ji-yeon’s eyes dropped to the watch.

Through the blue dial, the precision gears and balance wheel showed and hid, showed and hid; each part caught the light and threw back its own small glint of metal. Pure industrial aesthetics.

He reached out, picked it up, hefted it. Light, slim, very nice in the hand — the skeletonized look had style. But something was missing.

“And the other?” He set the first one down.

“The other is the Overseas perpetual calendar.” She opened the second box, a thread of pride entering her voice. “It carries one of the most complicated movements in our catalog — date, day, month, moon phase, and leap-year indication. A true complication watch. Steel case, deep blue dial, forty-one-millimeter case. One hundred five million won.”

He took it, turned it in his fingers, studied it.

Where the skeletonized piece had been light, this one was heavy — settled, sober, weighty in the hand.

On the deep-blue dial, several small subdials laid out the date, the day, the month, the moon phase — each in its own corner, each running its own job; the hands caught the light and threw elegant little glints back.

He slipped it onto his wrist, clicked the clasp shut, turned it under the ceiling light.

In the moon-phase window, a small golden full moon sat embedded in a deep-blue sky. As fine as any small painting.

The saleswoman watched him looking at it and knew the sale was already made. She leaned in, voice warm.

“Sir, this perpetual calendar is one of the most collectible pieces in the Overseas line. The perpetual calendar is one of the most difficult complications in watchmaking — it automatically tracks long and short months, and even leap years adjust on their own. You only need to manually correct it once, on March 1st each year. And the moon-phase display is exceptionally accurate — it accrues only a single day’s error every one hundred twenty-two years.”

He nodded. The more he looked, the more he liked it.

“This one.” No hesitation. He unstrapped the watch and handed it back across the felt. “Card.”

“Of course, sir!”

The smile on her face had nearly overflowed the container of her professionalism. She took the watch in both hands, moved briskly to the register, wrote the slip, swiped the card — one clean pass.

“Sir, your watch, the international warranty card, and all accessories.” She returned with a heavy Vacheron Constantin shopping bag, presented it in both hands. “Thank you for your purchase. We look forward to your next visit.”

Song Ji-yeon took the bag, turned, and walked out of the boutique.

The instant he cleared the door, the chime went off in his head.

Goddess Handbook — Tenfold Rebate Card #1 Triggered

Card consumed. One Tenfold Rebate Card remains on Sulli’s page.

Seoul Bank SMS · Account ending in 8888 — ₩1,000,000,000 received at 13:45. Available balance: ₩1,000,800,000.

Song Ji-yeon’s stride broke for half a step.

He stood in the middle of the department-store concourse, pulled out his phone, opened the banking app, and refreshed.

Right on cue — the new deposit sat at the top of the ledger.

“One billion eighty thousand…” He said it to himself, very quietly, and the corner of his mouth bent upward of its own will.

Spend one hundred million. Get back one billion-plus, that instant.

This is obscene. I am never buying anything the normal way again.

He pocketed the phone, in an excellent mood, took one step forward — and stopped.

He looked down at the Vacheron Constantin bag in his hand. A thought crossed his mind.

Both these rebate cards came from Sulli.

The little peach had kissed him — that was one card. She’d hugged him — that was the other.

And here he was, using her cards to buy himself a watch. There was something not quite square about that, if he thought about it.

“Fine, fine. Cards came from you, watch goes to you. That’s only fair.”

He muttered it to himself, turned on his heel, and walked back into the mall.

Just like the Stock Forecast Card had come from Eun-jung, and he’d brought Eun-jung along for the trade. A little late, but he’d brought her.

By the same logic — the rebate cards had come from Sulli. So Sulli should get a watch.

And he wasn’t going to forget Eun-jung either — next time a rebate card dropped off Sulli, that one was earmarked for her.

Decision made, he walked straight to the Cartier boutique next door.

Cartier’s stock-in-trade was high jewelry and women’s watches, and the only house that came to mind when he thought “women’s watch.” Forgive him his narrow horizons — beyond ONGI hotpot, fashion wasn’t his department.

He pushed open the door.

A saleswoman in a black skirt-suit came forward at once, smile professionally warm.

“Welcome to Cartier, sir. How may I help?”

“Sir, are you thirsty? Let me get you a bottle of water first.”

She’d already darted behind the counter and was pressing a chilled bottle of water into his hand. Song Ji-yeon waved it off.

Wait — Cartier staff are this attentive now?

She didn’t push it. She smiled.

“Sir, what are you looking for? I can recommend…”

What made her so warm, of course, was the Vacheron Constantin bag he was holding.

If the man could drop a hundred million at Vacheron, he could certainly add a Cartier on top. In her internal ledger, this sale was already closed. Hence the warmth.

“I’d like to see women’s watches. A gift.” He cut to it. “Budget around one hundred million.”

Her eyes lit up — just so — and she led him at once to the most prominent vitrine in the store.

“Sir, for a gift, our Ballon Bleu and Panthère lines are both classics — women adore them.”

She set out four or five pieces on the felt, one after another, in front of him.

Song Ji-yeon’s eyes moved across the lineup.

He had no real study in women’s watches — it was instinct from here.

After back-and-forth, his eye settled on a Ballon Bleu piece — the diamond-set model.

All silver, white mother-of-pearl dial set with a scatter of small brilliant-cut diamonds. Thirty-three-millimeter case. Small, fine, restrained.

The first time he looked at it, he pictured it on the little peach’s slim pale wrist.

“How much for this one?”

“Excellent eye, sir. This is the Ballon Bleu thirty-three-millimeter diamond-set model. The bezel is set with forty-six round brilliant-cut diamonds, total weight approximately 0.62 carats. Ninety-three million won…”

She lifted the watch and held it out to him, voice shifting into the practiced stream of the boutique pitch.

Song Ji-yeon took it, turned it in his fingers.

Small, fine, restrained with a thread of luxury underneath — and yes, that matched the little peach exactly: bright, lively, with a small, slightly princessy undercurrent.

Sulli’s everyday wardrobe tilted sweet and girlish. This watch would fit right in.

Plus — ninety-three million. Exactly the right figure to trigger the second rebate card. Short of the full ₩100 million cap, but close enough.

“Wrap it.” He held out the bank card without ceremony.

“Of course, sir!”

The saleswoman’s face bloomed. She moved briskly — slip, card, packaging — and a Cartier shopping bag was in his hands in no time at all.

The instant he cleared the door, the chime went off again.

Goddess Handbook — Tenfold Rebate Card #2 Triggered

Rebate (10×)₩930,000,000

StatusAuto-deposited to bank account ending in 8888

Sulli’s rebate cards — both consumed. The page on Sulli resets.

Song Ji-yeon pulled out his phone and looked at the balance one more time.

Seoul Bank SMS · Account ending in 8888 — Available balance: ₩1,930,800,000.

Spend two hundred million. Walk back with close to two billion.

In that moment, Song Ji-yeon finally understood — really understood — what people meant when they said the joy of spending money. This was money rolling on top of money rolling on top of money.
