Chapter Fifty-Eight

The Little Dragon’s Scheme

MONDAY AFTERNOON Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Cheongdam Daewoo Royal County — Unit 201

Ham Eun-jung set down the last suitcase, looked at it with the thousand-yard stare of the terminally exhausted, and collapsed onto the sofa.

“Ji-yeon — didn’t you say you didn’t have much stuff? Because it looks to me like you have more than I do. I am never. Helping you move. Again.”

“How was I supposed to know it would come to this much? It never looks like this much!”

The day had begun a little after nine, when Eun-jung had physically hauled the maknae out of bed and marched her to the sales office to sign. Lease in hand, elevator card in hand, the two of them had gone straight back to the dorm in Nonhyeon-dong and begun loading Ji-yeon’s entire life into boxes.

That had been ten in the morning. It was now two in the afternoon.

Even Park Ji-yeon hadn’t seen it coming. Possessions that looked modest in a dorm room, it turns out, multiply the instant you try to move them.

She dropped the last of her armfuls and melted into the sofa beside Eun-jung — and, because her legs had nowhere else in the apartment to be, swung them up and across her unnie’s lap, long and shameless and gently swinging.

Eun-jung looked down at the legs. Then up at their owner.

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon! I’m tired too — get your feet off me!”

Ji-yeon blinked, entirely unbothered by the wrath of her unnie.

“Oh — by the way. Unnie, you free tonight? I moved into a new place, right? A housewarming is basically the law. If you’re free, dinner. Here. Tonight.”

In her head, the plan was already drawn in permanent marker.

Invite Song Ji-yeon-oppa over under the housewarming banner. Buy soju. Get oppa drunk. Get Eun-jung-unnie drunk. And whatever two drunk people get up to after that— a beat, and the conscience put in one angelic day’s work —is none of my business.

If once wasn’t enough, then twice. Three times. She refused — point-blank refused — to believe that under repeated soju bombardment, nothing between those two would ever go off.

Hahahaha! I, the little dragon, am officially the smartest creature alive!

Eun-jung-unnie — your happiness rests entirely on me.

Eun-jung heard the invitation and frowned slightly. There was something off about the maknae’s expression — a suspicious little glint, like a cat that had swallowed something it had no plans to discuss.

“Tonight… all right, fine. But let’s call Bo-ram and the others too. They should see the place — know where it is.”

Ji-yeon turned her head and stared at her unnie with the naked despair of a coach watching her own team kick the ball into their own net.

No brain. None. Two people, one brand-new apartment, one whole evening — a table for two — and she wants to invite the entire roster. One lightbulb in this house is already plenty, unnie. Me.

Out loud, however:

“Great idea! Dinner at my new place tonight, then — I’ll text Hyo-min-unnie right now.”

Let them come look, anyway. The girls seeing the place with their own eyes would also stamp out the ten theories currently breeding in the group chat.

Eun-jung caught her arm before the phone even cleared the table.

“The house isn’t even unpacked and you’re summoning guests.” She looked out over the cardboard horizon, torn cleanly between laughing and despairing. “Are you hosting a housewarming, or recruiting porters?”

“Aigoo, unnie, don’t exaggerate~ More hands, more done! The two of us would be boxing till sunrise.”

What her unnie thought about any of this was, frankly, immaterial. She was the maknae; the labor of unnies was a natural resource, like rain.

She plucked her phone off the table and fired a message at Park Hyo-min at full typing speed: Unnie~ whatcha doing?

THE SAME AFTERNOON Gangnam, Seoul A fitness club — treadmills by the window

Park Hyo-min was eleven minutes into a run when the phone on the console lit up.

She read the sender’s name. Her stomach performed a small, involuntary drop — the kind usually reserved for elevator failures.

She knew this creature. She had known this creature for years. When Park Ji-yeon opened a conversation with unnie — sweetly, unprompted — nothing good had ever once followed. Not once. There were studies.

Hyo-min slowed the belt and read it twice, eyebrows climbing.

“New place. Dinner party.”

A thin film of sweat on her forehead; a lifetime of pattern recognition in her chest. Nine times out of ten, when the little dragon reached out first, the reaching-out was the trap.

She toweled her neck, found Lee Qri in her contacts, and called.

Outgoing call · Park Hyo-min → Lee Qri

“Hello?”

“Unnie. Whatcha doing?”

“Just finished my nails. Heading home.”

Languid, unhurried — the voice of a woman whose entire remaining ambition for the day was a sofa.

“Ji-yeon says she’s moved. New place. She wants us all over for dinner tonight — you in?”

“She moved? Where to?”

“No address. Just ‘come and eat.’” A beat, and the playfulness came off the bench. “My professional opinion? We’re being recruited as moving labor.”

A few seconds of silence on the line. Then a light, low laugh.

“Then we’ll simply have to go and see. I’m curious what state the place is already in — that girl, living alone in her very own apartment.”

“Could any state out-wreck Bo-ram-unnie’s room? — Fine. Meet at the dorm in a bit, we’ll head over together.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Back in Unit 201, Ham Eun-jung surveyed the cardboard mountain range and felt her headache quietly file the paperwork for a renewal.

Park Ji-yeon, entirely untroubled by the geography of her own home, was sunk into the soft sofa with a pumpkin-shaped cushion in her arms, firing messages into the group chat with one thumb, legs still swinging.

“Unnie! I told Hyo-min-unnie and the others — they’ll be here soon!”

Eun-jung sighed the sigh of the resigned, picked up a pair of scissors, and started on the nearest box.

“You could at least move the boxes off the walkway. A person can’t get through.”

“Aigoo, no rush~ When the reinforcements get here, many hands, light work.”

Delivered with the serene confidence of a woman reading aloud from scripture.

The doorbell rang.

Ji-yeon came off the sofa like a launched missile and hit the front door barefoot.

On the other side: Park Hyo-min and Lee Qri, each loaded down with enough gift bags to be relocating themselves.

“Ji-yeon-ah, congrats on the new—”

The sentence died in Hyo-min’s throat. Her smile fossilized mid-curve.

Behind her, Lee Qri leaned in, took one look past the doorway, and pressed a hand to her forehead.

“Park Ji-yeon. You are calling this ‘come over for dinner’?”

Hyo-min set her gifts down on the entryway cabinet, rolled up her sleeves, and let a dangerous light come into those famous fox eyes.

“And here I thought we were guests. This is a land-reclamation project.”

“Aigoo, unnies! You’re here!”

Not one gram guilty, Ji-yeon launched herself at them, one arm each, and went full aegyo.

“I can’t do it all by myseeeelf~ You’re the best unnies in the entire country — you’ll help me, right? Right?”

“Oh, save it—”

Hyo-min pinched her cheek hard enough to leave a memory — but her feet were already carrying her into the living room.

Jeon Bo-ram and Park So-yeon arrived before long, took in the same vista, and produced the same reaction: a matched set of frozen smiles.

And so, under the maknae’s soft-glinting, shameless, full-spectrum aegyo offensive, the grand “housewarming banquet” was formally reconvened as a moving-in cleanup mobilization.

Five women. Two hours and change. By the end of it, the big items in the living room and the bedrooms had been herded into something resembling order.

“Dead. I’m dead. I’m so hungry my stomach’s eating my spine.” Ji-yeon achieved the sofa first, spread-eagled, and addressed the ceiling. “Let’s order delivery! Fried chicken and beer! It’s the law!”

“You have the nerve to be hungry?” A cushion, thrown by Jeon Bo-ram, bounced off her face. “We’re the ones who did the work!”

“Oh — right!”

Ji-yeon sat up like a spring-loaded toy, eyes suddenly lit like a shop sign.

“Let’s call Song Ji-yeon-oppa over too!”

Lee Qri was wiping down the coffee table. Her hand paused mid-stroke.

“Why him?”

“To celebrate my new place! And oppa found me the apartment, negotiated the lease down himself — I owe him a proper thank-you. Besides—” she flung a hand at the floor, “—he’s right downstairs. Two minutes by elevator.”

“Downstairs?”

Park So-yeon’s gaze traveled, unhurried, to Eun-jung.

Eun-jung was shelving books — or performing shelving. She felt the weight of four pairs of eyes arrive on her at once, and her cheeks went faintly, traitorously warm.

“Mm… Song Ji-yeon-ssi lives on the first floor.”

“Ohhhh—”

Four voices. Perfect unison. Loaded with precisely the same tonnage of meaning. Eight eyes began a slow, silent shuttle between Eun-jung’s ears.

Eun-jung bent lower over the bookshelf and alphabetized with the focus of a bomb technician.

“I’m for it.” Park So-yeon, at last, evenly. She had her own reasons — she wanted a longer look at the man who kept saying he was going to buy them. “Besides, there are things we’ve wanted to ask him. Face to face.”

Lee Qri raised no objection.

Park Ji-yeon had her phone out before the sentence finished landing, typing at maximum velocity, delight coming off her like heat haze. She and Song Ji-yeon had traded KakaoTalk IDs only yesterday — which made this, officially, her first message to him ever.
