Chapter Thirty-Six

I’ve Eaten It — Yes, I’m Bragging!!

STILL EVENING Tteurak Hanwoo House, Private Room 3 the second course of the conversation

“You mean buying your contracts.” He looked up at So-yeon without breaking the tongs’ rhythm, the meat on the grill turning, turning. “Of course I’m serious. Legal is already drafting the papers.”

Set the Goddess Handbook aside entirely: even a man carrying nothing but a second chance would still have come for them — nothing to do with love, nothing to do with hate. He simply refused to stand by a second time and watch a group he’d stanned end its story in regrets. It really was that simple.

“Why?” The water glass came down with a small, deliberate click. Lee Qri’s goddess-tier eyes fixed on him, hunting for the flaw in whatever came out of his mouth next. “The way things stand right now, we’re rats crossing the street in this country.”

“Buying us won’t make you money. It’ll just hang a mountain of hate around your neck.”

She paused, the meaning naked: this was a thankless errand on its face — so why was this man sprinting toward it? Her eyes made one quiet, unhurried lap between Ji-yeon and Eun-jung and back.

Ji-yeon laughed, and set the tongs down on the rest.

“Qri-ssi. I’m young, but I am a businessman, through and through. What I’m buying is your brand value.”

“Brand value?” Park Hyo-min actually laughed this time — the whole industry knew T-ara’s brand was radioactive at the moment. “We still have one of those?”

“Not in Korea.” He leaned back against the chair, calm as a man discussing tomorrow’s weather. “That doesn’t mean it doesn’t exist elsewhere. Every agency on this Peninsula is staring at the Chinese market right now — and they’re right to. It’s going to grow bigger than any of them can picture. Over there, nobody is lining up around the block for gossip about a bullying rumor; they look at the work, and they look at the faces. Sign with me, and I can put you on China’s biggest stage inside two years. The number-one girl group on the mainland — that title is sitting empty, and I intend for the six of you to take it.”

That was no idle boast. He could see the whole road from where he stood — he had watched, in another life, exactly how enormous that market became, and the girl-group scene over there was wide-open ground. There was nothing in it a determined adult with a decade of foresight couldn’t outrun.

The private room settled into thought. So-yeon’s brow folded.

“But that takes connections. Brand-new songs, mainland stages, a promotional apparatus behind it all. I won’t pretend your restaurants aren’t successful — but do you actually have those resources? Can you really hand them to us?”

A fair question, and it landed square on a patch he hadn’t rehearsed. His original sketch had been exactly as simple as it sounded: found an entertainment arm, book showcase concerts across China, let T-ara carve their name in that market first. Held under So-yeon’s X-ray stare, though, the sketch showed its ribs. He had no showbiz network. He wasn’t even fully fluent in the process of reaching a mainland promoter. A new album’s title track, a wall of promotional material — items he had never once touched in either life.

But unfamiliar was not the same as cowed. He was, when you got all the way down to it, a max-level player who had already read this server’s entire patch history in advance. Whatever needed doing, would get done.

“You don’t need to worry about any of that. My legal team is already in contact with Kim Kwang-soo at CCM. I’m buying your contracts and T-ara’s entire sound catalog and MV library along with them. Once your footing in China is set, I’ll put producers on a brand-new album. And as for promotion—” a small beat, “—when the time comes, I’ll have my methods.”

Sincere as it sounded, So-yeon still hesitated. It was a beautiful speech, and every line of it was a blank check. There was nothing on that table yet that she could reach out and touch.

He read the hesitation off her face and raised.

“Then let’s talk terms. A three-year term. Group activities — fifty-fifty, and that split covers your overseas income too. Personal activities, eighty-twenty, with the eighty staying in your pockets. The second contract reopens every number for renegotiation. It isn’t that I don’t want to give more — but you know what it costs to crack open a new market, and I don’t run charity cases. And if, within those three years, I fail to deliver everything I promised you at this table tonight — you walk. Unconditional termination, no penalty, the contract dies on the spot.”

The Offer — T-ara, Six Members

Term

Three years · second contract fully renegotiable

Group Activities

50 / 50 · including overseas income

Personal Activities

80 / 20, artist’s share 80 · company’s share 20

Walk Clause

Promises unmet by year three → unconditional release

Drafted by SAVORY F&B legal; paper to follow the handshake.

As offers went, this one bit down hard. Their current deal with CCM split group activities fifty-fifty as well — but overseas income paid them only forty, the company taking sixty, and personal activities surrendered a full half upstairs. On paper, in one sentence, Song Ji-yeon had doubled their overseas rate and nearly doubled their personal one.

So-yeon was tilting, and she knew it. She had come tonight to take inventory of exactly what this man could hand them. If the sound catalog and the MVs truly changed hands, then CCM had nothing left to offer but the name on the door — after the bullying scandal, T-ara’s schedule book was blank paper. Every domestic booking canceled; a scatter of overseas ones the only survivors. Why, then, keep six girls parked at a company that provided nothing and bestirred itself to do even less?

Qri caught the flutter in her leader’s eyes and executed a discreet calf-kick under the table, glare attached. Weren’t we a united front against this man? Three sentences and you’ve already crossed the lines — what about me?

So-yeon sent back an apologetic look, sheepish around the edges. The truth was she had nothing against Song Ji-yeon at all. Whether he’d approached them with an agenda — she couldn’t have cared less. All she cared about was whether the man could pull them out of the mud. And twice now she had watched him operate: composed, reliable, quietly warm. A man it was dangerously easy to lean toward.

Qri didn’t carry any great grudge against him either, honestly. It was his motive that kept snagging her, and the timing. He had known Eun-jung a matter of weeks. By every public scrap of evidence they were not a couple — and yet here he was, moving to buy the entire group. As far as Qri could see, the man had no reason on this earth to be doing this.

What Qri didn’t know was this: the six of them had known Song Ji-yeon for a few short weeks. Song Ji-yeon had known the six of them — every stage, every comeback, every twist of road still waiting ahead — for years.

Silence held the table, so he took the floor back.

“Well? Where do we stand — anyone.” One meaningful glance slid sideways at Eun-jung. You told me you’d be the first to say yes. Curtain’s up.

Eun-jung shot him a look that said you are unbelievable, and then stood up and said it anyway.

“I trust him. There’s nothing left for us at CCM. If there’s a road in China, I say we go walk it and see where it leads.”

“I’ve been done with that company since forever!” Park Ji-yeon’s hand shot up beside her. “Wherever Eun-jung-unnie goes, I go.”

Jeon Bo-ram and Park Hyo-min, still grazing peacefully through the meat with the serenity of two girls who had never once been consulted about anything, mumbled their agreement in unison: “Either way is fine with us — just so long as… the six of us stay together.”

The pressure wheeled around onto the last two foxholes — five pairs of bright, expectant eyes fixed on So-yeon and Qri.

So-yeon held out a moment longer, then smiled. “Keep every promise you made at this table tonight, and you’ll have my signature on the transfer consent.”

And then there was one.

Wait — hold on. How is it just me? Were we not a team? A five-minute alliance, and everyone’s defected?

What is this — am I being isolated right now?!!

Something helpless crossed the fox eyes. Six years side by side — she didn’t want to be the wedge in that either. She nodded, once.

“No objection here!”

…delivered with one long, venomous, extremely memorable glare at Song Ji-yeon, for the record.

Ji-yeon, happily unaware that a certain scheming woman had just opened a file with his name on it, was busy being quietly delighted. One enormous piece of business, done. Only Kim Kwang-soo left to settle, and the T-ara question was dust and closed.

He smiled, picked the tongs back up, and got back to work. A long stretch of grilling later, everyone was finally full, and the room turned bright and chattery — and somewhere in the shuffle, Eun-jung simply reached over and took the tongs out of his hand. A gentle trade. Eat something. I’ve got this.

She’d been quietly mortified for a while, truth be told. They had invited him to this dinner — and he had spent the entire evening working their grill. She’d been watching. He’d barely taken three bites himself.

Ji-yeon gave her a look of such open gratitude that she seemed, in his eyes at that moment, to be outright glowing.

Re-stanned. On the spot. THIS is what a proper idol is supposed to look like. Set your standards accordingly, everyone.

Let the record ask the world a question: how many of you have ever eaten Korean barbecue grilled personally by your own bias?

That’s right. None of you.

I have. And yes — I’m bragging!!
