Chapter One Hundred Sixteen

Han So-hee Gets Sentimental

WEDNESDAY · LATE AFTERNOON Cheongdam-dong — Song Ji-yeon’s Mercedes One new page in the Handbook, one old neighbor watching from above

Song Ji-yeon sat in the Mercedes. He did not start the engine immediately. Instead, he opened the Goddess Handbook.

Goddess Handbook — Han So-hee’s Page

ContactHan So-hee — binding confirmed

Activation RewardRadiant Charm Card

Goddess AbilityOne Radiant Charm Card per week — automatically collected

Card EffectTemporarily heightens appearance and clears negative effects; duration varies with fatigue, sleep and diet

Base DurationSix months

Goddess BondDouble Wicked Beauties — Han So-hee + Park Ji-yeon (1/2)

The Handbook still refused to explain why beauty cards came with a bond diagram. It never explained anything that mattered.

He rubbed the side of his nose.

“A Radiant Charm Card? What am I supposed to do with that? I’m a grown man. Why would I need to raise my own looks?”

He considered it for half a second.

“Although… I could give it to Eun-jung or So-hee.”

Who would complain about having a prettier girlfriend?

Of all the Goddess Abilities he had received, Sulli’s Tenfold Rebate Card and Eun-jung’s Stock Forecast Card remained the most useful. The little dragon’s Good Luck Card had done absolutely nothing when he tried it, so he had stopped expecting much from that page.

Kim Ji-won’s Healing Card was still out of reach. He would reserve judgment until he got one.

That should not take long now. He knew where she lived, and he had a perfectly legitimate reason to appear there. He refused to believe the Handbook would keep the Healing Card from him forever.

He closed the Handbook, started the Mercedes, and drove out of the complex.

He did not know that, on the sixteenth-floor balcony of the singles’ tower, somebody was watching his car merge into traffic.

Kim Ji-won held a cup of barley tea against the railing and watched until he disappeared.

“He really did put her in the unit downstairs…”

✦ ✦ ✦

Song Ji-yeon drove around the neighborhood twice.

After an entire afternoon of errands and the trouble they had gotten into afterward, he was hungry enough to feel hollow. Han So-hee was worse — sore all over and close to collapsing.

She had only just lost her first time. Heavy, greasy food would probably turn her stomach.

He stopped at an old soup restaurant and ordered a soft, well-cooked samgyetang and a mild bowl of beef seolleongtang. Then he detoured to a porridge shop for seafood porridge, two bowls of rice, and several light side dishes.

By the time he returned to the apartment in Gaepo-dong with the heavy bags in both hands, the sky had begun to darken.

He took the elevator to the twelfth floor and opened the keypad lock. The apartment was black except for neon light falling across the wooden floor from outside.

He switched on the lights.

“So-hee?”

No answer.

He carried the food to the bedroom door. The quilt had risen into a small mound. Han So-hee was curled beneath it like a cat in winter, showing only a spill of dark hair and half a sleeping face.

He left the food on the table and returned to the bed. His fingers brushed over her hair.

“Wake up. Come eat something.”

The person beneath the quilt moved. When she opened her eyes and saw his face close to hers, the afternoon returned all at once. Color rushed into her cheeks. She pulled the blanket higher, hiding half her face and leaving only a pair of accusing eyes visible.

Her entire body felt as if it might fall apart.

Pain, soreness, pressure — every part of her complained.

“Why are you glaring at me?”

He laughed softly and touched the tip of her nose.

“I bought samgyetang. Eat a little. If you sleep like this, you’ll wake up starving.”

He was right. So-hee nodded, but her voice came out hoarse.

“I know.”

Hearing herself, she glared at him again.

Song Ji-yeon rubbed the back of his head, suitably embarrassed.

So-hee pressed her lips together and tried to sit up. She had barely raised her shoulders when a stabbing ache spread from her waist down both legs. Her strength vanished.

She sucked in a breath and fell back onto the bed, eyes shining.

“It’s your fault. It hurts so much I can’t move.”

She really could not move, so he lifted the quilt, slid one arm beneath her knees, and wrapped the other around her back. Then he picked her up.

Being carried like that brought the bathroom back to her mind.

“Yah! What are you doing?”

Her arms closed around his neck on instinct. She was afraid he might decide to touch her again.

“Taking you to dinner. Do you want to drink soup lying down?”

He carried her into the living room, pulled a cushion from the sofa, and placed it on a dining chair. Only then did he lower her onto it.

“Sit here. It’s softer.”

So-hee looked down at the cushion beneath her. Her cold expression cracked, and the corners of her mouth lifted despite herself.

“At least you have a little conscience.”

“I have plenty of conscience.”

He sat across from her and peeled the plastic film from the containers.

So-hee had planned to say she had no appetite. Then the smell of food reached her, and her stomach betrayed her with a loud growl.

He pretended not to hear. He ladled chicken soup into a bowl, pulled meat from the leg, and pushed it toward her.

“Try this. The place is famous. It’ll put some strength back in you.”

So-hee took a small sip.

The warm, rich broth slid into the empty space in her stomach. Heat spread through her, easing even the soreness in her mouth.

She nodded and began eating faster. After several mouthfuls of soup, color returned to her face.

Only then did Song Ji-yeon serve himself the seolleongtang. The apartment filled with the soft clink of bowls and chopsticks as he ate with the side dishes.

Han So-hee propped her elbow on the table and watched him.

Something about the scene felt warm.

She had never experienced this kind of warmth before.

As a child, she had been alone except for her grandmother. After coming to Seoul, she had spent her days running from one job to another and her nights in a basement with no sunlight.

MEMORY · ULSAN TO SEOUL

Her parents divorced early, started new families, and effectively stopped caring whether she lived or died. Her grandmother grew old and ill while collecting discarded vegetables from the market and selling small side dishes from a street stall to keep So-hee in school.

“A television, a basement, a market stall, one job after another — and nobody was waiting for me except Grandma.”

She had learned to measure a day by wages, a room by its dampness, and kindness by how quickly it vanished.

The people and events she had encountered in Seoul had been dark, one after another. She had never felt warmth from anyone else.

But today, Song Ji-yeon had given her some.

He was halfway through his meal when he bit into a piece of pickled radish and looked across the table.

Han So-hee was crying.

Not loudly. Not even with a sound. Tears simply kept sliding down her cheeks.

He panicked, pulled a tissue from the box beside him, and held it out.

“What happened? Why are you crying?”

His alarm made her mouth twist. Then she smiled through the tears.

“It’s nothing. Thank you, oppa.”

He blinked.

“Why are you thanking me all of a sudden?”

So-hee lowered her eyes to the bowl.

“Oppa, let me tell you about my story. I was born in Ulsan. My parents divorced when I was very young, married other people, and mostly stopped caring about me. I grew up with my grandmother.”

At the word grandmother, the coldness in her eyes softened.

“She was old and sick. Every day she went to the market to collect vegetable leaves, then sold little side dishes from a stall to pay for my schooling. I graduated high school this year. I became an adult, and I didn’t want to keep burdening her, so I stopped studying and came out to earn money.”

She poked at the meat in her bowl, her tone calm enough to sound as if she were describing somebody else.

“When I first came to Seoul — it was about two months ago — I worked at a draft-beer bar. Every day I started at five in the afternoon and worked until two or three in the morning. Drunk men touching me was normal. I didn’t tolerate it. I nearly got into fights several times, and the owner docked my wages.”

Song Ji-yeon did not interrupt. He only watched her carefully.

“Later I got tired of the noise and went to a barbecue restaurant, carrying plates and changing the grill mesh. The wire was hot enough to blister my hands, and that became ordinary. Then I worked in a clothing store, shook drinks at a café… I’ve done every kind of temporary job that paid. Two or three in a day. I was so tired I collapsed as soon as I got home, and every won I saved after living expenses went to my grandmother.”

Her fingers tightened around the chopsticks.

“Nobody except my grandmother had ever been good to me.”

The next words took longer.

“I’ve received mostly malice for as long as I can remember. Especially because I want to become an actress. I’ve auditioned for many agencies. Some saw my education and the fact that I wasn’t trained at an arts school, then rejected me immediately. Others only saw my face and wanted to keep me.”

Her mouth tightened.

“That’s why I misunderstood you too.”

She looked up at him.

“Oppa is the second person in my life who has made me feel that he was truly kind to me.”

Song Ji-yeon looked at the girl who was not yet twenty.

In his old life, Han So-hee had become a fierce, wild star on the screen. Right now she was only a young woman at the bottom of Seoul, spending every ounce of herself to stay alive.

For a moment she was silent. Then determination gathered in her almond eyes.

“That’s why I wanted to thank you. You’re the reason I can live like this now. I don’t know how you see this relationship, but I still want to thank you. From now on, I’ve decided that I’m going to choose you…”

Song Ji-yeon smiled. He had no idea why she had suddenly taken the conversation down such a sentimental road. He preferred her sharp, defiant version.

“Ne. Oppa understands. Eat before the soup gets cold.”

The meal settled warmly between them.

So-hee drank two full bowls of soup, and the color returned to her face in a way he could see from across the table.

When she tried to clear the containers, he pressed her back into the chair.

“Forget it. You can barely walk. You’ll drop a bowl.”

He packed the empty containers into a plastic bag, tied it closed, and wiped the table.

After washing his hands, he glanced at the clock. It was almost eight.

He put on his jacket and picked up his keys.

“I should go.”

So-hee’s eyes shifted.

“Go… back to Cheongdam-dong?”

“Where else? Do you want me to sleep here?”

His smile turned teasing.

“With you in this condition, if I stay, are you planning to crawl to the bathroom tomorrow?”

Her face went red.

“Who asked you to stay? Leave already.”

He carried her back to the bedroom, lowered her gently into bed, and pulled the covers up around her.

“Don’t go to the company for the next couple of days. Rest here. The refrigerator is stocked. If you’re hungry, order delivery or call Hye-jin and have her arrange something. Lock the door at night, and be careful living alone. I’ll come by when I have time.”

Before he left, he claimed one small benefit for himself and kissed her mouth.

So-hee stared at him, heart racing, then spat a little indignantly in his direction.

He was leaving and he still had to steal a kiss?

“I’m going. Call me if you need anything.”

He straightened, waved once, and left the bedroom.

Downstairs, he sat in the Mercedes and glanced back toward the twelfth floor.

Then he smiled and drove toward Cheongdam-dong.
