Chapter Twenty-Six

The Set Visit

SEPTEMBER 2012 Yeouido · Han River Park To the Beautiful You — outdoor set

Yeouido, Han River Park. The drama’s open-set build, right on the water.

The early-autumn wind came skimming off the river, carrying a chill laced with cut grass and damp.

Ji-yeon locked the car, hauled the two enormous, seriously heavy drink boxes out of the trunk, and started toward the convenience store at the park entrance.

On the store’s front steps stood a young man a year or two past twenty, neck stretched, scanning the crowd. Phone in hand, screen lit — and the moment he spotted Ji-yeon coming along with two trademark Ater boxes hugged to his chest, he broke into a trot.

“Annyeonghaseyo — Song Ji-yeon-ssi, is it?”

Manager Kim’s very first sentence arrived wrapped in that particular industry polish — courteous, thorough, calibrated to the millimeter.

“Ne, that’s me.” Ji-yeon smiled and hoisted the two still-cold boxes a little higher.

“Daebak — Song Ji-yeon-ssi, you brought this much?” Kim’s eyes went wide at the sheer size of the crates; he lunged to take one, still talking. “Sulli-ssi only said a friend was coming by the set. I never imagined — this is so thoughtful. And it’s Ater’s signature, too. The crew drinks this stuff in their dreams.”

“I was passing through Cheongdam anyway. Figured I’d load up.”

Ji-yeon dusted his palms together and fell in beside Kim, and the two of them walked deeper into the park.

Through a dense ginkgo grove — and the view burst open all at once.

The riverside lawn had been taped off. Broadcast vans with a station logo on their flanks idled along the access road, generators thrumming underneath everything like a second heartbeat. Crew in ball caps swarmed the grass, laying dolly track, angling reflector boards; a camera crane swung slow, high arcs overhead; behind the monitor tent, men in headphones hunched over their screens. The whole production ran on a wire pulled tight.

“Sulli-ssi’s on standby right now — the next setup is hers.” Kim shouldered through the set’s side flap and held it open for him, voice pitched low as he led the way.

Ji-yeon had barely stepped through the flap when he found her.

There she was, sunk into a folding canvas chair — a crew parka draped over her shoulders like a tent, and under it, the boys’-school uniform trousers that every member of the To the Beautiful You cast wore on set. For the role, the famous long hair was gone, cropped into a neat tomboy cut, fine bangs lying flat against her forehead. And none of it mattered: under the bounce of the reflector boards, her face was porcelain-fair to the point of translucence, a doll someone had finished by hand.

She caught the stir at the entrance and tilted her head.

The instant she made him out, the little frown she’d worn while running lines smoothed flat — and those lovely eyes lit up as if someone had crushed a handful of starlight into the river behind her.

“Song Ji-yeon-oppa!” She hadn’t actually believed he would come.

“Sulli — posture. The director will call you any minute now.” The stylist beside her was faster than she was, catching the parka as it slid off one shoulder and draping it back into place with a long-suffering hand.

“Mianhae, unnie.” Tongue out — but her chin stayed up, and she waved at Ji-yeon with her whole arm, a pretty pink climbing her cheeks.

He walked over and set the second box on the low table beside her chair. His eyes settled on the crop, and narrowed, judging.

“Though — this look. You genuinely read as a pretty boy now.”

“Yah, oppa!” The cheeks puffed. The tiredness that had been hanging around her face all morning was gone, replaced end to end by live comedy. “It’s called dashing. The writer-unnies on set keep telling me my girl-dressed-as-boy act is a complete success.”

“Fine. Dashing.” He fished a grapefruit soda out of the box and pressed the cold cup to her warm cheek. “Here. Fresh from the queue.”

The chill made her neck scrunch — and then she snatched the cup and clasped it to her chest like contraband.

“Daebak — it’s my favorite flavor! Oppa, you’re actually the best.”

“Ahem. Sulli — and this is?” A middle-aged man in a baseball cap had come over from the director’s chairs, walkie-talkie in hand, giving Ji-yeon a slow once-over.

“Director-nim, this is my good friend — he came all this way just to visit the set today.” Sulli grabbed Ji-yeon by the arm and pulled him forward, a note of pride in her voice that she didn’t quite manage to hide. “Song Ji-yeon-oppa brought drinks for the whole crew. Every single person.”

The stern face folded instantly into warmth. A set’s favorite guest, in Korea, is exactly this species: moneyed and mannered. It gives the actor face — and it sweetens the whole crew’s afternoon.

“Aigoo — Song Ji-yeon-ssi, you really shouldn’t have.”

“You’re too kind. Sulli’s in your care out here — yours and the crew’s. A few drinks; everyone works hard.” Ji-yeon shook the director’s hand, and the handshake was exact. Not a drop leaked anywhere.

“Haha — that kid’s got real spark, and she doesn’t flinch from the work either.” The director patted his walkie-talkie and turned to bellow at the crew. “Everybody hold! Sulli’s friend sent over Ater’s signature drinks — ten minutes, everybody hydrate!”

“Ohhh — thanks, Sulli-ssi’s friend!”

“Sulli-ssi, daebak!”

The set, gloomy a minute ago under the weight of the shoot, broke into open cheering. Staff gravitated to Manager Kim and the crates; the looks aimed at Sulli and Ji-yeon now carried gratitude, and more than a little envy.

Sulli held her grapefruit soda in both hands, sipping at it in tiny increments, sparkling eyes fixed on Ji-yeon without blinking.

✦ ✦ ✦

The crew buses stood in a row along the access road. In the shade of the trees, a few dozen staff sat with the river wind in their faces, savoring iced peach oolong straight from Ater’s signature cups.

“Wow — this shop’s grapefruit soda is usually impossible to get. Sulli’s friend went big.”

“It’s that Ater place in Cheongdam-dong — the signature line. Fifty cups, I heard. Even the director-nim got a special pour.”

The murmurs traveled the set at low tide. The heavy-shoot atmosphere, strung tight all day, loosened considerably.

“Oppa — you stood in that line forever, didn’t you?” Sulli cradled her drink, tiny sips again, and looked up at him, those pretty eyes bent into crescent moons.

“It was fine. Ran into two people I knew — they picked the flavors for me.”

He leaned back in the folding chair and studied her, back in the full boys’ uniform now. The crop, the bangs pasted to her forehead — and under all of it, a bare face still lit white in the sunlight, porcelain to the last inch.

“People you know?” A head-tilt, curiosity immediate. “Who?”

“Trainees at your company. A Kang Seul-gi — and one called Bae Joo-hyun.” Amused, he flicked the false zipper sewn onto her costume. “Kang Seul-gi says she’s been practicing for the month-end evaluations so hard she cracked her voice.”

“Chincha?! Seul-gi and Joo-hyun-unnie!” The gasp came out in a whisper. Then her cheeks puffed with sympathy. “Seul-gi’s truly next-level — she treats every evaluation like a death match. But Joo-hyun-unnie is usually so slow to warm up to people — she actually talked with you, oppa?”

“I bought them drinks. Call it the consulting fee for the menu advice.” He smiled, about to say more — and caught the sound of light footsteps behind them.

“Sulli — have you run the lines for the next scene?”

A gentle woman’s voice, from behind.

Ji-yeon turned without thinking.

She wore a plain white cotton tee over slim jeans, plain flats on her feet. A script clipped in a blue plastic folder in one hand; long hair pulled back into a loose low ponytail; a pair of rimless reading glasses on her nose — the whole of her giving off a quiet, bookish warmth, gentle as tea going cool.
