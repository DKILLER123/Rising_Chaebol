Chapter Seventy-Nine

Peach’s Stolen Kiss

SAME NIGHT · OVER DESSERT Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Umi barbecue house — the inner room

“Hara-yah, it’s not that I won’t manage it for you…” He set his water glass down. “We met three hours ago. You’re honestly going to hand your stock account to me?”

“Ah—” A small laugh, a wave of the hand. “I trust oppa. If Sulli’s account is already with you, what’s left for me to worry about?”

The logic underneath was simple and completely load-bearing. KARA’s schedule was a treadmill; Hara had no attention to spare for candlestick charts. Rather than lose sleep every night as an amateur gambler, she’d far rather pay a professional and keep her heart rate out of it. As for the fear of being scammed — her reasoning was cleaner than a receipt: he hadn’t stolen from Sulli, so he wouldn’t steal from her. And if he did — fine, she’d eat the loss. A few hundred million won to see a person clearly, and to let Sulli see his true face if it ever came to that — she’d call that money well spent.

What she could not allow was her Sulli trusting the wrong man.

She glanced, meaningfully, at the little peach currently hanging off that man’s arm and grinning like a sunlamp, and let out a sigh she kept almost entirely internal.

The way those two acted with each other — honestly. How was anyone supposed NOT to misread them?

Sulli, unaware that her unnie’s risk models ran this deep, saw only that oppa still wasn’t committing, and escalated: shook his arm harder, by now nearly pasted to his side.

“Oppa, just manage Hara-unnie’s account! Oppaaa…”

The shaking was genuinely destabilizing, and the peach had no working concept of personal distance. He got his right hand up, blocked her by the forehead, and pushed her back to a regulation minimum, sighing.

“Fine. But I can’t promise every single trade makes money.”

A purely ceremonial disclaimer. With the Forecast Cards in hand, a losing trade would require the timeline itself to file new paperwork. But disclaimers exist to be said.

“It’s fine!” Hara brightened at once, hand raised like a sworn witness. “Markets carry risk, invest with caution — I know the whole speech. Even if it loses, I would never blame oppa.”

Dinner done, he settled the bill at the register, and the three of them stepped out into the street. Full dark now.

The night wind had a cool edge to it; Hara put the trench coat back on, sunglasses back on top of that. He brought the car around to the curb, and the girls climbed in—

—both into the back seat, this time.

“Where am I taking you.” His eyes went to the rearview mirror.

“Nonhyeon-dong. The Shindongah apartments.”

The car slid into Seoul at night. Nobody spoke. Hara, fed and warm, dozed against the seatback; Sulli sat bright-eyed, watching the streetlights file past the window.

This little peach and her permanently full battery.

Twenty minutes later, the car stopped at the gate of a quiet, expensive-looking complex.

“Hara.” He turned to call the sleeping back seat.

Nothing. In the end it took Sulli pinching her nose to bring her back up to the surface of the world.

“Bwuh— how long was I out?” She swatted the offending fingers away, blinking through the fog.

“Not long! Ten-something minutes. Unnie, you were OUT — I didn’t dare say a single word the whole way, and I have so much to tell you—”

“Mm… sorry, Sulli-yah. Unnie’s just completely out of battery.” Hara rubbed her eyes. “Tomorrow. I’ll come find you tomorrow, okay?”

She did, in fact, have a full queue of questions to put to Sulli tomorrow. Item one: the exact nature of the relationship between one Choi Sulli and one Song Ji-yeon.

“Chincha?! Then it’s a promise, unnie — I’ll be waiting!”

“Song Ji-yeon-oppa.” Hara patted Sulli’s head once, then turned forward, formal again. “Thank you for today. And sorry for the long detour home.”

“No trouble at all.” He caught Sulli’s eye in the mirror, deadpan. “Serving a great beauty is an honor. Right, Sulli?”

“Hee-hee! Yes, oppa.” — thinking: I’ll introduce you to a total beauty, this afternoon. And he’d kept it on file all day!

The compliment landed on Hara’s cheeks and left a brief pink behind. Only brief — a working idol has heard every sentence that can be built out of the word “beautiful.” She carried a certain immunity.

But she didn’t get out. From her bag came a small notepad and a pen; she wrote a string of digits, fast, tore the page free, and leaned forward to pass it between the seats.

“Oppa — my private number. My Kakao’s the same. Add me when you get home — tomorrow, once I’ve opened the account at the exchange, I’ll send you everything.” She watched the slip of paper disappear into his hand. “That’s me done for the day. Please get Sulli back to the dorm.”

He dropped the note into his jacket pocket without ceremony and looked back at her. “Will do. Go up and get some real rest.”

The two girls pushed their doors and stepped out. By the time they reached the back of the car, he was already there — trunk open, the big silver suitcase out and standing on the curb.

“Thank you for today.” Hara took the handle and gave him a small bow. “Get home safe.”

He nodded once. Sulli waved. “Unnie — see you tomorrow!”

“Mm. Tomorrow!”

The two of them stood watching until Hara’s figure disappeared down the walk inside the complex, and only then got back in.

Sulli dropped into the passenger seat and buckled up.

“Sooo — isn’t Hara-unnie a total beauty.” The belt clicked; the interrogation began. “Oppa. Do you have a thing for my unnie.”

He glanced over. She wasn’t teasing. She was watching him with the gravity of a customs inspection.

“What exactly goes on in that head all day.” He reached over and flicked her forehead. “And where’s the evidence? Point to the evidence.”

“You kept staring at Hara-unnie! I saw it at the airport — and just now, when unnie walked in, you stared after her the whole way.” Forehead covered, mouth pouting at maximum projection. “Hara-unnie has a man, oppa. I’d give up early if I were you.”

He had no rebuttal. He’d been appreciating the woman — in a perfectly normal, professional, ex-fan-of-the-industry way. Where had “staring” come from?

And secondly — what was with that face? That was jealousy. Textbook, with page references.

He’d noticed it a while ago, honestly: the little peach’s feelings might have drifted past friendly. She’d stopped honoring physical distance entirely — tonight’s arm-shaking had been a world tour of violations. And this current performance read like the possessiveness arriving right on schedule.

“Relax. I do not have a thing for your Hara-unnie.”

The little face went from storm warning to full sunshine in a single frame. “Good! I was just scared oppa would fall for somebody he can’t have, get his heart completely broken, and end up crying about it—”

He frowned. Jealousy was one thing — since when did it ship with personal attacks?

Also — if he remembered the file correctly, Hara’s first boyfriend was Yong Jun-hyung, and those two had a breakup announcement scheduled for next March. Odds were good they were already strangers sharing a phone plan. Not that he was knocking Hara — he was judging him. That man’s reputation had a hard landing coming, and anyone whose name ends up stapled to a criminal case is, historically speaking, not a good person. Probably at that bar of his right now, living his best life.

Oh — right. That guy went on to marry HyunA, of all people.

Urgh. Something about that fact genuinely irritated him. Maybe he should just intercept her first—

…No. Focus.

He saw the scowl reflected back at him from the passenger seat.

“What’s that face? Sad you never got your chance to be heartbroken?”

A snort escaped him before he could stop it. “No. Don’t overthink it — when would I even find the time? I was thinking about something. Got absorbed.”

“Hmph.” Chin turned away. “As if I believe that.”

He really had been thinking. The three bound goddesses already took up his entire emotional bandwidth; there was no budget line for additional women.

HyunA and her whole file could wait for a later volume.

“Right. Let’s get you home.” She had her face aimed at the window, so he drove, and let the silence handle the conversation.

The whole ride back to the f(x) dorm stayed quiet.

At the dorm, he pulled to the curb. Sulli unbuckled and pushed the door open. One foot was already on the pavement—

—when she stopped. Turned. Looked at him.

One second later she was across the console, and her mouth came down on his.

For that one second, Song Ji-yeon’s brain produced nothing but static. Wait. Is this allowed? Is this how the story goes?!

He crushed down the frantic buzzing of the Goddess Handbook in his skull and shoved her back.

“Sulli! What—”

His fingers came away from his lips with a bright sting. Broken skin. Blood.

Sulli, for her part, cared exactly zero percent. She hopped out of the car and delivered her verdict over her shoulder—

“You are NOT allowed to fall for Hara-unnie!”

—and marched into the dorm building without a single backward glance, at the brisk ceremonial pace of a general reviewing troops.

Song Ji-yeon sat there alone, rattling gently in the night breeze.
