Chapter Five

Racing Heart, Trembling Hands

SEPTEMBER 2012 Asan Medical Center, Seoul Emergency observation room

The first thing Song Ji-yeon registered when he came to was the smell of disinfectant.

Cold white light forced his eyes into a squint. Somewhere on the ceiling, one fluorescent tube was dying in small flickers, giving off a thin electric hum.

He moved his fingers. His whole body felt wrung out, as if somebody had run off with every last ounce of strength in it — and his left hand was pinned under something warm and heavy.

He craned his neck up with some effort.

A peaceful sleeping face lay pressed against the edge of the mattress, one cheek resting on his left palm. Lips slightly parted, breathing light and even. At some point the pale baseball cap had slid off, and long hair spilled down, hiding half her face. Even bare-faced, the lashes were long and upswept, the nose bridge high, the skin so fair it nearly dissolved into the hospital sheets.

Well. A peach in human form.

Looking at that sleeping face, Song Ji-yeon honestly wondered how the netizens of the Peninsula, in his old life, had ever brought themselves to drag her the way they had.

The news footage from that other lifetime flickered through his head, and something hot and nameless rose with it. He pushed it back down and simply studied the girl beside the bed. She must have kept watch from the moment he dropped — and waited herself straight to sleep.

On the cabinet next to the bed sat a convenience-store bag: two bottles of water, one unopened pack of wet wipes. Beside it, a crumpled exam slip with a doctor’s chicken scratch scrawled across it — Sudden syncope, suspected hypoglycemia. Recommend two hours’ observation.

He eased his fingers, trying to slip his hand out from under her cheek.

That alone jolted Sulli awake. She shot upright, eyes still not fully open, mouth already at full speed.

“You’re up? You’re finally up! Do you have any idea how long you were out?”

Same machine-gun delivery as always — two hours of stockpiled worry coming out in a single burst.

Seriously, Peach. Has anyone ever told you that you’re loud?

“How long was I out?”

His voice came out dry, sanded down with sleep.

“Almost two hours!” Two fingers waggled in front of his nose, the drama dialed all the way up. “The doctor said it was hypoglycemia, plus acute energy depletion, plus severe fluid and electrolyte imbalance, plus circulatory collapse, plus suppression of higher brain function—”

“Human words.”

This peach wasn’t just loud. She had the attention span of a sparkler.

Sulli spread both hands like a doctor handing down a verdict.

“You fainted. From hunger.”

Song Ji-yeon stared at the ceiling, speechless, and cursed the moron system all over again. So that’s what 2.5× body enhancement feels like from the inside.

“All right, all right. I get the picture.”

He pushed himself up against the bed rail — much easier than he’d expected. The helplessness was draining out of his limbs fast, replaced by a steady, returning strength. Only his stomach was a hollow, growling pit. He felt like he could put away an entire cow, hide and all.

He rolled his wrist and found he was still in yesterday’s shirt, one cuff button undone — the blood-pressure cuff, probably.

“Thank you. For getting me here.” He turned to look at her, and meant it. “The taxi, the registration fee — I’ll transfer them to you in a bit.”

He had no intention of taking advantage of Sulli.

The girl ran on pure instinct and goodwill as it was. Shortchange somebody like that, and whatever went wrong was one hundred percent on you.

“No, no! It’s not like I carried you here myself or anything! You went down like a plank of wood, and you would not wake up no matter how hard I shook you, and there was zero chance I could lift you, so I just called 119… and then I rode along in the ambulance.” Both hands waved frantically, the canvas tote rattling on her arm. “So technically I spent no money. None. Zero.”

Song Ji-yeon was quiet for a few seconds.

Huh. Maybe this peach wasn’t quite as fuzzy as she looked.

“Sulli-ssi.”

“Ne?” She answered on pure reflex, big eyes locked on him, not blinking.

“You didn’t actually manage to help with anything. But thank you anyway.”

The deadpan sincerity of it caught her off guard. She reached up, tucked a strand of fallen hair behind her ear, and ducked her head.

“I told you, you don’t have to be so formal about it. You told me what stock to buy; I delivered you to a hospital. We’re even.”

She stood, fetched the plastic bag off the cabinet, twisted a bottle of water open, and held it out.

“Here, drink first. The doctor said lots of fluids once you woke up.”

He took the bottle and drank. The lukewarm water sliding down his throat helped more than it had any right to.

“Oh, right — your phone.” She dug it out of the canvas tote and passed it over. “The screen cracked in the corner when it hit the ground. I don’t know if it still works.”

The bottom-right corner was spidered with fine cracks, but the screen lit up fine. Three unread messages — all from Ham Eun-jung, sent an hour ago.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Ham Eun-jung

Song Ji-yeon-ssi, I went to the convenience store earlier, and I passed by that hotpot restaurant again.

Ham Eun-jung

The sign is actually really pretty when it lights up.

Ham Eun-jung

What time should I arrive on opening day? I’m worried there’ll be too many people and someone will recognize me…

Song Ji-yeon

Around 5:30. Come in through the side door, I’ll have someone meet you.

He hit send, set the phone down — and found Sulli watching him.

“Girlfriend?”

The tone was casual. The stare was not.

“No.” He blanked the screen. “A friend.”

Sulli went, “Oh,” and asked nothing further.

She settled into the chair by the bed, pulled the little memo pad out of her tote, and turned to a fresh page. With tremendous concentration she wrote something out, tore the page free, and held it out.

“The doctor’s instructions. I wrote them down for you.”

Sulli’s memo

Asan Medical Center — low blood sugar. Drink lots of water. Eat proper food. Get lots of rest!!

The handwriting was round as a first-grader’s, and at the very end she had drawn a smiley face.

A laugh escaped him before he could stop it.

“You’re good at taking care of people.”

Sulli froze — just for a beat. Then her head dropped, and the memo pad got stuffed back into the canvas tote a little too fast, as if she could bury an expression under the flap.

The room went quiet for a few seconds.

“Ne…” Her voice came out much softer than before. “I learned to take care of people really young. I have two older brothers. When we were little, Mom and Dad weren’t around much — and my brothers needed looking after more than I did. After I joined the company, it was… mostly every girl for herself…”

She said it lightly, the way you’d describe somebody else’s childhood. But he could feel it landing wrong somewhere inside her.

Aigoo. Little bitter melon — one question about her own life and the melancholy comes rolling in like fall.

“Sulli-ssi.”

“Ne?”

“Samsung Electronics opens tomorrow. How much you buy is up to you. But I’ll tell you one thing — I bought in. And I didn’t buy a little.” He set the water bottle on the cabinet and looked at her, calm and completely serious. “This isn’t a hunch. I have my own logic for it. If you trust me, buy some alongside me. If you don’t, pretend I never said anything.”

Sulli held his gaze for several seconds. Then the smile arrived slowly.

“You know what? The way you talk is kind of cool.” She stood and slung the canvas tote over her shoulder. “Fine, I’ll buy a little too. And if I lose money, I’m coming to your hotpot restaurant to eat for free until I break even.”

He blinked. He didn’t remember ever telling Sulli what he did for a living.

“How did you know I run a hotpot place?”

Sulli only blinked back and pointed at the business-card case lying by his pillow — the nurses had pulled it from his pocket during the check-in. It read: SONG JI-YEON · CEO, SAVORY F&B.

“Sorry. I peeked at your business card.”

There was not a single gram of apology on her face. Just that famous grin, all teeth.

“It’s fine.” He shook his head, helpless.

“Okay, I’m off — I have to get back to set or I won’t make it.” She crossed to the door, one hand on the handle, and glanced back. “Oh, and next time you see me on the street? Don’t just fall straight over. It’s terrifying.”

She laughed at her own joke first, then pushed the door open and was gone.

As the door swung shut, he heard her humming in the corridor — some light, skipping tune. The sound an ordinary eighteen-year-old makes. A girl life hadn’t started grinding down yet.

“Aigoo.” He let his breath out slowly. “A kid that alive shouldn’t get that kind of ending. This time, let me be the one to change it.”

✦ ✦ ✦

He settled back against the headboard, pulled up the system panel, and actually read through Sulli’s ability and bonds for the first time.

The goddess ability was nothing new — more or less the same deal as Eun-jung’s.

“Well, well. Two bonds.”

Goddess Handbook — Sulli

Goddess AbilityWeekly Tenfold Rebate Card — collect in person, through contact with Sulli

Bond · Human Trilogy0/3

Collect Goo Hara + IU + Sulli → 40% equity in a CJ ENM subsidiary

Bond · Mama V’s Love0/2

Collect Victoria + Sulli → 3% equity in Tencent

Hold on. Tencent equity?

Tencent as in the Tencent?

By 2025 that was a beast sitting on a market cap north of six hundred billion dollars. If he actually collected that set, he wouldn’t just take off — he’d leave the atmosphere.

Racing heart, trembling hands.

He very nearly bolted out of bed to chase Sulli down the hallway and demand an introduction to Victoria on the spot.

But then — activating a bond probably isn’t as simple as activating the card, is it.

He’d already activated Sulli’s golden card. By any reasonable math, Mama V’s Love should have ticked over to 1/2 — and it still read 0/2. Same story with Eun-jung’s Jewel of the Crown: card activated, progress still zero.

Which forced him to a suspicion he couldn’t shake. When the system said “collect,” did it really mean just… collecting?

Figures. Nothing that damn system handed out was ever going to come easy.

Also — what was the Goddess Handbook’s activation condition, exactly? Both activations so far had come completely out of nowhere.

Goddess this, goddess that. He’d met plenty of beautiful girls these past two days!

Is this system just… only into female idols?

Couldn’t figure it out—

✦ ✦ ✦

Three minutes later a nurse pushed in to take his blood pressure, and did a small double-take at the sight of him sitting up in bed, scrolling his phone.

“You recover fast. When they brought you in, you were white as the sheets.”

He smiled at her.

“I’m sturdy.”
