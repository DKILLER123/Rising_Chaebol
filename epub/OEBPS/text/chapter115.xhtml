Chapter One Hundred Fifteen

Bring It—How Could I Not?

WEDNESDAY · NOON Han So-hee’s new apartment, Gaepo-dong The first promise, one unopened drawer, and no room left for retreat

“Are you sure you want to take this step? You can still let go.”

Song Ji-yeon lay on the mattress, his back sinking into the soft quilt.

Han So-hee sat astride his waist and abdomen. The hem of her thin home shirt hung loose, exposing a long stretch of pale thigh. Both hands were braced beside his shoulders, her palms warm and faintly damp.

He did not push her away. He did not turn the moment into a joke. Instead, he tried once more to make her think.

He understood the girl who had spent so long fighting at the bottom of the city. She did not trust kindness that came without a visible reason.

The lease, the furniture, the contract — in Han So-hee’s eyes, all of it could be a favor waiting to be reclaimed.

She wanted security. To buy it, she could offer only the one thing she believed she possessed.

Herself.

Song Ji-yeon was different. He knew exactly why he had signed her: for the commercial value she would have one day. Everything he had invested in her so far was a drop in the ocean, and the return would eventually multiply.

Even without the Goddess Handbook binding Han So-hee to him, the account between them was balanced. She owed him nothing, and he had no reason to treat her help as payment.

She did not need to take this step. He would have done everything he could for her anyway.

But he could not tell her any of that. Was he supposed to say, I was reborn from the future, and I signed you because I know you’ll become a famous actress who makes me rich?

Or explain that the Handbook had chosen her?

Those were his two greatest secrets. He would not tell them to anyone.

All he could do was let her decide. If she changed her mind, he could pretend none of this had happened.

He lifted one hand and held her slender waist.

The instant his palm touched the skin at her side, So-hee’s body went rigid. A breath later, the tension left her completely.

She folded down over him and buried her face in the hollow of his neck, arms winding around his throat.

“I’ve thought it through. You’re my only hope right now… I have to hold on.”

Her voice was muffled against his ear, but the decision in it was clear.

All right. If she doesn’t care about the price, I’m not going to overthink it either.

He was no saint. There was a beautiful woman in his arms, and she had come to him of her own will.

If I can refuse this, I may as well shave my head and join a monastery.

She wanted a guarantee. He could give her one.

“If you’re certain, don’t regret it later.”

He rolled them over and covered her with his body.

So-hee gripped the sheet beneath her and closed her eyes.

She had prepared herself for every possible consequence. Even so, when his breath and heat surrounded her, the calm she had built inside herself began to tremble.

She was not afraid of paying a price. She was afraid she might not even be worth the price. Afraid of returning to the wet, foul-smelling semi-basement. Afraid of working her whole life and still never reaching television, never giving her grandmother the chance to see her on a screen.

She could not give up on Song Ji-yeon.

If becoming his woman was what it took for him to pour everything he had into her dream, then she would become his woman.

He lowered his mouth to hers. The first kiss was exploratory; the next was not.

They undressed one another piece by piece. Clothes slid to the wooden floor. The room grew close with the sound of breath and the small, startled noises neither of them could quite suppress.

When he reached for the last step, So-hee suddenly caught his arm.

He stopped, suspended between impatience and confusion.

“What is it? Have you changed your mind?”

She pushed herself upright and shook her head. Then she pointed toward the first drawer of the bedside table, lay down again, and pulled the new pillow over most of her burning face.

Song Ji-yeon stared at the drawer, got up, and opened it.

Inside was a long, narrow box.

He knew exactly what it was. Eun-jung kept a box at her place too.

The first time he and Eun-jung had slept together, they had not used one. She had taken emergency contraception afterward, but the worry had followed her for several days. The second time, she had kicked him out of bed and sent him to buy protection.

He picked up the box and smiled.

“When did you buy this?”

They had been together in the shopping center the entire time. He had not noticed when she slipped away with it.

So she had planned this carefully. That is almost flattering.

“You were plotting against me from the start? Did you already have this in mind while we were shopping?”

So-hee’s face turned even redder. He was right, but saying it out loud was unfair. She had some pride left.

She yanked the pillow away from her face and threw it at him.

“Are you using it or not? If you aren’t, don’t touch me!”

The pillow hit him. He did not mind. There was still a great deal of heat in the room, and he knew who would be responsible for cooling it.

“I’m using it. How could I not?”

The women around him were idols or actresses, all at the height of their careers. An unexpected pregnancy would be a disaster for them, and he was not ready to become a father. There was no reason to create trouble for himself.

He put it on and returned to the bed.

So-hee had lost even the second pillow that might have hidden her face. They lay there without a barrier between them, her pale skin flushed from throat to ears.

Her hands hovered uncertainly between covering her eyes and hiding herself. Then she seemed to decide that they had already crossed every line worth hiding behind. She lowered her hands and looked directly at him.

When she finally understood just how much of him she was looking at, her eyes widened.

How can anyone be that large? The videos never prepared me for this.

Oh, no. If that goes inside me, am I going to die?

Her stricken expression made him laugh under his breath. He was past the point of letting her imagination make the decisions for both of them.

He kissed her again, and the last of the distance disappeared.

So-hee’s arms wound around his back. She did not cry out. She only clenched her teeth and endured the first sharp pain.

This was the road she had chosen. This was the lifeline she had managed to catch.

She would endure the pain.

She had not chosen him because she was fearless. She had chosen him because returning to the dark was more frightening than taking his hand.

Afternoon sunlight moved slowly across the room. By the time the last tension left their bodies, only their uneven breathing remained.

The quilt came up to So-hee’s shoulders.

She lay on her side in Song Ji-yeon’s arms, one arm resting across his chest, the damp warmth of exertion still on her skin. Her head rested against his forearm.

“Oppa.”

It was the first time she had used a name meant for intimacy rather than distance. The word came out naturally, as if it had been waiting for permission.

“Hm?”

“Will you replace me later?”

That was what frightened her most. She had given him everything she had. If he abandoned her afterward, she would fight him with everything left in her.

Song Ji-yeon turned his head and looked at her. He understood what she was really asking.

Han So-hee would not believe an oath about eternal love. Their relationship was not a normal romance, and promises made in the heat of the moment would sound cheap to her.

So he answered in a language she might trust.

“Why would I replace you? I’ve invested four years of rent, signed an exclusive long-term contract, and now you belong to me. What kind of business buys an asset and immediately sells it at a loss?”

“I’m not an asset.”

She relaxed at the answer, reassured that he would not discard her easily — at least not until he tired of her. But she still made a small protest at being compared to property.

Instead of answering, he threw back the quilt and sat up. Then he scooped her into his arms and carried her toward the bathroom.

So-hee startled and quickly locked her arms around his neck.

“Oppa, what are you doing?”

“Taking you to shower.”

Her face went white. The memory of a moment ago was still too fresh.

“Yah, oppa! It hurts! We can’t do it again…”

The corner of his mouth rose.

He had not been entirely satisfied. She was a first-timer and had held on through sheer stubbornness, but she had surrendered long before he had.

Even so, he was not a cartoon bear with one thought in its head. His desire was not so bottomless that he could do nothing else.

He really only meant to wash the sweat from both of them.

It was October, but the midday temperature was still high. After that kind of exertion, standing around in stale sweat was miserable.

He tapped her lightly on the head.

“What are you thinking? Do you really want to lie there all day?”

“Gulp. But taking a shower together… isn’t that a little…”

She had intended to wait until he left and shower alone. She had never shared a bath with anyone, and certainly not with a man.

He laughed at her embarrassment.

“What is there left for me to see?”

Her face turned scarlet, but she said nothing. He was right. They had already seen each other without a stitch between them.

The bathroom in the single apartment was small enough that two people could barely stand in it. He set her on a low stool, stood behind her, turned on the shower, and began washing her hair and shoulders.

Then the water ran over her skin, her wet hair clung to her back, and the sight of her in the shower light startled him all over again.

His body responded before his reason could intervene.

“Gulp. You said you were only going to— how did it become this again?”

So-hee frowned. She could feel the hard pressure at her back and had no need to ask what it was.

He gave her an apologetic smile.

“What can I say? You’re too beautiful. I can’t always keep control.”

People called Han So-hee the dream woman of the women-loving women’s world for a reason. When she did not smile, she was cold and striking, all sharp lines and attitude. When she did smile, she became a flower in full bloom, bright enough to make a person squint.

Song Ji-yeon had never been shy about his own shamelessness.

“Why don’t you help me?”

So-hee froze.

She was already sore. How was she supposed to help? With her hand?

She turned around, meaning to ask whether he wanted her to use her hand.

Instead, she turned directly into him and found the answer waiting against her mouth.

The heat of his body left no room for misunderstanding. She hesitated, a little unwilling and a little overwhelmed, but she had already decided that following him meant crossing more than one line.

She lowered herself and helped him.

Half an hour later, Song Ji-yeon carried her out of the bathroom. He settled her on the sofa, changed the sheets and duvet cover, and only then lifted her back into bed.

He found his clothes, put them on, and dropped the used linens into the washing machine.

“What do you want to eat? I’ll go buy something. You can’t move in this state.”

Neither of them had eaten all day, and now that his thoughts had returned to ordinary human settings, he was hungry.

So-hee glared at him. Her inability to move was entirely his fault, and now even her mouth hurt.

“Anything is fine. I don’t have much of an appetite.”

She pulled the blanket over her head and stopped acknowledging him.

Song Ji-yeon gave a helpless laugh. He had gone too far today. Even Eun-jung had never made him work so hard for forgiveness.

“All right, I’m going. Don’t blame me if it tastes terrible.”

He picked up his car keys and left the apartment.
