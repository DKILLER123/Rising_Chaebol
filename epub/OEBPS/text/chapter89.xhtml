Chapter Eighty-Nine

They’re About to Be His

NEXT MORNING · 8:00 A.M. Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Ham Eun-jung’s apartment, sixth floor

Song Ji-yeon opened his eyes.

The bedroom curtains were still half-drawn, the morning a soft gray behind them. He turned his head.

Ham Eun-jung was still deep asleep, breathing long and even, curled on her side with one hand resting — light, unconscious — against his waist.

He watched her sleeping face for a long moment. Quiet. Unguarded. The girl who’d once been so careful with him — all formal bows and Ji-yeon-ssi — was lying there with her hair spilled across his pillow and her fingers curled into the fabric at his hip.

He leaned down and pressed a soft kiss to her forehead.

Eun-jung made a small, drowsy sound in her sleep — a faint, complaining murmur — and rolled away, settling deeper into the warmth of the covers without waking.

Ji-yeon didn’t disturb her again.

After last night, she deserved every minute of sleep she could steal.

He slipped out from under the duvet as carefully as he could, gathered his clothes from where they’d landed on the floor, dressed in the dim hallway, and eased the bedroom door shut behind him with a soft click.

In the living room he splashed water on his face at her kitchen sink, scrubbed the last of the sleep out of his eyes, and pulled out his phone.

Eight-thirty.

He’d head to the office.

Yesterday he’d wired four billion won into ShiningYouth Entertainment’s corporate account. Kim Min-hyun, on his end, had reached a verbal agreement with Kim Kwang-soo. The last moving piece was the business license — Park Seon-young’s errand — and once that paper landed, T-ara was his.

But Kwang-soo was the kind of merchant who’d flip a deal over a fly’s-weight of advantage. The longer this dragged, the more variables crept in.

So for the next few days, Ji-yeon planned to be at the office, watching the thing through to the end.

At the entryway he stepped into his shoes, turned for one last look at the closed bedroom door, and let himself out.

8:45 A.M. Cheongdam-dong → Mapo-gu SAVORY F&B headquarters

By the time he found the Mercedes in the building’s underground lot, Seoul’s morning rush had already thickened into a slow, brake-light river. He nosed the car into it and let the city carry him west.

Twenty minutes later the Mercedes pulled up in front of SAVORY F&B’s three-story building in Mapo. He cut the engine and took the lobby at a walk.

The lobby was nearly empty — work had started, the staff scattered to their floors. Only Lee Hye-jin remained at the front desk, her monitor glowing.

“Sajangnim, good morning!”

“Morning.” He smiled and nodded as he passed.

He took the stairs to the top floor two at a time, pushed open his office door, hung his suit jacket over the chair back, and dropped into the wide leather chair behind the desk.

His phone buzzed against the wood the moment he set it down.

A KakaoTalk message. From Eun-jung.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung ♥

Ham Eun-jung

oppa, you left without waking me?

[ pouty hamster sticker, one tear pooling at the corner of its eye ]

Song Ji-yeon

You were sleeping so sweetly, I couldn’t bear to wake you. Up? Want more sleep?

Ham Eun-jung

Up, up, I’m up. oppa, are you at the office? Was the traffic bad?

Song Ji-yeon

Just got in. Bit of traffic, but fine. You? Anything on for today?

Ham Eun-jung

No plans, just home. Oh — oppa, I checked the stock this morning. It’s really going up!

Ji-yeon read that and let out a small, helpless laugh.

Five hundred million won of her own money, sitting in Samjin Group. He’d figured — even with as much as she trusted him — she’d be refreshing the chart every five minutes. Five hundred million was not five thousand. To Eun-jung, that was a real sum.

And she’d gone a full day without even glancing at it.

That’s not trust. That’s the kind of trust that doesn’t have a name in any of the languages I speak.

He typed back.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung ♥

Song Ji-yeon

Still early. Five days, your ₩500M should return at least ₩200M. You’ll be a little rich lady.

Ham Eun-jung

Daebak! Then I’m dragging Qri-unnie into it right now. And Ji-yeon — she doesn’t even have a brokerage account yet. I’ll take her to open one today.

Song Ji-yeon

Take Ji-yeon along. The run has four more days in it — today’s entry is fine.

Ham Eun-jung

Ne, oppa. I’ll go find Qri-unnie and Ji-yeon now. You work hard, don’t tire yourself out. 🐰

Ji-yeon smiled at the screen.

Honestly — this job, he really didn’t want to do it. Nothing at the company was actually pressing; a couple of procurement forms to sign, that was it. Coming in every day was theater. The moment the T-ara deal closed, he was hiring two assistants and working from home.

That’s the life a regressor was supposed to be living. A man with future knowledge shouldn’t be punching a clock in Mapo.

He set the phone down and reached for the intercom.

He’d stopped by the planning department on his way up — Park Seon-young wasn’t at his desk. A round of questioning confirmed the man was out on fieldwork. Almost certainly the license. Ji-yeon had come straight up.

He dialed Park’s extension.

Internal line · Planning Department — Park Seon-young

“Director Park. Where are we on the business license?”

“Sajangnim! Rest easy — I’m on my way to the approval center, I’ve already called ahead. The license will be in my hands before ten o’clock, guaranteed. The second I have it, I’ll bring it straight to you.”

“Good. Once you have it, pass a copy to Director Kim — he’ll need it for the contract.”

“Yes, Sajangnim!”

He set the receiver down and leaned back, fingers tapping an idle rhythm on the desk.

Now — Kim Min-hyun to draft the contract, then a signing room with Kim Kwang-soo, and T-ara was his.

First small goal of the second life. Within reach.

✦ ✦ ✦

10:30 A.M. SAVORY F&B headquarters, top floor President’s office

A knock rattled the door at ten-thirty on the dot.

Ji-yeon didn’t have to guess who it was at this hour.

“Come in.”

The door swung open and Park Seon-young blew through it, document bag in hand, his voice arriving a half-second before the rest of him.

“Sajangnim! Got it — the ShiningYouth Entertainment business license is in my hand!”

The corner of Ji-yeon’s mouth pulled up despite himself.

“Good work, Director Park.”

“Not hard work! For Sajangnim, I’d walk through fire and not feel the burn!”

Park crossed the office in two strides and set the sheet down on the desk. The ink was still fresh — the faint chemical sheen of a newly printed official form, that new-license smell.

Ji-yeon picked up the single thin page. The company name was crisp and clean at the top, the legal representative’s information just below it.

First one.

This was the first company he had built with his own hands since he’d woken up in 2012. A milestone — a real one. SAVORY F&B was his father’s machine; he had inherited it, however capable he’d been at running it. But ShiningYouth — the corporate person, the registered capital, the legal signature — was his alone, every line of it.

His first wholly-owned company.

He let that sit for a beat.

“Copy over to Director Kim?” He looked up.

“Just got it — ran to the copier first thing, handed it to Director Kim on the way up. He’s drafting the contract now; should be ready any minute.”

Ji-yeon nodded.

He could not wait a single unnecessary day on this deal. T-ara had already eaten a month of his life.

“Sajangnim, if there’s nothing else — ONGI’s a mess, I should get back to the new store.”

Park was already rising. The man was stretched genuinely thin — running point on the new ONGI hotpot location and chasing down the new entertainment company’s paperwork at the same time.

One salary, two jobs.

The Korean-immigrant grind recognized its own. The man was a walking labor-law violation.

But — one task done. Which meant, by Park’s internal accounting, the coasting could now resume.

“Sure, Director Park. Good work — bonus this month.”

Ji-yeon wasn’t the type to run a horse to death and starve it. Park was competent, executed without back-talk, and got the small critical things done. He didn’t mind overpaying a lieutenant like that.

Park’s eyes lit up. The complaint in his posture evaporated.

“Sajangnim mansei! Loyalty!”

“Alright, save the lip service — go do your work.”

“Yes, Sajangnim. I’ll head down first.”

Park grinned and let himself out.

Kim Min-hyun’s efficiency was its own clock — within five minutes of Park’s exit, the legal director was rapping on the doorframe, a slim document bag under his arm.

“Sajangnim — the draft acquisition contract, based on our preliminary talks with CCM.”

Kim crossed to the desk and set the folder down. He adjusted his gold wire-rimmed glasses — and then, in the careful tone of a man choosing his next sentence, went on.

“Kwang-soo’s side is settled. He’s agreed to four billion won — packaged sale, all six members’ contracts, plus three-year secondary rights to T-ara’s songs. But, Sajangnim — once CCM has agreed, we still need to approach the six members themselves. To transfer their contracts requires their written consent. If they refuse to leave CCM, the buyout cannot complete.”

This was elementary law. Kim Min-hyun knew it the way he knew his own name.

What he didn’t know — what had been quietly eating at him for days — was why the Sajangnim had only ever contacted Kim Kwang-soo, and never the six girls.

Did he believe Kwang-soo’s yes was enough to buy T-ara?

As a legal professional, Kim Min-hyun was duty-bound to flag it.

Ji-yeon didn’t answer at once. He picked up the contract, leafed through it slowly, page by page. Clauses clean. Rights and obligations cleanly drawn. No linguistic traps, no grey zones.

He set the folder down.

“Director Kim — why do you think I told you to only approach Kwang-soo?”

Kim blinked.

“I’ve already lined up the six members. Tomorrow, I will personally hand you all six signed consent forms. Set the signing time with Kwang-soo — you can proceed in good faith.”

Kim inclined his head — and the puzzle finally resolved.

So that was it.

He’d advised against this purchase. A controversial group, distressed asset, a money-losing proposition from every angle. He’d made his case in writing, even.

And yet the Sajangnim had insisted on it.

Now the picture was clear: he already knew them. He’d planned it through. That was the only explanation for the Sajangnim’s persistence.

Which raised a much stranger question.

Which one of them had—

Some gumiho must have gotten to him.

Kim Min-hyun was, by training and temperament, an SNU-law creature of dry reasoning. He had run the figures on T-ara every which way — they came back negative every time. Since taking over SAVORY F&B, the Sajangnim had been, in Kim’s private assessment, a brilliant operator — a young man who’d taken a steady, slightly sleepy restaurant group and run it up into something louder, sharper, doubled the revenue in a quarter. A strategic mind.

And that same mind wanted to buy a troubled girl group at four billion in cash.

The only hypothesis Kim Min-hyun could produce was a gumiho in a T-ara uniform.

He nodded, neutral.

“Understood. I’ll contact Kwang-soo today — I’ll aim to close the T-ara acquisition inside the week.”

With the six signed consent forms in hand, this would be the final step.

“Good work, Director Kim.”

“Not hard work — that is my duty.”

The door closed behind him.

Ji-yeon didn’t move for a moment. Then he turned to the computer, opened a blank Word document, and started typing.

He drafted the contract-transfer consent form himself — clean, minimal, a single page. It stated, in plain Korean: T-ara’s six members’ contracts and all associated rights and obligations, currently held by CCM Co., Ltd., would be transferred to ShiningYouth Entertainment Co., Ltd.; all six members had been informed and consented. At the bottom, six blank lines for signatures, six blank boxes for inked thumbprints.

Standard form. The girls had signed a half-dozen variations of it over the years — every overseas trip, every cameo, every licensing deal.

He finished, photographed the screen, and sent the photo to Eun-jung over KakaoTalk.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung ♥

Song Ji-yeon

Ready? I’m about to buy you all.

✦ ✦ ✦

SAME MORNING · 10:40 A.M. Gangnam-gu, Seoul Park Ji-yeon’s apartment, bedroom

In a bedroom in Gangnam, Ham Eun-jung stood in the doorway, hands on her hips, looking down at the bed.

The bed contained a blanket-burrito. The burrito contained one Park Ji-yeon. Only the top of a head — dark, tangled — protruded from the bundle.

She had come straight here after persuading Qri-unnie to buy in. Ji-yeon had given her the door code weeks ago; the living room was empty when she walked in, and the bedroom door hadn’t even been latched. One push and there it was: the little dragon, in all her sleeping glory, sprawled across the mattress at an angle that defied geometry.

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon! Up! Get up!” Eun-jung crossed to the bed and tugged at the blanket.

“Mmm… no…”

The voice was muffled, but the register was instantly recognizable: Eun-jung-unnie. Ji-yeon registered this fact somewhere in her hindbrain — what is unnie doing here this early? — and then immediately rejected further inquiry, because sleep was more important. She pulled the blanket up over her ears, blocking the unnie signal at the source.

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon!!!”

A muffled groan from inside the cocoon. Ji-yeon pulled the blanket tighter, curling into a smaller ball.

“Unnie… I’m dying of sleep… let me have ten more minutes…”

“Ten more — it’s past ten! Did you game until dawn again?”

Eun-jung had heard the reports straight from Song Ji-yeon’s mouth — the little dragon’s recent solo-living habits. The girl was, frankly, sliding. Eun-jung was beginning to fear for what Park Ji-yeon would become if left to her own devices for one more month.

Ji-yeon made a small, noncommittal humming sound. Which was, technically, an admission.

Eun-jung sighed, sat down on the edge of the bed, and switched to the patient voice.

“Ji-yeon-ah. Listen to unnie. There’s a real opportunity right now and you have to move fast. Look — unnie already bought, the others already bought, you’re the only one still holding cash. You haven’t moved a single won.”

“I’ll take you to the securities center this afternoon. We’ll open your account, okay?”

“No… I don’t want to move…”

Ji-yeon had face-planted back into the pillow. Her voice came out smothered.

“What stock. I don’t even have an account. Unnie, you just buy for yourself—”

“How is that the same!”

Eun-jung’s voice rose again.

“This is a real opportunity, Ji-yeon. Miss it and it’s gone. Be good — get up. Come with unnie, open an account, I’ll teach you how.”

“Unnie… I really don’t want to move…”

Ji-yeon rolled over, attempting to play dead.

“Just let me sleep until the afternoon. I’ll go by myself then—”

“You have plans tonight!”

Eun-jung poked her in the shoulder, ad-libbing frantically.

“We’re having dinner together tonight — did you forget?”

“Eh? What dinner?”

The word dinner — food — caused one eye to crack open, then half a head to emerge from the burrito. Ji-yeon was, in fact, catastrophically sleepy, but the mention of food produced an unmistakable secondary signal from her stomach.

Yesterday she had called oppa to ask if he was coming home to cook. Oppa had said no — order takeout. She had agreed, fired up the game, and then promptly forgotten the takeout part for the entire night.

Which meant she hadn’t eaten dinner.

Which meant — actually — she hadn’t eaten anything all day yesterday.

A low, sad growl came from the burrito.

Ah. So that was it. Sleepy and hungry.

Eun-jung pounced on the opening.

“Get up now, come open the account with unnie, and tonight unnie will treat you to something good.”

“No… I just want to sleep…”

Ji-yeon had heard the trap snap shut. Obviously there was no dinner plan. Unnie was improvising to get her vertical.

She tucked her head back under the blanket.

Sleep versus food. Sleep won.

Idol life had long since cured her of being precious about skipped meals.

“Park Ji-yeon!”

Eun-jung’s hand closed on the edge of the blanket, preparing to rip.

And then — a small bright chime from her pocket.

<Ding.>

Her hand froze.

She pulled out her phone with the other.

The screen was lit. A message from Song Ji-yeon. A photo. And a single line beneath it.

She tapped the photo open.

CONTRACT-TRANSFER CONSENT FORM

We, the undersigned six members of T-ara, hereby consent to the transfer of our exclusive management contracts and all associated rights and obligations from CCM Co., Ltd. to ShiningYouth Entertainment Co., Ltd., effective upon execution of the acquisition agreement between the above-named entities.

All members confirm they have been informed of the transfer and consent to its terms.

[ Member signatures and inked thumbprints — six lines, blank ]

Standard paperwork. They’d all signed a dozen variations of it — every overseas trip, every cameo, every licensing deal.

Eun-jung stared at the photo.

Her pupils dilated.

She wasn’t slow. The second she saw that page, she understood.

Oppa buying T-ara had reached the last step.

They were about to be his.

No — they were about to be artists under his entertainment company.

The hand holding the phone trembled, a faint, fine vibration she couldn’t have stopped if she’d tried. The excitement was rising in her chest, pressing up under her ribs, and she could not hold it anymore.

She could not hold back.

Her fingers flew across the input box, typing out a line, and sent it.
