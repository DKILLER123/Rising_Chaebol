Chapter Thirteen

How Am I Supposed to Save You?

SEPTEMBER 2012 Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Song Ji-yeon’s apartment

The next morning, Song Ji-yeon was dragged out of sleep by a blade of sunlight through the gap in the curtains.

He rolled over and patted the nightstand until he found his phone. The screen lit up: 10:17.

He stared at the ceiling for a good half minute before it fully landed. Today there was, genuinely, nothing to do. The month before the openings had been one long blur — renovations, kitchen menus, walk-throughs, hiring — without a single day of six hours’ sleep in it. Now the three stores were running, head chefs holding down the kitchens, floor managers holding down the dining rooms, and the Sajangnim of the whole operation had somehow become the idlest man in the company.

He lasted until ten-thirty. Then he couldn’t lie still a second longer.

He excavated half a carton of milk from the fridge, poured a glass, and stood at the counter of the open kitchen, drinking it slowly.

The apartment was quiet. Beyond the floor-to-ceiling windows, Seoul sat under a hard, bright September sun; the streets below moved at a Sunday pace. Everything had a slightly unreal looseness to it, like the first morning of a vacation nobody had told him he was taking.

At noon, he went out.

He found a Japanese teishoku place near Cheongdam-dong and ordered the grilled mackerel set. The fish came off the grill exactly right — skin crackling, flesh still juicy — with seaweed soup and a scatter of small dishes alongside. Comfort, by the spoonful.

Afterward he didn’t hurry back. He ambled a full loop down the Cheongdam Star Road, Seoul’s famous idol-spotting street — on weekends you could barely move for cameras. Today, a weekday, it was nearly empty.

For all practical purposes, Cheongdam-dong was the idol capital of the Peninsula. Half the famous agencies kept their headquarters here, SM Entertainment and JYP Entertainment chief among them.

His total haul after one complete circuit: zero idols. Deeply disappointing.

✦ ✦ ✦

Two in the afternoon. Back at the apartment in comfortable loungewear, he opened the laptop and — pure muscle memory — logged into his brokerage.

The first line on the screen was Samsung Electronics.

005930. The anchor of the KRX, the weathervane of the entire Korean market. The price sat at ₩1,192,400 — down 7.45 percent from the previous trading week, volume running heavy.

He settled back into the sofa, fingers drumming the armrest.

He was holding ₩4 billion in cash. According to the September chart that had come bundled with Ham Eun-jung’s activation reward, foreign money would pile in today and drag the stock back up — and by mid-September a genuine rally would get under way, one with legs long enough to carry into the new year.

But he had no intention of sitting on the position until December. Three months was too long to have that much capital chained to a single ticker.

So he did a little engineering: he opened a 2× leveraged position on Samsung Electronics through an ETF, and the moment approval cleared, his buying power doubled.

An hour later, he was holding ₩8 billion of Samsung Electronics.

No three-month wait required. This one leg of the move would feed him plenty.

He closed the laptop and went to the window to roll the stiffness out of his neck.

His phone buzzed.

A message from Ham Eun-jung.

Good work today. What do you want for dinner? My treat.

He did, genuinely, want more contact with Ham Eun-jung. There were still rewards the Goddess Handbook owed him — the “collect through contact with Ham Eun-jung” kind. He’d assumed contact meant contact. Yesterday they’d shared a whole meal, face to face for two hours; if that didn’t qualify, what on earth would? But when he’d tried the claim last night, the Handbook had blinked back a refusal.

Goddess Handbook · Reward claim failed — required contact not yet achieved.

Which meant “contact” was not the ordinary, physical kind. Wonderful.

They had known each other four days. That was the entire history. Springing physical contact on her was presumptuous — and he had already spent one lifetime’s worth of luck avoiding being mistaken for a creep.

Her reply landed in under ten seconds.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Ham Eun-jung

Nope!! I ate way too much yesterday. We fly to Jeju on the eighth for the ICC concert, and I got on the scale today — I gained SO much — manager oppa scolded me and everything… Strict figure-watching this week!!!

Ham Eun-jung

[Sticker: a cartoon bear crying an ocean]

Three exclamation marks and a crying sticker. Classic Eun-jung.

He looked at the screen, and the smile drained out of the room.

If his memory was right — and his memory was the one thing about him that was never wrong — the Jeju ICC concert would be T-ara’s first offline show since the troll incident. And the opening ten minutes of it would be a Black Ocean. Thousands of lightsticks going dark at the same moment. No cheers. No fan chants. Ten full minutes of dead air, in a hall that size.

He couldn’t tell her not to go. What was the sentence, exactly — you’re going to be boycotted, they’re going to kill the lights on you, stay home?

Whether she’d even believe him was its own unsolvable problem. For all that she played the sweet one, there was rebar in that girl. She knew better than anyone how badly the group’s reputation had rotted — and she was still the last person on earth who would walk away from a stage because somebody told her to.

And the concert was under contract. Kim Kwang-soo couldn’t cancel it if he wanted to. He couldn’t afford the penalty.

Aish. Unsolvable.

Eun-jung-ah… how am I supposed to save you?

He let his head fall back against the sofa and stared at the ceiling. Why couldn’t the universe have introduced him to Ham Eun-jung six months earlier?

Sulli’s tragedy, he could still catch in time. But the troll incident had already detonated, and there was no un-detonating a bomb.

And as a Queens fan from way back, he knew something the comment sections never bothered to notice: politics was coiled underneath the whole thing. It traced back to the 2012 election season, when Kim Kwang-soo had planted his flag — loudly, publicly — behind a presidential candidate. His candidate lost. And a new administration’s first fires are always built from its enemies’ furniture; the incoming president came in burning. T-ara just happened to be the fish in the moat when somebody torched the gate.

He sighed and typed.

He laughed at her reply — and then the laugh settled into something heavier, because Ham Eun-jung was nowhere near as light as she typed.

It had been a month since the troll incident. A month in which the leader of T-ara had been standing at the exact center of the target.

He thought about the night he’d first met her — a small girl zipped up to the chin in layers, checking over her shoulder with flat warning in her eyes after he’d followed all of three steps behind her. He hadn’t needed an explanation even then. A month of malicious DMs and threats will teach anyone to move like prey.

✦ ✦ ✦

On the other side of the city, Ham Eun-jung agreed easily enough, phone loose in her hand.

She liked eating with Song Ji-yeon. That was the plain truth of it.

Since the bullying scandal broke, she had gone from leader of the nation’s top girl group to the industry’s resident bully. Strangers stopped her on the street to lecture her. Her friends — her friends said all the right things, and quietly stopped calling.

She still hadn’t worked out the arithmetic of it. Some friction inside a team of girls — how had it compounded into “bullying”? The worst she had ever said was that a teammate wasn’t being professional about practice, and she didn’t even rank that as a quarrel. Grumbling, at most. The kind of thing every group on earth whispered about somebody.

She felt — in the honest privacy of her own head — innocent.

For a month now she had collected hate mail the way she used to collect fan letters. She went out in full armor, cap and mask and everything, and everyone on the sidewalk looked like an Anti.

But Song Ji-yeon had been the first person since it all blew up who didn’t look at her differently.

Not one of the “I support you!” types with the shifty eyes. Not one of the smile-politely-and-delete-her-number-that-night types, either. He just treated her like a person. Bought her dinner. Asked whether practice had run long. When she complained that the manager had called her fat, he sent back a gloating little sticker of a cartoon bear laughing itself sick.

Normal. So relentlessly, extravagantly normal that it felt like a luxury.

Her phone buzzed again.

The corner of her mouth twitched upward — and stayed up.
