Chapter Eighty-One

Blushing Peach, Clever Mermaid

SATURDAY NIGHT · TEN O’CLOCK Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Song Ji-yeon’s flat, first floor

Chicken eaten, another stretch of chatter absorbed on the second floor, shower taken — by ten o’clock Song Ji-yeon was lying in bed. He lay there a while, weighing it, and then called Eun-jung.

She picked up on the first ring. She was still out with Mushroom, not home yet.

Phone · Song Ji-yeon → Ham Eun-jung

“What’s wrong, oppa? Calling me at this hour…”

“Eun-jung — what are you doing? Why are you out of breath?”

“I’m— outside! Walking Mushroom! He hasn’t been out in two days… he’s extra excited tonight, and he pulls—”

“Ah — no wonder your voice sounds so strange. This late and still out walking. That dog has a frankly excessive amount of energy. How about this: we get him neutered. Save ourselves the midnight marathons.”

Somewhere out on a dark Apgujeong sidewalk, Mushroom would like the record to show his deepest, most heartfelt gratitude.

“Pfft—” The laugh escaped before she could stop it. “Oppa, he’s one. Much too young. And… what if he hated me forever afterward? Anyway — the point. Nobody calls at this hour to discuss dog surgery.”

She knew how he worked. Evenings belonged to Kakao — pure nonsense, back and forth until one of them fell asleep mid-sentence. A phone call meant business. A call at ten p.m. meant real business.

“It’s nothing big. I only wanted to ask — do you have a stock account? If you do, the day after tomorrow I’ll send you one ticker. If there’s spare cash sitting around, buy a little. It’s guaranteed. No losses.”

“…Really?”

“Really, guaranteed. And if you don’t trust me, start small — this stock’s up-cycle runs about a week. Watch the gains for a day or two first, then add.”

Her hesitation he understood completely. What they’d been to each other this past week did not translate into blind obedience on everything else — least of all on something that smelled faintly of gambling. Nobody’s money arrived on the wind. Caution was the correct setting. So: buy a little. Watch two days. Confirm he wasn’t lying, then add.

On her end of the line, she turned it over in silence. His tone was the serious one — no riffing, no salesmanship. And stocks weren’t an abyss, either: worst case, you lost a little. No leverage, sell the moment it dips, the damage had a lid. Besides — she was already his, entirely. What on earth was left for him to con her out of?

“Okay, oppa. I do have an account. Send me the code — day after tomorrow I’ll go buy some.”

“That’s my girl. You won’t regret it.” A pause, and then: “Oh — right. Since you’re buying, why not pass this along to Bo-ram, Qri, So-yeon, Hyo-min… and Ji-yeon. Tell her too.”

“You want me to tell the members?”

“Just that the opportunity exists. Buying or not — entirely their own call. We’re not twisting anyone’s arm.”

“But… oppa, why not tell them yourself?”

“Think about it. Inside T-ara, the only two I actually know are you and Ji-yeon. With Bo-ram and the others I’ve barely exchanged ten sentences. Money matters — stocks, no less — coming out of an outsider’s mouth. How do you think that lands?”

She didn’t answer. She didn’t need to.

“Nine times out of ten they’d decide I had an agenda. Maybe even that I’m a con man. Then a good thing curdles into a bad one — suspicion, guard, all of it for nothing.” A beat. “But you’re different. You’re their teammate — their own person. The same words from you carry weight; they’ll at least think it over. And whatever they put in, even a small position, they’ll come out ahead.”

“I understand, oppa. I’ll tell them. I can’t promise anyone will actually buy, though.”

“Silly — buy or don’t buy, that’s their business. You just pass it along. Now, it’s late. Get Mushroom home. Don’t stay out too long.”

“Mm. We’re heading back now.”

“Get some sleep.”

“You too, oppa. Good night.”

“Good night.”

He hung up, dug out the slip of paper with Goo Hara’s number on it, and added her on Kakao. He waited a while. Nothing came back — she’d gone home and gone down for the count. Fine. He set the mermaid aside and opened the Goddess Handbook instead. Today’s harvest he still hadn’t inspected.

And what a harvest, if you ran the tape back. That afternoon: Park Ji-yeon, bound out of absolutely nowhere — reward, semaglutide synthesis technology. At the airport with Sulli: Goo Hara, contact acquired. In the evening: Sulli’s ambush kiss — one Tenfold Rebate Card. And tonight, one floor up over fried chicken: one Good Luck Card.

The handbook turned its own page. Park Ji-yeon’s little card lay there — eyes bugged wide, cuteness dialed up to a level that should have required a permit — and beneath it, the fine print on everything she’d brought him.

He focused first on the semaglutide.

Goddess Handbook — Binding Reward

RewardSemaglutide synthesis technology

ContentsComplete chemical synthesis pathway · enzyme-catalysis optimization · purification process · full production-line parameters

Cost EffectProduction cost reduced to 30% of existing processes

Purity99.8% or higher

ApplicationIndustrial-scale production of type-2 diabetes and obesity therapies

As ever — there was not a dud in the batch.

He knew exactly what semaglutide was. In his previous life it had been heralded as the miracle weight-loss drug — the one molecule the entire industry chased, Big Pharma’s single most reliable cash cow, the whole second act of Novo Nordisk. And now the synthesis route sat in his head, complete to the production-line parameters.

“Aish. A whole mountain of gold — and not one door in.”

Because here was the inventory of what it would take to actually use it. A pharmaceutical plant: none. An R&D team to turn formula into product: none. KFDA clinical-trial approval: none. Sales channels: none.

Laboratory. Team. Clinical trials. Approval. Channels. Each word landed like a hammer to the sternum. Each one was a line item with an astronomical price tag. A GMP-grade plant alone started in the tens of billions of won. Poaching a research team out of overseas labs — another ten billion, minimum. Clinical trials ran three phases, and every phase burned single-digit billions, sometimes into the tens. And channels — without a network and a pharma-rep army, you could manufacture a genuine miracle and watch it expire in the warehouse.

A bottomless pit with a furnace at the bottom. Everything he currently owned, dropped in whole, wouldn’t raise a splash.

He had the dragon-slaying art. He did not own a single decent blade.

A headache filed its paperwork. He forced himself out of the fantasy and put semaglutide back on its shelf — to be revisited on the day his capital started looking like capital.

He moved his attention to the second of Park Ji-yeon’s gifts.

Goddess Handbook — Item

ItemGood Luck Card (single-use consumable)

EffectOn use — within the following day, the user will encounter one piece of good fortune

Note: the scope of good fortune is random. Use with discretion.

“The scope of good fortune… is random.”

He turned that line over and over, brow folding. It sounded mystical. It sounded useful. It also sounded profoundly useless — good fortune, random scope: finding a coin on the sidewalk counted, didn’t it? Deeply underwhelming. He could hardly bank on the card steering him into ten billion won lying on the pavement.

Rebate card, luck card — neither of them moved his capital needle at speed. Hmph. Survey the whole rack as he might, Eun-jung’s Stock Forecast Card was still the MVP.

✦ ✦ ✦

SUNDAY MORNING · TEN Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Song Ji-yeon’s flat, first floor

He opened his eyes at ten, and — ritual — the first thing he did was reach for the phone and open Kakao. Eun-jung’s good-morning, Sulli’s good-morning, same as every day. And below them, a new avatar in the list: a cartoon self-portrait, all chin and bangs and mischief.

Goo Hara.

He opened the chat.

KakaoTalk · Goo Hara

Goo Hara

Song Ji-yeon-oppa — account’s open. Account no. XXXXXXXXX · password GH0113

Song Ji-yeon

Got it. Is the password your birthday?

He smiled at the screen. The little mermaid kept her word, right down to the hour.

He didn’t rush to log in and check the balance — it was Sunday, the market shut; nothing to see in there but numbers taking a nap. Instead he typed back. He’d seen the format ten thousand times — he used it himself: initials, then birthdate. G.H., January 13. If his memory held, that was in fact her birthday.

KakaoTalk · Goo Hara

Goo Hara

Yes — I’ve got too many passwords, I can’t keep them straight, so I always set them like that.

Song Ji-yeon

Same. That’s my usual scheme too.

Goo Hara

Then when’s Song Ji-yeon-oppa’s birthday? You know mine now — I still don’t know yours.

Song Ji-yeon

December 10. Though at home we run on the lunar calendar — Mom’s rule — so the solar one never gets a cake.

He waited a couple of minutes. Nothing came back. Phone down, out of bed, and into the bathroom — face to wash, and, frankly, a bladder that had been filing formal complaints since the second message.

✦ ✦ ✦

SAME MORNING the f(x) dorm, Cheongdam-dong Sulli’s room — and the blanket war in progress

In the f(x) dorm, Goo Hara was pinned flat on her back. On top of her: one Choi Sulli. Snaking around behind her back: one small, determined hand, hunting the phone she had hidden there. She couldn’t help yelling.

“Yah! Choi Jin-ri! Why are you stealing my phone?! Off — I’ll show you it! Quit getting handsy—”

Sulli’s mouth went into a full pout. “Hmph. Who told unnie to bully me first!”

“Sulli-yah — off! I’ll really show you, no more teasing, promise…”

How had it come to this? Rewind two hours.

Hara was already regretting poking the little peach — her hand really did act before her brain sometimes. But honestly, the record would show: Sulli had gotten mouthy first.

Yesterday she’d gone straight home from the dorm, showered, and slept early. This morning she was up past seven, opened her phone — one new friend request. Ah, right: today was the exchange, and the account, and Sulli. Up, second shower, breakfast, out. She reached the securities exchange hall in Yeouido early enough that the staff hadn’t arrived — 8:40 by her watch; nine was opening. She didn’t wait long. Materials submitted, ten-some minutes of processing, and the account certificate was in her hand. She sat down in the hall, fired the account number and password off to Song Ji-yeon, waited a bit, got nothing, and stopped waiting.

Then: taxi, straight to the f(x) dorm. There were things she wanted to ask the little peach — the kind you don’t ask with the man himself sitting across the table.

In the dorm lobby, waiting for the elevator, she ran into Victoria coming down.

“Annyeonghaseyo, Vic-unnie — heading out?”

Victoria, just out of the elevator, heard the greeting and looked up. Mask and hat — but they’d known each other for years, Hara had been up to the dorm plenty of times, and that tiny frame was unmistakable in a crowd of thousands. She smiled into the greeting.

“Hara-yah. Yes — I’ve got a schedule to run to… you’re here for Sulli? She’s upstairs. Knowing her, still in bed.”

“Ne — I came to haunt Sulli. Go, go, Vic-unnie, don’t let me hold you up. I can find my way.”

Victoria’s hurry was legible from a distance; Hara didn’t try to keep her.

“Then I’m off, Hara-yah. Take care of our Sulli.”

“Mm. Bye, Vic-unnie!”

They crossed in the lobby — Hara into the elevator, Victoria out into the morning at speed.

Upstairs, Hara knocked at the dorm door, found it unlocked, and let herself in. The place was silent. She crossed the empty living room and stopped at Sulli’s bedroom door, knocking lightly.

From inside, a mumble with no consonants in it: “Vic-unnie… not eating breakfast… sleeping…”

The little peach was infuriating and adorable in roughly equal measure. Hara smiled at the door panel.

“Sulli. It’s me, not Vic-unnie. I came to play. Open up.”

A beat of silence, then a burst of rustling. The door cracked one slit, and a bird’s-nest head poked through it, eyes at half-mast.

“Unnie… why are you here so early…”

“Early? The sun has been sitting on your tailbone for an hour.” She pushed the door open and squeezed in. “Up. Keep unnie company.”

Sulli yawned, crawled back into bed at a sloth’s pace, and rolled herself into a blanket roll with exactly one face showing.

“Unnie, I’m dying. I went to bed so late.”

Hara sat down on the edge of the bed, surveyed the lazy lump, and cut straight to it.

“Sulli. What exactly are you and that Song Ji-yeon-oppa?”

The sleepiness evacuated half her skull in a single second. Unnie does not ease in, does she.

And then — unwanted, unstoppable — last night’s footage started replaying. He’d walked her back to the dorm. And just before she got out of the car, one insane impulse had seized the controls, and she had pounced, and she had bitten him. On the lips.

Heat detonated in her cheeks. She shrank the visible half of her face deeper into the blanket, and her mouth went stubborn.

“We’re just friends, unnie. Why are you asking?”

Hara clearly was not buying a gram of it.

“That is not what ‘just friends’ looks like. Out with it — you like Song Ji-yeon-oppa.”

Cornered with no exit, Sulli chose the strategic option: one smooth slide downward, and the entire girl disappeared into the blanket — refusing, on structural grounds, to be interviewed face-to-face.

“Yah! Choi Jin-ri!” Hara laughed outright and hauled at the blanket. “Come out of there! Quit hiding! What is there to be shy about?”

But Sulli had gone full snail — shell sealed, body anchored. However Hara pulled, the blanket did not move. She caught her breath and sat back for a rest.

This little peach. Skinny, small — and strong as a dockworker.

And then: Bzzt. Her pocket.

She dug out the phone and lit the screen. Kakao. Song Ji-yeon.

KakaoTalk · Song Ji-yeon

Song Ji-yeon

Got it. Is the password your birthday?

She blinked. Oh — that was his reply to the account message she’d sent from the exchange hall. She hadn’t looked at her phone since. And here he was, answering at — she checked the clock — ten in the morning.

She typed back, fast:

KakaoTalk · Song Ji-yeon

Goo Hara

Yes — I’ve got too many passwords, I can’t keep them straight, so I always set them like that.

She was about to keep typing when the lightbulb went off. Her eyes slid to the lump in the blanket. A slow, wicked smile assembled itself at the corner of her mouth. She added one more line:

KakaoTalk · Song Ji-yeon

Goo Hara

Then when’s Song Ji-yeon-oppa’s birthday? You know mine now — I still don’t know yours.

Sent. Phone in hand. She counted seconds.

Sure enough—

KakaoTalk · Song Ji-yeon

Song Ji-yeon

December 10. Though at home we run on the lunar calendar — Mom’s rule — so the solar one never gets a cake.

Hara’s eyes lit. The smile deepened by a dangerous degree. She looked up at the little mound of blanket and thought: let’s see you stay in there now.

She cleared her throat and produced, at theater scale, pure shock:

“AH—! Song Ji-yeon-oppa replied to my message!!”

Two seconds of silence from the blanket. Then, at its edge, a small head slowly emerged. Eyes only. On full alert.

“Unnie. What did oppa send you?”

Hara waggled the phone, every tooth enlisted in the smile.

“Sulli-yah. Do you know oppa’s birthday? Because oppa just told me his birthday.”

The visible eyes brightened — visibly. But the head stayed in the blanket, and the mouth stayed stubborn.

“…Don’t care.”

“Oh? Really?” Maximum drawl. “Then I won’t say. Since you don’t care.”

And she began, with great ceremony, putting the phone away.

“WAIT—!”

The lump erupted. Sulli scrambled out of the covers and grabbed for the phone.

“Unnie, show me! Show me! ”

Hara was ready for her — hand snaked behind her own back.

“Nope. ‘Don’t care,’ she said.”

“UNNIE!”

Sulli launched. Full pounce — Hara flat on the bed, one small hand pinning her shoulder, the other reaching around behind her for the phone.

“Yah! Choi Jin-ri! Why are you robbing my phone?! Get off — I’ll show you! Quit getting handsy—” Pinned, breathless, hollering.

Sulli’s mouth pushed out to full projection, her face scarlet. “Hmph — who told unnie to bully me first!”

“Sulli-yah — off — I’ll really show you — no more teasing, promise…”

— which is, more or less, where this chapter came in.

A few minutes of tussling later, Hara threw in the towel — out-wrestled, and not for the first time wondering where so much strength fit in so little peach. Sulli released her, climbed off, and sat to one side with her arms crossed, still puffed up like a pellet frog.

“Now show me.”

Hara caught her breath, straightened her rumpled clothes, and handed the phone over. Looking at the blushing peach beside her, she thought of their first meeting — Sulli had been sixteen then, a whole head smaller, tiny enough to fold up and mail. Nineteen now.

“Before I knew it, our Sulli’s all grown up. Unnie can’t even out-muscle her anymore.”

“Heh-heh! Unnie thinks I’m grown-up now too?”

Sulli took the phone, grinning, and there on the screen was the whole conversation. December 10 jumped out at first glance. She filed the date away somewhere deep and permanent, handed the phone back — and the blush stayed exactly where it was.

Hara watched her and pounced.

“What’s this? Memorized it? Planning oppa’s birthday party already?”

“UNNIE!” Mortified beyond salvage, Sulli grabbed the pillow and fired it at her. “One more word of nonsense and I’m never speaking to you again!”

Hara caught the pillow and roared with laughter. Hmph. A peach taking on unnie? Unnie’s three whole years older and has had an actual boyfriend — I can read your current condition from orbit, and you think you’re hiding it.

But looking at that tender, red little face, Hara’s own situation floated up to meet it. Hers had been just this sweet, at the start. A pity, the way these things go. She thought about it — and decided to say it anyway.

“Sulli. How much do you actually know about Song Ji-yeon-oppa? What he does. How often you two see each other. Unnie’s been down this road. Before you let yourself start something, get those answers straight first. Otherwise — you’ll regret it.”

“Unnie~”
