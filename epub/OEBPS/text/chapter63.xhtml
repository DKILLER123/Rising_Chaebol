Chapter Sixty-Three

The Little Dragon’s Honor at Stake

TUESDAY MORNING · A LITTLE AFTER EIGHT Cheongdam-dong, Seoul first floor — Song Ji-yeon’s kitchen

Ding.

Song Ji-yeon pulled the pumpkin porridge and steamed egg out of the microwave.

Packed this morning — specifically, deliberately, for a certain someone. Who had since executed a flawless getaway and left the breakfast an orphan.

He looked at the takeout bags for a moment, thinking. Waste it? No. The little dragon one floor up had put away her share of last night’s alcohol too. Perfect. This was her breakfast now.

He changed shoes, gathered the bags, and rode up one floor.

✦ ✦ ✦

STILL THAT MORNING Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Unit 201 — Park Ji-yeon’s front door, one floor up

Park Ji-yeon’s unit sat directly above his own. The elevator doors opened — and there was her front door. Unlocked.

Not merely unlocked. Standing ajar, with a clear hand’s width of dark hallway showing through.

Song Ji-yeon stopped and blinked.

Then last night replayed for him. Eun-jung had walked him down fully intending to come back up — so of course she hadn’t closed the door on her way out. Because there had been no “way out.” There had been a kiss in the corridor, and then an interception, and then… a significant change of itinerary.

Meaning this door had stood open. All. Night.

He felt a small, private stab of guilt. He leaned in at the threshold and peered through. The living room was dark, and a smell drifted out to meet him — stale booze and fried chicken, the signature scent of any respectable afterparty.

“Ji-yeon?”

Quietly, from the doorway.

Nothing. Not a rustle.

Now genuinely a little worried — the door had stood open all night — he pushed it wide and stepped in. The scene finished assembling itself, and he couldn’t decide whether to laugh or sit the girl down for a safety lecture.

The living room was a battlefield. The coffee table displayed a full exhibit of empty bottles and chicken boxes in various states of autopsy. Throw pillows lay scattered across the floor like casualties. Textbook party aftermath.

No Park Ji-yeon on the sofa.

“Sleeping in her room?”

He was turning to check when the corner of his eye caught it: a curled-up shape on the carpet, beside the sofa.

Park Ji-yeon. Asleep on the floor. The oversized hoodie had rucked itself into a pile around her middle; her two long, straight legs — shameless to the very end — were propped up on the sofa at an angle she had clearly optimized for comfort. One foot still wore its sock. The other had lost the referendum hours ago. The thin blanket had slid down to her waist, and a slim strip of bare midriff was offering the room its opinions on the temperature.

Song Ji-yeon stood over her and honestly did not know where to file the image. This little dragon carried exactly zero grams of idol baggage. Anywhere. At all.

He crossed over and set the breakfast down on the one clean corner of the coffee table.

September in Seoul: the days still warm, the nights already lying in wait. And floors don’t care what month it is — spend a whole night on one, and a cold arrives as the delivery fee.

He sighed, crouched, and slid one arm under her knees, the other carefully behind her back.

Light. Barely any weight to her at all.

He had just begun to lift when she stirred — brow pinching, a small wordless sound escaping her, the sleep plainly not a restful one. He froze where he was, holding her suspended, and waited for her to settle—

Her eyes snapped open.

Hangover-fogged, unfocused — and aimed directly at his face, from a distance of ten centimeters.

Four eyes. Locked.

The air went solid.

Inside Park Ji-yeon’s skull, gears that had rusted overnight began, creak by creak, to turn. Fact one: she was… in someone’s arms. Fact two: the arms belonged to Song Ji-yeon. Fact three: she was lying on her own living-room floor. She blinked. She blinked again, as though a second blink might file a corrected report.

Song Ji-yeon looked down at the dazed little face and was just opening his mouth to say something reassuring—

“AAAHHH—!!”

A scream with paint-stripping capabilities detonated in the dead-quiet living room. His ears rang; his grip spasmed; she very nearly went straight back down onto the carpet — which, of everything that had happened to her this morning, would at least have been familiar.

Park Ji-yeon scrambled out of his arms like a cat off a hot stove — elbows, knees, pure panic — and backpedaled until the sofa caught her. Then she snatched up the nearest blanket, wrapped it around herself to the chin like a knight donning full plate, and stared out of it at him: a small rabbit making direct eye contact with a fox, and not liking the odds.

She moved — and her entire body began filing complaints. Bones: dismantled. Waist: sore. Back: sore. Aches arriving from districts she had never once been asked to hear from.

And this feeling… was exactly the feeling Hyo-min-unnie had once described, in luxurious detail, as the morning after your first—

Her brain went to pure white noise.

Last night began reassembling itself in fragments. The unnies had left. She was alone. There had been… a truly heroic quantity of soju. And then — then nothing. Blank. Then here.

Why is Song Ji-yeon-oppa in my house?

Why is he holding me?

Why does my whole body feel like it lost a fight with a truck?

The questions piled up and fused into one hypothesis her mind absolutely refused to house:

“We… last night… we didn’t…”

Regret flooded in on panic’s heels. The plan! The plan had been to matchmake oppa with Eun-jung-unnie — that was the whole plan, her ONE job — and somewhere between the soju and sunrise, had she… had she delivered herself instead?

Is the little dragon’s honor about to be forfeited? Here? On her own living-room carpet?

Her voice was shaking now. Her big, pretty eyes flooded fast, brimming with shame and disbelief.

“You— you— what did you do to me?!”

Song Ji-yeon looked at her sky-is-falling face and felt his temple begin to throb in a rhythm he recognized.

This scene. This exact misunderstanding. He had lived this scene before — from the inside. What in the world was loaded into this child’s head? Dramas? Was it all the dramas? I literally just walked in. He raised both hands into surrender position and did his level best to radiate harmlessness.

“Ji-yeon. Breathe. Listen to me for one second.”

“You drank yourself unconscious on the floor last night. I came up this morning to drop off breakfast, found you sleeping down there, and I was afraid you’d catch a cold — so I was carrying you to the sofa. That’s it. That is the entire incident.”

Clear. Reasonable. Airtight.

To Park Ji-yeon, mid-meltdown, every syllable arrived gift-wrapped as a cover story.

“Breakfast?!” She seized the one thread her panic could grip, and her voice climbed an octave. “Why are you bringing ME breakfast?? And how did you even get IN?!”

“Your door was open.” His patience was holding, barely. “I called you — nothing. I called again — nothing. I thought something had happened to you, so I came in. As for the breakfast—” he pointed at the pumpkin porridge on the table, “—that was for Eun-jung. She didn’t eat it. I didn’t want it going to waste. Congratulations. It’s yours now.”

Park Ji-yeon’s brain, already running hot, crashed harder.

Eun-jung-unnie?

What did Eun-jung-unnie have to do with any of this?

The suspicion in her eyes compounded like interest.

“Don’t come any closer!!”

He had shifted one foot. She shrieked and shrank back until the blanket was practically a wall.

He stopped. His head was now fully, genuinely aching. Nothing he said was going to land. Not one word.

“Okay. Okay. I’m not moving. Look at me — planted. Roots.” He spread both hands, palms out, and dialed his voice down to kindergarten-teacher frequencies. “Ji-yeon. Just think for one second. Check. Are your clothes still on you?”

Clothes. Check. She looked down.

Hoodie: present. Shorts: present. Rumpled, slept-in, but fully accounted for.

The relief that hit her was the relief of a pardon arriving mid-execution. Her honor was intact. She was, after all, barely an adult — she had never even dated anyone — and she had exactly zero plans to lose her innocence on a living-room carpet, to a misunderstanding, before breakfast.

She moved again, carefully. Everything still ached… but a full night on a floor. Yes. That would, in fact, explain every single thing that hurt.

She… may have… jumped directly to the worst possible conclusion.

Song Ji-yeon watched the emotions file across her face — terror giving way to embarrassment, embarrassment giving way to the special mortification of a person compiling her own incident report — and knew the gears had finally meshed.

He walked over and flicked her once on the forehead. Lightly.

“Figured it out?”

“Yah!”

Both hands flew to her head. The yelp was one hundred percent mortification.

Her face was burning; her eyes were pointed at everything in the room that wasn’t him; and her mouth was still out there somewhere, fighting a rearguard action on its own:

“I just woke up! I drank way too much last night, I blacked out a little! And everything hurts because of the floor — and because Hyo-min-unnie keeps filling my head with all that weird stuff!! She’s the reason I misunderstood oppa, so really this is all her fault—”

While she talked, she was also, for authenticity, rubbing her sore waist. And her tailbone. Which — in fairness — genuinely hurt.

Song Ji-yeon elected not to pursue the question of what, exactly, Park Hyo-min had been teaching the group’s maknae. Some investigations were better left sealed.

He just nodded, resigned, and pointed at the coffee table.

“You’re up — eat. Pumpkin porridge and steamed egg. I’ve got things to handle, so I’m leaving you to your reconstruction.”

He didn’t wait for an answer — he really had come up here with no agenda beyond rescuing one breakfast from the void — and turned for the door.

“Oppa, safe travels~!”

It came out grinning, all by itself, aimed at his brisk, no-drama exit.

The door closed. Only then did she fully deflate — a whole day’s worth of tension leaving her in one long exhale — and sink down onto the carpet.

The process had been an experience. But oppa had thought to bring her breakfast. He’d carried her — to protect her from the cold floor, no less. The warm half of the ledger kept shouldering the embarrassing half aside, and she decided to let it win.

She picked up the pumpkin porridge from the table. Still warm.

She drank it in small, careful spoonfuls. The warmth spread down through her stomach, and her mood climbed with it, degree by degree, until it reached something close to sunny.

Breakfast finished, she surveyed the state of her kingdom — the bottle graveyard, the pillow casualties — and lodged a formal complaint on behalf of the crown:

“The unnies are SO inconsiderate. Not one of them could clean up before leaving?!”

No response was forthcoming. The kingdom’s entire labor force was, as of this morning, one person.

She accepted her fate, fetched the vacuum and rags from the storage room, and began the long reconquest of her own living room. Bottles into trash bags. Boxes after them. Cushions back onto the sofa, one resurrection at a time.

She was working her way toward the entryway when her hands stopped.

On the shoe rack, beside her own shoes, sat a pair of black low-heeled pumps she had never bought. No — she had seen them. She had absolutely seen them.

And on the floor beside them, a woman’s shoulder bag. One she knew very, very well.

Ham Eun-jung’s shoes. Ham Eun-jung’s bag.

Her brain rang once, cleanly, like a struck bell.

She ran the facts back at speed. Oppa’s words from a minute ago: that was for Eun-jung. Eun-jung-unnie’s shoes: here. Eun-jung-unnie’s bag: here. Eun-jung-unnie herself: not here. And oppa had discovered the open door only this morning — which meant Eun-jung-unnie had never come back up here last night. Not once. Not at all.

So… where had she gone?

A hypothesis began assembling itself — bold, elegant, and completely disastrous — and her heartbeat picked up like a sports car leaving the line.

I think… I’ve just stumbled onto another enormous secret.

Park Ji-yeon dropped the rag where she stood, sprinted back into the living room, grabbed her phone off the sofa, and started typing at combat speed.
