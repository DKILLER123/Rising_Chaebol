Chapter Sixty-Six

Sulli’s Request

SAME NIGHT · PAST ELEVEN Cheongdam-dong, Seoul the drive to the f(x) dorm

The Mercedes rolled easy through the night streets of Cheongdam-dong.

At the wheel, Song Ji-yeon glanced now and then at his passenger.

Sulli had held the white roses since the moment she got in, and the smile had not once come off her face. Every so often she buried her nose in the bouquet, inhaled with her whole body, and let out a long sigh of pure satisfaction.

“So — you’re wrapped. What’s next?” He broke the quiet first.

“Mm...” She actually thought about it. “The company’s giving me a month off. They said I worked hard on the drama and I should rest properly. We don’t have a comeback scheduled, and no new drama is asking for me, so... October’s basically empty.” A pause, and then the addendum, delivered with the gravity of a business plan: “I can sleep in until my body wakes up on its own. I’m going to watch every drama and every movie I’ve missed.”

“Good. You’ve earned a month of that.”

She had, too. On the worst stretches of the shoot, the girl had been running on four or five hours of sleep.

How he knew: certain 3 a.m. photographs. During filming, his phone would light up at three or four in the morning with stills from her set — and by the time he answered them over breakfast, she was already awake again.

The car went quiet once more, nothing in it but the low hum of the engine.

Then, suddenly, she turned her head and looked at him — an uncharacteristically serious look.

“Oppa.”

“Mm?”

“Is Samsung Electronics going to keep going up?” Dead serious, the whole question. “This month’s living allowance, the money I squeezed out of Eomma, and a little I had saved — I want to put all of it in.”

He glanced over at that, then put his eyes back on the road.

“Samsung’s a supertanker. Too big — climbs by inches.” Offhand, like discussing the weather. “If you want the market working for you, sell it, and roll it into Sewon Electronics.”

“Sewon Electronics?” She turned the unfamiliar name over twice. “What company is that? I’ve never heard of it.”

“Recently listed. Precision auto components.” A shrug lived inside the sentence. “Decent upside.”

“Oh...” She nodded along at half-comprehension, and then hit the wall. “But... I don’t know how to do any of it. Selling, then buying, then... such a hassle...”

She bit her lip. Then her eyes went off like a bulb.

“Oppa — could you do it for me? You’re so good at this — it has to beat me fumbling around blind! And if we make money, I’ll give you ten percent. As your fee!”

He didn’t answer. He drove, eyes on the road.

Silence, in her interpretation, could mean exactly one thing: too small. And she knew he didn’t need her money — she knew that. She just couldn’t find another currency to thank him in.

“Twenty percent! Deal or not?!” Two fingers materialized in front of his eyes and waved. “That is my absolute ceiling! Any higher and there’s nothing left for ME!”

Listening to her negotiate against herself, he was somewhere between amused and something he couldn’t quite name.

This silly Peach. Trusting him just like that?

Her entire fortune, handed over for him to operate — no fear he’d make off with it? Or had she simply priced his character at exactly twenty percent?

“You’re not afraid I’ll lose it all?” he finally said, teasing on purpose. “Or transfer it out one day and disappear?”

“Nope.” Instant — no hesitation anywhere in it. “Oppa, you’re not that kind of person.”

Five plain words, and something in his chest moved without asking permission.

He looked at her again. In the on-off flicker of streetlight through the windshield, her eyes were clear all the way down — clean water, filled to the brim with trust.

“...Fine.” Surrender. “How much principal?”

She lit up, and with enormous ceremony held out three fingers.

He glanced over. “Thirty million won?”

A shake of the head.

“Three million?”

Another shake — and now a pout. “Oppa! You are seriously underestimating me!”

He laughed. “Don’t tell me it’s three hundred million.”

She nodded, hard, small pride written all across her face. “It’s three hundred million!”

Now it was his turn to be surprised.

Last time — down on the Korea Exchange floor — hadn’t she said a hundred million? Clearly the girl had been quietly loading up all month.

He knew the shape of Sulli’s money, more or less. As a minor, every won she’d earned had flowed straight into her mother’s keeping; she’d only come of age this year and finally held accounts of her own. But idol pay settled annually — year-end — so what she had in hand right now was appearance fees and allowance, saved fragment by fragment.

Three hundred million won. For the girl she was at this moment, that was very nearly the entire pot.

Though dropped into Sewon Electronics’ pool, it wouldn’t raise so much as a ripple. Nothing to lose sleep over.

“When you get home, send me your brokerage ID and password.” He said it like a standard instruction. “I’ll run it for you.”

“Okay! Sending right now!” She agreed on the spot, already radiating the aura of a woman on the verge of becoming rich. Out came the phone — account and password, straight to him.

Watching that fool’s grin of hers, he remembered the other thing. Had the little peach ever actually asked her leader?

“By the way — that endorsement favor. Your leader. Any news?”

There wasn’t so much as a blueprint for the soju plant yet — but this was exactly the kind of thing you roofed before the rain.

At the word, the glow on Sulli’s face dimmed a notch.

She slumped back against the seatback, deflating. “I talked to Vic-unnie... She said she’s an idol — it’s not her decision what endorsements she takes. That kind of thing goes through the agency’s business department.” A pause, and then, smaller: “She also said — if oppa is really sincere, he should put a proper proposal together and go straight to SM’s business division director.”

One look at her face told him how that conversation had gone.

He wasn’t surprised. Or rather — he’d called this exact result before she ever asked. Sending Sulli in had been a half-joking shot, and he’d never pinned real hope on it. SM was an empire. Its artists were not signing endorsement deals for a no-name brand nobody had heard of — and certainly not in a category as sensitive as liquor.

“It’s fine. I was only asking.” He patted the air between them. “There isn’t even a factory yet. No rush.”

“Mm...” The acknowledgment came out flat. She visibly felt she’d fumbled the errand — sorry toward him, with no way to say so.

The ride carried itself the rest of the way on small talk, and soon enough the dorm’s high-rise slid into the headlights.

An ordinary-looking apartment tower — and security tight enough to embarrass a bank.

He pulled to the curb.

“I’m here!” The seatbelt came off — but she didn’t move to get out.

Roses in her arms, she turned to face him. “Oppa — thank you for today. Really. I love the flowers.”

“Glad you like them.” A smile. “Go on up. Get some sleep.”

“Ne!” She pushed the door open and hopped out.

Standing at the curb, she waved at him through the glass. “Bye, oppa! Drive safe!”

“Got it. Go on inside.”

He watched her hug the bouquet and skip toward the entrance — watched until she was through the doors — before he restarted the engine, turned the car around, and fed it back into the night traffic.

He did not notice.

In the shadow across the street, a figure in a black baseball cap and a mask had watched the entire scene, frame by frame.

Sulli stepping out of the car. The smile on her face — radiant, with a thread of shyness woven through it. And the white roses in her arms, conspicuous as a searchlight.

In the dark, the corner of the figure’s mouth curved upward.

So the little peach went and got herself a boyfriend — without breathing a word to anyone. Good thing I came back today, or I’d never have caught it.

Only when the black Mercedes had vanished completely around the corner did she pull her eyes back.

One glance at the entrance Sulli had disappeared through. A tug of the cap brim. Then she stepped out of the shadow and followed at an unhurried pace — into the same building.
