Chapter Four

System, You Absolute Menace

SAME DAY Korea Exchange Yeouido, Seoul — the trading floor

Sulli finished her forms, did a full lap of the hall — the classic first-timer orbit — and finally folded herself into the chair beside him.

Ji-yeon was logged into his new account on his phone, waiting for the qualification review to clear. He worked unhurriedly, glancing up now and then at the big board to check Samsung Electronics against his memory.

₩1.12 million. Right on schedule.

“Annyeonghaseyo~ Um—”

He turned. Sulli had rotated her whole body toward him, chin up, eyes shining like a puppy that had decided this specific human was The One.

“You’re really good at stocks, right?”

The delivery was pure, unfiltered directness — a probe with zero camouflage.

Ji-yeon looked at the shine in those eyes and briefly lost the power of speech. What sequence of events, he wondered, had led this girl to conclude that this was a normal question to ask a total stranger?

Little Peach, do we know each other? Aren’t you being a little bold right now?

“I’m all right. Know a bit.”

Four words, clipped, tone carefully tuned to end conversations.

“Liar.”

No mercy whatsoever. She leaned in and pointed at his phone — at the transfer screen still open on it.

“I saw it just now. Your account has four billion won in it. Normal people don’t bring ₩4 billion somewhere to ‘know a bit,’ do they?”

Song Ji-yeon’s brain produced a long, silent ellipsis.

So Her Highness had been reading his screen this entire time.

What year was Sulli born, again? Last life, he’d been a casual visual-fan of the Peninsula’s girl groups, full stop — the type who fell in love with a new lineup every comeback season. And the idol industry refreshed faster than a phone screen; he’d never had reason to memorize any one member’s biography.

He’d known Sulli’s name at all because of the internet — Reddit, trending pages, allkpop comment sections. Even someone who barely followed Peninsula entertainment couldn’t have dodged it. He remembered opening those threads purely to snack on the gossip, and ending up reading everything anyway.

“And if I am?”

He turned the phone off and looked at her, interested now despite himself.

Being questioned back seemed to short-circuit her nerve. She tugged the cap lower and dropped her volume by half.

“It’s just… I have some money doing nothing. Bank interest is basically zero, and it’s not enough to buy a building or anything. So I thought I’d come look at stocks. And then I walked in here and realized I don’t understand a single thing on these screens.”

She paused, then gestured vaguely at the hall around them.

“All those ajusshis look terrifying. You look young. You seemed like the safest option.”

A laugh escaped him before he could stop it. There it was — she’d walked the entire trading floor, done her survey, and picked him out as the pushover.

His last-life impression of Sulli ran on two tracks: f(x) stages — and headlines that had made his chest ache to remember.

The girl in front of him, drowning in an oversized hoodie and genuinely stumped by a stock board, overlapped with the figure in his memory — the one standing under stadium lights, absorbing more cruelty than any twenty-year-old should ever have to hold. Something complicated moved through him.

He breathed in, pushed it down, and kept his voice flat.

“How much are you planning to put in?”

She hesitated, then held up one finger.

“Ten million?”

Head shake.

“A hundred million?”

Nod.

He raised an eyebrow. ₩100 million was modest for a member of a top girl group — but in the hands of a total beginner, it might as well be a donation with extra steps.

“My advice? If you don’t understand anything, don’t rush in at all.”

He stood and passed his completed forms to the clerk at the window.

“The stock market isn’t an ATM. You throw a hundred million in blind, and a month from now you might be looking at fifty.”

Sulli blinked. Clearly, the number fifty had landed somewhere painful.

But she didn’t retreat. If anything, she leaned closer.

“Then which one are you buying? Can I just… copy you? A small amount. If it loses money, that’s on me — I won’t blame you.”

He studied her for a moment.

Her eyes were dead serious. Not a joke in sight.

“You’re not worried I might be a con artist?”

He almost wanted to laugh. What a genuinely silly Peach.

Sulli tilted her head and gave him a slow, thorough once-over — the once-over of a professional — then delivered her verdict with total confidence.

“Your clothes aren’t designer, but they’re immaculate. Your cufflinks are custom — there’s a monogram on them. Your shoes are handmade, and polished. And you’re carrying Peninsula Bank’s black card — I saw it by accident, I wasn’t peeping on purpose. A con artist wouldn’t put this much care into a costume. And con artists don’t come to the exchange to open accounts through the front door.”

Ji-yeon went quiet.

He glanced down at the S.J. on his cufflinks — his mother had them made — then at the handmade shoes she’d ordered to go with them, and finally back at Sulli’s face.

“Your observation skills are no joke.”

Sharp eyes, quick inventory, confident delivery — the girl standing there glittering at him looked plenty clever. So why, in the last life, had she kept stepping on every landmine in sight?

“Of course. It’s how I eat.”

She beamed, a full row of neat teeth on display.

“So? Will you tell me which one you’re buying? Consider it — making a friend.”

He was quiet for a few seconds.

An interview clip surfaced in his memory, years out of order: Sulli laughing on some program, saying she didn’t understand investing at all, that she only trusted real estate. Rumor said her earnings from year after year sat locked in accounts her mother controlled — the work all hers, none of the money in her own hands.

Aigoo. Another little bitter melon.

If he could nudge the wheel a few degrees, he’d nudge it.

“Samsung Electronics.”

He said it lightly — but clearly.

Sulli blinked, then dug a memo pad out of her canvas tote and wrote the name down, letter by letter, with the diligence of a student transcribing the exam answer.

“How much do I buy?”

“That part’s yours to decide.”

He folded his receipt into his wallet.

“All I can tell you is what I bought.”

“Why that one?”

“Intuition.”

Sulli stared at him — and then burst out laughing, both rows of white teeth out and gleaming.

“You just finished lecturing me that the market isn’t an ATM — and you’re buying on intuition?”

The laugh was blinding, objectively beautiful. His considered professional assessment was still: what a dork.

Fine. A man doesn’t bicker with dorks. It’s beneath him.

“That’s why the money I put in is money I can afford to lose.”

He rose and gave her a small smile.

“So — you. If your hundred million disappears, does your life change?”

The smile stalled, then folded itself away. She looked at the floor for a few seconds.

“…It does.”

That ₩100 million was everything she’d saved since coming of age. Everything earned before it sat in her eomma’s accounts — and asking for it back was not a conversation she could win.

“Then put in less. Or nothing at all.”

He turned to go, made it two steps, then looked back.

“If you absolutely have to buy — only use an amount that wouldn’t hurt to lose.”

Sulli stood there hugging her tote bag, and waved at his back.

“Thank you! Oh — wait, what’s your name?”

“Song Ji-yeon.”

“I’m—”

“Sulli. f(x)’s Sulli. I know you.”

He waved without turning around and didn’t slow down.

Through the closing doors, he caught her trailing mutter:

“Aish… recognized after all… what a strange man, seriously.”

For a girl at the center of a trending group, successful men orbited her in a permanent asteroid belt. As far as she could remember, this was the first time in her life someone had recognized her — and simply left.

He only caught the first half of the mutter, and it earned a helpless smile. Did Sulli have some misunderstanding about her own fame? One month back in this era, and the face he’d seen most often on posters and billboards was Song Qian’s — the Mama V era was no joke, and the group’s maknae line had been surfing that wave of exposure all year. He’d been swimming in f(x) pictorials since the day he got here.

Which is also why he’d recognized her in one glance, hamster hoodie and all.

✦ ✦ ✦

He’d just stepped out of the exchange when the phone buzzed in his pocket. Eun-jung.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Ham Eun-jung

Ji-yeon-ssi — it’s a promise, okay? Soft-opening day, I’M treating. No more stealing the bill from me!

He stared for two seconds at the emoticon at the end — a tiny determined face, fists up.

He was halfway through typing a reply when a golden card bloomed into the air in front of him.

On it: Sulli, mid-laugh, both rows of teeth out, grinning like an absolute dork — rendered in crisp, living detail.

Goddess Handbook — Activation

GoddessSulli (f(x))

Activation RewardBody enhancement — 2.5×

Goddess AbilityOne Tenfold Rebate Card per week — collect in person, through contact with Goddess Sulli

Goddess Bonds — “Human Trilogy” (0/3) · “Mama V’s Love” (0/2)

He’d stopped walking. He hadn’t even finished reading the ability line, hadn’t touched the bonds — when his entire body seized.

Pain arrived. Not the polite kind.

His vision went black, and one final thought survived the collapse:

System, you absolute menace — so this is your idea of body enhancement.

Behind him, the doors swung open. Sulli came out — cap pulled low, tote hugged to her chest — glanced left, then right, and saw, not ten meters away, a man toppling backward, stiff as a felled board.

She was moving before she’d decided to move.

“Song Ji-yeon-ssi! Song Ji-yeon-ssi!”
