Chapter Eighty

Surprise After Surprise: The Dragon Is Bound

SATURDAY NIGHT · PAST EIGHT Cheongdam-dong, Seoul the f(x) dorm — the parking lot

“…”

“…”

“Hss—… this little peach.” He sat in the driver’s seat and did not start the car. “A kiss is a kiss. Since when is there biting?”

He raised a hand and touched his lip — a thin sting at the corner of his mouth. He drew the hand back and inspected it: a smudge of bright red on the pad of his thumb.

Frowning now, he pulled down the sun visor and flipped open the vanity mirror.

In the glass: a narrow cut on his upper lip, near the corner of his mouth. Small, but unmistakable — a fine bead of blood welling up out of it.

Hss. The little peach had not gone easy at all.

A tissue came out of the armrest, and he dabbed at the cut with the delicacy of a man restoring a painting.

The state he was in was roughly half fury, half laughter. A kiss, fine. But biting? That was industrial-grade jealousy.

He balled up the bloodstained tissue, dropped it into the armrest, and muttered darkly at the mirror, mid-dab: “Just wait, little peach. The next time I see you — see if I don’t deal with you properly.”

Then he leaned back against the headrest and replayed it.

Sulli, one foot out the door — then the lunge, executed with the commitment of someone storming a beach — then the pain arriving on schedule. Her hopping out. The sentence tossed through the window. Not one look back.

The whole thing had taken less time than a red light. His body hadn’t filed a single objection in time.

Song Ji-yeon was not a slow man. If anything, he was sharp — professionally sharp. He strung the day’s events into a single line.

It had started with the phone call, with a certain someone bubbling over about introducing him to a “total beauty.”

At the airport — he’d grant the charge — he had looked at Goo Hara several extra beats. That woman occupied a strange, aching shelf in his past-life memory; of course he’d wanted to see what she was actually like off-camera.

But those few extra beats had apparently rendered at a completely different frame rate in a certain someone’s eyes.

At dinner she’d been off — kept drifting toward his side of the table. In hindsight, the jealousy had already set in by then; every touch and lean after that had been her planting a flag against her Hara-unnie. Then the drop-off, the interrogation in the car, his denial, her flat refusal to believe it. And then — the parting bite, delivered with intent to punish.

That wasn’t a warning. That was a cat with its fur standing on end, announcing to the neighborhood whose human this was.

The more he turned it over, the more the reading held.

The little peach had actual feelings for him. First-crush-type feelings — clumsy, overbearing, pure enough to be visible from space, and far too direct to ignore.

He sighed. Somewhere behind his temples, a headache filed its arrival paperwork.

One Ham Eun-jung. Now, on top of her, one Choi Sulli. And the roster was only going to grow.

Well — he’d signed up for this. He’d bound the Goddess Handbook, and the bonds weren’t going to complete themselves. Fine. He’d eat the bitterness. Somebody had to.

Decision filed, he opened the Handbook in his mind’s eye. During the kiss — he’d crushed the sound down at the time — it had rung once.

Goddess Handbook · Contact with Sulli — obtained: one Tenfold Rebate Card.

“Right. That makes two Tenfold Rebate Cards now.” He ran the mental inventory. “Time to find somewhere to spend one.”

Hm?

While he was scheduling rebate logistics, his eye snagged on the Handbook’s card rack. There was a card sitting there that had not existed that morning.

“When did Park Ji-yeon get bound?”

He stared at the new card. Its face showed the little dragon mid-aegyo — eyes bugged wide, cuteness turned up to a level that should have required a permit.

Goddess Handbook — Bound Goddess

GoddessPark Ji-yeon (T-ara)

Activation RewardSemaglutide synthesis technology

Goddess AbilityOne Good Luck Card per week — collect in person, through contact with Park Ji-yeon

Goddess BondJewel of the Crown — the six jewels (2/6)

“Unless— Aissh—!” He slapped a palm against his forehead. The phone ringing him awake this afternoon. The little dragon scrambling for the study door, flustered, guilty, with nowhere to spend the guilt. That was the moment. He’d simply been asleep at the chime.

What he couldn’t reconstruct was the mechanism. He had the Handbook’s rules mapped by now: binding required making a goddess’s heart move.

But this one defied the paperwork. He had done nothing. He had been asleep. How do you flutter a heart from inside a dream?

What exactly had Park Ji-yeon done to him while he was unconscious?

He shook his head and let the question go — the scalp-prickle of the answer was somebody else’s problem. The dragon was one floor up and had nowhere to run; he’d go up, look her in the eye, and assess the situation. And if he could pick up the Good Luck Card while he was up there, so much the better.

The engine woke. The black Mercedes rolled smoothly out of the f(x) dorm complex and merged into Seoul’s night traffic.

MEANWHILE · MINUTES EARLIER the f(x) dorm the elevator, and the corridor outside 904

The other half of the story had bolted out of that car like a startled fawn, torn across the lot, and gone through the dorm doors without once looking back.

Only when the elevator doors slid shut between her and the outside world — and the world’s current leading source of cardiac arrhythmia — did she let herself sag against the cold metal wall of the car and gulp air.

Her heart was going off in her chest with no plan and no volume control.

Her face was burning at a temperature that required no mirror to confirm. She knew. She was red. Everywhere. Completely.

Just now… what had she just done?

She had bitten Song Ji-yeon-oppa. On the lips.

Both hands came up over her face. Behind them, her brain finished liquefying into porridge.

She had just been so angry.

From the airport onward, oppa’s eyes had kept drifting — not even bothering to hide it — toward unnie. He’d called unnie a great beauty with something in his eyes Sulli couldn’t parse. Admiration, and… pity? Pity, for Hara-unnie? What was that supposed to mean?

At dinner, too. He’d grilled and served and looked after both of them equally — but part of his attention had stayed parked on unnie the entire time, and Sulli had felt it. It sat in her chest like a wad of cotton: stuffed in, stuffy, and sour at the center.

She’d wanted oppa to look at her more. To coax her, even once.

And the whole way home, he hadn’t said one coaxing word.

The grievance and the temper had peaked at precisely the instant her foot hit the pavement.

She’d wanted payback. She’d wanted to leave a mark on him — one that was hers — so that he’d remember: you do not look at other women.

So at the moment of getting out, one insane impulse had seized the controls, and her body had executed an action that not even she could explain.

But now the fever was cooling, and the shame arrived all at once, like a tide with a grudge.

How could she have done that?

What was the difference between that and stealing a kiss?

What was oppa going to think of her?

And why — why — had she been that angry? Why, of all the available options, did she want to bite his lip?

Unless… unless she really had—

The thought sprouted, and then it grew the way vines grow — fast, everywhere, in loops — until it was wound around her chest and she could barely breathe.

Ding.

Her floor.

She inhaled, held it, and reassembled herself by force. At the dorm door she didn’t reach for her keys. She paced the corridor instead — three steps, turn, three steps — giving the flush time to drain and her heart time to clock back down to a lawful rate.

About five minutes of that, and she judged herself approximately a person again. Then the keys came out of the bag, into the lock, around.

Click.

The door swung in on a living room with no main light — just one dim standing lamp. A figure sat on the sofa, back to her, its shadow drawn out long across the floor.

Her heart dropped half a floor.

Vic-unnie.

“You’re back.” The voice came off the sofa with no readable temperature.

“Mm — unnie’s still up?” Door shut, slippers on, casual voice deployed.

Victoria’s brow creased faintly. It was barely past eight. Sleep? Who?

Sulli angled toward her own room, and Victoria spoke again.

“Where were you today? Not a trace of you all day.”

Her step caught. She turned back, ran her options, and chose the edited one — smiling as she did. “Hara-unnie flew back from Japan today. I went out to the airport to meet her, and we got dinner.”

“So Hara’s back.”

A fine brow arched. The look had a lens in it. Did this child honestly not know that when she lies, her smile arrives five seconds early and confesses everything?

“Just the two of you?”

“And… a friend.”

The pause had been small. But it existed.

She did not dare produce the name. Vic-unnie had issued the rule in writing, more than once: no contact with Song Ji-yeon-oppa.

Victoria looked at her for several seconds and said nothing. The living room went quiet enough that the wall clock’s ticking had the floor to itself.

Under that gaze, Sulli’s conscience shifted in its seat and studied the baseboard.

“Enough. Get some rest — I have a schedule tomorrow.” In the end, that was all Victoria said. She didn’t press.

“Mm — sleep early, unnie. I’m heading to my room.” Sulli, granted a full amnesty, answered at once and speed-walked into her room, easing the door shut behind her.

In the living room, Victoria watched that closed door, her brow not quite relaxing. Something about Sulli today had been off-key. But if the girl said she’d been with Goo Hara — then there was nothing to pursue.

THE DRIVE HOME Cheongdam-dong, Seoul a fried-chicken detour, then Unit 201

On the way home he passed a fried-chicken shop, let off the gas, and pulled over. One order of original, straight out of the fryer.

He carried the hot bag into the complex, down into the underground lot, up the elevator to the second floor.

Just past eight. The little dragon would still be up.

He wanted to see what she was doing — and, in the same visit, run a collection attempt on that Good Luck Card.

The Park Ji-yeon card was still glowing faintly in the Handbook. The ability needed collecting in person, and who knew what a Good Luck Card even did. He hoped it wasn’t like Sulli’s rebate card, which took an act of God to fire.

At her door, before his finger reached the bell, a familiar roar came through from inside.

“Ah, ssibal!! Are you SERIOUS with that positioning?! You son of a— trash yourself and blame ME?!”

Followed instantly by a keyboard under machine-gun fire.

A discreet line of black rain ran down the back of Song Ji-yeon’s head. Right. Gaming. And not calmly.

He shook his head, pressed the bell — and waited, fruitlessly, through a stretch of ranting that decisively outvibrated the doorbell. So he simply reached out and keyed in the door code.

Park Ji-yeon had given it to him herself — so he could come up and duo whenever he liked.

The door opened on an entryway in a state of mild collapse: one pair of slippers toppled every which way. The living-room light was on; the coffee table held a half-drunk cola and an opened bag of chips.

Confirmed: no actual dinner. She’d foraged.

No wonder the kid was all elbows. A diet of cola and chips wasn’t a diet. It was a rumor.

He set the chicken on the dining table and followed the noise to the study.

The study door stood half open, the racket arriving in perfect fidelity. Park Ji-yeon — headset on, planted in the gaming chair, eyes locked on the screen, fingers hammering — was muttering along in real time: “Ssibal, you mongrel — trash at the game and blaming your team — did your mother cut the umbilical cord and take the brain with it?!”

Song Ji-yeon stood in the doorway and felt the corner of his mouth twitch.

Korean girls and their mouths. The reputation: deserved. And then some.

But Park Ji-yeon — a word. You are an idol.

He pushed the door open quietly and went in.

Her back was to the door; she registered nothing. He ghosted across the room, bent, and leaned his forearms on the back of her gaming chair, eyes going up to the scoreboard in passing.

0/8/2.

Mm.

This bad. And flaming other people.

She was walking her champion out of the fountain when the chair dipped under new weight and a patch of warm breath arrived on the top of her head.

Her face tipped up on pure reflex—

—and a human face was hanging directly over her skull, looking down at her screen.

“AAAAAHHH—!!”

Her whole body launched. Her head went up like a surface-to-air missile, and somewhere in the middle of its arc it connected — with a solid, woody thunk — with Song Ji-yeon’s chin.

“OW—!!”

Tears arrived at his eyes roughly on schedule. Clutching his jaw, staggering two steps back, he hissed air in through his teeth. “Hss… Park Ji-yeon! Is that skull of yours iron?!”

The impact had rung her bell too. The headset went flying off her head, and her scream very nearly took the roof off. “Aissh!! A GHOST!!!”

Both hands clamped on the back of her head, she spun around — and, resolving his face out of the chaos, arrived at shock and fury in roughly equal parts. “Song Ji-yeon-oppa!! How do you WALK without making any sound?! You nearly killed me!!”

He held his jaw, teeth bared in pain, and shot her a glare with no charity in it whatsoever. “I rang the bell. Your cursing was louder than the doorbell — expecting you to hear it over that would have been superstition.”

“Then you could’ve at least—”

“At least what? The door code was your gift to me.” He cut her off, worked the jaw once, and jerked his chin at the screen. “Ji-yeon-ah. Zero, eight, and two. And you have the audacity to flame your team? Also — you’re an idol. Come on. Let’s review the chat log.”

“YAH!!!” Full detonation — the face went red from two directions at once. She had BRAGGED to this man about being a gamer! A losing record she could survive, but being caught flaming mid-game? In front of him? She lunged across to block the monitor with her whole body, one hand slapping down on the power button. “You don’t understand ANYTHING! That was mechanics! MECHANICS! It wasn’t typing, and it wasn’t flaming—”

She could not, under any circumstances, let oppa scroll that chat log. The shame would be fatal.

Song Ji-yeon’s position, for the record: he had heard the entire performance. Live. From the hallway.

“Mechanics. The computer is now off. What exactly are you mechanicking?”

Watching her flail, he had to admit it was, objectively, a little cute.

“…”

She turned her head, confirmed the dead black screen, and the steam went out of her instantly. When she came back around, she was all heh-heh and folded hands. “Mechanics? What mechanics? Flaming? Who, me? I would never. Oppa must have misheard… Yes — that’s it — oppa was having auditory hallucinations just now.”

Song Ji-yeon: …

Park Ji-yeon. Is that skull running two operating systems at once?

He gave up on prosecution, amused despite himself. “Right. My mistake. Our Ji-yeon, flame someone? Impossible.”

He’d only come up to tease her anyway. Blowing off steam in a game was harmless — and honestly, if she could spend her evenings like this, loud and furious and gloriously alive, then the thing he quietly worried about for her, the long dark he remembered from the last timeline, had that much less room to grow.

She peered up at him, sheepish. “Oppa — what’d you come up for? To game?”

“Was afraid you’d game yourself to death up here and no one would find you till Tuesday.” He turned back toward the living room and lifted the takeout bag in evidence, right hand still cradling his jaw. “Brought you a midnight snack. Didn’t expect to get assaulted on entry. That skull really is iron-grade.”

Her eyes snapped to the bag — that familiar, glorious, golden-brown bag — and her stomach filed a loud, unpatriotic growl.

She’d been gaming all evening. Dinner had been half a cola.

“Thank you for the feeding, oppa~” The transformation was instant — all sweetness now, both hands reaching for the bag.

…Feeding.

What am I, then — the dragon’s personal zookeeper?

He laughed anyway, and smacked the reaching hand out of the air. “Wash your hands.”

“Yah… oppa~” The hand withdrew, the mouth grumbled — and the feet trotted off toward the bathroom with perfect obedience.

By the time she came back, hands washed, he was on the sofa with the box open: golden, crackling chicken breathing heat into the room, two little boxes of rice cakes alongside, and a small tub of pickled radish.

Her eyes lit up. She planted herself on the sofa without ceremony and had a piece in her mouth within the second.

“Mmph… so good—” Outside crisp, inside tender, hot enough that she was fanning her open mouth with one hand — and still refusing, on principle, to spit it out.

He watched her eat from his end of the sofa, the corner of his mouth trending upward entirely on its own.

This little dragon. Angry, she was a dragon with her fur standing on end. Eating, she was a hamster with its cheeks full. Admissible levels of cute.

And — an idea. He reached over, set his hand on her head, and patted.

A small head, round, the hair shockingly soft.

Mm. Excellent texture. Approved.

Park Ji-yeon froze mid-bite. By the time her brain caught up, the pat was already half completed.

…What is happening?

The question flashed through — and was instantly bulldozed by a second, more urgent development: heat, flooding up her neck and face at scandalous speed.

She knew this scene. She had seen this scene — in roughly four hundred dramas. Next, the male lead gazes at her, deeply. Then comes the kiss. Then the— MM! Park Ji-yeon! What is playing in your head right now?!

Why would oppa even— oppa’s got that whole UNCLEAR THING with Eun-jung-unnie, doesn’t he?

Mm. So then what is this? A change of target?

While her internal drama committee tore itself apart, Song Ji-yeon had already received the system’s small tremor — and his hand had already left her head.

He was, honestly, a little stunned.

The ability reward, he had learned the hard way, only extracted if the goddess’s heart genuinely moved on contact. Sulli, Eun-jung, Kim Ji-won — extraction from that roster ranged from hard to functionally impossible. He had field-tested this exact head pat on Sulli right after binding her; results: negligible. Today’s pat had been a pure whim — a probe, to measure how difficult Park Ji-yeon’s reward would be to collect.

In other words: to measure the little dragon’s heart-flutter threshold.

And the threshold turned out to be… this? A head pat?

Truly. Truly the little dragon.

“Yah — oppa.” She came back to earth, executed a full purge of the drama committee, and pushed her mouth out at him. “What was that?”

“Nothing. You eat like a hamster. It was cute. I patted.” Reward collected, mood excellent, grin at maximum.

She pouted harder and gave him a long, suspicious squint — privately praying she had just read far too much into a hamster.

“…”

“Mm — SO full. Thank you for the chicken, oppa.” She patted her stomach with both hands, satisfaction radiating off her in visible waves.

“Right. Eaten, done — I’m heading down.” He packed the boxes, stood, and dusted himself off.

The mission up here had been the Good Luck Card, and the card was now in hand — obviously the next item was going home and finding out what the thing actually did.

“Oppa — you’re not staying to game?! What about duo queue?!”

“Tomorrow. Today wiped me out completely. Tomorrow we play together.”

“Chincha?! Oppa, we are AGREED on it now. You stand me up tomorrow, and I will cry at you.”

“…”

He said his goodbyes and went straight down the stairs, home.
