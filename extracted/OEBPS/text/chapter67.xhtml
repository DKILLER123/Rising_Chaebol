Chapter Sixty-Seven

Peach Is No Fool

SAME NIGHT · NEAR ELEVEN THIRTY Cheongdam-dong, Seoul f(x) company dorm — the front door

Sulli stepped out of the elevator, reached the dorm door, and started excavating her bag for keys.

A body pressed in against her back — and a pair of arms wrapped her waist from behind.

“Ah!”

She yelped, and the white roses very nearly went airborne.

A voice arrived at her ear, already laughing.

“Sulli-yah — home this late. So who, exactly, was that man?”

The voice registered, and her whole body unlocked. She twisted around — and there was a familiar, pretty face grinning at her.

“Soo-jung?! Way to scare a person!”

Krystal released her, folded her arms, tilted her head, and conducted a full survey — with a several-second stopover on the white roses.

“I didn’t scare you.” The corner of her mouth stayed bent. “I’m just curious. Isn’t Manager Kim-oppa your ride? Company rule — an idol running a schedule alone gets a manager glued to her, door to door. How come it wasn’t him tonight?”

She leaned in a step, dropping her voice into the register reserved for state secrets.

“Unless... Sulli-unnie is DATING. That man in the Mercedes — he’s your boyfriend, isn’t he?!”

Sulli’s face went up in flames. She shoved her.

“Don’t talk nonsense!”

She fumbled out her keys and got the door open. “Inside. Then we talk.”

They filed in one after the other.

The company kept f(x) in a three-bedroom apartment — all five members under one roof. It was closing on eleven thirty now, and the living room lay pitch-dark and silent.

Sulli assumed the unnies were asleep. She switched on the entryway light, set the white roses down on the shoe cabinet with great care, toed off her shoes, and padded toward the living room, asking softly as she went—

“Soo-jung-ah, why are you back tonight? Not staying over at Sica-unnie’s?”

With f(x) between comebacks, Krystal spent most of her downtime sleeping at her older sister’s villa — Jung Soo-yeon’s place.

Krystal followed her in, reached past her, and put the living-room light on. Then she dropped into the sofa like a puppet with its strings cut, and pursed her lips.

“I heard my Sulli wrapped her drama and was coming back to the dorm — so I thought I’d come see you. Surprise you.” A lift of her chin toward the entryway. “And then I got the bigger surprise instead. Now answer the question. Who was that man?”

Sulli settled onto the opposite sofa, and under direct fire, a smile broke containment.

“Hee-hee... he’s a very nice oppa I know.”

“A very nice oppa.” Krystal’s eyebrow climbed — the non-answer had been weighed and found adorable. “How nice? Drive-you-home-personally-in-a-Mercedes nice? Bring-you-a-bouquet-that-big nice?”

The interrogation was just warming up when a bedroom door opened behind them.

Click.

Victoria came out in pink cartoon pajamas, rubbing her eyes, sleep still graveling her voice.

“What ‘very nice oppa’?”

Sulli and Krystal both jumped, heads swiveling in unison.

“Vic-unnie! You’re still up?”

“Yeah, unnie — we thought you were asleep.”

Victoria shook her head, walked into the kitchen, took a bottle of juice from the fridge, cracked it, and drank a third in one go. Then she leaned a shoulder against the kitchen doorframe, rolled a flawless white-eye at the two of them, and smiled.

“It’s eleven thirty. Sleep — what sleep? For idols, the night is just getting started.”

Both of them understood her exactly. Eleven was nothing. Pre-debut, they’d danced in practice rooms well past it, every night of the week — and that was before the red-eye flights started stacking up after debut.

Her eyes settled on Sulli, and she repeated the question of the hour.

“So. Which ‘very nice oppa’ were we discussing? Since when does our Sulli know such a personage?”

Sulli hadn’t even opened her mouth yet when Krystal jumped the queue.

“Unnie, you don’t know the half of it — a man drove Sulli home just now. In a Mercedes! And he brought her that enormous bouquet of white roses!” A beat, delivered in singsong. “And Sulli says he’s a very nice oppa.”

Victoria’s gaze made one slow lap of Sulli’s face, then drifted out to the white roses parked on the shoe cabinet, and settled into something with layers.

She ambled over and took the armchair beside Sulli.

“This ‘very nice oppa’... wouldn’t happen to be the one you mentioned to me before — the oppa who wants me for an endorsement?”

Sulli hadn’t expected her to remember. She nodded, hard.

“Ne! Song Ji-yeon-oppa! Vic-unnie — oppa grew up over in America, and you came all this way from China. You both know exactly what it’s like to be the outsider here. Finding someone who understands that, in a country that isn’t yours — that’s not so easy. Won’t you reconsider the endorsement? Oppa is very sincere.”

Victoria looked at that face — the naked earnestness, the eagerness to close the deal — reached out one finger, and flicked her on the forehead. Not hard. Not soft, either.

The tone was pure why-won’t-this-iron-become-a-blade.

“How many times have I told you? Endorsement talks go through the company. That is procedure. You’re an idol — meeting brand people privately, on the side. What does that even look like?”

Her face went serious.

“And another thing. You trust this Song Ji-yeon-oppa of yours that much? How long have you even known him? A few hellos and he’s filed under ‘good person’? A man circles you this deliberately — and it never once occurs to you to ask what he’s after?”

In Victoria’s ledger, this Song Ji-yeon’s motive might as well have been wearing a name tag. First reaching her through Sulli about the endorsement; now flowers, now personal door-to-door delivery service — plainly working the most guileless member of her team as a lever toward his commercial goal.

Sulli, stung, rubbed her forehead. Then she latched onto Victoria’s arm and turned on the aegyo.

“Aigoo, Vic-unnie~ Song Ji-yeon-oppa really is a good person. He’s not what you think. Just today he agreed to manage my stock account for me!”

She’d meant it as the closing argument. The sentence landed — and Victoria’s face changed temperature in an instant.

“What?!”

Her voice spiked, and she came up off the sofa like a released spring.

“You gave him your STOCK ACCOUNT?!”

The volume knocked Sulli’s hands clean off her arm. She stood there at a loss.

Krystal had frozen too, eyes ping-ponging between the two of them.

“Sulli — are you out of your mind? You can’t just hand someone your stock account!”

Victoria was too angry to sit still. Two laps of the living room later she planted herself in front of Sulli, hands on hips — full interrogation posture.

“How much money is in it? Choi Jin-ri — was your brain caught in a door?! This is the kind of thing you DARE to do? And you’re not afraid he’ll con you out of every last won?!”

Sulli had never once seen Vic-unnie at this volume. She shrank into her shoulders — but her mouth held the line.

“Song Ji-yeon-oppa isn’t that kind of person. I believe him. Vic-unnie...” Her voice dropped low. “How can you talk about oppa like that? He’s not a con man.”

It was a small voice. The steel in it was audible to everyone in the room.

Victoria looked at that set little jaw and felt a breath jam in her chest — going neither up nor down.

She knew this child. Doughy-soft on the outside, iron all the way through. And these past two years — older, and deep in the age of rebellion — the harder you clamped, the further she bounced. Going head-to-head would only shovel her farther away.

To cut off her contact with Song Ji-yeon, then... the only angle of attack left was the man himself.

She pulled a long breath in through her nose and forced it back down. She sat. Her face smoothed out, and even her tone changed instruments.

“Sulli-yah. Unnie isn’t calling your friend a liar. Unnie is worried about you. A girl, out on her own in this world — it never hurts to keep your guard up. You understand?”

Sulli read the thaw and answered at low volume. “Mm.”

“So here’s what we’ll do.” Victoria carried on, mild as milk. “You said this Song Ji-yeon-oppa wants to discuss the endorsement with me, yes? As it happens, I have time right now — I’d like to speak with him myself. Give me his number. I’ll contact him tomorrow.”

Sulli’s whole system went on alert.

This peach was no fool — she could read Vic-unnie’s play without subtitles. The number would be used for exactly one thing: calling Song Ji-yeon-oppa and telling him to keep his distance.

She refused without spending a single second on deliberation.

“No.”
