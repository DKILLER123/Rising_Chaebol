Chapter Fifty

Oppa, I Believe in You — Fighting!

SAME MORNING Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Song Ji-yeon’s apartment

Park Ji-yeon moving out — that was genuinely news to him. But with Eun-jung’s explanation, it clicked into place. Terminate with the company, then keep squatting in the company dorm? Yes, that would be poor form.

He simply hadn’t imagined that one decision of his would butterfly-effect the maknae into the girl left behind. In the last life she’d at least had Hyo-min for company. In this one she was on her own — poor kid. And frankly, the thought of the little dragon living alone put a low hum of worry under his morning.

“Where were you thinking of looking?” He’d turned it over; the whole thing had started with him, so at minimum he owed the question.

“Nowhere yet. Gangnam somewhere, probably — closer to the company, easier for schedules.” Eun-jung rubbed her temples, the house-hunting headache already visible.

“Perfect — Gangnam I know.” He stood and took his jacket off the hook. “Let me come along. Another pair of eyes, and someone to talk you out of bad floor plans.”

His reasoning was simple: come the near future, all six of T-ara would be on his payroll — which made their maknae’s housing at least partly a company matter.

Eun-jung looked up at him, hesitating. She’d just gotten off a plane from Hong Kong; hadn’t seen her own home yet; had come straight here for the dog — and now she was going to drag the man out apartment-hunting on top of it? It felt like too much of him.

“It’s fine, we can manage by ourselves.” She tried anyway. “Didn’t your company just sign a lease? You must be run off your feet.”

“Not busy at all.” A wave of the hand, spoken with total entitlement. “The stores are open and the store managers run them. My entire job description as Sajangnim is to stay out of the way.”

He was already changing his shoes mid-sentence.

“Let’s go — daylight’s burning. The sooner she has a place, the sooner your maknae can stop chewing on it.”

The tone accepted no rebuttals, and the rest of her sentence died quietly in her throat. She watched him, and something warm moved through her. This man — the mouth never spared anyone, and yet the hands were always doing something like this.

“Then… all right.” In the end she nodded, and stopped refusing him.

“That’s the spirit.” In a visibly improved mood, he bent and scooped up Mushroom, still mid-roll on the rug. “Little guy — you’re coming too.”

✦ ✦ ✦

MINUTES LATER The Mercedes, en route Cheongdam-dong → Nonhyeon-dong

One man, one woman, one dog, out the door and into the familiar Mercedes. The car pulled smoothly out of the complex and merged into Cheongdam traffic.

Eun-jung rode shotgun with Mushroom in her arms — Mushroom, for his part, doing his level best to get over the center console and into the driver’s seat.

“He really does adore you.” She said it sideways, not-quite-accusing, at the man driving.

“Well, what can I tell you.” Eyes on the road. “Your little overlord shadows me from room to room. I can’t use the bathroom without him standing guard at the door — apparently he’s worried I’ll fall in. Yesterday, for the storefront meeting, I had to slip out ninja-style while he wasn’t looking.”

It was an exaggeration; she laughed anyway.

“Aigoo, listen to you. Completely dog-whipped.”

“That word is for people.” A sniff. Then, aggrieved, he went on. “Yesterday I teased him exactly once — didn’t give him bread — and he took a grudge. Spent the whole afternoon pointing his butt at me… I had to cook the little ancestor a proper dinner before he forgave me.”

She pictured the scene, and laughed harder.

“Good. Serves you right for bullying him.”

“Bullying? That was me doing you a favor. Quality control — keeping him from eating things he shouldn’t.” The picture of innocence.

And so they went, line for line, the mood easy and unforced — until, soon enough, the car drew up outside T-ara’s dorm in Nonhyeon-dong.

He parked, and Eun-jung called Park Ji-yeon. Barely two minutes later, a whirlwind burst out of the entrance.

The girl wore an oversized hoodie over denim shorts, a pair of long straight legs on full display. Her face was bare — and still the fine bones and the sheer pressure of youth came right through it.

T-ara’s little-dragon maknae, Park Ji-yeon.

She yanked the car door open and dropped into the back seat — and clocked the driver.

“Huh? Song Ji-yeon-oppa? What are you doing here?” Her eyes lit instantly, familiar and delighted; clearly she hadn’t expected to find him behind the wheel.

“Ji-yeon-ah. Long time no see.” He found her in the rearview mirror and smiled.

“Long time no see, oppa!” Sweet-mouthed as ever, she scooted forward and draped herself over the back of his seat. “So why are you with Eun-jung-unnie, hmm? Don’t tell me you two are actually dating.”

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon! What nonsense!” Red-faced, Eun-jung twisted around to glare at her maknae.

Tongue out, cross-eyed face at her — and then straight back to Song Ji-yeon, expectation dialed to maximum, waiting.

“Not yet.” Unhurried, he said it — and slid one meaningful glance at Eun-jung.

Eun-jung went redder. As far as she could tell, the cabin temperature had just jumped several degrees. This Song Ji-yeon — now he’s joining the maknae’s nonsense too?

The maknae, however, found the answer deeply satisfactory. She gave his seat an approving pat.

“Oppa, I believe in you — Fighting!”

He laughed, about to start the car — and then remembered something, turning his head.

“Ji-yeon-ah. Any requirements for the new place? Budget, roughly?”

She tilted her head and thought it over.

“Gangnam somewhere works. Close to the company would be best, nice environment, security’s got to be solid. Budget-wise… deposit up to ₩800 million, monthly ₩7–8 million is fine. I’m living alone — I want to live well.”

Breezy. As if an eight-hundred-million-won deposit and ₩8 million a month were loose change.

In the driver’s seat, Song Ji-yeon’s eyebrows quietly climbed. Good grief. Popular member of a trending girl group — the private savings have to be real. Here I am scratching my head raw over one storefront’s rent, and this girl quotes ₩7–8 million a month without so much as a blink. Such is the spread of human lives.

For anyone struggling to picture those numbers, allow an illustration: her stated budget could comfortably rent the flat Song Ji-yeon was currently living in.

A beat — and a new idea surfaced.

“Ji-yeon-ah — my complex is nice. Security’s strict, everything’s card access, Cheongdam’s shopping district is right downstairs, and it’s ten minutes’ drive from your company. Want to think about renting there?” He kept it casual, tossed off like an afterthought.

“Really? Which complex do you live in, oppa?” Her eyes flew alight.

“Cheongdam Daewoo Royal County, Phase Five — 30, Dosan-daero 100-gil, Cheongdam-dong, Gangnam-gu. Mine’s a first-floor flat; the light and views aren’t the best, but it comes with a private courtyard, and personally I like it. And I happen to know there’s a show flat sitting on the second floor. First and second floors don’t move well with buyers, so it’s stood empty a long while. I can ask the sales rep for you.”

Warmer and warmer to the idea, Park Ji-yeon twisted toward the front.

“Unnie — what do you think?”

Eun-jung was a little surprised he’d floated it himself; she turned the layout of his place over in her head and found no complaints.

“Cheongdam-dong really is convenient. And Song Ji-yeon lives there — if anything comes up, someone’s close by.”

“Then it’s settled!” A clap of the hands, pure excitement. “Oppa, let’s go see your complex right now! And give us the grand tour of your place — I’ve never even seen what a big flat looks like inside!”

Her point-blank manner amused him.

“Sure. Grand tour first.”

He started the engine, swung the car around, and pointed it back toward Cheongdam-dong.
