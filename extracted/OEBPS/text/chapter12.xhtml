Chapter Twelve

A Familiar Girl

SAME NIGHT · 10 P.M. Gangnam-gu, Seoul SAVORY F&B · FINANCE DEPARTMENT

Song Ji-yeon shouldered open the finance department’s glass door with a carrier of ten iced americanos. Across the whole floor, this was the only room still lit.

Finance wasn’t a small team — the director plus his staff came to ten.

Director Kang Jin-ho was bent over his computer, the monitor’s cold white light carving his crow’s feet into high relief. Forty this year; close to twenty of those years spent at SAVORY. At the sound of the door he looked up and pushed his gold wire-rims back along his nose.

“Sajangnim. You came in?”

“Wasn’t going to sleep anyway.” He smiled, setting an americano down at each desk. “Thank you all for the grind tonight. Get through today and I’ll give everyone a day off — tonight’s the last hard push, and I know it’s asking a lot.”

“You’re a lifesaver — thank you for the americano, Sajangnim!”

“Ne! It’s nothing, Sajangnim!”

“Sajangnim — how many days off are we talking?”

…

He let the chatter run for a moment, smiling, then committed. “Everyone burns the midnight oil on the data tonight, and everyone gets a day off — plus Saturday and Sunday. Three days.”

“DAEBAK!” Nine employees, thrilled to the marrow — three-day weekends did not grow on trees — and loud enough to lift the ceiling. “Long live Sajangnim!”

The last americano went down on Director Kang’s desk. Ji-yeon dragged a chair over beside him, sat, and loosened his collar a notch. “So. Are the numbers out?”

“Not yet — the dinner rush isn’t over. Final figures come after midnight.” Kang tipped his chin at the wall-to-wall spreadsheets. “I’m running all seven stores separately. Once we’re past twelve, we’ll see the company’s first full day of revenue.”

Today was ONGI’s first day in its new skin. In his first life he’d watched the service-first model win in market after market — but until the numbers actually landed, he couldn’t swear it wouldn’t faceplant on Korean soil.

Which was the whole reason he’d driven over — to be in the room the moment the answer existed.

The office settled. Keyboard clatter, the hum of the AC, nothing else.

“I’ll rest my eyes. Wake me when the report’s up.”

“Ne, Sajangnim!”

He leaned back into the chair and let his gaze fall out the window.

Ten o’clock at night, and Seoul was lit to the horizon — distant neon cooking half the sky a dull burnt orange. For no reason he could name, he wanted to photograph the skyline and send it to Eun-jung.

Her message arrived first.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Ham Eun-jung

Did you make it to the office?

Song Ji-yeon

Just got in. Now we wait for the numbers.

Photo sent — the Gangnam street below, still glowing at ten p.m.

Ham Eun-jung

Ohh… then what time will you be finished?

Song Ji-yeon

Around midnight, probably. A few late orders are still trickling in.

Ham Eun-jung

[Sticker — a small cartoon bear, patting its own back]

You’ve worked hard!

He smiled and didn’t reply. No money on this earth was ever earned comfortably. And honestly — what had he even done? He was the decision layer. His entire day consisted of sitting in an office signing papers.

✦ ✦ ✦

12:30 A.M. SAVORY F&B FINANCE DEPARTMENT · THE DAY-ONE LEDGER

Half past midnight, the finance report was final.

“It’s out.” Kang’s voice reeled him up out of shallow sleep. Ji-yeon sat up straight and looked at the screen.

On the spreadsheet, the last line of numbers had just landed. Kang stared at the string of digits for several seconds. Blinked behind his lenses. Blinked again. Then he took the glasses off, polished them on the hem of his jacket, and put them back on.

“Sajangnim.” His voice had gone slightly unsteady. “You’d better look at this yourself.”

Ji-yeon leaned in.

ONGI HOTPOT · DAY ONE LEDGER

Total revenue, seven stores₩105,487,000

Average per store≈ ₩15,070,000

Gangnam flagship₩16,200,000

Gwanak store₩14,800,000

Mapo store₩14,470,000

Net profit per store, after food, labor, utilities, building fees & depreciation≈ ₩1,200,000

He blinked, and turned to Kang. “Fifteen million?”

“Yes.” Kang drew a long breath and scrolled down. “The Gangnam flagship is highest — sixteen-two. Gwanak, fourteen-eight. Mapo, fourteen-four-seven…” A pause, and he pulled up a second sheet. “And after food costs, labor, utilities, building fees, and depreciation, today’s net profit is this number — one point two million won.”

The cursor sat parked on it. Per store. On average.

Ji-yeon looked at the figure, cross-checked it against the memory of his first life, and nodded. The stone he’d been carrying all evening finally set itself down.

One day. ₩1.2 million net per store — barely a thousand US dollars. Not a number that impresses anybody at a dinner party. But this was day one. Day one — no reputation, no regulars, no reviews anywhere online. Pure opening promotion and street traffic, and the seven stores had still turned their tables nearly three full cycles.

Beside him, Kang was already running the math out loud, excitement leaking through every seam of his professionalism. “Sajangnim — today was opening day. The promotions ran deep, some dishes went out at cost, so net margin is under ten percent. Once the promotions end and full pricing resumes — same traffic — net profit at minimum doubles. And judging by tonight’s table-turn rate, once word of mouth starts compounding on the portals and the social platforms… I’d estimate next month’s revenue doubles. At least.”

Ji-yeon was happy too. If ONGI held this pace, he had full confidence: every district in Seoul within a year, the whole Peninsula market soon after — and SAVORY listed on the KRX inside three years.

With the weight off his chest he stood and clapped his hands. “Finance team — thank you for tonight. A hundred-thousand-won bonus for each of you, paid out with this month’s salary. Wrap up, go home, and enjoy your three days.”

“Sajangnim, long live!”

“Loyalty!!”

…

The congratulations wound down. He left a couple of final instructions, stretched, swung his jacket on, and picked up his phone. “It’s late, Director Kang. Finish up and get home yourself.”

“Ne, Sajangnim — let me walk you out.”

“Not necessary. Pack up, go home.” Ji-yeon waved him off, but Kang had already lifted his suit jacket off the chair back, set on it. Forty years old, and the finance director walked with a back like a yardstick, gold rims flashing under the corridor’s motion-sensor lights, the excitement of the report still not fully drained out of his face.

Ji-yeon gave up and let him come. The two of them left the finance department together — and at the ground-floor lobby, Ji-yeon’s gaze was hooked, mid-step, by a figure over on the lounge sofa.

✦ ✦ ✦

PAST ONE SAVORY F&B GROUND-FLOOR LOBBY · LOUNGE

Someone was curled up on the leather sofa. A young girl, lying on her side with a thin denim jacket draped over her, face buried in the crook of her arm — half a cheek showing, and a spill of long hair. She was deep asleep, breathing slow and even, entirely undisturbed by the approaching footsteps. On the coffee table in front of her sat a takeout box, half finished, chopsticks resting across the rim — and beside it, a milk tea drunk all the way down to the bottom.

Kang saw her too. He frowned, crossed over quickly, and stood at the edge of the sofa. Then he bent down and tapped her shoulder gently, voice pitched low, carrying that particular gentleness only parents have. “Seul-gi. Wake up. You can’t sleep here — you’ll catch cold.”

Two taps. Her shoulder shifted. A shapeless murmur rose out of the dream; she curled deeper into her arm and mumbled something no one could parse.

“Wake up now.” Two more taps, firmer.

She finally surfaced. Her head rose slowly, one hand scrubbing at her eyes, hair hanging over her face in a wild curtain. The lobby light was dim to begin with, and she sat backlit on top of it — her face almost entirely in shadow. She blinked, groggy, working out who exactly was standing over her.

Ji-yeon stood two steps behind Kang, his gaze grazing across the girl’s face without meaning to.

He couldn’t have said why. But the instant her head came up, something in him flickered — a blurry, secondhand familiarity, as if he’d seen her somewhere before. And had absolutely no idea where, or when, or under what circumstances.

She looked about eighteen. Even, tidy features — not the kind of face that stops traffic — plus the freshly-woken daze of a puppy roused mid-nap. She scrubbed her face with both hands, finally brought the people in front of her into focus, and said, still half in the dream, voice soft: “Appa… you’re off work.”

Kang nodded, and put the sternness into his voice on purpose. “Didn’t I tell you to go home and rest early? How did you fall asleep here?”

Ji-yeon drew his gaze back. From the exchange, the situation assembled itself: the girl had to be Kang’s daughter — and she must have come to the office to fetch her father during the stretch he’d spent dozing.

“Director Kang.” His voice was easy and even, with not a trace of official weight in it. “Your daughter’s here to collect you — go straight home. There’s nothing left at the office anyway.”

Kang agreed with a nod and bent to gather his daughter’s things.

“Hello there. I’m Song Ji-yeon — I run this place.” One last look at the still-rebooting girl, and a smile went with the greeting.

Kang Seul-gi finished rebooting at last. Confronted with the smiling man greeting her, she went a little shy — but her answer still came out bright and crisp. “Hello, Sajangnim.”

Greetings exchanged, Ji-yeon didn’t linger. He said his goodnights to the two of them, walked out of the building, and drove for home.
