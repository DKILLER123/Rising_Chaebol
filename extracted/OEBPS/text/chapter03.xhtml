Chapter Three

Sulli

THE NEXT MORNING Seoul Song Ji-yeon’s apartment — breakfast of instant noodles

Ji-yeon was up early. He opened the Peninsula Bank app, stared at the screen for a long moment, and let out a slow, deliberate breath.

The balance read a little over ₩4 billion.

That was what remained of the ₩10 billion his father had handed him for the Korean launch. Six billion had been swallowed by renovating the seven ONGI locations; four billion was still breathing.

And the reason he was up staring at it at the crack of dawn: he was going shopping. The item in question was CCM Entertainment.

If his memory held, Kim Kwang-soo had bought CCM outright for ₩2.1 billion — a left-pocket-to-right-pocket shuffle that had cost the man essentially nothing.

Ji-yeon’s war chest covered that price tag several times over.

Two reasons to buy. First: assemble all six members of the Crown, trigger the “Jewel of the Crown” bond, collect the Kakao equity. Second: turn T-ara’s reputation around. He had absolutely no intention of standing by in this life and watching the girl group he’d stanned get shredded the way it had last time.

But there was no rush. Move on CCM today and that old fox Kim Kwang-soo would smell intent and open with an astronomical number. Let him enjoy a few more days of prancing.

The ₩4 billion, meanwhile, had a real job in front of it.

Samsung Electronics’ chart was sitting in the Handbook’s space, gathering dust. Not milking that for everything it was worth would be an insult to Eun-jung — she was, at this very moment, the only reason he had it.

Washed up, he wandered into the kitchen, dumped a brick of instant noodles into a pot, and lit the burner.

Honestly, the Peninsula’s food scene had been oversold to him. It was hanwoo this, budae jjigae that — he’d burned out on all of it inside a month. At this point the instant noodles were outperforming the restaurants.

While the pot burbled, he leaned a hip against the counter and scrolled through a folder on his phone titled ONGI.

Design drafts. Supplier quotes. Staff résumés. A first-pass menu. Page after page of dense little files — a month of his life, assembled one night at a time.

Location: locked. Supply chain: stable. Kitchen team: solid.

By his own audit, all three pillars were set. The one unknown left was the customer’s tongue.

Last life, China’s hotpot juggernaut hadn’t had it easy here either — its first Seoul flagship opened in 2014, and for a long while the clientele was mostly Chinese expats and tourists. Locals? Scarce.

That was the exact trap ONGI was built to sidestep: six soup bases tuned for the Peninsula palate, a spread broad enough that anyone could find a door in. On paper, every condition for success was checked off. His stomach, apparently, hadn’t received the memo.

He shook it off, tasted the broth — a touch salty — and added a splash of water.

His phone buzzed. Park Seon-young, SAVORY’s planning director.

KakaoTalk · Park Seon-young (Planning Director)

Park Seon-young

Sajangnim, we have a hiring session at 2 p.m. — 180 applicants. Will you be coming in person?

Song Ji-yeon

Something came up this afternoon. You have full authority.

Standards as I sent before — service instinct first. Clean, tidy look. Restaurant experience preferred.

Park Seon-young

Understood. Leave everything to me, Sajangnim.

The reply had come back in under ten seconds, by the way. The man was efficient.

Seven ONGI locations across Seoul, all renovated, upstream supply chain threaded through. The last variable was staff. The plan: twenty servers and six kitchen staff per store, split across morning and evening shifts — one hundred and eighty-six hires in total. With Park Seon-young on it, that number would take care of itself.

He set the phone down and ate his noodles over the Samsung Electronics chart.

The system’s graph was etched into his brain with laser precision: through September, Samsung Electronics would grind out a strong, steady climb — starting just over ₩1.12 million a share, 1.7 percent at a time, four straight weeks of it.

Last life, he’d traded stocks. Final tally: lost everything down to his underwear.

This life came with the answer key attached.

The Play — Samsung Electronics

Capital₩4,000,000,000

LeverageYes. Aggressively.

WindowThree weeks

Projected return+₩4,000,000,000 — capital doubled

House viewThe closest thing to free money the universe has ever offered anyone.

He drained the last of the soup, dropped the bowl in the sink, changed into a crisp shirt and slacks, grabbed the car keys, and went.

Early September had already put a chill into the air; pedestrians hurried past in thin puffer jackets. Ji-yeon took his brand-new Mercedes first to the Gangnam branch of Peninsula Bank.

Account opening, forms, identity verification — the full gauntlet, done in under forty minutes.

The teller glanced at his paperwork, then up at him, and something flickered across her face — surprise, hastily filed away. A twenty-two-year-old with ₩4 billion settling into his account. Anyone would have looked twice. She looked three times.

“Song Ji-yeon-ssi — you’re certain you’d like to transfer the full amount into this account?”

“Certain.”

“And the stated purpose of the funds?”

“Securities investment.”

She nodded and asked nothing further. Sufficient — and perfectly legal.

Forty minutes later, ₩4 billion lay quietly in his brand-new account.

(The new card was strategy. His old one was a supplementary card under his father — open a brokerage account with that, and every transaction would ping Dad’s phone. Ji-yeon loved his father sincerely. He did not love the idea of the ₩4-billion interrogation.)

He came out of the bank, stretched on the front steps, and was mapping the drive to the brokerage when his phone buzzed again.

KakaoTalk. Ham Eun-jung.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Ham Eun-jung

Ji-yeon-ssi, I still feel terrible about yesterday. I couldn’t sleep last night thinking about it… is your arm all right?

Song Ji-yeon

Arm’s fine. Not even a bruise. You, on the other hand — that’s a serious grip for a girl. You trained?

Ham Eun-jung

Ne… some taekwondo. I’m really sorry!!

[a cartoon cat bowing in apology]

Song Ji-yeon

Told you — all forgiven. Oh, right: the restaurant officially opens in about four days. Soft opening in two. If you’re free, come by — I’ll feed you something good.

Ham Eun-jung

No, no, no — we agreed, I’M treating YOU.

Song Ji-yeon

Fine. You treat. I supply the ingredients.

Ham Eun-jung

Yah! How is that treating?!!!

[a cartoon tiger throwing a full tantrum]

He grinned at the tiger and didn’t reply.

This era was Eun-jung at peak ripeness, honestly — the best-by date on this level of dorky-sweet ran maybe two, three more years. Past that, no force on earth could tease it back out of her.

He pocketed the phone and drove toward Yeouido.

✦ ✦ ✦

LATER THAT DAY Korea Exchange Yeouido, Seoul

The trading hall wasn’t crowded. Red and green numbers flickered and rolled across the boards. Ji-yeon found the new-account window, stated his business, and received a joyous stack of forms.

2012: still no online account opening. You wanted to invest, you showed up in person, you filled out paper, you linked your bank card, and you were grateful for the privilege.

He was head-down in the paperwork when a bright voice landed just behind him.

“Um… excuse me — is this the window for individual investors?”

He turned on reflex. And stopped.

Behind him stood a young woman in a pale baseball cap and enormous glasses — bare-faced, swimming in a beige hoodie, bundled up like a hamster in a sock. She was clutching a canvas tote with a cartoon cat printed on it, and she looked mildly terrified.

Even so, he clocked her in under a second.

Sulli.

f(x)’s Sulli.

His first thought: seriously? Is this week haunted? Another girl-group member?

His second thought: what on earth is Sulli doing at a stock exchange? Alone?

He returned his eyes to his forms, face neutral. The Handbook hadn’t indexed her. They’d never met. There was no earthly reason to go starting conversations.
