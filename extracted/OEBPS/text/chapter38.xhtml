Chapter Thirty-Eight

Binding

SAME AFTERNOON · CONTINUOUS Yeouido, Han River Park To the Beautiful You — location set

The girl in his arms was soft and warm and smelled faintly of peach, and Song Ji-yeon registered approximately none of it.

Because that other feeling — the unmistakable, long-missed sensation of the system’s chime asserting dominion over his brain — had his pulse up far worse than the girl did.

He looked at Sulli’s face, flushed scarlet with shock and embarrassment. Then, in his mind’s eye, at the Tenfold Rebate Card glowing its faint, patient blue. Then he sank, wholeheartedly, into thought.

So what, exactly, was the definition of “contact”?

He’d pinky-promised this girl. He’d ruffled her hair — repeatedly, expertly. He had patted her forehead, the finishing move of finishing moves — and the Goddess Handbook hadn’t so much as cleared its throat.

Today he’d caught her by accident, one arm, maybe two seconds of contact — and the reward landed in his lap?

Unless… the surface area of contact had to be large enough?

His gaze performed one entirely involuntary lap of Choi Sulli before he caught himself and shook the thought out of his head like a man shooing a fly out of the car.

No. Absolutely not. Delete.

“Oppa?”

A small voice. She was still in his hands, and he had been holding on — and holding on — with the facial expression of a man receiving telemetry from orbit. Her ears had gone red enough to fry an egg on.

“Ah — sorry. Nothing.” He surfaced, released her shoulders, and took a full step back, restoring between them a distance that was safe, sanitary, and above all lawyer-approved.

“You okay? Didn’t roll an ankle?”

“I— n-no. I’m fine.” Her voice had shrunk to the size of a mosquito’s. That collision had been thorough — she’d been buried against his chest, nose full of the clean, soapy smell of him, and her heartbeat still hadn’t gotten the memo that the emergency was over.

“Stupid cables.” She glared down at the ground, muttering, mostly to herself. “Who leaves them lying around in a heap like this…”

He followed her glare. The grass was webbed with cable runs — power for the fill lights, the cameras, the recorders, all of it braided into trip hazards in half a dozen places. For a person who watched her feet, manageable. For a certified klutz of Choi Sulli’s caliber, a minefield.

“You klutz.” He gave her a look. “Never again, understood? If I hadn’t been standing right here, you’d have eaten that pavement face-first.”

✦ ✦ ✦

A short distance away, Kim Ji-won stood with a script hugged to her chest, and she was watching all of it.

Her brows drew together a fraction. The wariness she kept for Song Ji-yeon ticked up by several more degrees. That catch had happened fast — too fast to judge. From where she stood, what it looked like was less “accident” and more “the young Sajangnim, manufacturing a hero-catches-the-girl moment with excellent reflexes and suspicious timing.”

Choi Sulli was a child. A sweet, trusting child. What chance did a child stand against a practiced player with a company and a wardrobe?

Kim Ji-won sighed inwardly and made a quiet resolution: after wrap today, she was taking Sulli aside for a proper talk. A word to the wise. Keep a polite distance from the Sajangnim, hmm? There was no such thing as a good chaebol heir — none—

A screech of metal tore across the set.

One of the lighting techs, adjusting a huge floor fill light, had slipped somehow — and the sandbag anchoring the stand had shifted loose. Nearly two meters of top-heavy metal stand wobbled once, twice, groaning, and toppled — directly toward where Kim Ji-won was standing.

“LOOK OUT!”

The assistant director’s shout detonated across the set.

Kim Ji-won had still been watching Sulli and the Sajangnim. By the time she turned, the mass was already falling, filling her entire field of vision, and her pupils shrank to pinpricks.

I’m dead. I’m toast. Forget Choi Sulli, forget chaebol heirs — I am never minding anyone’s business again—

Everything after that happened inside a single bolt of lightning.

Song Ji-yeon moved before thought — the 2.5×-enhanced body simply detonating off the mark. Ten meters, three strides, a blur crossing the corner of her eye; his left arm locked around her waist, his right hand cupped over her head, and the world spun — grass, sky, grass — as he hurled them both sideways onto the lawn.

CRASH.

The stand slammed into the ground close enough that he could feel the impact through his shoes — half a meter from his toes, no more. The entire set went dead silent. Every single person stood exactly where the noise had frozen them.

Song Ji-yeon released the shaking girl, rose, and turned. His face had gone dark enough to wring water out of.

He looked once at the fallen stand. Once at the lighting tech, whose face had drained to chalk. And then he asked, perfectly levelly:

“Is this how your production handles safety?”

The director and assistant director came scrambling over at a dead sprint and bowed so deeply and so repeatedly that they looked like those drinking-bird toys.

“Mianhae — Song Ji-yeon-ssi! This was our oversight, entirely ours!”

“We are deeply, truly sorry! Are you all right — you and Ji-won both?!”

Sulli finally rebooted. She looked at the metal tower lying in the grass, and her calves started to shake. If that had come down on Ji-won-unnie — if Song Ji-yeon-oppa hadn’t been standing there—

Kim Ji-won sat where she’d landed, pale to the lips, muscles still locked. Her mind was a blank with a single clip playing on loop: his speed. The force of the arm that had pulled her out of the light stand’s path. Over and over.

She watched Song Ji-yeon’s back as he stood over the wreckage — and the careful little tower of labels she’d built for him, chaebol heir on top, came down all at once. What filled the space instead was something reliable. Something strong. Something that made a corner of her chest feel, absurdly, safe.

“Song Ji-yeon-ssi — here, some water, steady your nerves.” The assistant director materialized with a bottle, both hands offering it. “This accident was just that, an accident. Thank heavens you’re unharmed.”

Song Ji-yeon stared at him.

Not to be difficult, but there is a girl still sitting on the ground. You’re thanking the heavens for MY health? Shouldn’t the first of your concern go to your own actress?

Something in him genuinely flared hot. What kind of food chain was this industry running? Idols ranked near the bottom — fine, he knew that, everyone knew that. But an actress too? One of the leads of this very production, still on the grass, and the water goes to the visiting businessman?

He took the bottle without a word of thanks, cracked the cap open, turned, and crouched to press it into Kim Ji-won’s hand.

“You okay? Not hurt anywhere? Here — drink. Steady yourself.”

She obeyed before she had decided to obey. Their fingertips brushed in the handoff — his hand steady, his palm dry and warm — and her heart, entirely without permission, dropped a beat.

And in that exact instant, at the back of Song Ji-yeon’s mind, the Goddess Handbook — silent for weeks — burst into golden light.

A brand-new page turned itself.

Goddess Handbook — Activation

GoddessKim Ji-won (actress)

Activation RewardSaero Zero-Sugar Soju — full production technology

Goddess AbilityOne Healing Card per week — collect in person, through contact with Goddess Kim Ji-won

Goddess Bond“The Queen” — a bond of one

Upon the throne, only one may sit. This bond can be forged with no other goddess, joined to no other set. Activate it to obtain the underlying source code of TikTok.

Song Ji-yeon stood there, stunned, staring at Kim Ji-won as she took a small sip of water.

Sunlight on her profile; her cheek faintly flushed; one last unbrushed teardrop still hanging on her lashes. It was, objectively, a very good profile. He barely saw it.

So…

The key to binding wasn’t touch at all. It ran on something else — affection? Or some honest, overwhelming connection of the heart?

He looked at the eyes Kim Ji-won turned up at him — gratitude in them, and apology, and a thin thread of open curiosity — and thought very hard about what he’d just felt pulse through the handshake.

Choi Sulli, meanwhile, had noticed that the Sajangnim appeared to have zero intention of helping her unnie up off the ground — he was just standing there, conducting invisible math — so she scrambled over herself and got her arms under Ji-won’s.

“Unnie! Are you okay? That scared me half to death — good thing Song Ji-yeon-oppa was here!”
