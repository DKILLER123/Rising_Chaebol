Chapter Seven

Jeon Bo-ram, the Group’s Pushover

SEPTEMBER 2012 Seoul The three days before opening

The three days vanished in a blink. Apart from the occasional glance at Samsung Electronics, Song Ji-yeon practically lived at the office.

Samsung stayed lukewarm — the price seesawed back and forth inside a narrow band, moving too little to be worth feeling anything about.

He wasn’t in any rush. The smartphone wave hadn’t truly made landfall yet; the chain reaction up and down the industry still needed time to build.

So each morning went the same way: open the trading app, one glance, confirm nothing strange, close it, get back to work.

Away from the trading screen, his one source of entertainment those days was chatting with Eun-jung on KakaoTalk.

Ever since they’d swapped IDs outside PIEN PLUS, the thread had never once gone quiet.

In the beginning it had been Eun-jung, all careful apologies, asking when the restaurant planned to open. By now they had graduated to trading pieces of their ordinary days.

It wasn’t that Eun-jung had fallen for him. It was that T-ara was being boycotted by the entire internet at the moment; friends in the industry were keeping their distance, and contact had thinned to almost nothing. She understood why. It still stung. Song Ji-yeon had appeared at exactly the right moment to become her hollow tree — that one safe place where she could whisper both the bitterness and the joy.

Eun-jung texted precisely the way she talked: every conversation opened in courteous, respectful honorifics, and five exchanges in she’d forget them entirely. Occasionally she’d make a typo, unsend it within a heartbeat, and follow up with a sticker radiating pure embarrassment.

He found it quietly delightful. They talked about nothing at all, really.

What choreography she’d drilled that day. What she’d eaten. Which café was good. Whether the new tteokbokki place downstairs from her was worth the hype.

Eun-jung had a very pure, uncomplicated passion for food. The moment the subject turned to something delicious, she’d fire off three or four messages in a row, exclamation points baked into every syllable.

Sometimes he teased her on purpose — that some barbecue joint in Seoul had the best hangjeongsal — pork skirt — in all of Gangnam, say. She’d demand the address instantly, then add, sheepish, not that I’m going right now.

You could say the two of them had become friends.

✦ ✦ ✦

SEPTEMBER 2012 · OPENING DAY T-ara’s Dorm, Seoul Living room, late afternoon

Meanwhile, in the T-ara dorm, Ham Eun-jung sat curled cross-legged on the sofa, thumbs flying over her screen, the faint ghost of a smile on her lips — one she hadn’t noticed herself wearing.

“Unnie.”

Park Ji-yeon was sitting cross-legged on the rug, a bag of chips in one fist, head tilted, and she had been studying Eun-jung for a long, long time.

“Eun-jung-unnie. What is going on with you lately?”

Eun-jung didn’t even look up.

“What’s ‘going on’ supposed to mean?”

“This.”

Park Ji-yeon copied her expression — a wildly exaggerated, syrupy smile aimed at the empty air — then dropped her head and mimed typing.

“Like this. Then this. And then this… who are you texting?”

At the far end of the sofa, Jeon Bo-ram had been leafing through a magazine. She looked up too, pushed her glasses up her nose, and peered over the rims with the air of a judge.

Eun-jung instinctively flipped her phone screen-down against her chest.

“Nobody. Just a friend.”

“A friend.” Park Ji-yeon set the chips down, dusted off her hands, and sprang up off the rug with a freakishly flexible twist, planting herself at the edge of the sofa. Her eyes were gleaming now, a cat that had cornered something small and delicious. “What kind of ‘friend’ makes you smile like that? We’ve known you how many years? Since when do you use exclamation points on us?”

“I do NOT use exclamation points!!!”

Jeon Bo-ram turned a page at her own unhurried pace.

“You do. I saw. Three of them.”

Eun-jung’s face went red at a speed visible to the naked eye.

Watching that, Park Ji-yeon’s smile curdled into pure villainy. She threw an arm around Eun-jung’s shoulders, leaned in close to her ear, and dropped her voice low and sweet.

“Unnie. Confession earns leniency. Who is he? How did you meet? How far has it gone? Have you held hands? Is he handsome? How old? What does he do?”

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon!”

Eun-jung shrank back under the machine-gun barrage, the tips of her ears going pink to match her cheeks.

“What is this, a household-registry interview?”

“An interview? Please.” Park Ji-yeon looked magnificently righteous. “I could go apply for a search warrant right now. Right, Bo-ram-unnie?”

Jeon Bo-ram closed her magazine, pushed up her glasses, and nodded with tremendous gravity.

“As a witness to the incident, I am prepared to testify in court.”

Tag-teamed into speechlessness, Eun-jung pulled a cushion over her face. Her voice came out muffled from behind it.

“He’s really just a friend… he runs a restaurant. He did me a favor once. That’s how we met.”

“He runs a restaurant?” Park Ji-yeon’s eyes lit up another fifty lumens. “Which restaurant? Can we go eat there?”

“It’s not open yet.”

“Oh-ho~”

Park Ji-yeon drew the syllable out forever, traded a deeply meaningful look with Jeon Bo-ram, and patted Eun-jung’s shoulder like a doctor delivering a diagnosis.

“Unnie. You’re done for.”

“Done for how?”

“It’s not even open and you’re already defending the place.” One finger rose, wagging back and forth with great ceremony. “Symptom one. Symptom two: that blushing speed just now was not normal. Symptom three: you still haven’t denied my question about how far this has gone.”

Eun-jung lowered the cushion and glared at her. Her lips moved several times. Finally, from somewhere deep in her soul, she produced:

“…Your chips are going stale.”

Park Ji-yeon glanced down at the chip she had been holding this entire time, grinned, and popped it in her mouth.

“Stale chips, happy me,” she said through the crunch. “After all these years, my unnie’s old tree is finally flowering.”

“YAH, Park Ji-yeon!”

Jeon Bo-ram chuckled softly beside them and turned back to her magazine.

“Mm. The old tree really is blooming at last,” she murmured. “Shame nobody knows for whom.”

Bo-ram hadn’t even finished the sentence before Park Ji-yeon launched herself off the rug like a starving tiger — and her target wasn’t Eun-jung. It was the gleeful witness on the sofa, the one who had been enjoying the show a little too much.

“What did Bo-ram-unnie just say, hmm?”

She seized Bo-ram’s arm and began dragging her toward Eun-jung, grinning ear to ear.

“She chimed in! She did! Bo-ram-unnie is my accomplice!”

Caught completely off guard, Bo-ram lost her magazine to the air. Her glasses slid to the tip of her nose as she scrambled backward.

“Yah! I was stating facts! What do facts have to do with accomplices—”

“Stating facts makes you an accessory!” Park Ji-yeon was radiantly righteous. She twisted around to yell at Eun-jung: “Eun-jung-unnie, why are you just sitting there?! If you get Bo-ram-unnie, you can’t get me!”

Eun-jung — still blushing into her cushion — blinked at that. Then her gaze began, very slowly, to slide toward Jeon Bo-ram.

Bo-ram read something deeply ominous in that gaze and started waving both hands.

“Wait, wait — Eun-jung-ah, I’m on your side… Park Ji-yeon, you stop stirring discord this instant—yah!”

She never finished. Eun-jung had already set the cushion aside and risen to her feet, wearing a smile rich with meaning.

Bo-ram saw the tactical situation deteriorating and attempted to evacuate the sofa. Park Ji-yeon, who had planned for precisely this, cut off her escape route.

One hundred sixty-eight centimeters, twice over, looked down at the woman wedged into the corner of the sofa. It made for a very oppressive skyline.

“So.” Eun-jung folded her arms, head tilted. “Bo-ram-unnie. Who is it, you were saying?”

Bo-ram pushed up her glasses and aimed for dignity.

“I was merely curious.”

“Curious?” Park Ji-yeon heh-heh-ed and went straight for her ribs. “Then Eun-jung-unnie is curious too — about who a certain someone drunk-dialed for twenty whole minutes last time!”

“Park Ji-yeon, don’t you dare—hahahaha—Eun-jung, let go—YAH!!”

Three sets of screaming and laughing detonated through the dorm. Cushions went down in every direction, the magazine took two footsteps, and somebody’s elbow sent the remote control off the coffee table. Bo-ram, exploiting the single advantage of being built like a sparrow, broke free exactly once — and made it not quite two meters before Park Ji-yeon caught her around the waist from behind, Eun-jung sliding in to complete the encirclement. Together they pressed their eldest back down onto the sofa.

The battle lasted approximately three minutes.

Final result:

Jeon Bo-ram lay pinned at the bottom of the pile, one dongsaeng on each side. Her hair was a single large knot, her glasses had slid to her chin, one slipper was missing in action, and overall she resembled a ragdoll cat that had been through the wash twice. Her face said, plainly and with great grievance, I am the unnie here. The unnie.

Eun-jung and Park Ji-yeon held her down, looked at each other over the wreckage, and high-fived.

“Are we being too mean?” Slightly out of breath, slightly embarrassed, Eun-jung peered down at the casualty.

“Not even a little.” Park Ji-yeon was at complete peace with herself. “She’s Bo-ram-unnie. It’s the job.”

Bo-ram stopped struggling and stared at the ceiling with the light of someone whose soul had briefly left her body.

“…Why am I even here.”

Park Ji-yeon looked down at her, smiling like a cartoon villain.

“To be the cushion.”

“Yah!!”

Achievement unlocked: Only Jeon Bo-ram Got Hurt. Once again.
