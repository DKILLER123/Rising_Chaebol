Chapter Fifty-Two

Park Ji-yeon, Girl Next Door

STILL THAT MORNING Song Ji-yeon’s flat, first floor the kitchen island, where the evidence lives

Eun-jung was mid-sip on her sparkling water when the corner of her eye caught it: Park Ji-yeon, staring at her. Dead on. Unblinking.

The look was — there was no better word for it — odd. It glimmered. It probed. It was curious, and underneath the curiosity sat something she flatly could not parse.

Under the stare, unease crawled up her spine. She glanced down at herself, quick and casual — clothes in perfect order, nothing on her face. What is with that look, maknae?

She wanted to ask — but asking might come off as guilty. So she arranged her face into elaborate nothing and kept drinking her sparkling water.

Song Ji-yeon, for his part, registered none of the undercurrents. He was crouched at the open cabinet, filling Mushroom’s bowl with kibble, drawing half a bowl of warm water from the purifier, setting both down in front of the dog — the whole operation one unbroken, unhurried flow. Natural as breathing.

Mushroom fell on the food, tail swinging, and buried his face in it.

Park Ji-yeon’s gaze swept the room: Mushroom, eating. Song Ji-yeon, unbothered. Eun-jung, failing to look casual. Back, and forth, and back.

Gulp.

She had swallowed — and with it arrived the certainty that she had just uncovered a secret of national importance. But no interrogation came. She only tilted her head, ran her eyes a few more laps between the two of them, then quietly reeled the look back in and lowered it to her bottle of sparkling water. Behind those big, rolling eyes: a ledger no soul alive could read.

Eun-jung exhaled in secret relief at not being questioned — and then found the silence too eerie to be any comfort. Surely the maknae isn’t down there somewhere, bottling up something wicked?

Song Ji-yeon, oblivious to all of it, set down his phone and smiled at the pair of them.

“Rep messaged back. Twenty minutes and he’ll be here to show us the second-floor show flat.”

He’d seen that unit himself, in fact — both floors were developer-finished show flats, only in different styles, and the first floor had simply suited his eye better. Hence: first floor.

“Twenty whole minutes?” Park Ji-yeon was already vibrating. “Let’s wait outside, then. We can snoop around the complex.”

He checked the time and stood.

“Sure. Let’s go.”

Out they went — three humans, one dog, a grand procession.

✦ ✦ ✦

MORNING The complex grounds a bench by the main gate

Beyond the lobby doors, the air was clean. A September morning, not yet committed to heat, the breeze carrying the green, planted smell of the landscaping.

He walked Eun-jung and Park Ji-yeon a full loop of the complex, and the three of them ended up on a bench facing the main gate. Park Ji-yeon, entertaining herself by studying the guards on duty in the guard house, got curious.

“Oppa — is security in your complex always this strict? Can delivery even get in?”

“Delivery doesn’t come in. It stops at the guard house and you go down for it yourself.” He’d never actually ordered delivery — he cooked at home — but the dedicated parcel shelves at the guard house made it a safe guess.

“What about packages?”

“Same. There’s a shelf for them at the guard house.”

“Works for me. Safety first.” She nodded, satisfied with the arrangement.

The Peninsula in 2012 did have its courier and delivery industries — but the machine was young. Half the city’s restaurants weren’t on any platform at all; in a complex like this, you generally phoned the restaurant yourself and met the driver at the gate. Nothing like the app-era convenience he remembered from further down the timeline.

Before long, a black sedan pulled up at the main gate. A man in his twenties stepped out — pressed suit, leather portfolio under one arm — signed in with the guards, and came striding across.

“Sajangnim — my apologies, my apologies, for the wait.”

Kim, the development’s sales rep, hurried up and bowed, the courtesy polished to a shine. Understandable. Song Ji-yeon was his VIP — the first unit Kim had ever closed was Song Ji-yeon’s, and now the man was handing him a fresh client. A rental, sure. But people are sentimental animals; live in a place long enough and the heart starts doing math about owning it. And if you felt like objecting that affording rent wasn’t affording purchase — well. Kim was a young man who kept current, and he recognized both women on sight. Ham Eun-jung and Park Ji-yeon of T-ara. He was not about to stand there doubting whether members of the nation’s trending girl group could buy a large flat. One VIP, two prospective VIPs — there was, formally, no ceiling on how polite a man could be.

“It’s fine — we only just came out.” Song Ji-yeon waved it off. “Let’s go see it.”

“Right this way, please.”

Kim led the three of them in and called the elevator. One household per floor: he swiped his card and pressed 2.

“Wow — one elevator, one home. The lift’s basically your front hallway. No corridors at all.” Park Ji-yeon inspected the private elevator lobby with open approval.

“Mm. Privacy, too.” Offhand.

The elevator rose quickly. The doors opened onto a private landing identical to the one downstairs. Kim stepped out first, swiped the unit open, and stood aside.

“All three of you, please — this is the one.”

Same floor plan as Song Ji-yeon’s, down to the centimeter. Different soul. And the sound Park Ji-yeon made was identical to the one she’d made downstairs.

“Wah—”

His place was modern minimalism — black, white, gray, composed. This one was warm cream and bare wood.

Pale wood ran underfoot; the walls glowed a warm off-white. A soft cream fabric sofa stood piled with squishy pumpkin cushions; the coffee table was round, raw wood; beside it spread a big, fluffy lamb’s-wool rug. The whole space came out warm and healing, aggressively, unapologetically girly.

Which was precisely why he hadn’t taken it.

“Oh my god, I LOVE this style!” Shoes off, in like a happy bird, one full lap of the living room — then a running dive, and she threw herself bodily into the sofa.

He genuinely hadn’t pegged the stage-cool Park Ji-yeon as the girly-style type. Though thinking it over, it tracked. The girl was nineteen. Peak innocence.

“Sooo comfy!” She hugged a pumpkin cushion and rolled the length of the sofa, happy as a three-year-old.

Eun-jung watched her maknae’s utter artlessness and shook her head — while her mouth curved up anyway. This kid: the moment she saw something she loved, it went straight through her, no filter.

Song Ji-yeon followed Kim through the unit, taking in the pitch.

“Two hundred seventy-eight square meters built — identical to your unit downstairs, Sajangnim. Four bedrooms, two living spaces, three baths. Developer-finished throughout, furniture and appliances all included. You could move in with a toothbrush.”

The warmth in Kim’s voice was fully professional.

“Because the first- and second-floor flats have limited views, our high-end clients pass on them — both units have sat vacant. On price, the company has authorized meaningful concessions. If Miss Park is renting: ₩800 million deposit, and we can bring the monthly down to ₩6 million.”

For Cheongdam-dong — renting a nearly three-hundred-square-meter flat at that number — a genuinely good deal.

Park Ji-yeon finished her roll, bounced up barefoot, and set off to inspect the rest of the unit. The master bedroom first: some serious bouncing on the king-size bed. Then the walk-in closet, where she turned in front of the full-length mirror, left profile, right profile.

“Unnie! Oppa! Come look! This closet is HUGE! My clothes finally have a home!”

Then the guest rooms, then the study — a gasp at every station of the tour.

Full circuit complete, she floated back to the living room lit up like a shop sign.

“I’ve decided! This one!” She planted herself in front of Song Ji-yeon and Eun-jung and announced it with total gravity.

“That fast?” Eun-jung blinked.

“Of course!” Righteous, matter-of-fact. “Location: good. Security: good. Place: gorgeous. And most importantly—” she scrunched her eyes at the two of them “—the neighbors are good.”

“From now on I live directly upstairs from oppa. If there’s good food, you’d better not forget me!”

That set Eun-jung laughing — and most of the morning’s strange tension finally let go of her.

“Oppa, I’m taking this one!” Park Ji-yeon turned to Song Ji-yeon, a thread of aegyo winding into her voice. “You talk to rep Kim for me. Contracts and things — not my department.”

“No problem.” He agreed with a smile. “Leave it to me.”

With that one sentence, Park Ji-yeon’s mind was fully at ease. She grabbed Eun-jung by the arm and started planning the new home out loud.

“Unnie, look — next to the sofa, a beanbag. When my hands give out from gaming, I can just collapse sideways into it.”

“And that balcony — we’re getting one of those hanging chairs. Afternoon sun, tea, pure bliss.”

“Oh — oh — and the study! I’m converting it into a proper e-sports room. Biggest monitor they make. Coolest gaming chair on the market.”

Each sentence wound her tighter with excitement — she was already living there, in her head.

Eun-jung caught the happiness straight off her, and laughed before she knew it.
