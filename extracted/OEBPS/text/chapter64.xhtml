Chapter Sixty-Four

This… Counts as Seduction, Right?

THE SAME MORNING Apgujeong-dong, Seoul Ham Eun-jung’s home in Apgujeong — the front door

Eun-jung got home, pushed the door open — and a white cannonball hit her at the ankles.

“Mushroom.”

She said it softly, and her face produced its first real smile of the day.

Mushroom orbited her feet at full throttle, tail going like a small outboard motor, his throat making hopeful little engine sounds — feed me, feed me, feed me.

She bent down to scoop him up — and a bright, tearing pain presented its invoice from below.

“Hnn—”

She straightened back up at once, wincing, and retired the idea with full honors.

“Okay, okay. Unnie’s got your food. Hold your horses.”

Into the kitchen: kibble poured to the top of the bowl, water changed, bowl on the floor. Mushroom hit it the way a vacuum hits fresh carpet.

Only then did she drag her spent body into the bedroom — and straight into the bathroom. The shower came on. Hot water poured down from overhead, washing at the fatigue, the stickiness, and a whole night’s worth of evidence. Steam climbed the glass and fogged the mirror white.

When she was done, she toweled off, wrapped the towel around herself any old way, and wiped one hand’s clearing across the glass.

The mirror told on her instantly.

Fair skin — and scattered over it, from her collarbone all the way down past her waist, a constellation of red marks. Deep ones, shallow ones, none of them shy.

Last night hit the play button without asking. Scene after scene, out of control, detonating one after another through her head.

Eun-jung’s face went up in flames — cheeks first, then the ears, then wherever the ears were forwarding it.

And she was — there was no other word for it — stunned. By Song Ji-yeon’s capability. Park Hyo-min had supplied their dorm with a substantial library of “study materials” over the years, and Eun-jung was now in a position to report, as a field researcher, that next to Song Ji-yeon every male lead in that entire archive had been an unfledged chick. And then there were the abs — those eight ruthlessly delineated blocks—

She remembered her own fingers wandering over them last night, mapping the terrain; remembered the hard, scalding heat of it under her palm—

A full-body shiver ran through her, and her knees quietly filed for leave.

“Yah! Ham Eun-jung! Have you no shame?!”

She said it out loud, to her reflection — whose face was by now red enough to wring blood from.

Then she cranked the cold tap, scooped up two hands of ice water, and slapped it onto her face. Once. Twice. The cold shocked her pulse back down out of its gallop and rebooted her brain to a legible screen.

Out of the bathroom, she fell face-first into the soft bed, dragged the blanket over her entire body, and lay there in her cocoon.

Then the phone came out. Several unread messages were lying on the screen.

The newest was from this morning. From Song Ji-yeon.

Don’t you have anything you want to say to me?

Eun-jung stared at that line for a geologic age.

What did she want to say?

She didn’t know. That was the honest, hopeless truth of it — she didn’t know.

What were they now, even? Last night, both of them had been drinking. Not heavily — but alcohol is a famous accelerant. And the kiss… the kiss had been hers. She’d started it. Up on her toes, eyes shut, no plan whatsoever.

This… counts as seduction, right?

But he’d kissed her back. He’d carried her inside. He’d… and then they’d… So — were they together now? Was this “together”?

Except he had never actually said it. Not one word of “I like you.” Not one sentence of “go out with me.” Nothing with edges. Nothing with a name.

So was it together, or wasn’t it?

She didn’t know. Her brain was porridge — overcooked, sticking to the pot, burning at the bottom. Coherent thought had left the building.

And how, exactly, was she supposed to reply to that message?

She clawed at her hair. She rolled over. And over. And over. The blanket got kicked into a ruin; the phone skated across the mattress twice and nearly defected to the floor.

Then it buzzed.

Park Ji-yeon.

KakaoTalk · Park Ji-yeon

Park Ji-yeon

Unnie. Why are your shoes and bag at my house?? Did you not go home last night??

Ham Eun-jung

I did go home!! I drank a little too much last night and completely forgot to grab them. I’ll pick everything up next time I come over — just hold onto them for me until then, okay?

Eun-jung read the incoming message, and her headache — barely stabilized as it was — deepened by a full floor.

Fine. Nearest fire first.

She took a deep breath, typed at speed, hit send, exhaled one small sigh of relief, and flung the phone to the far side of the bed, where it couldn’t editorialize.

✦ ✦ ✦

MEANWHILE Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Unit 201 — the sofa

One floor up, Park Ji-yeon read her unnie’s reply.

“Heh.”

“Heh heh.”

She did not believe one syllable of it.

She couldn’t technically prove where Eun-jung-unnie had spent the night — but the exhibits were all in custody. The shoes: present. The bag: present. The owner: absent. Add to that oppa’s own sworn testimony this morning — the breakfast was for Eun-jung — and the question practically wrote itself: why, exactly, would Song Ji-yeon-oppa be packing breakfast for Ham Eun-jung at dawn?

Truly a mystery. A very hard mystery.

Conclusion: last night, those two had definitely gotten up to something interesting together. They were — at minimum — a case.

Park Ji-yeon hugged a cushion and rolled the full length of the sofa, her brain already in pre-production on a prime-time melodrama: casting locked, soundtrack temped, the corridor kiss storyboarded three different ways before the second commercial break.

But she wouldn’t expose them to their faces. Uh-uh. A true professional never burns her own source material.

Her thumbs tapped out a short reply, and off it went.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Park Ji-yeon

Okay!! Remember to come get them, unnie~

✦ ✦ ✦

TEN O’CLOCK Mapo-gu, Seoul SAVORY F&B — third floor, Planning Department

Song Ji-yeon left Unit 201 and rode the elevator straight down to the underground garage. He had an office day ahead of him.

Back when he’d first decided to buy T-ara, he’d handed Park Seon-young in Planning a side assignment: register a brand-new entertainment company, ready to take over the girls’ contracts when the ink dried. Then yesterday at the housewarming, So-yeon had asked where things stood — and he’d realized, with a small internal flinch, that he’d never once checked on it.

Ten o’clock: the Mercedes rolled to a stop outside the SAVORY F&B building. He parked by the entrance, walked straight in, and made for the third floor.

The Planning Department wasn’t large — one classroom’s worth of floor — but it was packed: twenty-some heads at a glance, all of them busy. At the center of it, Park Seon-young stood over a wall map of Seoul, gesturing at districts and muttering to himself — a general deep in campaign planning. Scouting the next ONGI branch, clearly.

“Old Park.”

Knuckles on the desk edge.

“Sajangnim!” Park Seon-young shot upright like a student caught napping.

“That entertainment company I asked you to register — where does it stand?”

A blink — then he surfaced, excavated a blue folder from somewhere in the paper strata, and presented it with both hands.

NEW ENTITY · REGISTRATION STATUS
Approved nameShining Youth (SY Entertainment)
Business licenseClearing within 1–2 days
Corporate registrationSame window
Initial addressCare of SY Dining (independent office later)
Representative directorSong Ji-yeon
Capital injectionDeferred — funds land Monday, at latest

“Sajangnim — nearly done. The name has cleared approval: ShiningYouth. What do you think? The business license and the corporate registration should both come through within a day or two.”

“ShiningYouth… ‘Shining Youth.’”

Song Ji-yeon took the folder, opened it, read the name once in English and once in Korean — and the laugh got out before he could stop it.

It was a good name. Genuinely. And it had landed, entirely by accident, in a place Park Seon-young couldn’t have known about: if what he was buying, in the end, was six girls’ shining youth — returning it to them with interest — then the letterhead might as well come right out and say so.

Besides — someday, on some group chart, ShiningYouth would sit right beside SAVORY F&B and look perfectly natural there.

“This name. Approved.” He closed the folder. “Once the license lands, find a solid accounting firm and stand up the company’s bookkeeping framework. The registered address rides under SAVORY F&B for now — when the company has earned offices of its own, we’ll get it some.”

“Understood, Sajangnim.” A nod — and then, carefully, “Then, Sajangnim… the legal representative?”

“Me. Put my name on it.”

“And the registered capital — how much does Sajangnim plan to inject?” Park Seon-young asked it lightly, taking the folder back.

“No rush on that one. Give yourself a busy couple of days — get the corporate account opened. I’ll wire the funds by Monday, at the latest.”

The truth of the matter was that Song Ji-yeon’s liquid capital currently amounted to a parking card and whatever lint was living in his jacket. Every won to his name was riding the Sewon position, and nothing freed up until Monday, when it came off the board.

Instructions complete, he clapped Park Seon-young once on the shoulder.

“That’s all. Back to your map — I’m off.”

“Ne, Sajangnim. Safe travels.”

Song Ji-yeon walked out of the Planning Department and headed back toward his own office.
