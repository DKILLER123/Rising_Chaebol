Peninsula · Version 3 English Edition

The Work

Peninsula: My Rise to Chaebol Status Began with Ham Eun-jung — a one-hundred-nine chapter Korean-entertainment web serial, adapted into English as a publisher-grade edition: restructured prose, naturalized dialogue, restored emotional beats, and full typographic treatment.

This Edition

Version 3. The text has been translated and re-edited for fluency and emotional fidelity rather than literal correspondence; dialogue follows the source’s convention of unattributed speech, carried by voice and action. The protagonist is rendered as Song Ji-yeon, a Korean-American of Los Angeles, consistent throughout. Korean honorifics (-ssi, oppa, unnie, sunbae, Sajangnim) are preserved as spoken.

A Note on Real People

This is a work of fiction. It depicts real public figures of the 2012 K-pop industry — among them members of T-ara, f(x), KARA, and Girls’ Generation — in invented situations that diverge freely from the historical record. Where the story and reality disagree, the story wins; no claim of fact is made or implied about any real person. Character portraits are drawn from public press and fan archives and remain the property of their respective owners; original characters are depicted by commissioned artwork.

Typography

Set in Lora, Crimson Pro, and Cormorant Garamond, with IM Fell English, Courier Prime, Caveat, Permanent Marker, Bebas Neue, and Inter for the edition’s display contexts. All typefaces embedded as WOFF for offline reading. Typeset for the Peninsula Press edition, MMXXV.

Produced as a demonstration of professional ebook adaptation: research, translation, and multi-pass editorial review.

The World of 2012 · A Reader’s Orientation

For readers arriving fresh: the story opens in September 2012, weeks after the “T-ara bullying scandal” — a wildfire of rumors following member Ryu Hwa-young’s departure — turned the Peninsula’s biggest girl group into the industry’s favorite target. Concert crowds staged “Black Oceans,” silencing their performances in the dark. The group’s agency, CCM, bled money as bookings vanished.

Our protagonist knows how every thread of that year ends — the groups that rise, the stocks that soar, the tragedies that never needed to happen. He has one month’s head start, ten billion won of family money, a system that rewards devotion to extraordinary women, and absolutely no intention of watching the ending repeat.

The rest is hotpot, high finance, and one very complicated love story with the Crown’s jewel.
