Chapter Twenty-One

T-ara’s Closed-Door Meeting

SATURDAY, SEPTEMBER 8 · AFTER THE FINAL POSE ICC Jeju backstage — the waiting room

The backstage waiting room. The moment the door shut, whatever had been holding the six of them upright drained out of them.

They had assumed this storm would pass the way every storm before it had — lie low for a few days, keep their heads below the parapet, come out the other side intact. For a month they had followed the company’s instructions to the letter: stay in, go out as little as possible, give the cameras nothing. It hadn’t been nearly enough. Offline, in the actual world, the Black Ocean had followed them all the way down — and not one light had stayed on for them.

So-yeon dropped into the chair at the vanity and tipped her head back toward the ceiling. Her stage makeup was still on; sweat had dragged half her eyeliner down her cheek. Her mouth opened once before the words came.

“It’s worse than I thought.”

Five words, and nobody had anything to put after them.

Qri stood against the far wall with her arms wrapped around herself, and even her quicksilver fox eyes had gone heavy. She had seen trouble coming, in a general way — she had worried that the members’ little Instagram digs at Ryu Hwa-young would cost them eventually. She had just never priced the bill this high.

“I thought the boycott would stay online.” A pause. “That they’d tire themselves out cursing, and scatter in a few days.”

She didn’t get to the end of the thought. Hate online, you could switch off — lock the phone, and it ceased to exist. There was no such setting for tonight’s dark. Thousands of people sitting below them, thumbing their fan lights dead. No cheering. No applause. Not one voice calling anything supportive.

They sang, and the hall paid them in silence.

Hyo-min sat on the edge of the sofa, hands braced on her knees, and murmured it half to herself.

“How much longer can we keep doing this?”

The waiting room went quieter still.

“Hyo-min-unnie isn’t wrong.” Park Ji-yeon stood at the water dispenser, and the paper cup in her hand had crushed itself half out of shape. She watched the water shiver inside it. “If every stage is going to be like this one — is there even a point in going on?”

“Stop.” So-yeon’s voice came out raw. “Not here. We don’t say things like that here.”

She was the leader. If words like that got loose outside this room, it was finished for all six of them.

Ji-yeon’s head came up, and her voice climbed with it.

“Then where? When exactly is it okay to say it?”

“So-yeon-unnie, you saw the same hall I did. Thousands of people out there. Not one of them lit a light for us.”

“So what are we up on that stage as?”

“Clowns?”

The cup finished collapsing in her hand. Cold water overflowed the rim, ran off her knuckles, and dripped onto the floor.

So-yeon closed her eyes and did not argue. She was the leader.

This was the moment she was supposed to step into the middle of the room and hold everyone upright — hang on a little longer, it will pass, one more push. The words simply weren’t in her. They were too hollow to fool even herself. Better to let them vent it here than have them swallow it until it came out somewhere worse.

Bo-ram had shrunk into the corner of the sofa with the throw cushion locked in a full-body hold. She shot a glance at the fuming maknae, held it in for a long moment, and finally squeezed out a sentence of her own.

“Okay — enough. We’ll talk about it back at the dorm. There are staff everywhere out there. If someone hears this, it’ll be bad.”

Through the door, down the corridor, other artists were laughing. Someone had just come off their stage and was reliving their encore at full volume. Each burst of it came through the door panels and landed like a slap. Six girls had held their spines straight through ten minutes of Black Ocean; back here, they couldn’t hold themselves up for one minute.

So-yeon turned away from the others and pressed a palm over her nose. Ji-yeon dropped the ruined cup into the bin, then pulled out tissues and went down after the water on the floor.

Everyone was finding something to do with her hands. Nobody was talking. As long as the hands kept moving, whatever was inside could be kept from tipping over.

Eun-jung sat in the corner. From the moment she’d walked in, she hadn’t said a word.

Her phone sat in her hand with the screen lit, and it stayed lit. The stage was still running behind her eyes, and it would not be chased out.

That sea of darkness. And in the middle of it, the one yellow light board, burning.

She had recognized Song Ji-yeon from the stage — the third row is close enough to count eyelashes. He’d stood in the middle of that crowd with the board held high over his head. Everyone around him had been staring: some taking pictures, some laughing outright, a couple making little gestures of disgust in his direction.

Eun-jung stared at her phone. Her thumb found the input field.

She typed: Thank you.

Deleted it.

Typed: Why did you come?

Deleted that too.

In the end, only three words survived.

KakaoTalk · Song Ji-yeon

Ham Eun-jung

Where are you?

Song Ji-yeon

Outside the venue. Eating tteokbokki. I was hungry.

The reply landed in under ten seconds.

Eun-jung stared at the line on the screen, and the faintly frosty, faintly adorable set of her face finally came undone. This man had flown to Jeju for them. He had stood up alone in a hall full of turned backs and held a light for them through two full songs. And the moment the stage went dark for good, he had gone outside to eat tteokbokki.

Incredible.

She didn’t type back. She called. One ring.

Phone · Ham Eun-jung → Song Ji-yeon

“It’s over?”

His end was all noise: oil hissing in a pan, a vendor calling out in broad Jeju dialect, plastic bags cracking in the sea wind.

“Don’t go.”

“Hm?”

A beat of pure bafflement. Don’t go — from where? He was standing exactly where he was.

“Which gate are you at? I’m coming out.”

“Don’t you still have the group curtain call?”

“I don’t care.”

The baseball cap was already off the table and in her hand.

“Tell me. Which gate.”

“…East gate. There’s a tteokbokki stand next to a grilled-squid cart. The smell carries — you’ll hit it the second you’re outside.”

“Wait for me.”

She hung up, jammed the cap down over her hair, yanked the mask up over her face — the whole operation executed at a speed that frightened even her.

So-yeon’s eyes opened at the commotion.

“Eun-jung. Where are you going?”

“Something came up.” Eun-jung was already at the door, hand on the handle.

“There’s the closing photo in a minute.”

“Cover for me. I’ll be right back.”

“What’s so urgent?” Bo-ram’s face had appeared over the edge of the cushion.

Eun-jung’s feet stopped. She didn’t turn around.

“Meeting someone.”

Click.

The door shut again. The five left in the room traded a long look, and curiosity lit in every pair of eyes at once — none of them had ever known Eun-jung to have friends in Jeju. And just like that, curiosity hauled them up out of the wreckage of the last hour.

The six of them were close, the kind of close where anything that happened to one of them became everyone’s business within the hour. In their ledger, Eun-jung was the shy one on the surface and the steady one underneath — she always knew what belonged in which moment. This was the first time in their entire career that she had walked out of a schedule to go meet somebody.

“Who’s she meeting?”

“No idea.”

Qri dropped back onto the sofa, pressed a tissue to her face, and lay there with doubt and a thread of certainty fighting it out in her voice.

“It’s not as if she went to meet the manager.”

“And would she need the full cap-and-mask treatment to meet the manager?”

At the vanity, Hyo-min caught So-yeon’s eye in the mirror. She peeled off her crooked false lash, wiped at the ruins of her liner, and went looking for a lighter register.

“At the speed she just moved? Even if Jeon Do-yeon herself had been standing outside that door, she’d have been made to wait.”

Bo-ram’s arms tightened around the cushion. A possibility arrived, and she sat bolt upright.

“Don’t tell me — a guy friend?”

Park Ji-yeon had drifted back from the water dispenser with a fresh cup, and a thoroughly evil little smile was climbing her face.

“Bingo. Correct.”

Four of the five heads swung toward her. Hyo-min’s reaction was the largest — she came straight up out of her chair.

“What guy friend?”

“Since when does Eun-jung have a guy friend?”

“Why am I always the last one to hear things?”

Ji-yeon took a sip of water.

“I never said it was actually a guy friend.”

“You just said ‘Bingo’!”

“I said it might be a future one.”

“Yah! Park Ji-yeon!”

Hyo-min lunged over and caught her by the arm.

“Spill. Now.”

“Stop shaking me — the water—”

Ji-yeon shielded her cup with her elbow and looked past Hyo-min at the other three.

“You didn’t see it? From the stage?”

“See what?”

“The one man in the building who was cheering for us.”

She set the cup down on the table.

“The whole hall went dark, and one yellow light board in the third row burned from the first note to the last. I saw it. There’s no way the rest of you didn’t.”

Bo-ram went still.

“The ONGI owner? The one who had hotpot with Eun-jung that time?”

“That one.” Ji-yeon nodded.

The ONGI owner? What ONGI owner? Since when were Ji-yeon and Bo-ram-unnie talking in riddles — wait. Not good. She’d been frozen out.

Hyo-min put on an aggrieved face and pinched the little dragon by the scruff of her neck, fox eyes brimming with injury of her own manufacture.

“So Ji-yeon keeps secrets from me now, is that it? Won’t tell me anything anymore. And after I raised you. With these two hands.”

She remembered that light board. By the back half of the set she had stopped letting herself look at the audience at all — every glance knocked her timing down another notch. But the board had kept moving through all of it, a small knot of yellow light swinging from side to side, on from beginning to end.

Ji-yeon pressed her lips together, privately dying to point out exactly whose fault it was that Hyo-min-unnie hadn’t been in the dorm on the relevant evening. But Qri-unnie and So-yeon-unnie were now staring at her with precisely the same hunger, so in her infinite magnanimity she forgave Hyo-min-unnie and deigned to explain.

“That day, the two of you weren’t at the dorm. It was just me and Bo-ram-unnie. Eun-jung-unnie said she was going home for dinner, and lied to our faces that it was with her eomma. We got curious and followed her—”

“I wasn’t curious.” Bo-ram cut in, faint but immovable on the point. “I was taken against my will.”

Who asked? Truly. Who, anywhere on this earth, asked whether you volunteered?

“—so we followed her, and she went into a private room at a hotpot restaurant, and a little while later this man walks in. And then they’re in there for ages…” Ji-yeon rolled onward, undeterred. “So Bo-ram-unnie and I got a table at the same restaurant and ate hotpot while we waited. Which — that place is genuinely good, by the way.”

Three unnies’ worth of fierce stares landed on her at once, and a cold draft ran down Ji-yeon’s spine. Who cares whether the hotpot was good? Would it kill you to stay on topic?

She course-corrected at speed.

“What you don’t know is what happened after. Eun-jung-unnie stood there watching him leave. Two whole minutes. If Bo-ram-unnie and I hadn’t gone over to her, she’d probably still be standing there now… Right, Bo-ram-unnie?”

Bo-ram nodded along, and at the memory, the corners of her mouth rose entirely without her permission, dimples and all.

“Ne. We put Eun-jung through a full interrogation on the spot… Per her confession, the man’s name is Song Ji-yeon. She’d known him for five days. And now he’s followed her to a concert, apparently.”

Qri heard the whole file through, then gave a small approving nod.

“Well. The man’s got nerve. Known her for a minute, and he shows up to a concert to hold a light board for her. I saw about fifty phones pointed at him. After tonight, he’s famous.”

The room’s mood dropped a few more degrees at that. These days, anything that touched T-ara drew hate the way sugar draws wasps. The moment those videos spread, Song Ji-yeon would become a fresh target. But they were women, and as women they permitted themselves one honest second of it: they had liked watching what that man did.

Hyo-min’s mouth twisted. She looked over at So-yeon, who was wiping her makeup down, and bumped her leg with her foot.

“Look at that, and then look at the one you picked. How long has it been since the scandal, and Oh Jong-hyuk still hasn’t managed a single visit? Before all this he couldn’t go two days without needing to see you. Trouble arrives, and watch how beautifully he evaporates.”

“Hyo-min-ah… Jong-hyuk-oppa is still enlisted.”

Hyo-min snorted, cracked open a bottle of mineral water, and drank.

“Enlisted. It’s an army base, not a bunker on the moon. They’ve confiscated his phone? Outlawed phone calls? The day the scandal broke, I scrolled past his Instagram — a big group-dinner photo with his unit. Grinning like he’d won the lottery.”

So-yeon lowered her head. Her fingertips worried at the edge of the vanity without her noticing, and her lips moved once and settled into silence.

He genuinely hadn’t reached out since the whole thing detonated. Her messages sank like stones. All that ever came back was a single, solitary “read” hanging in the chat window. She had constructed his excuses for him — the base runs strict, training runs him into the ground, maybe he doesn’t want to add anything to her load. But stack the excuses high enough, and even she could tell she was drafting alibis for a man in retreat.

Her own schedule ground her to powder, and she still carved out hours to visit his base with a lunch box in her hands. Why couldn’t he spare her one phone call? One message?

Bo-ram leaned half out of her corner, hugging the cushion a little tighter. Nothing cuts like comparison. She didn’t think much of So-yeon’s boyfriend either — and she was the group’s eldest, with zero dating history but a working eye for people.

“So-yeon… I also think you should sit down sometime and really look at what’s between you and Jong-hyuk-oppa. The two of you barely had any time together before he enlisted. Do you actually know what kind of man he is?”

So-yeon and Oh Jong-hyuk had dated for only a few months before his enlistment, and counted honestly, almost none of that had been spent in the same room. An idol at the top of the charts does not get to date. Take T-ara: one month off in a year counted as a generous year. Whatever So-yeon knew about Oh Jong-hyuk, she hadn’t learned it from time.

“Exactly.” Hyo-min set the bottle down hard enough that water slopped over the rim onto the cleansing pads. “Nobody’s asking him to stand in a crowd holding a light board like Song Ji-yeon. One text. Two syllables — ‘Fighting.’ That’s all it would have taken, and he’d never have heard a word about it from me.”

“Enough.” Qri sat up. Her voice was quiet, and it shut the whole room down anyway. She looked at Hyo-min, still burning, and at So-yeon, whose face had gone gray, and sighed. “How did this become about So-yeon? We were talking about Eun-jung. God knows what’s happening out there with her — shouldn’t we be working out how we’re getting through the closing photo?”

The five of them were still designing their closing-photo survival strategy when the manager pushed the door open: they were leaving. The organizers had smelled the wind and decided the six of them would not be joining the finale’s all-cast photo.

Relief came first. And immediately behind it, a second wave — colder, heavier.

A tiger down on the flats gets bitten by dogs. There had been a time, not long ago at all, when closing photos had begged for them — invited them, held their places, thanked them for coming. Now they were being sent home before the finale. They could already hear exactly how the morning would write it.

Entertainment wire · tomorrow’s cycle, already writing itself

“T-ara Skips the Closing Photo After Black Ocean — Snubbing Their Own Audience?”

“A Top Girl Group Falls Off the Altar — Too Ashamed to Face the Camera After the Offline Black Ocean…”

…and so on, all the way down the page.
