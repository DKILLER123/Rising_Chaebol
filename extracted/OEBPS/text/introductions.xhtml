Song Min-seok

Father · Founder & Chairman, SAVORY

The man who built the family business twice — once in LA, once in Seoul

Early fifties · Mapo-gu, Seoul

A first-generation immigrant who washed dishes in someone else’s Koreatown diner until he could buy it, then built the SAVORY name across a decade of six-day weeks. Handed his son ten billion won and a company to prove himself with, then — when the boy delivered — took the keys back with quiet pride and told him to go chase whatever an entertainment company was. His love language is revenue reports. As of Chapter 107, he is fighting off a CJ Foodville acquisition siege — suppliers severed, cold chain cut, costs doubled — with the loudest move available to a company under siege: opening another store, ribbon and all. The ribbon itself is now the son’s problem.

OC

Kang Seol-ah

Mother · Commander of the Mapo Household

Typical Korean-immigrant mother, in the fullest and most loving sense

Early fifties · Mapo-gu, Seoul

Calls every day. Notices skipped meals through the phone. Has mounted a full-scale marriage campaign on her only son, complete with intelligence gathered from Auntie Jang’s network. Fed him a twelve-dish table in Chapter 18 and extracted a promise to visit weekly — a more binding contract than anything CCM ever signed.

OC

Park Seon-young

Representative Director, ShiningYouth Entertainment

The silver tongue of the office · “Old Park”

32 · married (the office has questions)

Architect of the ONGI launch marketing, retriever of impossible storefronts, registrant of ShiningYouth Entertainment — and, as of Chapter Ninety-Nine, its Representative Director, with full authority over the new agency: scouting the Seoul headquarters, staffing the org chart, and deploying the startup capital at maximum velocity. Delivered the Cheongdam-dong headquarters in under a day (Chapter 104) — four stories, one corner up from JYP, ₩500 million deposit, lease signed before dinner. A man whose flirt radius covers every receptionist in Gangnam. Comic relief with a scary work ethic. His loyalty to the young CEO is total, and only slightly motivated by fear — and by the stock options.

Kim Min-hyun

Legal Director, SAVORY F&B

SNU Law · poached personally by the chairman years ago

Mid-thirties · gold wire-rimmed glasses

The calmest man in every negotiation. Ran the CCM acquisition from first probe to final signature — pressure on the price, patience on the timeline, and the quiet reminder that a contract signed by the wrong legal entity isn’t worth the paper it’s printed on. Speaks in numbered clauses; smiles roughly once per arc.

Kim Kwang-soo

CEO, CCM Entertainment

T-ara’s agency head — the old fox of the Peninsula’s idol industry

Veteran producer · founder of the MBK/CCM lineage

The man who assembled the Crown and then watched a scandal strip its value to zero. Opened negotiations demanding fifty billion won with a straight face, settled at forty in cash, and never once stopped mourning the merchandising empire that almost was. In the story as in life, an operator first — political bets, sub-unit gambles, and an exit strategy always close at hand.

Old Chen

Chef & Owner, the Sichuan restaurant at 312 Cheongdam-dong

Eleven years on the Peninsula

Forties · from Sichuan, by way of homesickness

Cooked real mapo tofu in a city that wanted it sweeter, and kept the place spotless for a decade regardless. Bonded with Ji-yeon over the memory of proper Sichuan heat — the boy knew it from the San Gabriel Valley, the man knew it from home — and sold him the storefront with a regular’s discount. Wants nothing more than to get back to his wife and kids before they forget his face.

Kang Jin-ho

Finance Director, SAVORY F&B

Twenty years with the company · father of a trainee

Late forties · the ledger’s conscience

Keeps SAVORY’s books honest and its CEO’s flights of fancy grounded in cash-flow reality. Small, white, aggressively friendly

About one year old · answerable to Bae Joo-hyun

The SAVORY Staff

Lee Ji-hoon · Lim Woo-hyun · Park Min-suk · Lee Hye-jin · Manager Kim

Logistics & Training, store manager, floor manager, front desk, road manager

Mapo headquarters & the Apgujeong flagship

Lee Ji-hoon heads Logistics & Training — personnel checks, uniform doctrine, and the ceremonial greeting of Sajangnims at grand openings; he ran the Cheongdam ribbon-cutting like a wedding planner and personally pulled the Sajangnim through the crowd (Ch. 108). Lim Woo-hyun runs the Apgujeong ONGI with drill-sergeant precision and a loyalty verging on the religious. Park Min-suk commands the dining floor, the training demos, and — as of late — the Sajangnim’s increasingly complicated social calendar. Lee Hye-jin held the front desk until Monday, when she and the new girl walked out of the lobby as T-ara’s managers — taking with them the company’s best intelligence on Director Park’s arrival times. Manager Kim shepherds Sulli through drama-set logistics with the thousand-yard stare of a veteran. None of them are paid enough. All of them would say yes to more.

Kwon Young-il

Suitors’ Circle · “the man with the fashion connections”

The all-talk, no-deliver suitor circling Victoria

Early thirties · Seoul social circuit

Has been chasing Victoria for months with the same pitch — endless promises of magazine covers, brand ambassadorships, a fashion line with her name on the label. Not a single one has materialized. Has never once put anything worth eight figures in her hands. All of it stays exactly where it started: on his lips. Measured against a man who quietly drops a hundred-million-won Cartier on a girl who kissed him on impulse, Young-il comes out, frankly, looking very, very bad — and Krystal is the first to file the comparison away for the next time her unnie needs to hear it.

OC

Jeong Soo-ah

SAVORY front desk · future T-ara manager

The new hire with photo-editing skills

Early twenties · Seoul

Two weeks into the front desk when the Sajangnim walked into the break room and overheard her dreaming out loud about Girls’ Generation fanmeet tickets and “ending fairies.” Twenty minutes later she had been quietly, offhandedly, offered the position of T-ara’s manager at the new ShiningYouth Entertainment — alongside her unnie Lee Hye-jin. Card-carrying Eun-jung-unnie stan. Cannot hold her Americano steady in the presence of management, but learns fast and arrives with a useful side skill: she can edit photos. The roof arrived in Chapter 104 — four stories in Cheongdam-dong, one corner up from JYP — and in Chapter 107 the transfer cleared the Chairman’s desk: boxes packed (top layer: snacks), handover files ready since the weekend. As of Monday morning, she and her unnie are T-ara’s managers. The Americano has not yet learned to stay steady.

Hirai Momo

JYP trainee · future TWICE — dance line

“The raccoon” — first bow: Chapter 104

November 9, 1996 · Kyotanabe, Japan

Fifteen — sixteen next month — and fresh enough off the plane that her Korean is good for bows, greetings, and one deeply sincere “we will work hard,” delivered twice. Recognized Ham Eun-jung and Park Ji-yeon across a restaurant in a single glance — T-ara was the soundtrack of her middle-school years — and nearly levitated with the shock of it. Known to exactly one man in that restaurant by a different name entirely: the raccoon, one-ninth of a future legendary group, filed under acquire later. The filing cabinet in question has a ₩5 billion budget and no patience. Time will tell.

Minatozaki Sana

JYP trainee · future TWICE

“The shiba” — first bow: Chapter 104

December 29, 1996 · Osaka, Japan

Fifteen, Osaka-born, Korean at the politely-hopeful stage. Bowed beside Momo, thanked the sunbaenims twice, understood approximately none of what followed, and laughed at the menu with her whole body — round face, eyes that curve up when she smiles, a soft sunny squishiness that one dog-owning unnie present on the spot pronounced “a genuine shiba.” The internet of a future decade will agree with the unnie. At length. In side-by-side comparison charts. The man who has seen those charts said nothing, paid for her dinner, and left.

Kim Tae-yeon

Leader & main vocal, Girls’ Generation — the nation’s kid leader

“The homebody, transfigured” — first bow: Chapter 108

March 9, 1989 · Jeonju

Spotted first as a probability — Girls’ Generation’s short roster runs to exactly two names, and the small shape glued to Yoon-ah’s arm read Tae-yeon, not Sunny. Unmasked at ONGI Cheongdam’s grand opening: bare-faced, fair enough to look Photoshopped by weather, and pink to the ear-rims over one well-aimed visual. The fandom’s certified introvert, who in theory never walks through a door that doesn’t open onto a stage — and who nonetheless hauled her center out of bed at dawn for the entire Cheongdam fashion street, pronounced the signature broth “a crime,” and left planning a nine-member company dinner at parade volume. One lunch in, the MC’s address book holds the era’s most recognizable voice — and a certain golden Handbook has quietly turned a page.

Han So-hee

Lee So-hee · ShiningYouth actress — stage name decreed, goddess bound, Gaepo resident

“The Uninvited” — first bow: Chapter 108, the ribbon-bearer

Nineteen · Ulsan · one backpack, then four prepaid years of rent

The cool, clean-lined hostess whose face itched at the Sajangnim all through the ribbon-cutting — some stranger from the last life, he figured, glimpsed once in a magazine margin. The corridor supplied the name; the car ride supplied the stage name (his pick, ceremonially offered as her choice); the contract she signed unread in one page-flip, because what the pages said had never mattered to a girl auditing a sponsorship. It wasn’t a sponsorship — a fact that arrived one towel at a time and ended in a self-administered kiss that rang the Goddess Handbook like a bell: fifth goddess, mixed martial arts included. Now housed twelfth-floor in Gaepo with Kim Ji-won three floors up as her assigned sunbae, and lobbying hard for a fuller merger before the market closes. “Oppa” remains in service; the audit continues.

Also in Orbit

Heo Min-hoe (CJ Foodville) · IU (Lee Ji-eun) · Ryu Hwa-young · Amber & Luna · Krystal’s sister Jessica · Park Jin-young (JYP) · Song Hye-kyo

Referenced luminaries and adversaries of the 2012 scene

The Peninsula, at the turning of the era

Heo Min-hoe, president of CJ Foodville, made the error of the season: offering to buy ONGI from a man who had spent twenty years being underestimated, and taking the refusal personally. IU exists at the corner of the “Human Trilogy” bond and one very good ramen recommendation. Hwa-young haunts the edges of the bullying scandal that started everything. Amber and Luna round out f(x)’s Californian wing. Park Jin-young’s headquarters holds the corner one down from ShiningYouth’s new front door — close enough that the MC’s first reaction to the address was a joke about renting it out from under him. (The same agency’s trainee rooms, for the record, house the raccoon and the shiba a few blocks up this page.) Sunny, Tiffany, and the rest of the nation’s girl group — the whole nine now on notice for a hotpot team dinner — drift past in endorsements, broadcasts, and building lobbies. And somewhere out in the wider firmament hangs Song Hye-kyo — the era’s untouchable goddess, referenced exactly once, as the other half of a bond called the Twin Flowers Mirror that the Handbook refuses to explain. A world fully populated, one cameo deep.
