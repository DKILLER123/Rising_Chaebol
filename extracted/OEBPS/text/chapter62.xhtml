Chapter Sixty-Two

She Ran

THE SMALL HOURS Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Cheongdam Daewoo Royal County — Unit 201, the living room

Deep in the night, Park Ji-yeon rolled over in her sleep, rolled clean off the sofa, and hit the carpet with a solid thud — seat-first.

“Hnn…”

A pained little groan. She cracked her eyes open to half-mast and surveyed the terrain.

The living-room light was still on. The coffee table was still a graveyard — chicken boxes, empty bottles. And every other human being was gone.

“Oh… they all went home…”

She rubbed her eyes. Her brain was still mostly porridge, and with the total absence of suspicion available only to the truly drunk, she concluded that the unnies and Song Ji-yeon-oppa had all gone home too. She staggered upright, slapped the light off, crawled back onto the sofa, hauled the blanket up, and was gone again before her head fully landed.

✦ ✦ ✦

TUESDAY MORNING · FIRST LIGHT Cheongdam-dong, Seoul the same complex, first floor — Song Ji-yeon’s bedroom

Dawn squeezed through the gap in the curtains, laid one long stripe across the gray carpet, climbed the bed, and landed squarely on Song Ji-yeon’s face. He frowned and threw a hand up against the glare.

He opened his eyes to his own familiar ceiling. One second. Two. Then the second data point arrived: something warm in his arm. Something soft. Something breathing.

He looked down.

Eun-jung was asleep against his chest — curled on her side, cheek over his heart, breathing slow and even. The morning light didn’t dare be loud on her; it just sat quietly on her face. Her lashes lay like two small fans beneath her eyes. She looked — the word arrived uninvited — peaceful.

Song Ji-yeon slid out from under the blanket one millimeter at a time, bare feet onto the cold floor, weight distributed like a man defusing something. He found his clothes and put them on. Then he bent, picked up the cream-white sweater and the jeans, folded them into a neat stack, and set it on the nightstand — where she’d see it first thing.

Only then did he slip out of the bedroom and ease the door shut.

The door had barely clicked before the woman on the bed opened her eyes.

She had, in fact, been awake for a while now. She simply could not make herself open her eyes. Open eyes meant morning. Morning meant it had all been real.

She sat up slowly. The blanket slid from her shoulders, and the room made its inventory plain: smooth skin — and written across it, from her collarbone downward, red marks in every shade of last night.

Last night replayed in full, all at once. Walking Song Ji-yeon to his door. The kiss she had started herself, on champagne and borrowed courage. And after — everything that came loose in the dark, after that.

Ham Eun-jung’s cheeks went hot enough to fry an egg on.

Oh my god.

What did I do?

This kind of thing — it was always the girl who ended up holding the short end. What was she supposed to do now? What would Song Ji-yeon think of her? Would he think she was that kind of woman — easy, thoughtless, anyone’s for a glass of champagne—

Chagrin, shame, and a panic she couldn’t put a name to chewed through her chest like a colony of very small, very persistent ants.

What now? How was she supposed to look him in the face tomorrow? Pretend it never happened? Or—

She didn’t let herself finish the thought. She hauled the blanket up over her head and burrowed into total darkness. Full ostrich. No survivors.

Meanwhile, out in the living room, Song Ji-yeon sat down on the sofa and opened the Goddess Handbook.

Sometime last night — twice, in fact — the Handbook had given off that faint tremor he knew very well. The one it made when a goddess bound to him, or when an ability paid out. Its version of a message tone.

And there had been no version of last night in which he was going to pause — mid-anything — to check his rewards.

So the notifications had sat there. All night. Patient.

Goddess Handbook — Overnight Notifications ×2

Goddess CollectedHam Eun-jung (T-ara) — bond activated: Jewel of the Crown (1/6)

Bound & RegisteredGoddess Ability fully unlocked — one Stock Forecast Card per week, delivered automatically. Contact no longer required.

He closed the Handbook and settled back into the room.

So the theory was confirmed. To collect a goddess… took that.

“Aish. And what exactly am I supposed to do with this information?”

He leaned back into the sofa and muttered it at the ceiling. There was no joy in it at all. He’d activated a bond — first progress marker on the Crown’s jewels — and what he actually felt was cornered. Because now he knew the price of admission. If every bond on that list cost this, he’d be doing well to complete a single one per lifetime.

The math was brutal, and the math was simple: even a professional playboy requires willing goddesses. Leaving aside entirely whether Eun-jung would stand for him circling the other five members of T-ara — Park So-yeon and the rest would never so much as glance his way once they knew Eun-jung liked him. Collecting all six jewels of the Crown? He might as well climb the sky on a ladder.

He ran back through the bonds he knew. Eun-jung’s Jewel of the Crown. Sulli’s Human Trilogy. Mama V’s Love. Kim Ji-won’s Queen. Looked at coldly, the Queen was the only solo project on the board — at least it didn’t require juggling five separate relationships on a knife’s edge.

“Aish. Enough. It comes when it comes. Even without the system, I’ve got the next ten years in my head — I’ll do more than fine.”

Only then did he get up and head for the bathroom. With Eun-jung asleep in the bedroom, he took the common one off the living room — a slapdash wash, cold water, done. Then jacket, shoes, out the door. Breakfast errand.

In the bedroom, Eun-jung heard the front door close — a small, faraway click — and finally let her head poke out of the blanket like a periscope.

Coast clear.

She collected her clothes from the nightstand. Folded — he had folded them, and that detail would be dealt with at length in a full private post-mortem later — and got out of bed.

“Hss—!”

Pain arrived the moment her feet took her weight. Bright. Completely unscheduled. She sucked a breath in through her teeth, silently reclassified Song Ji-yeon from human being to farm animal, and got dressed in slow, wincing stages.

Dressed, she stood at the bedroom door and listened hard. Nothing. Not a step, not a faucet, not a television. She eased the door open a crack, confirmed the empty living room, and slipped out.

“Whew…”

No Song Ji-yeon. She had no idea where he’d gone at this hour, and she was not going to investigate. Having just — just that — with him, she could not currently survive eye contact. Wherever he was and whatever he was doing, it was a gift. She could be gone before he got back.

She tiptoed to the entryway to change into her shoes and go home —

Her shoes weren’t there.

Neither, she now noticed, was her bag.

She smacked her forehead. Of course. Her shoes. Her bag. Both still sitting in the entryway upstairs. In Park Ji-yeon’s entryway.

Gulp.

Her heart dropped a full floor. How did she forget that? Was the little dragon awake by now? Would she notice? Would she ask the question — unnie, where exactly did you sleep last night? — and what on earth was Eun-jung supposed to say to it?

Her face cycled white and red like a pedestrian signal. If Park Ji-yeon ever found out what had actually happened last night — with that girl’s personality? With that girl’s mouth? It would be the end of the world. Or close enough.

Forget it. Retreat first, regroup later. If Ji-yeon asks: she drank too much, she forgot her shoes and bag, keep them safe for me, the end.

Plan assembled, Eun-jung tiptoed out of Song Ji-yeon’s apartment and fled the scene.

✦ ✦ ✦

Eight a.m., Cheongdam-dong: full sun, traffic thick, a perfectly ordinary Tuesday for everyone else in the city. Song Ji-yeon jogged out of the complex to the little breakfast shop that already knew his order — abalone porridge, steamed egg — and applied himself to both with the focus of a man refueling after a long and expensive campaign.

Whatever else the ledger said about last night, breakfast was not the place to economize.

Done, he wiped his mouth, had them pack one more order — pumpkin porridge and steamed egg — and headed back. Code in, door open, breakfast into the microwave to heat. Then down the hall to the bedroom, to wake a certain sleeping beauty.

The bed was empty.

Not just empty — made. Blanket smoothed square, like housekeeping had come through. No Eun-jung on it. He checked the bathroom — no Eun-jung in it. He walked the full circuit of the apartment, twice, and came back around to the kitchen.

Standing there in front of the humming microwave, he laughed out loud.

“She ran?”

No, hold on — what was this? What did it mean?

He took out his phone and sent her a message.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Song Ji-yeon

Where’d you go?

Ham Eun-jung

Mushroom’s home alone with no one to feed him breakfast. I had to go back.

Song Ji-yeon

Don’t you have anything you want to say to me?

Read · no reply

The reply had come back fast — and he read it grinning. An excuse that transparent could double as a window. He typed his second message, sent it, and then stared at the screen for a very long time.

The status under the bubble changed to Read.

Read — and nothing. No typing indicator. No reply. Nothing, nothing, nothing.

Honestly, KakaoTalk had plenty of shortcomings next to the messaging apps he’d grown up with back in the States — but on one point it outclassed every one of them, and this was the moment to say so: the read receipt was an honest feature, and the whole world of apps ought to learn from it.

He would simply have preferred, this once, a little less honesty.
