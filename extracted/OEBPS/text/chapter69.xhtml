Chapter Sixty-Nine

Victoria’s Cold Shoulder

THE NEXT MORNING Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Cheongdam Daewoo Royal County — first floor, Song Ji-yeon’s door

He was mid-brush, foam and all, when the knocking started.

Song Ji-yeon padded over with the toothbrush still hanging out of his mouth and opened the door.

Park Ji-yeon stood in the corridor. Black trackwear, ponytail cinched high, a fine sheen of sweat still sitting on her forehead — the uniform of a girl who had just put in her morning kilometers.

“Morning, Ji-yeon.” He gave her trackwear a once-over and grinned around the toothbrush. “Sunrise cardio?”

“Morning, oppa.” She nodded, brisk and businesslike about it.

He genuinely had to admire what idol life did to people. Park Ji-yeon was already thin enough to slip under a door, and she still ran mornings to keep it that way. This rice bowl was not for everyone. Him? It took two alarms — three on a bad day — just to get him upright for an office. Anyone expecting him to run was expecting fiction.

“The breakfast tray from yesterday — I meant to bring it back down last night, but I knocked and knocked and you weren’t home.” She held the tray out with both hands, ceremonially, like the return of a state treasure.

It was, indeed, the tray he’d carried up with Tuesday’s breakfast. He took it.

“So this morning after my run, I picked you up breakfast on the way. Tray returned, debt cleared.” She produced a second bag and handed that over too.

Inside: one triangle gimbap, one iced americano.

His eye snagged on the americano. Iced. Caffeinated. Before eight in the morning, on an empty stomach. He winced in sympathy for her insides — some old restaurateur reflex he’d never managed to shake.

“That’s sweet of you.” He took bag and tray both and stepped aside. “Come in for a minute?”

“Pass. I’m all sweat.” She flapped a hand, then tilted her head, curiosity overtaking her. “Oppa… were you out all night? I knocked around ten-something and there was no answer.”

“Sulli’s wrap party. Got home past eleven.”

“Sulli?” Her eyes went instantly, dangerously round. “The Sulli? f(x) Sulli?”

“That’s the one.”

“Waah…” Delight broke across her whole face. “Oppa, you KNOW Sulli? She and I are chingus!”

He nodded — that much he’d have bet on himself. T-ara, f(x), both second-generation girl groups, the two of them close in age. Their paths had crossed a hundred times by now; being friends surprised no one.

“We’re friendly.” He smiled and gave her the short version. “Met her down on the Korea Exchange floor, of all places, and it went from there. Sent support over to her drama set twice — and when they wrapped yesterday, Director Jeon Ki-sang had her invite me along. I figured, why not. Free party.”

Park Ji-yeon listened with her eyes shining, and — for once in her life — did not follow the thread to its gossip. She just sighed it out.

“Oppa knows everybody…”

He let that one pass unclaimed. Something else had occurred to him.

“By the way, Ji-yeon. Eun-jung’s place in Apgujeong — the PIEN PLUS building. Do you happen to know the floor and unit?”

He knew the building. The unit he’d never learned — the plan had been to stand in the lobby and call her like a delivery man. But with a walking T-ara directory standing right here, why not just ask.

“Eun-jung-unnie’s place?” A blink — why would oppa want that was written all over her face — but she answered honestly anyway. “Sixth floor, I think… 603?”

“603.” He filed it away.

“Are you going to unnie’s place?”

“Mm. There’s something I need to see her about.” He didn’t elaborate.

“Oh…”

She knew better than to push. But something had clearly happened between those two — she could smell it — and it had clearly happened the night before last, and she was already half out of her body with the itch to run home and file a full report with Hyo-min-unnie. She pointed at the bag in his hand.

“Eat your breakfast, oppa. I’m off!”

A wave, a spin, and she was gone up the stairs.

Song Ji-yeon watched her bounce out of sight and shook his head. So the iced-americano theory had legs. Look what the caffeine had done to the little dragon.

He shut the door and laid breakfast out on the table. The gimbap, he’d eat. The americano — the less of that, the better. He traded it for a bottle of milk from the fridge, sat down, and, between bites, checked his phone.

Still nothing from Ham Eun-jung.

From yesterday morning until now — past the twenty-four-hour mark.

He set the phone face-down, finished breakfast at speed, changed, and left for work.

TEN O’CLOCK Mapo-gu, Seoul SAVORY F&B — the Sajangnim’s office

A few documents were waiting on his desk for signature. He sat, skimmed, signed. Then he logged into Sulli’s account and did the housekeeping he’d promised her: Samsung Electronics out — the whole position liquidated — and every won of her three hundred million rolled into Sewon Electronics.

Ten minutes. Everything squared away.

The rest of the day, then, was dedicated to slacking. Not that there was much to slack with — this was 2012, and the office computer’s gaming menu ran about three titles deep. And the company network was bad. League of Legends lagged on it. Genuinely lagged.

He was not citing his 2/12/6 record as evidence.

He absolutely was not.

FIVE FIFTY Apgujeong-dong, Seoul ONGI Apgujeong — second floor, the Bamboo room

At five-thirty he left the office and drove straight for Apgujeong. By five-fifty he was coming in through the restaurant’s back door, and the store manager hurried over at once.

“Sajangnim, welcome. Miss Sulli and her friend are already here — the Bamboo room, upstairs.”

“Mm.” He peeled off his coat and handed it to the server at his elbow. “Put a pot of Da Hong Pao together for the room, would you.”

He straightened his collar and headed for the stairs.

The Bamboo room door swung open on Sulli in mid-sentence, leaning across the table, vividly briefing the woman opposite. She spotted him and shot upright so fast the tableware jumped.

“Oppa! You came!”

He smiled at her — and then his eyes moved to the woman across the table.

She had risen too.

White shirt. Black trousers. Long hair loose, makeup light. Taller than he’d pictured, and carrying herself with that composed, quietly competent air — a leader’s posture, the kind that gets appointed once and never questioned again.

Victoria. Song Qian. f(x)’s leader.

He looked at her, and she looked right back — a slow, level sweep, top to bottom and back, taking measurements.

Her gaze was calm, appraising, and shuttered. A door politely closed before it could be opened.

“Annyeonghaseyo, Song Ji-yeon-ssi.” She spoke first, and the words came out starched and formal, a degree of frost folded into the politeness. “I’m Victoria.”

“Annyeonghaseyo, Victoria-ssi.” He put his hand out. “I’ve known your name a long time. It’s good to finally stand in front of it.”

The handshake lasted exactly one pump and ended. On her side, at least, it had clearly been scheduled as a formality.

He took it in stride, walked around the table, and drew out the head seat as naturally as if he were home — then gestured the two of them down with an easy please.

Sulli immediately towed Victoria into the chairs beside each other, directly across from him.

“Oppa, this is our leader — Vic-unnie!” She presented her like a prize. Then, swiveling: “Unnie, this is him! The Song Ji-yeon-oppa I told you about!”

“We’ve been introduced.” Victoria’s voice stayed level and unornamented. She looked at him without one gram of smile, all business, all border checkpoint. “Song Ji-yeon-ssi. Let’s skip to the matter at hand. Sulli says you want to discuss an endorsement with me.”

Well, then.

He blinked once. The Victoria of his memory — assembled across another lifetime of variety reruns and fancams — had never been this cold. Had he offended her somehow? In this life or the last?

He had no idea. So he did the sensible thing and glanced sideways — at Sulli, who had gone stiff and was studying the ceiling with the diligence of a scholar preparing for exams.

Ah.

So the problem lived on your side of the table.
