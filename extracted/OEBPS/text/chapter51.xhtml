Chapter Fifty-One

Smart Little Dragon!

MID-MORNING Cheongdam Daewoo Royal County, Phase Five Song Ji-yeon’s flat — first floor

The car threaded a few busy streets and pulled back into his complex.

The building was modern — dark-gray stone and glass curtain wall, lines cut clean and unhesitating, conspicuous even among Cheongdam-dong’s towers. At the entrance sat a guard house with actual guards on duty; getting in meant a card swipe or a face scan.

He usually took the car down into the underground lot — one swipe and the elevator carried you straight up to your floor, the least hassle available. As it happened, this was his first time driving into the complex since the day he’d viewed the unit.

“Wow. This place is fancy.” Park Ji-yeon climbed out and tipped her head back at the building.

“It’s decent. The selling point is the security — you sleep easy here.” He walked up to the door, keyed in the code, and the lock chirped open. He pushed the door wide and stood aside. “Come in.”

Park Ji-yeon was through the doorway in a heartbeat — and then she stopped dead on the spot.

She stood there, mouth slightly open, for a long moment, words entirely unavailable.

The living room opened in front of her: enormous, unpartitioned. Sixty, seventy square meters at the least, pale-gray hardwood underfoot, warm and yielding. No main light on the ceiling — instead, ranks of recessed spots and one hidden run of cove lighting, all of it glowing a soft, unhurried warm that wrapped the whole space.

What pulled the eye hardest was the wall of glass at the far end — floor to ceiling, giving the courtyard back whole.

A deep-gray fabric sofa anchored the middle of the room, a marble coffee table before it, a spare tea set and a single book arranged on top. Opposite the sofa ran a full television wall, a huge LCD sunk flush into the cabinetry, open shelving to either side holding a few ceramic pieces and a run of hardcovers.

To the right, the kitchen — open, minimal, pure-white cabinetry with black stone counters, a line of metal pendants hanging over the island. Stove, oven, dishwasher: all built in, all invisible until wanted. Beside the island, a small breakfast bar with two tall stools.

To the left, a corridor ran back toward the bedrooms.

“Oppa, this place… is AMAZING!” Park Ji-yeon finally rebooted. She kicked off her shoes, went barefoot in a full lap of the living room, then planted both palms on the floor-to-ceiling glass. “And the VIEW! You can see the entire courtyard!”

A low wall of pale beige stone closed the courtyard into a whole, its top thick with green vine. From the door, a flagstone path unspooled and wandered off between scattered plantings; on the far side lay a lawn leveled like carpet; in the corner, a set of raw-wood outdoor furniture waited for nobody in particular.

“Oppa, you live TOO well!” She was still inventorying everything in sight.

“It’s alright.” He rubbed his nose and laughed, a little sheepish. “The private courtyard is exactly why I picked this unit. And then after I bought it, it turned out I never once had time to actually sit out there…”

“A big flat PLUS a private courtyard?” Her eyes went perfectly round. “How big even is this place?”

“Not that big. The house is a bit over two hundred square meters — with the courtyard, call it three hundred-odd.” He said it the way a person mentions the weather.

“Three hundred-odd and that’s NOT big?” Park Ji-yeon sucked in a sharp breath and whipped around to Eun-jung. “Unnie. Are you hearing this? Three hundred square meters and he says it’s not big!”

Eun-jung had long since built up the immunity — this was hardly her first visit; today’s pickup run had begun, after all, in this exact living room.

Mushroom, the moment the door opened, had gone feral with joy — a few victory laps of the living room, then he collected his chew bone, flopped onto the mat by the glass, and let the sun do its work, eyes slitting shut in luxury.

Eun-jung changed her shoes and came in, looking around, and even she felt the small lift of it. The interior genuinely was good — minimal and generous at once, with something underneath the surface.

“Come on — grand tour.” He beckoned and led the two of them down the corridor.

An abstract canvas hung along the hallway wall — bold color, loose confident line, a touch of gallery in an otherwise quiet space.

Three rooms opened off the corridor.

The master, first. Spacious, with its own wall of glass and light pouring in. A wide double bed in pale-gray linens stood against a dark upholstered headboard wall, a wall sconce to each side throwing soft light; a bench lay at the foot of the bed, and opposite it stretched an entire wall of wardrobe — matte white, handle-less, quietly expensive. The en suite behind it kept the shower and the tub separated, with a double-sink vanity, toiletries ranked along the counter in perfect order.

“Master bedroom. Own bath, own walk-in closet.” He kept the commentary minimal.

Then the second bedroom — smaller than the master, but roomy all the same, with its own bathroom and a compact walk-in closet.

“The two guest rooms are about the same spec, both with en suites. Very livable.”

Last, the study. Not large, but put together with care: one full wall of shelving, Korean titles and English originals side by side with a slim row of Chinese cookbooks he’d hunted down for the hotpot canon, a desk set by the window, laptop and lamp squared away on top.

“When there’s nothing on, this is where I read. Handle a bit of work.”

By the end of the circuit, Park Ji-yeon had fully, unconditionally surrendered.

She drifted back to the living room and dropped into the sofa, sinking to a considerable depth into the cushions, and let out a long sigh of pure satisfaction.

“Oppa, your house is TOO comfortable. I don’t want to leave!”

“So? Going to consider renting in my complex?” He settled into the armchair and put the question to her with a smile.

“Rent! Absolutely rent!” She sat bolt upright, every word nailed down. “Oppa, ask the rep if that second-floor unit’s still free — I want to move in TOMORROW!”

The naked urgency cracked both of them up, and he went to the fridge and came back with two bottles of sparkling water.

“Drink something first. I’ll message the rep about viewing the second-floor show flat.”

She took hers with a word of thanks, twisted the cap, and drank a third of it in one pull — cold bubbles sliding down her throat, her eyes narrowing in comfort.

And it was right about then that she noticed Mushroom, off to the side, set down the chew bone he’d been working at. Which raised a question she’d been quietly sitting on: where had the chew bone even come from? She was fairly sure there hadn’t been one the entire way over.

Before she could pursue it, Mushroom stretched — a long, luxurious, full-body stretch — then set off on four short legs, unerringly, for the kitchen.

Curiosity officially hooked, she propped her chin on her hand and tracked the white furball.

Mushroom walked straight up to the row of pure-white cabinets, stopped at the end door, lifted one front paw, and began — pat, pat, pat — worrying at it.

Strange.

What’s he doing? Thirsty? But then how does he know there’s anything in there?

She glanced instinctively toward Eun-jung, the question already forming—

—but before a word got out, Song Ji-yeon, as though summoned, set down his phone and crossed the room. Bent. Opened the cabinet door with the ease of long practice. Produced: dog food, and a dog bowl.

Gulp.

And the facts began arriving in Park Ji-yeon’s head at high speed. Why did Mushroom navigate Song Ji-yeon-oppa’s apartment like a cab driver on his home turf? Why did oppa’s kitchen stock Mushroom’s dog food? Wasn’t Mushroom Eun-jung-unnie’s dog? Everything — everything — was connecting.

There could be only one truth: the two of them were LIVING TOGETHER!!!

Smart little dragon — that’s me.

She transferred the full beam of her wisdom onto Eun-jung and held it there, and held it there, until Eun-jung’s scalp began to prickle.

What in the world is this little dragon thinking up now?
