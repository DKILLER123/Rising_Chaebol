Chapter Sixty-Eight

Two Birds, One Stone

SAME NIGHT · ALMOST MIDNIGHT Cheongdam-dong, Seoul f(x) company dorm — the living room

“You—!”

The freshly suppressed fire roared straight back up Victoria’s throat.

Krystal lunged into the breach.

“Sulli — Vic-unnie’s on your side here! The man’s a total unknown. Unnie running a background check for you — isn’t that a good thing?”

“He is NOT a total unknown!” Sulli fired back. “He’s a hotpot-restaurant Sajangnim!”

“So what if he is?” Victoria laughed — the angry kind. “A hotpot Sajangnim can’t run a scam? What is it going to take with you?”

She looked at Sulli’s pressed-together lips and understood the arithmetic: pressure had officially stopped working. Time for another strategy.

If she couldn’t farm the number out of her — then she’d meet the man face to face, and deliver the warning in person.

Her voice came down to nearly gentle.

“I only want to set a time with him and discuss the endorsement properly. We can’t keep routing this through you as messenger girl — how professional would that look?”

A reason that sounded airtight.

Sulli still wasn’t buying it. She tilted her head, thought it over — and came back with her own terms.

“The endorsement, fine — but we all go together. And the number stays with me.”

The meaning needed no translation: full supervision, start to finish. Zero window for any private contact.

Victoria took one look at that fortification — the you-two-will-never-be-alone-in-a-room fortification — and didn’t know whether to laugh or cry. What settled in, in the end, was a deep, quiet helplessness.

What could she do? The chick’s wings had come in. She flew her own routes now.

Still — the plan had landed where she wanted it. Get the three of them into one room, and Sulli would see this “very nice oppa” of hers with her own two eyes, clear as daylight.

She sighed, nodded, and arranged her face into surrender.

“Fine. Settled, then.” The ruling came down. “You contact your Song Ji-yeon-oppa and pick a time. The three of us will sit down — and settle this endorsement face to face.”

She laid her full weight on the three of us, and on face to face.

Sulli finally exhaled. The sun came back out on her face, and she nodded along, bright again.

“Okay, Vic-unnie! I’ll contact oppa tomorrow!”

Krystal, spectating as the mother-daughter exchange volleyed to its finish — ending, of all things, in Victoria’s capitulation — could only shake her head.

She leaned in to Sulli’s ear and lowered her voice to a whisper.

“Yah, Choi Jin-ri. Bold these days, aren’t you — even Vic-unnie gets a no from you. All of this for a man. Worth it?”

Sulli only grinned at her, and said nothing.

Victoria, watching that unkillable grin, felt one more wave of helplessness roll through. She reached over, picked up Sulli’s phone from the coffee table, unlocked it, and handed it back.

“Call him. Now.”

“Eh — now?” Sulli blinked at the phone. “It’s nearly twelve. Oppa’s probably asleep.”

“Then text him.” No give anywhere in it. “Say I want to discuss the endorsement. Ask him when he has time.”

There was no exit. Phone in hand, pinned under two sets of eyes, Sulli opened KakaoTalk at sloth speed, found Song Ji-yeon’s avatar, and started typing.

For a while, the living room held nothing but the small percussion of her thumbs.

✦ ✦ ✦

MIDNIGHT Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Cheongdam Daewoo Royal County — the first-floor flat

Song Ji-yeon walked into his flat at midnight.

Underground garage, elevator, door. He had spent half the evening marinating in wrap-party noise, and the whole of his remaining ambition was one shower and one bed.

The entryway sensor light came on as he stepped in.

Shoes changed, keys tossed onto the cabinet, straight into the kitchen, one bottle of ice water out of the fridge.

The cold ran down his throat and jump-started the higher functions.

He was leaning at the counter, raising the bottle for a second go—

Bzzt. Bzzt.

—when the phone on the living-room coffee table buzzed twice.

This late — who?

Bottle down. He walked over and picked it up.

A new KakaoTalk message. From Sulli.

Odd — hadn’t she just gone upstairs? Forgotten something?

He unlocked the screen and opened it.

KakaoTalk · Sulli

Sulli

Oppa — are you asleep?

Sulli

Vic-unnie says she wants to discuss the endorsement with you in person. She’s asking when you’d have time.

He read it twice, blinking.

Victoria wanted to talk endorsement — in person?

Less than an hour ago, by Sulli’s account, the answer had been: not my call, go through SM’s business division. And now the posture had flipped?

He didn’t chew on it long. A Chinese woman captaining a girl group on the Peninsula — a certain carefulness came with the job description. Presumably she’d talked it through with Sulli once she was home, and the thinking had moved. If she was willing to talk, he was happy to talk.

He picked the phone up properly and ran tomorrow’s grid.

Wednesday. Nothing much waiting at the company — only a few purchase orders needing his signature. Which meant: office day.

His thumb paused over the screen. Something else surfaced.

Ham Eun-jung.

All day today — the one message he’d sent her that morning. Don’t you have anything you want to say to me?

It had gone into the ocean like a stone. Morning to nearly midnight.

A full day.

He didn’t know what she was thinking.

Last night — they’d both been drinking. Not much, but alcohol is a catalyst, and there was no arguing with the chemistry. And it was she who had kissed him first.

And then — he’d woken, and she was gone.

Not one word left behind.

He leaned back into the sofa and stared at the ceiling.

He needed to see Ham Eun-jung face to face. Put the whole thing into words. What exactly were they now? This needed a name. And she couldn’t hide from him forever.

And as it happened — Eun-jung’s family home was in Apgujeong. And ONGI kept a branch in Apgujeong.

The idea assembled itself. He typed.

KakaoTalk · Sulli

Song Ji-yeon

Tomorrow works.

Song Ji-yeon

I’m at the company during the day, though. How about six o’clock at the ONGI Apgujeong branch? I’ll book us a private room.

Apgujeong, for two reasons stacked on top of each other: it was his turf, for one — and it sat a short walk from Eun-jung’s front door. He’d leave the office, keep the six o’clock appointment, then go up and knock on Eun-jung’s door.

Two birds, one stone.

The reply landed before he’d even set the phone down.

KakaoTalk · Sulli

Sulli

Daebak — oppa’s hotpot restaurants are everywhere?! There’s one in Gangnam too?!

Song Ji-yeon

ONGI has seven stores in Seoul, all company-run. We’re expanding — there’ll be one in Cheongdam-dong before long.

Sulli

Jinjja?! Then it’s a date — tomorrow at six! Be there or be square!!!🍑🔥

He remembered explaining the store count to her at dinner once — in detail. This little peach had retained exactly none of it.

He read the string of exclamation points — pure Sulli, her whole fingerprint right there in the punctuation — and the corner of his mouth curved on its own.

He tossed the phone onto the sofa, drained the ice water in one go, binned the bottle, killed the lights, and headed for the bedroom. Shower. Sleep.
