Chapter Eight

Jeon Bo-ram: Are You the Devil?!

SEPTEMBER 2012 · OPENING DAY T-ara’s Dorm, Seoul Late afternoon — the clock is ticking

The three of them were still tangled in the wreckage when Eun-jung caught the time on her phone screen — and shot off the sofa like she’d been ejected.

“Yah, what time is it?!” One hand scrambled to gather her wrecked hair; her bare feet slapped across the floorboards hunting for slippers. “I’m going to be late!”

Park Ji-yeon was still draped over Bo-ram. She lifted her head at that, eyes lighting up like a leopard cub that had caught the smell of meat.

“Late? Where? With who?”

“With… my eomma.”

Eun-jung braked just in time, told the small lie, and committed to it — she bent down with elaborate calm, fished the missing slipper out from under the coffee table, and pressed it into Bo-ram’s hands.

“Sorry, Bo-ram-unnie, I’ve gotta run. Eomma’s waiting for me at home.”

Bo-ram accepted the slipper and sat up out of the ruins, hair still a bird’s nest, glasses still crooked across her face. She delivered her verdict in the voice of a wronged ghost.

“That’s it, then? Leaving? Abandoning your two dear sisters here?”

“Exactly!” Park Ji-yeon picked up the thread instantly, vaulting off the sofa and planting herself at the entryway, arms crossed, auditing Eun-jung’s shoe-tying like a customs officer. “You’re being bizarre, unnie. You never rush anywhere. Today you’re flying out the door without even brushing your hair. You are going to see the restaurant man.”

Eun-jung’s hands paused on her laces. The tips of her ears were going pink again. Her mouth, however, refused to surrender.

“I’m going home. To eat. Dinner.”

Park Ji-yeon and Jeon Bo-ram answered in flawless unison, their skepticism so thick it was practically dripping off them.

“Dinner.”

Eun-jung gave up on logic entirely, grabbed her bag, and pulled the door open. Even as it swung shut behind her, Park Ji-yeon’s voice came chasing down the hallway.

“Unnie! We’ll miss you!”

The door clicked fully shut. The corridor outside went quiet.

At the elevators, Eun-jung took a deep breath and fanned her burning cheeks with one hand. That Ji-yeon kid… entirely, entirely too much.

Back inside the apartment, the instant the lock dropped, seventy percent of the mischief drained out of Park Ji-yeon’s face. What replaced it was the focus of a hunter who has just found the trail.

She turned, slowly, toward Jeon Bo-ram, who was on the sofa re-tying her hair.

The two of them stared at each other for roughly two seconds.

“Tail her?”

Bo-ram clamped the hair tie between her teeth and mumbled around it.

“Bad idea.”

Park Ji-yeon did not argue. Her thumbs were already moving.

KakaoTalk · Park Ji-yeon · sitting one meter away

Park Ji-yeon

Doesn’t unnie want to see what Eun-jung-unnie’s guy friend looks like?

Park Ji-yeon

Unnie. Aren’t you scared she’s being sweet-talked by some creep?

Park Ji-yeon

As Eun-jung-unnie’s very best friends, don’t we owe it to her to vet this man?

Jeon Bo-ram

Are you the Devil?!!!

Fifteen seconds later, two figures slipped out of the apartment, moving with the practiced stealth of career criminals.

By then Eun-jung’s taxi had already rounded the corner. Park Ji-yeon snatched the very next cab off the street and stuffed Bo-ram — still tying her shoes — into the back seat.

“Ajusshi! Follow that orange taxi!”

She hung over the front seatback, vibrating, excited as though this were a stakeout on a cheating husband.

Stakeout! Stakeout! Go, go, go!

The driver, a man in his fifties, gave the two of them exactly one glance in the rearview mirror. His expression did not move. He flicked the turn signal and pulled out after the orange taxi without a word. Twenty years driving a cab in Seoul — there was nothing left for him to see.

SEPTEMBER 2012 · OPENING DAY Cheongdam-dong, Seoul Song Ji-yeon’s apartment

The bathroom mirror had fogged over with the steam of a hot shower. Song Ji-yeon toweled his hair and swiped a hand across the glass.

Then he went still.

The man in the mirror.

He frowned, turned sideways, turned back, and double-checked twice before he could accept that it was actually him.

Abs.

A full eight-pack, laid out in tidy rows.

He looked down at his stomach, then up at the mirror, expression deeply complicated. In his past life he had never exactly had a gut, but nobody would have called that a physique either. At best, the category had been “not fat.”

This body, though. The lines of the waist and stomach were clean and sharp, the muscle cut so distinctly under the bathroom’s warm light that it looked sculpted — some artist’s finished work, signed and stepped away from.

He tensed his core experimentally. The eight-pack popped out even harder.

He was quiet for a moment, poked the abs with one finger — real — and then couldn’t hold back a laugh.

So the body enhancement ships with fitness results included? Honestly? I’d faint ten more times for this.

He fixed his hair in the mirror, went into the walk-in closet, and came out with a navy casual shirt, sleeves rolled carelessly to the forearm, dark-gray slacks, and a pair of clean white sneakers. Simple and sharp. Not trying too hard, not sloppy.

One last look in the mirror. A satisfied nod. He took the car keys off the tray in the entryway and went out.

Walking through the complex, an utterly unrelated detail surfaced in his mind.

Would Eun-jung tie her hair up today?

Every time he’d seen her, she’d worn it down. It was short, sure — but for eating, up would be easier, wouldn’t it? High ponytail, or low? He caught himself actually deliberating the question in earnest, laughed at himself, and shook his head.

The black Mercedes blinked its lights twice. He opened the driver’s door and dropped in, and the moment the engine turned over, his phone screen lit. A message from Ham Eun-jung.

KakaoTalk · Ham Eun-jung

Ham Eun-jung

Are you on your way? I’m already on my way!

Song Ji-yeon

Just pulled out. Isn’t the restaurant basically downstairs from you? Sit tight when you get there — I’ve got us Room 3.

He hit send, pressed the gas, and the Mercedes rolled up out of the underground garage and melted into Cheongdam-dong’s evening traffic.

SEPTEMBER 2012 · OPENING NIGHT Apgujeong-dong, Seoul One orange taxi, one black Mercedes, one hotpot house

The taxi followed at a leisurely pace, through two intersections, then turned down a street lined with plane trees.

Jeon Bo-ram had her face practically against the window, watching the storefronts slide past one by one. The excitement on her face was curdling, gradually, into confusion.

“Ji-yeon-ah.” She nudged her crooked glasses back up. “Isn’t this the way to Eun-jung’s house?”

Park Ji-yeon had noticed too.

The orange taxi up ahead was slowing. It stopped in front of an apartment building.

They had been there countless times.

Eun-jung’s family home.

“Huh?” A thread of disappointment crept into Park Ji-yeon’s voice — but she refused to fold. “Wait. Maybe she’s just making a stop first…”

Eun-jung climbed out — and didn’t head for the building. She turned instead toward the shop next door.

It wasn’t a big storefront. The sign read ONGI HOTPOT, warm yellow light spilling out through the glass doors, a red lantern hanging at either side of the entrance.

“Look! LOOK!”

A smack landed on Jeon Bo-ram’s thigh, hard enough to suck air in through her teeth.

“Unnie, look! She’s not going home! She’s going into the restaurant!”

Bo-ram rubbed her reddening thigh and leaned over to look anyway.

Eun-jung’s figure disappeared through the hotpot house doors. Bo-ram turned back to Park Ji-yeon and found the girl’s eyes already ablaze — a bonfire of gossip, fully lit and hunting for fuel.

“Unnie. Out. Now.”

“W-wait a second, Ji-yeon-ah — isn’t this, I don’t know, a little—” Bo-ram made her final stand. “If Eun-jung catches us, can you even imagine how awkward—”

Park Ji-yeon had already paid the fare and was hauling her out of the cab by the wrist.

“She won’t catch us! We’ll watch from far away — one peek, and we go!”

The two of them slunk into the hotpot house and slid into a booth by the corridor to the private rooms, doing their best impression of casual.

It was the dinner rush. The main hall was dotted with occupied tables, and the steam rolling up off a dozen bubbling pots blurred everything pleasantly.

Park Ji-yeon went up on tiptoe and swept the hall once. No Eun-jung.

“She’s in a private room.” Bo-ram pointed one finger at the closed door at the end of the corridor, her tone perfectly flat. She genuinely did not care about her bandmates’ love lives — if Park Ji-yeon hadn’t nagged her into this, she would never have come along on such an escapade at all.

Park Ji-yeon clicked her tongue and was about to say something when her gaze snagged on a black Mercedes gliding to a stop in front of the restaurant.

The driver’s door opened. A young man stepped out, shut the door, locked the car with a flick, and walked toward the hotpot house at an unhurried pace.

Park Ji-yeon yanked Bo-ram’s sleeve. The two of them shrank deep into their booth, fast.

The man came right past them without a glance, pushed through the inner door, and went on into the restaurant.
