Chapter Seventy-Two

Keep Hiding, Eun-jung-unnie!

THE NEXT MORNING Apgujeong-dong, Seoul PIEN PLUS, sixth floor — Eun-jung’s bedroom

First light squeezed in through the gap in the curtains and laid one thin stripe across the dark floor. The room held nothing but the sound of two people breathing, slow and even.

Eun-jung’s lashes trembled, once, and lifted.

The first order of business was the careful extraction of her long, very pretty leg from where it had spent the night — draped across a certain someone’s waist.

So unruly. First thing in the morning. She spat the accusation silently, at no one, in the privacy of her own skull.

The firmness she’d felt through the cotton brought the whole of that night back in a single flash replay, and her cheeks began warming up all on their own, from the inside.

Song Ji-yeon slept on — flat on his back, one arm threaded around her waist and anchored over her, holding her in place with a sleeper’s honest grip. Deep asleep. Guard entirely down.

Eun-jung lay on her side with only his profile in view. And asleep, he was a different product line entirely: the permanent flicker of a joke had left his face, the contours had gone soft, and his lashes — unfairly long — threw small shadows beneath his eyes.

She lay there, perfectly still, and watched him. Her thoughts ran in circles.

We’re… together.

We’re just — together. Like that.

Last night he’d said let’s be together, and she had never actually said yes. But both of them had known. The silence that nodded. The staying. Those were the answer.

It didn’t feel real yet. It felt like dreaming with her eyes open.

From that absurd night at the hotpot restaurant, to him surfacing again and again wherever she turned, to last night’s dinner at her own coffee table and that blunt, unguarded confession — all of it had happened too fast. Fast enough that when she looked back on it now, she felt like she was standing on a cloud: lovely view, no floor.

She lifted one finger and traced him through the air, an inch above his skin. Forehead. The high bridge of his nose. His lips.

Her gaze slid down his jaw and along the blanket’s edge — and her brain, uninvited, replayed her own hand wandering across his stomach that night. The heartbeat picked up. Considerably.

Heat climbed her face. She shook her head, hard, trying to fling the whole thought-collection out of it.

The body, however, was more honest than the brain.

Her hand had already slipped out from under the blanket — she had no memory of authorizing this — crossed the small neutral zone between them, and slid, slowly, experimentally, to his waist. Through the thin cotton: warm skin, firm muscle. Her palm was scalding. Her fingers moved, one slow line at a time, reading the ridges of his stomach like text in the dark.

And just as the hand prepared to push its investigation further south — toward the exact firmness that had woken her—

Her wrist was seized.

A large hand, strong beyond appeal, clamped around hers and put a full stop to the entire expedition.

Her heart dropped a beat.

She jerked her head up.

Song Ji-yeon was awake — had been awake for who knew how long — and his eyes were on her, unblinking. The eyes that were always carrying some smile had gone dark and deep, with a small flame burning at the bottom of them. Bright enough to light the whole room by.

The air in the bedroom congealed on the spot.

“Eun-jung…”

Sleep still rough in his voice, every word unhurried, and threaded through with something not remotely innocent.

“Don’t play with fire.”

In his defense, there were rules. The abs were, technically, public property — window-shopping tolerated, opening hours generous. But territory south of a certain border operated on a strict touch-at-your-own-risk policy, and the policy was enforced by a junior partner who did not, as a rule, accept returns.

Boom.

Eun-jung’s face went from warm to fry-an-egg hot in one second flat. His eyes. That sentence. Her whole body had lost its standing instructions.

She tried to reclaim her hand. His grip didn’t move a millimeter.

“I— I wasn’t—”

The stammer arrived in pieces, and the pieces did not assemble into a sentence.

He looked at her — panic in full bloom, ears crimson, an entire crime confessed in the attempted cover-up — and finally, mercifully, let go.

Amnesty. Her hand shot back into the blanket like a startled turtle. And then, as if the blanket itself had bitten her, she bolted upright, flung the covers off, and vaulted off the bed—

“I’m going to wash up!”

—and fled, announcing her destination to no one, into the bathroom.

Slam.

Peace, at last, returned to the world.

Song Ji-yeon lay there staring at the shut frosted-glass door. A good few seconds passed. Then a low laugh finally worked its way out of him, unstopperable. He raised his hand and touched the spot her fingers had mapped — her warmth apparently still on file there.

“Heh.”

So cute.

He sat up and stretched until every joint filed its report — all systems realigned. He’d slept wonderfully. The steadiest sleep in months, actually. Whether that had anything to do with the person sleeping attached to his ribs was a matter he declined to investigate under oath.

He took a slow look around her bedroom. Not large, and immaculate. A white wardrobe. A vanity in pale natural wood. The wide bed under gray sheets. On the vanity, a rank of skincare bottles; beside them, a small jewelry box. Everything in the room smelled faintly of her life.

His mood, already good, improved further.

Out of bed, barefoot across the floor, straight to the window — and the curtains came open with one pull. Sunlight flooded the entire room at once.

He squinted through the brightness, adjusted, and walked out.

In the living room, Mushroom lifted his head from his dog bed at the commotion, took his measure of the situation, wagged his tail twice at half power — greeting concluded — and put his head back down.

Song Ji-yeon went to the kitchen and opened the fridge. The inventory: several bottles of water, a few beers, some eggs, and a bag of frozen dumplings. He considered the shelves, extracted the eggs, and went prospecting through the cupboards until he struck a frying pan and a bottle of cooking oil.

Fried-egg sandwiches. Simple, honest, achievable. Breakfast would be served.

Ten minutes later, he was beating eggs in a bowl when the bathroom door opened. Eun-jung shuffled out in a soft loungewear set, damp hair hanging loose on either side of her face, her skin still dewy from washing — pink and fresh as a perfectly ripe peach.

She spotted the man cooking in her kitchen and blinked.

“I thought you’d left.”

“Making breakfast.” He glanced back and grinned. “Unlike a certain someone, I don’t do dawn getaways.”

“Yah! That was… that was…”

Her face went red on the spot, and the excuse she needed failed to arrive. The truth was, that morning, she hadn’t known why she’d fled either — her head had simply gone white, and her legs had filed for departure, and that had been the entire decision-making process.

“All right, all right — I know you’re shy.” He took one look at her bashfulness and graciously retired the topic, turning back to the stove. “Fried-egg sandwiches for breakfast, then? There’s nothing else in this kitchen.”

She nodded and took up a post leaning against the kitchen doorway, watching him work the burner — flame, oil, egg poured in — smooth, practiced, entirely at home.

Sizzle.

The smell of eggs cooking spread fast.

She watched his back. Tall, lean, dressed in the plain T-shirt she had bought — sleeves rolled to the elbows, one clean line of forearm showing. The morning sun came in through the window behind him and edged his whole silhouette in gold.

And somewhere in her chest, quietly, a locked place gave way.

Having a man around the house… isn’t bad at all.

✦ ✦ ✦

THAT MORNING Seoul, en route to CCM the Mercedes — idling at a red light

After breakfast he drove her out. Today was Friday — the last day of September — and Eun-jung was due at CCM for the month-end review and the planning meeting for October.

“Eun-jung. How much is actually left on your schedules for the year?”

A red light; he stopped the car properly and turned to ask. The buyout was coming. Whatever overseas work Kim Kwang-soo had already booked would travel with the contracts when they changed hands — and every one of those bookings was a hole shot through Song Ji-yeon’s own plans for the group’s year.

Eun-jung had no idea what he was asking for, but she pulled out her phone anyway and found the schedule. The page was sparse enough to hurt. Her brows folded together as she read it out, deflating by the word.

“From October through the end of the year… the group has five overseas engagements. Domestic — not even one… I don’t know about the other members’ individual schedules… I don’t have any of those, anyway.”

He heard it — the sag in her voice, the arithmetic of a group that used to run on all cylinders now down to a trickle. The gap between what they were and what they’d been was doing what gaps do.

“Don’t sound so glum.” He smiled. “Once I’ve signed you all, the schedules will stack up faster than you can complain about them. Just don’t turn around and call me a heartless capitalist then.”

Because they would stack up. The China market didn’t open with one concert poster — it opened the old-fashioned way, city after city, stage after stage, commercial gig upon commercial gig. There was no skipping that step and no substituting for it. Which meant T-ara living out of suitcases, Seoul in one hand and Shanghai in the other, until the map was theirs.

Eun-jung laughed despite herself and nodded.

“Then be careful with Ji-yeon. She’ll never survive one of your death-march schedules.”

He laughed too. The light went green, and the car pulled back into the flow.

At the CCM building he brought the car to the curb. Eun-jung unclipped her belt and reached for the door—

“I’m here. Going in—”

—and a warm hand closed around her wrist.

She turned. He was looking at her, with no visible intention of letting go.

“What is it?”

“Did you forget something?” Utterly casual — the same tone a man uses when he’s reminding you not to leave your phone behind.

Forget something? Baffled, she inspected her little bag. Unzipped it. Phone: present. Wallet: present. The car key was his — not hers to forget. She patted her pockets: empty.

“No.” She looked up, puzzled. “I’ve got everything. Nothing forgotten.”

He shook his head. Released her wrist. Then raised his right hand and, with one index finger, tapped his own right cheek. Once.

No words. He just looked at her.

A simple gesture.

Eun-jung decoded it in real time.

Boom. The heat went off like a struck match — neck first, then cheeks, then the very tips of her ears.

“You—”

Her mouth opened. Nothing equipped it.

This was the foot of CCM’s building. Colleagues, hoobaes — anyone could walk past at any second. If someone saw—

She put in her plea with her eyes alone. Those pretty eyes, begging and blushing in equal parts — a startled fawn asking a hunter for mercy.

The hunter was unmoved. Same posture. The faintest smile. Waiting, patiently, like a man who had cleared his entire morning for this.

Stalemate. The cabin was small; the air in it thinned by the second. Her heartbeat had taken up drumming directly on her eardrums. She stole one more look at him — plain white T-shirt, sun through the windshield, clean and fresh and entirely too pleased with himself. His patience was invincible. She had no move left on the board.

She bit her lip. One lightning glance out the window — early still, the entrance nearly clear, just a few trainee-looking girls walking in together, still a good distance from the car.

It should… it should be safe.

The war inside her ran hot for a while longer. Then she surrendered.

“Just… just one, okay.”

A voice the size of a mosquito.

She drew one deep breath — the breath of a woman signing something binding — closed her eyes, leaned in, and went for his cheek. Lightly. All-in. Done before she could lose her nerve.

The instant her lips were about to land on that cheek—

Song Ji-yeon turned his head.

Her lips landed, warm and soft and precise, on not-cheek.

His mouth.

Eun-jung’s eyes snapped open. Her entire body went to stone. Her brain presented a clean, blank sheet.

His face hung enlarged in front of her — close enough to count lashes — and the eyes above the smile were lit up like a trap that had worked exactly as designed.

The kiss was light. Lips only; nothing deeper. But the impact, for her, exceeded spec by a wide margin.

One second. Two. Then she came back online like she’d touched a live wire — recoiling so hard her back slammed the seat.

“You— you cheated!”

Red as a ripe tomato, and the sentence barely survived the trip out.

He watched her — flustered, furious, adorable beyond all reason — and the low laugh escaped before he could vet it.

“You kissed me yourself.” Innocence itself.

“I—”

Checkmate. She glared at him with everything she had left — a glare with the destructive power of a plush toy; if anything, it landed as aegyo with extra steps. She was done spending words on this man. One more second in this car and her face was going to file for independence.

Hands scrabbling — one try, two tries, three — she finally got the door open and practically bailed out onto the curb.

“I’m going in!”

And she marched for the building at fugitive speed, her whole back radiating retreat.

Song Ji-yeon sat there watching the little rabbit flee into the lobby, and the smile kept widening with nobody around to check it.

What a gap. He’d had Ham Eun-jung filed under bold — the woman had kissed him first, after all. One kiss on the mouth and this was the result? Stage persona and off-stage person weren’t just different; they were different operating systems.

He raised a fingertip and touched his lips. The soft press was apparently still on file there — along with a faint trace of her scent.

He leaned back into the headrest and let it all replay once, unhurried. Then he started the engine, swung the car around, and merged back into traffic.

✦ ✦ ✦

MEANWHILE CCM Entertainment the first-floor lobby

Eun-jung didn’t stop until she was inside the lobby, back against the wall, gulping air and fanning her burning face with one hand.

Mortifying. Mortifying beyond survival. Right in front of the company building—

Every replay of the car reignited the fire. That sneak! That con artist! Tricking an honest woman with a trick like that!

She cursed him through a hundred silent iterations — and somewhere around the fiftieth, her mouth corners started sneaking upward entirely without authorization. She slapped her own cheeks lightly with both palms. Calm down. CALM down.

She dug out her phone and opened the front camera — and the screen handed her back a face flushed from cheekbone to cheekbone.

Even she was startled. This red? Anyone who saw her would assume a fever of real consequence.

She pressed the backs of her hands to her cheeks and held them there, the coolness soaking in like mercy.

She was mid-cooling when a familiar voice arrived from the side.

“Eun-jung-unnie? What are you doing standing there?”

Every muscle locked. She whipped around.

Park Hyo-min stood a few steps off, studying her with wide-open curiosity.

“Hyo… Hyo-min-ah.” One hand flew up on reflex, shield position, guarding the evidence.

“Unnie, why is your face so red?” Hyo-min closed the distance in two steps and leaned in for a closer look, inquiry mode fully engaged. “Red as a monkey’s backside. What exactly have you been doing?”

“Is… is it?” Eun-jung’s gaze slid off toward a decorative plant. “N— no, it isn’t. It’s probably… the weather. Too hot outside.”

“Hot?” The disbelief was its own performance. Hyo-min looked pointedly back at the entrance. “It’s actually a lovely cool morning. Not remotely hot. Are you sure you’re not running a fever?”

And up came the hand, aiming for her forehead.

“I am NOT!”

A cat with its tail stepped on, executing an emergency one-step retreat.

The reaction was, generously, three sizes too large. Hyo-min’s suspicion compounded by the second.

“Really no fever?” Eyes narrowing now, scanning her top to bottom like customs. “Then why the red face? And why are you panting? You came by car, didn’t you? You look like you just ran the 800 meters.”

“I— I just—” The brain sprinted for cover and grabbed the first thing it found. “I just got out of the car too quickly! Yes! Got out too fast!”

She heard the excuse hit the floor and lie there. Even she wouldn’t have bought that.

Hyo-min, visibly, bought none of it. She folded her arms and settled into the full go on, keep digging posture.

“Got out fast? Fast enough to bloom peach blossoms all over your face? Because I distinctly watched you climb out of a certain someone’s car just now…”

She had, in fact, watched the entire transaction. She’d hopped out of a taxi at the curb and spotted the Mercedes instantly — she’d seen that car several times now; a car like that files itself in the memory — and she’d been about to walk over and say hello when the passenger door popped open and out came Eun-jung, flustered as a fugitive in a drama. Her heart had dropped a full floor on the spot. All this time she’d assumed Park Ji-yeon’s gossip was, professionally speaking, fiction. Apparently not.

Eun-jung’s heart dropped a floor of its own. Her fingers rose, traitorously, to her lips.

That kiss…

It was only a second — but did she SEE?

“Nothing happened! You saw wrong!”

“I saw exactly right.” No retreat. If anything Hyo-min leaned in closer, lowering her voice into interrogation range. “Confess. What were you and Song Ji-yeon-ssi doing in that car that isn’t suitable for minors?”

“We weren’t doing anything!”

She was seconds from tearing up out of pure frustration. There was one plan left on the board and it was LEAVE.

“Okay — okay, enough, I’m done — I have a meeting!” She shoved past Hyo-min and struck out for the elevators at march speed.

“Yah, unnie, don’t run away!” Delight pouring out behind her. “You still haven’t explained the red face!”

Eun-jung performed a full deafness. The elevator opened; she flashed inside—

“Eun-jung-unnie. Did you forget? We’re in the same group. Same meeting. And this company has exactly one elevator…”

Hyo-min stepped in after her, savoring each word, voice brimming with laughter, and pressed the button for their floor.

Eun-jung clapped a hand over her own face. Betrayed by heaven, abandoned by earth, outmaneuvered by architecture. Of every possible escape route, she had chosen the one with the door that reopened at her own floor. And Park Hyo-min — the company’s chief director of gossip operations — was not now, not ever, going to simply let something like this go.

The elevator climbed with unbearable dignity. Hyo-min took up station against one wall, side-eyeing her the whole way — those pretty fox eyes working at X-ray capacity, reading the case file straight off her bones.

“Unnie. Just confess. It’ll feel better.”

One head, two sizes too big. “What do you want from me?”

“Nothing much.” The doors opened; they walked out together toward the meeting room, and an elbow found her ribs in transit. “Confess properly. Are you dating someone?”

“NO!”

Denial at full volume, before the question had even finished landing.

“Still no?” A disbelieving smirk. “Unnie. Look at yourself. You’re glowing like the first page of a romance drama — who exactly is that fooling? Is it Song Ji-yeon-ssi? I saw his car out front with my own eyes, and you came flying out of it in a state. Textbook up-to-no-good.”

Park Hyo-min’s instincts were famous for a reason. And as longtime custodian of the dorm’s substantial study-materials library, she had consumed enough content of every rating to reconstruct this exact scene — the car, the kiss, the sprint — with her eyes closed.

Eun-jung’s heart skipped. Her mouth, however, held the line to the last.

“It’s not! Stop guessing!”

“Tch. Stubborn.” Hyo-min’s mouth twisted sideways. “Fine, keep your secrets. As if I need you to tell me. Who else on this Peninsula could turn our Eun-jung-unnie’s face that color? Famously impossible standards, our unnie — and she blushes like this for just anybody?”

The two of them talked their way to the meeting-room door. Eun-jung stopped, turned, and put on her most serious face — the one reserved for contract negotiations and group votes.

“Hyo-min. This morning — you tell no one. Especially not Ji-yeon. And especially not Qri-unnie.”

Because Park Ji-yeon could not keep a secret in a bank vault, and Lee Qri’s eyes were simply too fine — one pass across Eun-jung’s face this morning and the entire case would be reconstructed, footnoted, and distributed.

Hyo-min took in that grave expression and nearly laughed out loud on the spot. Ji-yeon? Ji-yeon had probably cracked this case before either of the parties had — she’d been trading intelligence on it just yesterday, complete with Song Ji-yeon asking around for Eun-jung’s home address. But sell out a little sister? Never. Let Eun-jung-unnie enjoy her little secret a while longer.

Hmph. Dating a man and keeping it from us?

Then suffer. Serves you right.

She had, in fact, already drafted three separate ways to share today’s major discovery with Park Ji-yeon. And the beautiful part — the part she was genuinely savoring — was that Eun-jung-unnie had never admitted a single thing. She didn’t have to. Keep this morning between us was the confession. Certified. Notarized. Time-stamped.

“Don’t worry. I know the score.” A pause — and the gossip engine, briefly idling, roared back to life. “But… how far have you two—”

Eun-jung’s face went up in flames for the hundredth time that morning.

She shoved the meeting-room door open and walked straight in, granting no comment — leaving the wicked laughter trailing in behind her like a tail.

Keep hiding, Eun-jung-unnie!
